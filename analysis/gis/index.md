## What General Mills is

General Mills sells packaged food and pet food through grocery and foodservice channels. It is a business of familiar brands that is losing volume and earnings at the same time. Fiscal 2026 net sales were $18.42 billion, down 5.4% from $19.49 billion, adjusted operating profit fell 16.1% to $2.81 billion and adjusted earnings per share fell 15.7% to $3.55.

Reported results are far worse than adjusted ones. GAAP operating profit was $886 million against $3.30 billion a year earlier, and GAAP earnings per share were negative $0.16, after $1.80 billion of goodwill and brand impairments (including $1.5 billion on the North America Pet reporting unit) and a $1.03 billion valuation loss on the planned Brazil divestiture. Those charges are non-cash and I do not subtract them from cash flow. They do tell you what the company paid for some of its past acquisitions and what it now thinks they are worth.

Management guides fiscal 2027 to a further decline: adjusted operating profit down 8% to 13% in constant currency, adjusted earnings per share of $3.00 to $3.20, organic sales between down 1.5% and up 0.5%. It plans at least $750 million of cost savings in the year, toward a $3 billion cumulative target through fiscal 2030. The business is being asked to shrink profit while savings hold the cash together.

If cash stays near $1.5 billion and the dividend near $1.3 billion, the room left to repay debt is about $0.2 billion a year. At that rate leverage does not come down by paying it off. Net interest rose to $538.6 million for the year and to $142.2 million in the first quarter, so cheaper debt is being replaced with dearer debt. I did not have the full debt maturity ladder or the lease liabilities, and both are listed under data limits.

## The primary valuation: owner cash times a multiple

The primary method capitalizes fiscal 2026 owner cash of $2.876 a share. The value is cash times a multiple, and the multiple is where the whole valuation sits, so here is what each one assumes.

| Scenario | Multiple | Implied cash yield | Implied perpetual growth at a 10% discount rate | Value per share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear | 8.0 times | 12.5% | -2.5% a year | $23.01 |
| Base | 11.8 times | 8.5% | +1.5% a year | $33.93 |
| Bull | 15.3 times | 6.5% | +3.5% a year | $44.00 |

The 10% rate is my assumption and a simplification (value equals cash divided by the rate less growth). It is there to show the reader what the multiples assume, not to claim precision. In the Base, owner cash has to stop falling and grow 1.5% a year indefinitely, from a base that guidance says will be about 3% lower in fiscal 2027. That needs the savings programme to offset inflation and reinvestment, and volume to stabilize. The Bear allows a persistent 2.5% annual decline. The Bull requires volume recovery with profit, which nothing in the current guidance shows.

Reinvestment is low, with capital spending about equal to depreciation, so little of the value depends on new projects earning a return. The weak point is leverage: net debt is 3.9 times operating profit plus depreciation, and interest takes about 19% of adjusted operating profit ($538.6 million of $2,811.5 million). The multiples are a starting point, not a table default, and the Bear is not a floor. If cash settled at $1.4 billion and the market paid 6 times, the shares would be worth $15.62, 52% below the price. I show that as a stress and not as a scenario.

All value in this method sits in the capitalized figure: there is no explicit forecast period, so the continuation value is 100% of the total and the dividend of about $1.3 billion a year is not modeled separately. The dated check below puts the first 19 months on a calendar.

## What the price already assumes

At $32.57 and 537.9 million shares the market value is $17.52 billion, or 11.3 times fiscal 2026 owner cash. At a multiple of 12 times, the price needs $1.46 billion of sustainable owner cash, 5.6% below last year and 3.0% below what guidance implies for fiscal 2027. At 11.8 times it needs $1.48 billion. In plain terms, the market is not paying for a recovery. It is paying for a stop.

That makes the downside case hard to ignore as well. If owner cash falls at the rate of the last four years, 12.3% a year, then fiscal 2028 would be about $1.3 billion, and the Bear value of $23 would look more like an expectation than a stress.

## How the buy price is set

Confidence C calls for a margin of safety of 35% to 50%. I use 40% with no add-ons, because leverage, opacity and the terminal dependence are already in the multiples and in Confidence, and the framework does not let me count the same risk twice. The maximum buy price is $34 x (1 - 40%) = **$20.40**. Even at a 30% margin of safety the ceiling would be $23.80, still 27% below the price. It is a decision reference, not an order to place.

Three things are reported separately: what the shares are worth ($34), the modeled return from buying at $32.57 on the dated Base (12.2% a year, assuming the exit multiple holds), and the price that earns a chosen return. At a required 10% the dated Base gives $33.54. The 12.2% is not an invitation. It depends on the unchanged multiple, and a price with a 40% margin of safety is $20.40.

## The dividend

The quarterly dividend is $0.61, $2.44 a year, a yield of 7.49% at $32.57. In fiscal 2026 dividends of $1.315 billion were 80.9% of free cash flow of $1.626 billion and 85.0% of owner cash. On the fiscal 2027 estimate the payout is 82% of free cash flow (cover of 1.21 times). The first quarter's dividend of $330.5 million was more than its free cash flow of $207.3 million, which is a seasonal pattern as much as a warning.

A high yield is a sign of risk as much as a promise of return. The market is asking for 7.5% because it expects either a cut or a falling price. If cash stays near $1.5 billion, the dividend leaves $0.2 billion to repay $13.6 billion of debt. I do not add the dividend to the value because the owner cash method already values the cash once, and I make no claim about whether the board will cut.

## Reasons to own

- **Brands that sell.** Organic net sales were flat in the first quarter, and guidance for the year spans down 1.5% to up 0.5%, so volume is not collapsing.
- **Cash generation survives the earnings decline.** Free cash flow was $1.63 billion in a year that included $1.8 billion of impairments, and capital needs are modest at about $540 million.
- **Savings are large against profit.** At least $750 million in fiscal 2027 is 27% of fiscal 2026 adjusted operating profit. If they hold while volume stabilizes, owner cash could be flat.
- **The price is already low.** At 11.3 times owner cash and near the bottom of the 52-week range, the market is not paying for a recovery. A stop in the decline is worth something.
- **Financing is in place.** A $2.7 billion undrawn facility to 2029, covenant compliance and the early 2027 maturities already replaced.

## Main risks

- **Volume erosion.** Adjusted operating profit fell 16% in fiscal 2026, 11% in the first quarter, and is guided down a further 8% to 13%. Guidance has organic sales roughly flat, so the profit decline comes mostly from costs and reinvestment, not from lost sales.
- **Debt of $13.6 billion.** Net debt is 3.9 times operating profit plus depreciation and, on guidance, rises to about 4.3 times. Refinancing at higher coupons raises interest of $538.6 million further.
- **Acquisition economics.** $1.8 billion of goodwill and brand impairments in one year, including $1.5 billion on Pet, say something about what the company paid. The annual report flags a risk of decreasing coverage on the Blue Buffalo and Progresso brands, and the carrying values were not available to me.
- **Dividend coverage.** The dividend takes 81% to 85% of cash flow, so there is little room for a bad year. A cut would hurt the shares even if it is the right decision for the balance sheet.
- **Savings that do not reach profit.** At least $750 million of savings has to offset inflation, price investment and volume. Management also flags a nine-point drag on operating profit from the 53rd week, incentive pay and divestitures.
- **Valuation construction.** All the value sits in a capitalized figure, and the multiple is a judgment. The Bear is not a floor.
- **Currency.** For an investor who does not hold dollars, the return also depends on the exchange rate.

## Management execution

The record is mixed. Management has delivered the lower numbers it guided to, and the first quarter was consistent with the full-year outlook, which it reaffirmed. But adjusted earnings per share fell 16% in fiscal 2026 and 13% in the first quarter, and the acquisition record is weakened by the Pet impairment. I rate management credibility 10 of 20 (provisional, my estimate) and management and allocation 6 of 10 inside business quality. The buyback was cut from $1.2 billion in fiscal 2025 to $0.5 billion in fiscal 2026 and to none in the first quarter, which is the right call with leverage at 3.9 times.

## What I would watch from here

### Supports the thesis

- Organic sales above 0%, adjusted earnings per share at or above $3.20, and annual free cash flow at or above $1.7 billion.
- Dividend cover above 1.5 times and net debt falling.
- Pet economics recovering with no new impairments.

### Watch closely

- Organic sales between -1.5% and 0%, adjusted earnings per share of $3.00 to $3.20, free cash flow of $1.4 billion to $1.7 billion (guidance implies about $1.58 billion).
- Dividend cover of 1.1 to 1.5 times (my estimate for fiscal 2027 is 1.21) and net debt flat.
- Mixed Pet results.

### Breaks the thesis

- Organic sales below -1.5% repeatedly, or adjusted earnings per share below $3.00.
- Free cash flow below $1.4 billion, dividend cover below 1.1 times, or net debt rising while cash falls.
- New impairments in Pet. The entry test is also red today: the price is far above $20.40.

The thresholds are my monitoring triggers, not company guidance. The next review is the second quarter of fiscal 2027.

## The thesis breaker

Continuing cash decline on top of the debt. If owner cash keeps falling at the pace of the last four years while the dividend stays near $1.3 billion, either the dividend is cut or deleveraging stops, and in both cases the Base multiple of 11.8 times is too high.

## Final verdict: hold at $32.57

The yield is the story, and it is the part to be careful with. A 7.5% payout from a business whose cash fell 29.7% last year is a price, not a gift. The shares are worth about what they cost today, and the safety you would want is another 40% lower.
