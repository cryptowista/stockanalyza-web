## What Applied Optoelectronics is

Applied Optoelectronics (AOI, ticker AAOI) grows its own laser chips and builds them into optical transceivers, the modules that turn electrical signals into light for data center links at 400G, 800G and 1.6T speeds. It also sells amplifiers and other equipment to cable operators (CATV) and a small amount to telecom. Engineering and manufacturing sit in Sugar Land, Texas, Taipei and Ningbo, China, with research in Atlanta. In the second quarter Datacenter revenue was $107.7 million (up 140.4%), CATV was $80.6 million (up 43.8%) and Telecom was $3.4 million.

Management's capacity numbers are the story. Combined 800G and 1.6T capacity was close to 100,000 units a month at the end of the first quarter and is approaching 200,000 now. The targets are more than 650,000 units a month by the end of 2026 and more than 930,000 by the end of 2027, with over half of that in Texas. The company also holds more than $200 million of 1.6T orders and expects more than $70 million of 1.6T revenue in the fourth quarter.

Concentration is the other side of it. In the second quarter one CATV customer was 42% of revenue and two data center customers were 26% and 24%, so three customers were 92%. The top ten were 99%. Management says demand will outpace production through mid-2027. With three buyers behind 92% of sales, that statement describes three purchasing plans, and the customers are not named. A warrant held by Amazon covers 7.9 million shares at $23.6956. Of those, 6.6 million vest only if Amazon and its affiliates buy $4 billion of products over up to ten years.

## Why GAAP earnings and headline cash flow cannot be used

GAAP earnings were negative in every year from 2021 to 2025, and non-GAAP net income turned positive only in the second quarter ($5.5 million, after a loss of $4.9 million in the first). The hardest metric for this business is free cash flow after capital spending, stock-based compensation (SBC, pay in shares) and the working capital the growth absorbs. The most generous metric is non-GAAP EPS, which excluded $28.3 million of items in the second quarter alone. Free cash flow cannot anchor a value today because it is deeply negative, so I anchor on normalized 2027 EBITDA after deducting the capital still to be spent, and I test it against a ten-year cash flow model.

## Primary valuation: normalized EV/EBITDA

Enterprise value (EV) is market value plus debt minus cash. I apply a multiple to normalized 2027 EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, and before SBC), then add the net position from the bridge above, so that the build needed to produce that EBITDA is paid for. 2027 is not a mid-cycle year. It is the first year in which the announced capacity should be running, which is the best available stand-in. The multiples are assumptions: **13x** in the Bear case, **18x** in the Base and **23x** in the Bull. They sit below what I would give a business with 50% gross margins because this company has a 30% gross margin, no profitable year and three customers behind 92% of revenue. ## Historical cross-check and growth diagnostics

A historical multiple check is not meaningful, because the company has had no positive earnings in the five years of data I have, so the historical weight goes to a forward P/E cross-check. It uses 2027 non-GAAP EPS from my own scenarios, which are $1.61 in the Bear, $4.55 in the Base and $6.60 in the Bull (consensus is $4.60), at 16x, 22x and 28x. That gives $26, $100 and $185. These EPS figures add SBC back, as non-GAAP EPS does.

The growth valuation cross-check (SM-19) is a diagnostic only. The forward P/E is 21.9x on consensus 2027 EPS of $4.60, and consensus 2026 EPS is $0.68, so EPS growth of about 577% gives a PEG of 0.04. That supports nothing. GAAP EPS is negative (-$0.79 over twelve months), the growth comes from a very low base, and non-GAAP excludes real costs. Growth durability: uncertain. Cyclical distortion: high, because AI infrastructure spending is cyclical and the company sells to very few customers. Flags: LOW\_BASE, PEG\_NOT\_MEANINGFUL on GAAP, ACCOUNTING\_BASIS\_MISMATCH. SM-19 does not set fair value.

The free cash flow growth quality check (SM-20) says conventional growth rates are not meaningful, because free cash flow is negative and was -$900.5 million over twelve months. The useful figure is the path to positive free cash flow. In the Base it is about -$1.9 billion in 2026, -$483 million in 2027 and -$31 million in 2028, then $297 million in 2029 (7.7% of revenue) and $605 million in 2030 (14.0%). On the $9.9 billion economic market value, that is a free cash flow yield of -4.9% in 2027, 3.0% in 2029 and 6.1% in 2030. Funding the early years adds about 6.7 million shares, so 2030 free cash flow per share is about $5.75. Stress test: cutting free cash flow from 2029 onward by 20% gives a Base DCF of $48 and by 40% gives $35. Distortion flags: low base, capital spending timing high, working capital high, dilution high. Estimate reliability: low, since management has not guided to total capital spending and consensus is dated. Classification: structural with execution risk, diagnostic only.

## Reasons to own AAOI

- **Demand shows in the guidance.** The third-quarter revenue guide of $255 million to $290 million is 42% above the second quarter at the midpoint, and management expects 800G revenue to rise nearly fivefold sequentially.
- **It makes its own lasers.** Management says in-house lasers helped it avoid shortages, that substrate supply is secured into next year and that inventory covers the rest of 2027. It also plans to expand laser capacity by about 350% by the end of 2027.
- **Operating leverage is large.** Non-GAAP operating expenses of $70 million to $80 million a quarter sit against revenue that goes from $192 million to a guided more than $500 million. At a 32% gross margin and $80 million of expenses, fourth-quarter operating income would be about $80 million (my estimate).
- **The mix points the right way.** 1.6T is the highest-margin product, and management cites margins of 55% to 65% on laser products for co-packaged optics (CPO) and above 50% on EOSAP products later.
- **Liquidity is adequate for now.** About $1.0 billion of liquidity after debt, with the only convertible notes not due until 2030.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: funding and dilution.** The build needs more money than the company has. If the shares fall, the next raise costs more shares, and the 20.8% rise in the share count this year is a precedent.
- **Customer concentration.** Three customers are 92% of revenue and one counterparty owes 67% of receivables. A design loss, a delayed deployment or a pricing negotiation at one of them changes the whole growth path.
- **Capacity and cycle.** Net property rose 85% in six months and inventory rose 52%. If demand normalizes after the equipment is installed, utilization and margins can reverse quickly.
- **Margins.** Non-GAAP gross margin was 29.8% in the quarter and the 40% goal has no date. Management also named constraints on DSPs, TIAs and lasers for 1.6T.
- **Trade and geopolitics.** Production sits partly in Ningbo and Taipei. Tariffs cost $1.9 million in the second quarter, there was a $5.7 million refund after IEEPA tariffs were overturned, and management said it was too early to quantify possible U.S. restrictions on Chinese transceivers.
- **Competition and technology.** New Chinese laser capacity, CPO and silicon photonics can change customer choices. Management put the time for new Chinese capacity to reach comparable quality at two to three years.

## Management execution

Dr. Thompson Lin leads the company and Dr. Stefan Murry is chief financial officer. The record is mixed. On the positive side, revenue has set a record for five straight quarters, 800G volume more than doubled sequentially in the second quarter, and the company gives quarterly guidance and discloses customer concentration. On the other side, the operating loss in every year from 2021 to 2025 ran between $41.4 million and $70.9 million, the share count rose 20.8% in under ten months, and the plan triples 800G and 1.6T capacity in about six months. The 40% gross margin goal has no date and the company gives no total capital spending figure. **Assessment: 9/20, mixed. The next test is whether capacity arrives on schedule without another large raise.**

## Final verdict: hold at $100.68

Applied Optoelectronics has orders, a ramp and a capacity plan that the market is willing to fund. The shares already pay 16.3 times the EBITDA I expect after the money has been spent, and the money has not been spent yet. The buyers in the $600 million ATM paid an average of $105.36, which is 4.6% above today's price.

**Verdict: HOLD at $100.68, do not add. Triangulated Base value $94, downside 6.6%, Confidence D (9/25). The required margin of safety of 75% exceeds the framework's practical ceiling, so no maximum buy price is issued.**

The main risk to this verdict is that the capacity arrives on time, the 1.6T and laser mix lifts gross margin toward 40%, and the company funds the rest with cash flow instead of new shares, which would put the value near my Bull case of $168. New money belongs on the watchlist. Demand is the part of this story that has already been confirmed. The unconfirmed part is how many new shares it takes to build the supply.
