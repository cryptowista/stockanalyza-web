# Schneider Electric: 16.9% organic growth, a $22.6 billion acquisition and 25 million new shares, so hold at €237.50

SU, Euronext Paris. Published November 2, 2026. Price as of the October 30, 2026 regular session close. Values in euros. Latest reported period: third quarter 2026 revenue and first half 2026 results.

## Verdict: HOLD at €237.50

The two methods behind the Base differ by 15%: a five-year earnings value gives €212.73 and an enterprise value to adjusted EBITA cross-check gives €248.43. The new PTC debt of €16 billion to €17 billion roughly doubles net debt, and the buyback stops in 2027 and 2028. A 42.5% margin of safety puts the buy range at €122.32 to €142.85. Strong business, full price.

Schneider Electric reported organic revenue growth of 16.9% for the third quarter eleven days after its shares fell 9.97% in one session on the announcement of a $22.6 billion acquisition of PTC. Four days after the results it sold 25 million new shares at €240. At €237.50 the shares are priced as a data center electrification business that will also run industrial software well, and the second half of that sentence is not in the numbers yet.

At €237.50 on October 30, 2026, Schneider trades 3.0% above my weighted Base of €230.58. The Bear value of €147.31 is 38.0% below the price and the Bull value of €313.85 is 32.1% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. A required margin of safety of 42.5% gives a buy range of €122.32 to €142.85 and a conservative limit of €122.32. The verdict is Hold, and no new money at this price.

Valuation route: a capital-goods group with software and services, valued on five-year earnings power with the PTC purchase counted at what it is worth to Schneider, and cross-checked by enterprise value to adjusted EBITA on 2027 pro forma profit. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What Schneider Electric is

Schneider has two segments. Energy Management (€9.6 billion of third-quarter revenue) sells electrical distribution, power equipment and services, and it is where the data center demand lands. Industrial Automation (€1.8 billion) sells machine and process control and software. By business model, systems are 35% of revenue, products 48% and software and services 17%. North America was 40% of the third quarter.

PTC, announced on October 5, adds engineering and product lifecycle software. Management expects completion by the third quarter of 2027, subject to PTC shareholders and regulators.

Reported growth was 17.9% in the quarter and 12.5% over nine months, because currency took 2.2 points from the nine-month figure. The backlog is a record, but the company gave no amount. For the first half, adjusted EBITA was €4.1 billion, up 22% organically, a margin of 19.3%, up 120 basis points. Net income was €2.5 billion and free cash flow €1.6 billion, both well above last year.

Management raised its 2026 targets in October to organic revenue growth of 12% to 14% and an organic improvement in the adjusted EBITA margin of 100 to 120 basis points. I use the margin implied by that, about 19.8%, and revenue of about €45.2 billion for 2026 after currency of -€300 million to -€400 million.

## What the cash flow is made of

- **Growth is mostly volume.** Management said product growth was predominantly driven by volumes, with the price contribution increasing from the second quarter. So the 15% is not price.
- **Profit is converting.** First-half net income of €2.5 billion was 61% of adjusted EBITA. In 2025 reported net income was €4.2 billion on revenue of €40.2 billion. I use 58% of adjusted EBITA as net income in the model.
- **Free cash flow is rising from a low base.** €1.6 billion in the first half is up 244%, which shows how weak the comparison period was. I did not find a full-year free cash flow figure.
- **Software is the slowest part.** Software and services grew 7% in the quarter against 22% for systems, and PTC is a software purchase.

## Primary valuation: five-year earnings value

Schneider on its own: I project revenue and the adjusted EBITA margin to 2031, convert to net income at 58%, apply an exit multiple to 2031 net income, and add dividends at an assumed 45% payout, all discounted at 9%. Then I add PTC at what I think it is worth, subtract the €16 billion of new debt, and divide by 588.5 million shares. The Base value is **€212.73**.

- **Revenue:** €45.2 billion in 2026, then growth of 9%, 7%, 6%, 5% and 5%. That fades from 15% today toward the pace of electrification demand once the data center build-out matures.
- **Margin:** 19.8% rising to 22.0% by 2031, a gain of about 45 basis points a year, less than the 100 to 120 this year.
- **Exit multiple:** 22 times 2031 net income. Schneider's one-year average forward multiple was 25.8 times and its low, in March 2026, was 22.9 times, so 22 times assumes growth has slowed to 5%.
- **PTC:** worth 90% of the €21.1 billion enterprise value Schneider pays, or €19.0 billion. Management expects returns above the cost of capital only from year five, and one outside estimate puts the return on the purchase price near 8%, below a cost of capital near 9%. The Bear uses 70% and the Bull 110%.

## Secondary valuation: enterprise value to adjusted EBITA

I take 2027 pro forma adjusted EBITA of about €11.1 billion (Schneider on its own plus €1.0 billion from PTC, which is its 2027 EBITA before synergies at the price paid), apply **16 times**, subtract pro forma net debt of €31.4 billion and divide by 588.5 million shares. That gives **€248.43**. 16 times is my judgment, close to 22 times earnings after interest. The Bear uses 13 times on lower profit and the Bull 19 times.

## Growth diagnostic

Growth is high and mostly volume. It is structural in electrification and data centers and cyclical in the capital spending of customers who build them, and the company gives triple-digit growth for data centers only qualitatively. I classify the growth as strong but concentrated in one end market. The share count rose 4.3% in October to pay for an acquisition, so per-share growth for the next two years will trail profit growth.

## Dividend and buybacks

Schneider pays an annual dividend; I did not confirm the amount per share and I model it as 45% of net income. The 2026 buyback of €600 million is on track (€497 million done by October 12) and is then suspended for 2027 and 2028 to protect the rating. The 2026 to 2030 target of €2.5 billion to €3.5 billion is kept, which implies buybacks resume in 2029.

## Reasons to own Schneider Electric

- Organic growth of 15% over nine months with a margin rising by more than 100 basis points a year is rare for a group of this size.
- The backlog is at a record and data center demand grew at triple-digit rates in the quarter.
- Both rating agencies confirmed A-category ratings after the PTC announcement.
- Software and services are 17% of revenue and are expected to rise with PTC, which should add recurring revenue.
- Pricing is starting to contribute, so margin gains are not only operating leverage.

## What could go wrong?

- End-market concentration. Data center demand drives the growth and the capital spending of a small number of customers can change quickly.
- PTC. The purchase price is 21 times 2027 EBITA before synergies, 8 times 2026 revenue and 26 times targeted free cash flow, and the buyer needs year five to beat its cost of capital.
- Leverage. Net debt roughly doubles to about 3 times adjusted EBITDA, with a rating at stake.
- Dilution. 25 million new shares, 4.3% of capital, were sold at €240 and buybacks stop for two years.
- Industrial Automation. Process automation and hybrid markets declined in China, and the segment grew only 8.1%.
- Execution. The company is integrating Cognite and preparing for PTC while the core business grows at 15%.

## Management execution

- The company raised targets twice in 2026 and delivered record revenue in the second and third quarters.
- The acquisition was announced on October 5, before the quarterly release, and the share sale followed within two weeks, which secured the funding but cost the shares about 10% on the day.
- Management kept the 2026 buyback and paused it for 2027 and 2028, which is a clear statement of priorities.
- Guidance for PTC is low single-digit adjusted earnings accretion in year one, before purchase accounting, and mid-to-high single digit with full synergies.

## What I would watch from here

### Green

- Organic growth stays above 12% in the fourth quarter with the margin at or above 19.8%.
- PTC regulatory approvals arrive early and the debt is placed at the expected cost.
- Software and services growth rises above 10%.

### Yellow

- Backlog growth slows while revenue growth stays high.
- North America organic growth falls below 15%.
- The PTC price paid is followed by a second large acquisition.

### Red

- Net debt to adjusted EBITDA heads above 3.5 times.
- Data center orders fall for a quarter.
- The company misses its raised 2026 targets.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Data center capital spending slows before the margin gain is locked in.
2. PTC earns less than its cost of capital past year five.
3. Leverage and the buyback pause cost the company its A rating.

## Final verdict: hold at €237.50, do not add

Schneider is turning a data center electrification business into an industrial software company, and shareholders pay for the change twice: once with 25 million new shares and again through a two-year buyback pause.

**Verdict: HOLD at €237.50, do not add. Weighted Base €230.58, 2.9% below the price, cross-check €248.43, Confidence C (17/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, buy range €122.32 to €142.85, conservative buy limit €122.32.**

For an existing holder, the original cost is irrelevant. The business is strong and the price is about fair, so I would not sell on valuation alone and I would not add. New money waits for a price at or below €142.85. The next tests are the full-year results in February and the PTC shareholder vote.
