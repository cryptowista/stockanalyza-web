In September Broadcom said its AI chip revenue would be about $58 billion in fiscal 2026, about $115 billion in fiscal 2027 and about $230 billion in fiscal 2028, and that it had secured the supply to deliver it. Two months later, on November 5, the stock closed at $291.53, down 10.4% on the day and 39% below its June high of $481.57. Nothing came from Broadcom that day. Chip stocks fell across the board, and Trefis linked the drop to fears that newly elected governors in Texas, Ohio and Georgia would slow data center construction.

At $291.53, Broadcom trades 14.9% below my Base value of $342.39. The Bear value is $171.30, 41.2% below the price, and the Bull value, which is management's own AI path, is $529.44, 81.6% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The verdict is Hold, with new money waiting for $196.88 or less.

Valuation route: a semiconductor and infrastructure software company with very high free cash flow, now dominated by custom AI accelerators for a few large customers. The primary method is a ten-year free cash flow model after stock-based pay; the cross-check is a forward multiple of enterprise value to fiscal 2028 operating profit after stock pay. Both use the same estimated debt and the same share count. Broadcom's fiscal year ends in early November, so fiscal 2027 began on November 2, 2026. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Broadcom is

Broadcom has two halves. Semiconductors were 70% of third quarter revenue: custom AI accelerators (which it calls XPUs) designed with customers such as Google, whose TPU chips Broadcom co-develops, Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta, the Ethernet switches and optical parts that connect them, and a steady non-AI business in broadband, storage and wireless chips. Infrastructure software was the other 30%, mostly VMware, bought in 2023, plus mainframe and security software at an operating margin of about 84%.

The shape of the company is changing quickly. AI was 56% of revenue in the third quarter, up from 49% in the second, and on management's outlook it will be about two thirds in fiscal 2027.

The third quarter beat its own AI guidance of $16.0 billion, and the fourth quarter guide of $34.8 billion of revenue is 93% above last year. The cost of that growth shows in the gross margin: whole racks of accelerators and more memory content carry lower margins than chips, so the guide of about 73% compares with 78% a year ago. Operating margin holds at about 66% because expenses grow far more slowly than revenue.

Broadcom also helps customers pay for the hardware. In June it set up a financing platform with Apollo and Blackstone aimed at more than 20 gigawatts of computing capacity for OpenAI and Anthropic by the end of 2028; the first $35 billion tranche funds Anthropic's first gigawatt. Management calls its residual value guarantees "modest" but has not put a number on them.

Which numbers to trust

  • Free cash flow is real and large. $31.9 billion in nine months, 45% of revenue, with capital spending of only 1.4% of revenue. It rises in the fourth quarter, about $1.4 billion guided, as Broadcom adds substrate capacity; I use 1.5% of a much larger revenue base.
  • Stock-based pay is a cost. About $8.4 billion in fiscal 2026 (my estimate for the full year), around 8% of revenue. The company's operating income excludes it; I deduct it and assume it falls to 4% of revenue as sales grow faster than headcount.
  • Amortization is not a cash cost. The gap between GAAP and adjusted profit is mostly stock pay and amortization of intangible assets from the VMware deal. I ignore the amortization and count capital spending instead.
  • The tax rate is low. Broadcom guides a 16% adjusted tax rate. I use 17% for fiscal 2027 and 18% after that.

After those adjustments, fiscal 2027 operating profit in my Base is $89.6 billion and free cash flow to the whole company is $69.6 billion.

Debt, shares and capital returns

Broadcom has been paying down the VMware debt: $5.6 billion in the third quarter and $1.5 billion more after it. Gross fixed-rate debt was $59.6 billion with an average coupon of 4% and 7.4 years to maturity. Adding the fourth quarter's expected cash flow and the dividend, I estimate net debt of about $21.5 billion on November 1, roughly a third of a year's operating profit.

There were 4.77 billion shares on August 28; the adjusted diluted count is about 4.94 billion. Buybacks of $8.45 billion in the first nine months, mostly at prices above today's, kept the count roughly flat. The quarterly dividend is $0.65 a share.

Primary valuation: ten years of free cash flow

The Base does not take management's AI outlook at face value. It assumes $105 billion of AI revenue in fiscal 2027 (91% of the outlook) and $175 billion in fiscal 2028 (76%), with other chips and software growing slowly. Total revenue goes from about $106 billion in fiscal 2026 to $158.5 billion and $230.5 billion, then growth slows to 10% in fiscal 2029, 6% in 2030 and 3.5% to 4% after that. Operating margin before stock pay drifts down from 62% to 55% as racks, memory and customer bargaining power weigh on it. I discount at 9.5% a year with 3% growth after fiscal 2036.

Secondary valuation: a forward multiple

The cross-check applies 14 times to fiscal 2028 operating profit after stock pay of $127.9 billion, as a value at the end of fiscal 2027, discounted back one year. That gives an enterprise value of $1,635.6 billion and $338.12 a share.

Why 14 times? At my cash conversion it is a free cash flow yield of about 5.6%, which at a 9.5% cost of capital is consistent with growth of about 3.7% a year forever. That fits a business whose growth fades to mid-single digits after the AI build-out. I did not build a dated peer comparison for this analysis, so the multiple rests on that logic rather than on peers. The market currently pays 11.0 times the same number.

History and earnings check

The stock closed as high as $481.57 on June 2, 2026, then fell 12.6% in a day on June 4 and has slid since; November 5 was its lowest close in a year. On trailing revenue it still looks expensive, about 15.9 times enterprise value to sales, because trailing revenue lags the AI ramp. On forward earnings it does not: $291.53 is 17.9 times my Base adjusted earnings per share for fiscal 2027 ($16.32) and 12.6 times fiscal 2028 ($23.13). Management talks about fiscal 2028 earnings above $30 a share, which would put the price below 10 times. The growth-adjusted price to earnings ratio is about 0.4, but this is a cyclical boom in AI spending, so I treat that as a flag, not as evidence of cheapness.

Valuation triangulation

  • Cash flow model (weight 60%): $345.24.
  • Forward multiple (weight 40%): $338.12.
  • Base value: $342.39, inside the method range of $338.12 to $345.24.
  • The cash flow model gets more weight because it captures the fade after the AI build-out and the cost of stock pay year by year.

With my margins and my growth after 2028, the price fits about $141 billion of AI revenue in fiscal 2028, roughly 61% of what management has guided and about $85 billion in fiscal 2027. In other words, the market is either assuming Broadcom misses its own plan by a wide margin or that the AI business will be far less profitable than today. Either can happen. Neither is what the company is reporting right now.

Where the return would come from: if the market keeps paying 15.8 times forward operating profit, the Base gives about 22.1% a year to the end of fiscal 2029 including dividends, from profit growth and the cash that piles up. In the Bear, the same multiple gives -1.5% a year. This stock is a bet on the AI number, not on the multiple.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is C, which carries a margin of safety of 42.5%. The grade is lower than the quality of the business would suggest because the model leans on two years of AI revenue from a few customers, depends on power and financing outside Broadcom's control, and I have no up-to-date peer comparison. That gives a maximum buy price of $196.88.

Twenty percent more or less AI revenue in fiscal 2028 moves the Base by $51.19 a share, the largest single effect at 15% of the value. A point of discount rate moves it by $29.10 to $39.26, three points of operating margin by $19.76, two turns of multiple by $19.58 and one point more stock pay by -$6.59. Each extra $10 billion of net debt costs $2.09. If stock pay were ignored, the Base would be $370.53; with management's full AI path and my margins, $423.39.

For reference on the other side: at about $345 the expected return under my Base falls below the 9.5% cost of capital. That is where I would review the position, not a target.

Dividend

The dividend is $0.65 a quarter, $2.60 a year, a yield of 0.9%. In the first nine months of fiscal 2026 it cost $9.28 billion, 29% of free cash flow, so it is well covered. Broadcom has historically raised it with the fourth quarter results in December. It is a nice extra, not the reason to own the stock.

Reasons to own Broadcom

  • AI revenue of $16.7 billion in the third quarter, up 221%, guided to $21.7 billion in the fourth, with an outlook of about $115 billion in fiscal 2027.
  • Free cash flow of $13.7 billion in a single quarter, 46% of revenue, with almost no capital spending.
  • Named multi-year customers: Google, Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta, with gigawatt-scale plans for 2027 and 2028 and supply that management says is secured.
  • A software business of about $35 billion a year at an 84% operating margin.
  • The price assumes only about 61% of the guided fiscal 2028 AI revenue.

What could go wrong?

  • Concentration. A few customers drive most of the AI revenue, and their spending depends on financing, power and land as much as on demand.
  • The cycle. AI infrastructure spending has doubled year after year; a digestion year would hit revenue hard, as in my Bear.
  • Financing exposure. The Apollo and Blackstone platform comes with residual value guarantees that management has not quantified.
  • Margins. Racks and memory content lower gross margin, and large customers have bargaining power and their own chip teams.
  • Software. VMware growth was helped by price increases and is expected to slow.

Management execution

  • Third quarter AI revenue of $16.7 billion beat the $16.0 billion guide, and the fiscal 2026 AI outlook rose from $56 billion to about $58 billion.
  • The VMware purchase turned into a software business with an 84% operating margin, and the debt taken on for it is being repaid.
  • Adjusted earnings per share rose 96% in the third quarter.
  • Buybacks of $8.45 billion this year were done mostly at prices above today's.
  • Adjusted profit excludes stock pay of about 8% of revenue, and the financing platform's guarantees are not quantified.

On my ten-point management check (two points each), Broadcom scores 17 of 20. Execution and capital allocation are excellent; disclosure of the customer financing could be better.

What I would watch from here

The fourth quarter report in December decides the next leg. It has to show revenue of at least $34.8 billion and AI revenue of at least $21.7 billion, a gross margin near 73%, fiscal 2027 commentary that still points to about $115 billion of AI revenue, and, ideally, a number on the financing guarantees.

Green

  • AI revenue on track for $105 billion or more in fiscal 2027.
  • Gross margin of 72% or more.
  • Stock pay falling below 5% of revenue.

Yellow

  • AI revenue tracking between $90 and $105 billion.
  • Gross margin between 68% and 72%.
  • Software growth slowing to between 5% and 15%.

Red

  • AI revenue tracking below $90 billion for fiscal 2027.
  • Gross margin below 68%.
  • Financing guarantees that turn into debt-like exposure.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Fiscal 2027 AI revenue tracking below $90 billion, which leads toward the Bear.
  2. The financing platform's guarantees turning out to be large, which adds a debt-like claim to every case.
  3. The second quarter of fiscal 2027 confirming the path to about $230 billion in fiscal 2028, which leads toward the Bull.

The weakest point is the fiscal 2028 AI number: it carries most of the value and comes from a few customers whose plans depend on things Broadcom does not control. The strongest bull argument is that the price assumes a miss of almost 40% on an outlook management says is backed by secured supply and named customers. The strongest bear argument is that every AI build-out so far has been followed by a pause, and the stock's own history shows how fast the market reprices one.

Final verdict: hold at $291.53

Broadcom is one of the best businesses in this archive, and at $291.53 it is priced as if its AI plan will fall well short. That is a reasonable worry for a company whose revenue now rides on a few customers' data center budgets, but it is not what the numbers say today. The stock is cheaper than my Base, not cheap enough for the uncertainty.

Verdict: HOLD at $291.53, watchlist for new money. Base $342.39, 17.4% above the price, Bear $171.30, Bull $529.44, Confidence C (17.5/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $196.88.

New money buys only at or below $196.88. For an existing holder the original cost is irrelevant; the price is below fair value and the December report will show whether fiscal 2027 is on track. Broadcom does not need to hit $230 billion of AI revenue to be worth more than $291.53. It needs to get well past $141 billion.
