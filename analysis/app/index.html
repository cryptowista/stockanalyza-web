AppLovin reported third-quarter revenue of $2.19 billion, up 55%, a profit margin of 66% and free cash flow of $1.4 billion. Revenue came in about $100 million above the top of its own guidance, and the shares jumped 23% after hours on November 4. They closed at $398.94 on November 17, and at $358.27 today, down another 5%, they are about 52% below the September 2025 high of $745.61. The market is not arguing about last quarter. It is arguing about whether a business that earns 78 cents of operating profit on every dollar of revenue can keep that margin and keep growing.

I value AppLovin on owner free cash flow after stock pay, and the answer is above the price: $464.92 in my Base case, which is 30% above $358.27. Revenue grows 30% in 2027, as management's own long-term ambition says, and then slows to 10% by 2031; the operating margin after stock pay slips from 78.5% today to 73% as pay and computing costs rise. The Bear value of $109.85 assumes that the gains from new advertising models fade and customers move budgets elsewhere: revenue falls from 2029 and the margin drops to 45%. The Bull value of $743.40 assumes growth of 40% in 2027 and a margin that stays near 79%. I assign no probabilities. Confidence is grade C, because the whole result depends on a model-driven advertising engine whose results are volatile and partly disclosed. That sets the required margin of safety at 42.5% and the maximum buy price at $267.33. The price is 34% above it, so the verdict is hold, not buy.

Valuation route: a high-margin advertising software platform, valued on owner free cash flow: GAAP operating profit (which already deducts stock pay), less cash tax at a normalized 20% rate, plus depreciation and amortization, less capital spending and working capital. Primary method: a ten-year path with a terminal value, discounted at 10%. Cross-check: a multiple of 2031 owner free cash flow. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What AppLovin is

AppLovin, based in Palo Alto, California, sells the software that matches advertisers with mobile apps. Its machine-learning engine, Axon, bids for ad space inside mobile games and increasingly in online shops, and AppLovin keeps a share of the spend; it also runs MAX, which helps app developers sell their ad space, and Adjust, which measures campaigns. It reports one segment, and the advertising platform produces substantially all of the revenue. The United States is 50% of revenue and the rest of the world 50%.

Growth now comes from two places. Gaming grew nearly 50% in the third quarter. Spending from e-commerce and other consumer advertisers, the new vertical, grew by double digits from the previous quarter and by triple digits from a year ago, though management gave no dollar figure (ppc.land, from the quarterly report). In the quarter, revenue per install rose 55% on 2% more installs: the company is earning more from each advertisement, not showing many more of them. Over nine months, revenue per install is up 68% and installs are down 7%.

The path has not been smooth. In August, when AppLovin reported second-quarter revenue of $1.92 billion and guided below expectations, management said the usual model improvement had not arrived, and the shares fell about 19% in the session (StockStory). In the third quarter a partial rollout of a new, more complex model helped, and management said further gains are possible but gave no date for a full rollout (Quartr). Investors have also noticed a securities class action filed on September 16, covering February 12 to August 5, which alleges that AppLovin misled investors about the pace of model improvement and the timeline for an ad-generation tool; the company says the claims lack merit and cannot estimate any exposure.

Owner free cash flow

Receivables rose from $1,819 million to $2,389 million in nine months, and nine-month operating cash flow of $3,589 million was well below net income plus stock pay and depreciation, so I charge the working capital at a high rate.

Buybacks and no dividend

AppLovin pays no dividend. It returns cash through buybacks: $2.8 billion in the first nine months of 2026, $1.4 billion of it in the third quarter at an average of about $340 a share, and $9.4 billion since the program began at an average of $90. The company says it has returned 85% of free cash flow. The board added $3.2 billion in October to bring the authorization to $3.6 billion, and the nine-month buyback of $2.8 billion is about 78% of the $3.6 billion of operating cash flow in the same period. At today's price, the $3.6 billion authorization would retire about 3% of the shares. The buyback helps per-share value only if the price paid is below value, which is why I do not count future repurchases in my share count.

Reasons to own AppLovin

  • The business converts revenue to cash at a rate few companies match: an 84% adjusted EBITDA margin, a 78% GAAP operating margin and free cash flow equal to 77% of adjusted EBITDA in the quarter.
  • Growth is real and accelerating: revenue rose 55% in the third quarter against 53% in the second, and the fourth-quarter guide is 43% to 44%.
  • There is a second engine: consumer and e-commerce advertisers are growing at triple digits from a small base.
  • The balance sheet is clean: net debt of $0.4 billion and no note maturities before 2029.
  • The price does not assume a perfect outcome: my Base cash flows reach it at a discount rate of 13% or a margin 17 points lower.

What could go wrong?

  • Model dependence. A single partial model rollout lifted revenue by $100 million above guidance in a quarter, and the absence of one in the second quarter caused a miss. Revenue can therefore surprise in either direction, and management gave no date for a full rollout.
  • Margin. A 78% margin invites competition and regulation; my Bear case, at $109.85, is 69% below the price.
  • Litigation. Two securities class actions and related derivative suits are open; the company cannot estimate any exposure, and no ruling on the motion to dismiss in the 2025 case had been reported.
  • Quality of earnings. Stock pay is rising quickly, especially in research and development, cash taxes have risen sharply, and working capital absorbs cash as revenue grows.
  • Fourth-quarter slowdown. Guidance implies sequential growth of 8% to 10% after 14% in the third quarter; a weak first quarter of 2027 would be read as the end of the model gains.
  • Governance. Class B shares carry 20 votes each, and two directors ended their planned share sales in August and September, which is a small positive but does not change the structure.
  • Data gaps. Capital spending, segment detail inside gaming and e-commerce, customer concentration, competitor data and the detail of the cloud contract were not available to me.

Management execution

Adam Foroughi, the chief executive, and Matt Stumpf, the chief financial officer, have built AppLovin into an advertising platform with an 84% adjusted EBITDA margin, returned most of the cash to shareholders and bought most of the third-quarter shares at $311 to $317 after the August fall. The offsets: a revenue miss in the second quarter and two securities class actions that name both of them. Assessment: 14/20, strong on margins, capital return and product, weaker on forecasting and disclosure. The next test is the fourth-quarter report, which should show whether the model gains hold.

What I would watch from here

The next results are for the fourth quarter, expected around February 10, 2027 (the date is not confirmed by the company). They need to show revenue near $2.38 billion and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 84% to 85%.

Green: moves me toward the buy zone

  • Revenue at or above the fourth-quarter guide, with a first-quarter 2027 guide that implies growth above 35%.
  • A full rollout of the new models with the GAAP operating margin staying near 78%.
  • A price at or below $267.33, or e-commerce revenue disclosed in dollars and growing.

Yellow

  • Revenue per install flat or falling while installs stay flat.
  • Stock pay rising faster than revenue.
  • Cash taxes or working capital absorbing more than 25% of operating profit.

Red

  • A revenue miss against guidance, or growth below 30% in the first half of 2027.
  • A GAAP operating margin below 70%.
  • A court ruling or settlement with material cost, or new regulation of advertising targeting.

Today profit, cash conversion and balance sheet are green; litigation, model dependence and the fourth-quarter slowdown are yellow, and the price is yellow, because it is below my Base but well above my buy price. That is why the verdict is hold: the business is worth more than the price, and my rules ask for a margin of safety of 42.5% on a case this wide.

The three things that would make this verdict wrong

  1. The margin falls further than my path. At 10 points lower in every year the value is $403.62, still above the price; the Bear, where margin falls to 45% and revenue shrinks, gives $109.85.
  2. Growth slows faster than my path. At 3 points less growth every year the Base is $395.64.
  3. A higher discount rate or a lower exit multiple. At 13% my Base equals the price; at 12% it is $389.17. At a 12 times exit, the return from this price is about 11% a year.

The verdict could also be wrong in the other direction: a fall of 25% would put the shares in the buy zone, and my rules would then say buy.
