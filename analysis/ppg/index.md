## What PPG is

PPG makes paints and coatings and reports three segments. Industrial Coatings (automotive original equipment, general industrial and packaging coatings) had $1,744 million of sales in the third quarter. Performance Coatings (including aerospace, automotive refinish and protective coatings) had $1,554 million, and Global Architectural Coatings (decorative paint) had $1,055 million.

## Cash flow, capital and share count

Cash from operations was $1,158 million for the nine months against $1,054 million a year earlier, and capital spending was $464 million against $477 million. Free cash flow before stock compensation was therefore about $694 million, up from about $577 million. For the full year I estimate cash from operations near $1.95 billion, which assumes a strong fourth quarter, and I use capital spending of $675 million, the midpoint of a $650 to $700 million range.

Total debt was $6,841 million, including $686 million due within a year, and cash and short-term investments were $1,642 million, so net debt was about $5.2 billion, or 1.8 times my normalized EBITDA. In the nine months the company paid $482 million of dividends and repurchased $322 million of stock, $804 million together against $694 million of free cash flow.

Diluted shares averaged 223.2 million in the third quarter, and I use that figure. The release did not show pensions, leases or minority interests, so the enterprise-to-equity bridge uses net debt only, and I test $1.0 billion of other claims in the robustness table.

## Primary valuation: mid-cycle EV/EBITDA

I normalize EBITDA at $2.90 billion against my estimate of about $2.97 billion for FY26, because part of the quarter's strength came from aerospace and Latin America and the largest segment is still losing margin. That is about 17% of estimated sales, below the 18.2% margin of the third quarter.

I apply 11.0 times, the upper part of the 8 to 12 times range I use for a moderately cyclical manufacturer and below the 12 to 18 times range for a quality compounder. Industrial Coatings is about 40% of sales with a 14.4% margin that is falling, earnings are growing at roughly 5% a year, owner cash flow is about two thirds of adjusted net income, and net debt is a manageable 1.8 times EBITDA. At 11.0 times, the equity value of $26.7 billion equals a 4.5% owner cash flow yield, which at a 9.0% cost of equity implies growth of about 4.5% a year. That matches the growth in my second model.

The model gives **$119.62**: EV of $31.9 billion less net debt of $5.2 billion, divided by 223.2 million shares. The normalized EBITDA and the multiple are my estimates.

## Secondary valuation: owner cash flow

The second method values the cash left for shareholders. FY26 owner cash flow is $5.40 a share: $1.95 billion of cash from operations less $675 million of capital spending and $70 million of stock compensation, which is my estimate, divided by 223.2 million shares. I grow it 5% in FY27 and 5% a year in years 2 to 5, then 4% in years 6 to 10, and 2.75% after that. Buybacks help the per-share growth. I discount at 9.0%.

The model gives **$101.90**. Terminal value is 57% of it over a ten year horizon, below my 75% attention level. Cash taxes were not in the release, so owner cash flow rests on reported operating cash flow.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $119.62 and $101.90, a divergence of 16.0%, below the 20% warning level. They share the same view of sales, margins and capital spending, so agreement is not independent evidence. I weight the EV/EBITDA method 60% and the cash flow model 40%, because the first normalizes the cycle and the second does not capture other claims such as pensions. The weighted value is $112.53. ## What is the market already pricing in?

The price of $117.12 equals an EV of $31.3 billion, or 10.8 times my normalized EBITDA and 10.6 times my FY26 estimate. That is close to my 11.0 times. In the cash flow model the price requires a cost of equity of 8.2% instead of 9.0%. The owner cash flow yield is 4.6%, the dividend yield 2.5% and the price is 14.6 times the $8.00 guidance midpoint. ## What could go wrong?

- Industrial margin. It fell 260 basis points in the quarter. Normalized EBITDA of $2.75 billion gives a Base value of $108.10.
- Quality of the record. If the strongest businesses cool, the earnings base is lower than the quarter suggests.
- Multiple. A multiple of 10.0 times gives a Base value of $104.74.
- Other claims. Pensions, leases and minorities were not in the release, and $1.0 billion of them would take $4.48 off the primary value.
- Cash. Shareholder returns exceeded free cash flow in the first nine months.

## Management execution

The company delivered record earnings, higher organic sales and lower capital spending in the first nine months, and raised the guidance midpoint. What it has not shown is a fix for Industrial, where margin fell 260 basis points on 4% organic growth. The fourth-quarter report will show whether that segment stabilizes.
