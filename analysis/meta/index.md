## What Meta is

Meta is an advertising business with a very large AI and hardware bill attached. Advertising was 97.6% of third quarter revenue ($63.06 billion of $64.60 billion, up 26%). The Family of Apps segment earned $17.12 billion of operating profit, while Reality Labs lost $4.13 billion on $443 million of revenue.

Growth is coming from the auction, not from new users. Ad impressions rose 10% and the average price per ad rose 14%, while daily active people rose 3% to 3.64 billion. A 14% rise in price is what a better ad stack can deliver for a while, not every year, which is why my Base slows revenue growth from about 27.5% this year (my estimate, using the middle of the fourth quarter guidance) to 19.0%, 13.1% and 11.6% over the next three years.

The investment side is where the story changed. Capital spending including finance leases was $34.9 billion in the quarter against $19.4 billion a year earlier, up 80% (my arithmetic). Meta now guides 2026 capital spending at $135 billion to $145 billion, against $115 billion to $135 billion in January. Depreciation was $7.2 billion, up 44%, and long-term debt rose from $58.7 billion at the end of 2025 to $80.9 billion.

The primary Base is **$465.59**. The exit value is 91.4% of equity value (98.4% in the secondary method), over a horizon of 3.2 years and with a dividend of roughly $5.4 billion a year, above the 85% level where I start treating the answer as mostly an assumption about 2029 and beyond. The Bear is at 97.2% and the Bull at 92.4%. I price this dependence once, in the valuation robustness component of Confidence, and not again in the margin of safety. The explicit horizon is short and Meta pays only a small dividend, so none of the owner cash is distributed in the period; the model counts it as value available to owners.

A table default is not a justification for 22 times, so here is what it assumes. It is my build: 18 times as a mature cash multiple, plus 5 for continued growth and franchise strength, minus 1 for capital and governance uncertainty. As a check, if owner cash grew for five years after 2029 and then 3% forever, 22 times at a 10% discount rate needs about 12.7% annual cash growth in those five years (my arithmetic). That is demanding. The $50 billion gap between 2029 capital spending and depreciation would have to earn its keep: at a 20% to 30% after-tax return, it would add $10 billion to $15 billion of annual profit after a ramp of a year or two. That return is my plausibility bridge, not a measured project return. I challenge the multiple from 18 to 26 times and do not present it as a historical median.

The primary Bear is $122.18 (2029 owner cash of $23.01 billion, 18 times) and the primary Bull is $945.82 (owner cash of $117.26 billion, 26 times).

## How the buy price is set

Confidence C calls for a margin of safety of 35% to 50%, and I use 40% with no add-ons. The reasons you might want one are already in the confidence score: the exit value is 91% of the primary value and the two methods differ by 42.6%, and both are charged there once (valuation robustness scores 1 of 5). Capital intensity sits in the cash flows, interest in owner cash, the legal settlement in the equity bridge, stock compensation in the cash cost, and concentration and governance in the scenarios and the exit multiple. A 45% margin would put the ceiling at $297.69. The maximum buy price is $541.26 x (1 - 40%) = **$324.75**. It is a decision reference, not an order to place.

Three numbers answer different questions: what the shares are worth ($541.26), the annual return from buying at $697.52 and following the Base cash path (1.5%), and the price that would give a chosen return on that path. A 12% return is my illustration, not a requirement I know you have: the model gives $511.31 for 12%, $541.26 for 10% and $573.57 for 8%. The return is a modeled owner cash return, not a forecast of dividends, buybacks or share price. At $697.52 the shares are 28.9% above Base, so they do not qualify.

## The dividend

Meta declared a quarterly dividend of $0.525 a share, paid on September 28, 2026 to holders of record on September 21. Annualized, that is $2.10 a share and a 0.30% yield at $697.52, assuming the board keeps declaring it. This is a growth position, not an income holding, and the dividend competes with infrastructure spending for the same cash. I do not add it to the value because the owner cash method already values the cash once. The Class C stock dividend is not a cash payment and not a free increase in wealth.

## Reasons to own

- **The ad engine works.** Revenue grew 26% in the quarter on 10% more impressions and 14% higher prices, and the Family of Apps segment earned $17.12 billion of operating profit.
- **AI feeds an auction that already pays.** Better ranking and ad tools earn cash through a monetization system Meta already runs, so they do not need a new business model.
- **Operating leverage is possible.** Headcount is down 15% to 66,775 and, if the new capacity becomes productive and spending stops climbing, owner cash could recover quickly. The Bull case of $1,023.37 shows how quickly.
- **The distribution is large.** Messaging, AI assistants and wearables can reach the existing user base. I put no stand-alone value on them in the Base.
- **Meta can fund itself.** With $82.4 billion of cash and securities and operating profit of $22.9 billion in a quarter before the charge, it can keep investing, provided commitments stay in proportion.

## Main risks

- **Replacement cost.** AI hardware may need renewing faster than depreciation suggests, leaving owner cash weak for years.
- **Return on the spending.** Frontier model spending may earn less than its cost of capital even if advertising stays strong. Reality Labs lost $4.13 billion in the quarter.
- **Price-led growth.** A 14% rise in price per ad, with user growth of 3%, cannot repeat every year.
- **Tax and adjusted-earnings optics.** Unusual tax rates (87% last year, 17% to 24% this year) can make earnings growth look better or worse than the underlying business.
- **Legal and regulatory cash.** The $9.89 billion settlement is not the end of regulatory exposure, and the reserve in my bridge is not a cap on future cases or product restrictions.
- **Attention and access.** Users and advertisers can move, and privacy, platform or content rules can lower ad efficiency.
- **Governance and financing.** Meta is controlled by its founder and will have a new non-voting stock to issue. Non-pro-rata issuance could dilute owners.
- **Off-balance-sheet commitments.** The data center venture and other infrastructure obligations need a full filing review that I could not complete from the release.
- **Valuation construction.** The exit multiple and 2029 cash decide about 91% of the value, and the Bear value is not a floor.
- **Currency.** For an investor who does not hold dollars, the return also depends on the exchange rate.

## Management execution

Execution on cost and execution on capital look different. Total expenses, excluding the legal charge, are guided at $165.1 billion to $169.1 billion for 2026 (my arithmetic, $175 billion to $179 billion less $9.89 billion), against $162 billion to $169 billion guided in January, so the operating cost line is where it was promised. Capital spending is the one that moved: $115 billion to $135 billion in January, $130 billion to $145 billion before this release, and $135 billion to $145 billion now.

The outlook is also precise about what Meta is committing to: operating profit above 2025 only before the legal charge. Fourth quarter revenue guidance of $73 billion to $76 billion is concrete. The payoff from the AI products is an expectation and has not yet turned up as cash. I could not rebuild a multi-year record of guidance against results, so I give no accuracy percentage. I rate credibility 6/10 (my estimate) and management and allocation 7/10 inside business quality.

## What I would watch from here

### Supports the thesis

- Advertising revenue growth of 15% or more with stable demand (third quarter: 26%), and an operating margin of at least 35% excluding discrete charges.
- Rolling owner cash turning positive, 2027 capital spending at or below $135 billion and 2028 owner cash at or above $32.5 billion.
- The Class C distribution carried out pro rata with the share count reconciling, and Reality Labs losses narrowing with less capital needed.

### Watch closely

- Advertising growth of 5% to 15%, or growth led mainly by price. A margin of 30% to 35% (the third quarter was 35.4% before the charge, right at the green line).
- Owner cash negative but improving (it is -$1.8 billion over twelve months today), 2027 capital spending of $135 billion to $150 billion, 2028 owner cash between $20 billion and $32.5 billion.
- Longer asset lives, more outside funding of data centers, or a rise in stock compensation or share issuance that the denominator does not explain.

### Breaks the thesis

- Advertising growth below 5% for two quarters, or a normalized margin below 30% for two quarters.
- 2027 capital spending above $150 billion without better economics, 2028 owner cash below $20 billion, or 2029 below $45 billion.
- A new judgment or restrictive remedy that changes the legal reserve or the ad product, or a non-pro-rata share issue without the proceeds modeled. The entry test is also red today: the price is above my Base.

## The most important thesis breakers

- **Capital spending that does not fall.** If replacement cost stays above depreciation and 2028 owner cash lands below $20 billion, the exit value shrinks with it.
- **Growth that leans on price.** Price per ad rose 14% on a 3% rise in users. If impressions slow while price gains fade, the revenue path in the Base fails first.
- **Costs that rise with revenue.** The Base needs costs other than depreciation to grow about 4.9% a year. A margin below 30% for two quarters means they are not.
