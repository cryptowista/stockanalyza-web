Nexi: priced for zero growth, but not yet a buy

Every screener I checked has Nexi at 0.8x sales and 0.7x book value. Both numbers are arithmetically right and neither one helps. The "sales" are about €6.3 billion of gross revenue, while the company reports €3.6 billion of net revenues, and the "book" is mostly goodwill: tangible book value is negative, about -€4.42 a share. The number that does help is plainer. Nexi guides to about €750 million of excess cash this year, which is 15% of its €4.9 billion market value, and at €4.20 the price assumes that cash flow never grows.

At €4.20 on October 9, 2026, the shares trade 32.7% below my triangulated Base value of €6.24, which means 48.6% upside. The Bear value of €3.18 is 24.3% below the price. The required margin of safety of 50% puts the maximum buy price at €3.12, and the price is 35% above it. For existing holders this is a hold. For new money it is a watchlist name. The shares traded around €4.31 on October 12, up 2.4%, but that session had not closed when I wrote this.

Route: SF-01 Standard value. Nexi is a payments processor with no loan book and no recurring-revenue model of the SaaS type, so the bank and software routes do not apply. What Nexi is

Nexi S.p.A. is an Italian payments group with three segments. Merchant Solutions (about 57% of 2025 net revenues, €2,037.6 million) runs card acceptance and terminals for shops and businesses. Issuing Solutions (32%, €1,156.5 million) processes cards for banks. Digital Banking Solutions (11%, €391.1 million) sells software and services for bank accounts and payments.

The business model is fees on volume plus multi-year contracts with banks, and the bank contracts are where the story gets complicated. Reported net revenues grew 2.1% in 2025 and 1.0% in the first half of 2026. Management puts underlying growth in the first half at about 5%, which excludes the effect of bank customers lost to M&A and of bank contract renegotiations. The gap between 1% and 5% is the price Nexi pays for its largest customers having bargaining power.

Management confirmed the 2026 guidance: net revenue growth broadly in line with 2025, absolute EBITDA broadly stable after strategic investments, about €750 million of excess cash, and a dividend of €0.30 a share (about €350 million). The phrase "after strategic investments" appears in the guidance twice. The cash flow statement will have to show what they are.

Read the second half closely. The first half produced €400 million of excess cash, so the €750 million guide implies about €350 million in the second half, which is a lower conversion than the first half (about 34% of second-half EBITDA against 46%). Management also guided second-half cost growth of 5% to 6%. On my estimate, second-half 2025 costs were about €831 million (full year €1,681 million minus €850 million in the first half), so 5.5% is about €46 million, against about €19 million from 1% revenue growth. Unless revenue reaccelerates, second-half EBITDA falls by roughly €27 million, which fits the -1.9% in the second quarter. The guide may be conservative. I do not assume it is.

Germany is the visible weak spot. On the call management described DACH (Germany, Austria, Switzerland) as growing only about 2%, with weak hospitality and high corporate insolvencies in Germany, and said its direct sales channel is struggling. Management expects Merchant Solutions to improve in the second half.

Why the screener numbers mislead

Financial data providers show Nexi's 2025 revenue at €6,273 million. The company's own net revenues are €3,585 million. The difference is a gross figure that includes costs passed through to banks and card networks (my reading; the press release reports only net revenues). Price to sales of 0.8x on the gross number is a statement about accounting, not about value. Use net revenues, and note that EBITDA margin is 53.1% on them.

Price to book of 0.70x has the same problem. Equity is €7,058 million (€6.03 a share), but goodwill is €8,456 million and other intangibles €3,772 million, together €12.2 billion, more than the entire €7.1 billion of equity. Strip them out and tangible equity is about -€5.2 billion. The €3.7 billion goodwill write-down in 2025 already conceded that the acquisitions made around 2020 and 2021 are worth less than was paid, and goodwill still stands at €8.6 billion. A stock at a discount to that book is not at a discount to anything you could sell.

Reported earnings have their own issue. Nexi's 2025 net loss was €3,377 million, almost entirely the non-cash write-down. In the first half of 2026 reported net profit was €115 million against normalised net profit of €354 million. The gap is largely amortisation of acquired contracts, which normalised profit excludes. I look through that because it is non-cash, which is why I anchor on cash flow instead of earnings, but it is a measure of what Nexi paid.

The hard metric: excess cash

Nexi defines excess cash generation as operating cash flow after capex, cash interest, cash taxes, lease payments and other cash items, before dividends, buybacks and acquisitions. It is a company-defined managerial figure, so I checked it. The 2025 value of €806 million equals operating cash flow of €1,226 million minus capex of €420 million in the cash flow statement of the financial data provider I used, so it is not a number built from adjustments. Stock-based compensation is only €19 million a year, which I test below.

Per share, €750 million is €0.64 on 1.17 billion shares.

Cash, debt and shares

Net financial debt was €5,098 million at June 30, 2026, up from €4,942 million at the end of 2025, because the €350 million dividend and the roughly €105 million paid for Banca Popolare di Sondrio's merchant acquiring business (completed March 17, 2026) exceeded the €400 million of first-half excess cash. The ratio is 2.7x EBITDA, 2.4x before those two items. Cash is €1,882 million, the weighted average debt maturity about three years and the average pre-tax cost of debt about 2.5%. Nexi repaid about €1 billion of maturities in the first half. The next one is a €500 million equity-linked bond due April 24, 2027, which cash already covers. Fitch and S&P reaffirmed investment grade in July.

That is a comfortable structure with one catch: debt has risen €156 million since December while guided excess cash falls. I treat the 2.5% cost of debt as a feature of the past. Cheap bonds roll off, and every extra point of interest on about €6.7 billion of gross debt costs about €67 million a year before tax.

The share count is about 1.17 billion. Market value divided by price gives 1.174 billion, and €350 million divided by €0.30 gives 1.167 billion. In 2025 Nexi bought back about €300 million of shares (May to September) and cancelled 57,646,861 of them, an average of about €5.20 a share, 24% above today's price. The conversion price of the 2027 bond is not available to me, so I treat it as debt and test 1.20 billion shares below. Minority interests are €23 million. IFRS 16 lease liabilities are not disclosed in the releases (the provider shows €79 million of non-current lease obligations). Excess cash is already after lease payments, so leases stay out of the cash flow multiple, and I test about €110 million of leases in net debt below.

The primary Base value is €6.41. Excess cash is already after interest, so the multiple is applied to equity and net debt is not subtracted again. The SF-01 tier for a stable business with moderate leverage is 10x to 12x with a default of 11x. I take 1x off for debt rising while cash flow does not (the framework's downward adjustment), which leaves 10.0x. As a check, 10.0x is a 10% equity cash yield, which is an 11% cost of equity less 1% growth. The Bear multiple of 7.5x is a 13.3% yield (a 13% cost of equity with no growth), and the Bull multiple of 12.0x is an 8.3% yield.

The scenarios have a bridge. Bear EBITDA of €1,750 million (8% below the flat guide) at a 34% conversion gives €600 million, Base EBITDA of €1,900 million at 39.5% gives €750 million, and Bull EBITDA of €2,050 million at 41.5% gives €850 million. These are run-rate values for roughly 2028, not 2026, because 2026 is largely guided. The 10x multiple is my judgment. I found no peer table on the same cash flow definition, so the only anchors are Nexi's own history and the cost of equity.

Secondary valuation: unlevered DCF

The secondary method is a ten-year discounted cash flow of unlevered free cash flow. It starts from excess cash plus about €92 million of after-tax net interest (my estimate: about 2.5% on €6.7 billion of financial debt, less about 2.5% on €1.8 billion of cash, about €123 million before a 25% tax), which gives €842 million in 2026. Growth is 1% in 2027, 2% in 2028, 3% in 2029 and 2030, 2.5% in 2031 and 2032 and 2% from 2033 to 2035, with terminal growth of 1.0% and a 8.5% discount rate. The framework's default range for the discount rate is 8% to 10%, and its range for terminal growth is 2% to 3%. I use 1.0% because Merchant Solutions pricing is under pressure, and 8.5% sits at the low end because 40% of the funding is cheap, investment-grade debt.

Free cash flow growth quality (SM-20, diagnostic only). Excess cash rose from about €720 million in 2024 to €806 million in 2025 (+12%) and is guided to €750 million in 2026, a compound growth of 2.1% a year over two years and an absolute increase of about €30 million. Per share it grew from €0.56 to €0.64, or 6.8% a year, because the share count fell 4.4% a year (buybacks). So the P/FCF of 6.6x divided by per-share growth gives 1.0, while divided by total growth it gives 3.1, and the buyback is the whole difference. I see no LOW_BASE_DISTORTION and no cyclical rebound. The Banca Popolare di Sondrio payment of €105 million sits in net debt, not in excess cash. Stress test: excess cash 20% lower (€600 million) gives a primary value of €5.13 at 10x, and 40% lower (€450 million) gives €3.85.

What is the market already pricing in?

At €4.20 market value is €4.9 billion and enterprise value €10.0 billion. At an 8.5% discount rate, an enterprise value of €10.0 billion needs unlevered cash flow of about €853 million a year forever with zero growth, against my €842 million for 2026. So the price assumes growth of 0.1% a year. Put another way, flat forever at my cash flow is worth €4.09 a share, within 3% of the price. The market is not pricing in a collapse. It is pricing in a business that never grows again, after management itself said the return to mid-single-digit revenue growth would take until 2028.

For the Base, an investor needs cash flow to grow 1% to 3% a year from 2027, after a 2026 in which guided excess cash falls 7%. The data so far: Merchant Solutions is still shrinking, Issuing and Digital Banking grow 3.0% and 4.5%, and the second-quarter EBITDA fell 1.9%. The digital euro, for which Nexi was selected in the ECB pilot, is a 2028 and later option. Management expects modest revenues at first and a launch around 2029, so I give it no value.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C starts at a 40% margin of safety, in the lower half of the framework's 35% to 50% range, because the cash flows come from multi-year contracts and the group is investment grade. Five points are added for financial leverage (2.7x EBITDA, with a €500 million bond due in April 2027) and five for accounting opacity (a company-defined cash metric and EBITDA, a €3.7 billion write-down, and a wide gap between normalised and reported profit). I add nothing for structural disruption, because the Bear already cuts cash flow by 20% and the multiple by 25%, and counting the risk in both places would double it. That leaves 50%. A 50% discount to the €6.24 Base gives a maximum buy price of €3.12. A deeper 60% discount gives €2.50, so the buy zone is €2.50 to €3.12. The 52-week low of €2.64 is inside it. The current price is 35% above the top of the zone.

Dividend

Nexi paid €0.25 a share in May 2025 and €0.30 in May 2026 (+20%, about €350 million). At €4.20 the yield is 7.1%. The plan commits to raising the dividend by at least 5% a year, with total distributions of about €1.1 billion over 2026 to 2028 (as reported; I could not read the plan itself). The €350 million is 47% of the €750 million guided excess cash, a coverage of 2.1 times, and leaves about €400 million a year for debt repayment. Two payments are not a track record. The dividend framework needs 10 years of growth, so I do not apply it, and I treat the dividend as a use of cash that competes with deleveraging.

Reasons to own NEXI

  • A cash yield that already assumes no growth. Excess cash of €750 million is 15.3% of market value, and the price equals a flat cash flow forever.
  • A covered 7.1% dividend. The €350 million cost is covered 2.1 times by guided excess cash.
  • Underlying growth of 5%. Reported growth of 1% is held back by lost banks and bank contract renegotiations, and management expects Merchant Solutions to reaccelerate in the second half.
  • Growth outside merchant acceptance. Issuing grew 3.0% and Digital Banking 4.5% in the half year, .
  • A funded balance sheet. Cash of €1.9 billion covers the next maturity, the rating is investment grade, and the share count already fell by 57.6 million shares in 2025.

What could go wrong?

  • Merchant pricing and bank power. Merchant Solutions is 57% of net revenues and is still shrinking (-0.8% in the half year). Terminal pricing and bank renegotiations hold reported growth well below the underlying rate.
  • Guidance arithmetic. Costs rise 5% to 6% in the second half, while revenue grows about 1%. EBITDA can fall even if guidance is met, and excess cash is already guided 7% below 2025. This is the main thesis breaker.
  • Germany. Slow DACH growth, weak hospitality, high insolvencies and a struggling direct sales channel.
  • Leverage and refinancing. Net debt is 2.7x EBITDA and rose €156 million in six months. A 2.5% average cost of debt cannot be repeated on refinancing, and the dividend grows at least 5% a year.
  • Accounting basis. Excess cash, EBITDA and normalised profit are company-defined measures, and goodwill plus intangibles exceed total equity after a €3.7 billion write-down. A second write-down would not change cash, but it would say something about the carrying value of the contracts.

Management execution

Management delivered what it guided for 2025: excess cash of €806 million (+12%), a 20% higher dividend and a €300 million buyback completed in September. Against that, the 2026 guidance came in below consensus on March 5, return to growth moved out to 2028, the shares fell roughly 18% to 20% that day, and a €3.7 billion write-down concedes that the earlier acquisitions did not earn their price. The buyback was done at about €5.20 a share, and the stock is now at €4.20. Leverage rose from 2.6x to 2.7x. Management credibility scores 10/20, mixed.

Adversarial review

I rebuilt the key figures from the 2025 and first-half 2026 results releases and the call commentary: net revenues by segment, EBITDA, margin, normalised and reported profit, net financial debt and leverage (the 2.7x ratio is as reported by the company), capex, excess cash, the dividend, the buyback and the guidance. Excess cash of €806 million in 2025 equals operating cash flow minus capex in the provider's cash flow statement. The share count is from two routes that agree within 1%. The price is the weakest verified input: the history page of the quote source had reached October 8 (€4.13) when I checked, so the October 9 close of about €4.20 is the prior close implied by two quote feeds (a broker feed showing €4.307 and +2.43% on October 12, and the data provider's €4.30 and +0.10), not a reading from an exchange list.

Weak points, in order. First, both valuation methods rest on the same guided cash flow, so their 6.9% divergence is not independent confirmation, and Confidence for valuation robustness is 3, not 4. Second, excess cash is a company-defined managerial measure, and I could not check it against a full IFRS cash flow statement, which is why accounting transparency scores 2. Third, the 10x multiple is my judgment, anchored on the cost of equity and Nexi's own two-point history, because I found no peer table on the same basis. Fourth, the DCF terminal multiple of 13.5x is above today's 11.9x, and the interest bridge for the unlevered cash flow is my estimate. Fifth, I do not know the conversion price of the 2027 bond, and I did not use the third-party EBITDA consensus because it is on a different basis from the company's.

The strongest counter-argument is that Nexi is simply cheap: a 15% cash yield, a 7% covered dividend and a price that equals zero growth forever, while the group has €1.9 billion of cash and an investment-grade rating. That case is the Bull at €8.63, and the robustness tests below show the Base staying well above the price in almost every combination. The reply is that the evidence is thin. Two-thirds of the value is the 2026 guide, which is already 7% below the previous year, the second-half arithmetic points to falling EBITDA, the only history I can compare is the 2025 de-rating, and tangible book is negative. A stock can be cheap and still deserve a deeper margin of safety than a stock with a steady cash flow. That is the 50% I use.
