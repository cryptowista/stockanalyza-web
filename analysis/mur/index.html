Murapol reported third quarter results on the morning of November 25, and the shares rose 4.06% to PLN 35.90. The PLN 4.91 a share paid for 2025 equals 13.7% of that price, which is the number a data screen will show. But the PLN 2.94 advance for 2026, PLN 119.9 million in total, goes to whoever held the shares on the record date of November 25 and is paid on November 30. The shares have been ex-advance since November 24, so a buyer today starts the clock on the next payment, not the one in the headlines.

At PLN 35.90 the shares trade 15.7% below my triangulated Base value of PLN 42.59, which is an upside of 18.6%. The Bear value is PLN 21.33 and the Bull value is PLN 62.26, so the price sits 68% above Bear and 42% below Bull. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 42.5%, so the maximum buy price is PLN 24.49. The shares are 46.6% above that and would need to fall 31.8% to reach it. The two valuation methods give PLN 38.98 and PLN 46.21, so the maximum buy price runs from PLN 22.41 to PLN 26.57 depending on which one you trust; neither is near today's price.

Valuation route: residential property development, valued on the cash that reaches shareholders. The primary method adds the dividends expected through 2028 to a discounted exit value set as a multiple of 2028 earnings, and the second method sets the exit value from cash flow instead of a multiple. The framework has no dedicated homebuilder module, so I used its general route for operating companies and adapted it: a developer's cash flow follows land purchases and handovers, not a smooth yearly pattern, so earnings and dividends carry the model. Economic classification: Standard (4/12). Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Murapol is

Murapol is a residential developer based in Bielsko-Biala that builds apartments mainly for retail buyers in large Polish cities. At the end of March 2026 it had 7,321 apartments under construction in 14 cities and a land bank for about 20,700 more. In 2025 it sold 3,140 apartments to retail customers at an average net price of PLN 9,900 per square meter, 6.5% more than a year earlier, with Gdansk (818 units), Lodz (494) and Poznan (288) the largest markets. A smaller segment sells whole buildings to institutional landlords (the rented-housing segment, PRS).

Murapol books revenue when keys are handed over, not when an apartment is sold, which makes every quarter a function of the construction calendar. In the first quarter of 2026 it handed over 256 apartments and revenue fell 57% to PLN 142.3 million. In the third quarter it handed over 711 and revenue rose 80% to PLN 353.7 million. Management's target for 2026 is about 3,000 handovers, which it describes as growth of about 10%.

Why I value this on earnings and dividends, not free cash flow

A developer's cash flow moves with land purchases, which are decisions, not results. Operating cash flow was PLN 310.7 million in 2025 and only PLN 100.3 million in 2024, while net profit was PLN 235.8 million and PLN 242.0 million. That is 1.3 times net profit in one year and 0.4 times in the other, and two years together average 0.86 times. A discounted cash flow built on either year would mislead, so I model earnings, the share of earnings that reaches shareholders and the dividends that follow.

My Base assumes that 75% of earnings turns into cash available to shareholders, below that two-year average, because a developer that wants to keep growing has to keep buying land. The dividend record supports the idea that cash is real: Murapol paid PLN 4.91 a share for 2024 and for 2025, and the 2025 total of PLN 200.3 million was 84.9% of that year's net profit.

The margin is the weaker point. Net margin was 18.7% in 2025, 16.1% over nine months of 2026, and my Base falls to 15.0% in 2027 and 14.5% in 2028 as the lower gross margin of the projects now being handed over works through. If the 30.5% gross margin proves to be the bottom, that is conservative; if it keeps sliding, it is not.

Balance sheet, the advance dividend and shares

Net debt was PLN 299 million at the end of 2025. I could not reach the consolidated balance sheet at September 30, so net debt at that date is my estimate of about PLN 375 million: the reported 1.08x ratio applied to last-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA that I put at about PLN 350 million, close to the 2025 figure of PLN 352.6 million. Treat it as accurate to roughly 10%. It does not drive the valuation, which works from earnings and dividends, but it matters for how much room is left after the advance.

The advance of PLN 2.94 a share is PLN 119.95 million and is paid on November 30. It reduces cash after the September 30 balance sheet date, so on my estimate net debt rises to about PLN 495 million, or about 1.4x adjusted EBITDA, before the fourth quarter's cash from handovers. The PLN 119.95 million divided by PLN 2.94 gives 40.8 million shares, which is the count I use. I found no sign of dilution, but I did not review the ownership structure in this update.

At PLN 35.90 the market value is PLN 1,465 million. On my estimates the enterprise value is about PLN 1,840 million before the advance and about PLN 1,960 million after it.

Primary valuation: discounted dividends and an earnings-multiple exit

I use a transition model because the dividend stream of the next two years is not representative of the whole life of the business. The Base path follows.

Those PLN 9.06 are 25% of today's price, and their present value at 12% is PLN 7.79 a share. The rest of the value, PLN 31.19, comes from the exit at 7.0 times 2028 earnings of PLN 224.0 million, adjusted for cash that the business keeps or borrows along the way. In the Base case distributions are PLN 46 million below the cash my model says the business generates, so that amount is added to the exit value. In Bear distributions are PLN 67 million above the cash generated, which I charge to the exit value as extra debt. The primary value is PLN 38.98.

Why 7.0 times. The shares trade at 6.2 times 2026 Base earnings today. A 7.0 times exit with an 80% payout and a 12% required return implies earnings that grow about 0.5% a year forever, which fits a Base in which earnings are flat to slightly lower through 2028. I do not pay more because developer earnings follow a housing cycle, the margin is falling, and a developer must keep spending on land to stand still. I do not pay less because net debt is only 1.08 times adjusted EBITDA and the dividend record is long. Bear gets 5.0 times (about 2.6% shrinkage a year forever at a 75% payout) and Bull gets 9.0 times (about 2.9% growth a year at 80%).

Why 12%. The framework's default discount rate is 10%. I use 12% for a Polish developer whose earnings depend on mortgage rates and construction costs, and I show 10% and 14% in the stress test below. At 10% the Base would be PLN 48.99, which is the largest swing of any single input.

Exit value share: 80.0% of the primary value comes from the exit, 2.1 years away, which is below the 85% level where I check that the exit is supported by sustainable earnings. The Bull case is above it, at 85.1%, and that is one reason Bull is the least reliable of the three.

It assumes 85% of 2028 earnings is available as cash and capitalizes that at the 12% required return less 2% growth. In Base this gives PLN 46.21 per share, and 83.1% of its value comes from the exit.

What is the market already pricing in?

Run backward on the Base case, the price of PLN 35.90 needs one of three things: a required return of 15.3% instead of 12%, or net margins of 12.6% in 2027 and 12.1% in 2028 instead of 15.0% and 14.5%, or, on the earnings-multiple method alone, an exit multiple of 6.3 times instead of 7.0 times. None of them is dramatic. The market is pricing a developer whose margin keeps falling, and my Base is only a little more generous.

As a return, buying at PLN 35.90 and holding the Base case to early 2029 gives about 22.3% a year including dividends. Of that, about 11.2 points remain if the exit multiple stays at today's 6.2 times, so roughly half of the Base return depends on the multiple moving to the 7.0 to 8.7 times I assume. The dividends alone, PLN 9.06 a share, return 25% of the price in just over two years.

Growth-adjusted, there is nothing to measure: Base earnings per share are flat to lower (PLN 5.81, 5.51, 5.49), so a growth-adjusted price-to-earnings ratio (PEG) is not meaningful and I do not use it. On 2026 Base earnings the shares trade at 6.2 times, or 6.7 times if the PLN 2.94 advance is added back, because that advance is paid out of the same 2026 profit. A free cash flow growth check is not meaningful either, for the reason given above.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C starts at a 42.5% margin of safety. I added nothing for cyclicality, mortgage-rate exposure, margin pressure or reliance on exit value, because they are already charged elsewhere: cyclicality and margin pressure in the Bear case and the 12% discount rate, exit dependence in the Confidence score. A 42.5% discount to the PLN 42.59 Base gives a maximum buy price of PLN 24.49. A single input moving by 15% of its own size changes the Base by less than 15%, so the buy price is not hostage to one assumption.

Reasons to own MUR

  • A paid-out business. Murapol returned 85% of its 2025 net profit to shareholders, and the Base case pays PLN 9.06 a share, 25% of the price, before any exit value.
  • Handovers are accelerating. 1,552 apartments in nine months, a 711-apartment third quarter, and a 2026 target of about 3,000 that management repeated in May.
  • A long land bank. About 20,700 apartments of land is roughly seven years at the current handover pace.
  • Moderate leverage. Net debt is 1.08 times adjusted EBITDA, and PLN 150 million of bonds were refinanced.
  • The multiple is low. At 6.2 times 2026 earnings the shares need only flat earnings to justify the price.

What could go wrong?

  • Margin. Gross margin fell to 30.5% from 33.3% in a year. If it keeps falling, my Base is too high.
  • Demand and mortgage rates. Retail unit sales rose only 2.2% in nine months, and the third quarter's jump came from handover timing.
  • Handover timing. The first quarter had 256 handovers and the third had 711. A slip of a few hundred apartments from December into January moves a whole year's reported profit.
  • Dividend dependence. About a quarter of my Base value is paid as dividends in two years, and a cut would hurt both the yield and the multiple.
  • Leverage after the advance. My estimate puts net debt near 1.4 times adjusted EBITDA after November 30, before fourth-quarter cash.
  • PRS concentration risk. The rented-housing segment fell 25.9% in nine months and depends on a few institutional buyers.

Management execution

Management announced a strong acceleration of handovers earlier this year and delivered it in the third quarter, with net profit 3.7% above the consensus. It repeated its 3,000-handover target in May after a first quarter that was 61% below the prior year, and the nine-month count now makes the target reachable. Against that, margins fell and the dividend has not grown in two years. Management credibility is 14/20: reliable on the calendar it announces, with a margin trend that is moving the wrong way.

Final verdict: hold at PLN 35.90, watchlist for new money

Murapol is a profitable, moderately leveraged developer that pays out most of its earnings, and at PLN 35.90 it trades below my Base value of PLN 42.59. That is a reason to put it on a watchlist, not a reason to buy, because the framework asks for a 42.5% margin of safety at Confidence C, which sets the buy price at PLN 24.49. The two methods, PLN 38.98 and PLN 46.21, do not change that.

VERDICT: HOLD (WATCHLIST) at PLN 35.90; Triangulated Base PLN 42.59; upside to Base 18.6%; required MOS 42.5%; maximum buy price PLN 24.49. The main thesis breaker is a gross margin that keeps falling below 28%.

New money waits for PLN 24.49 or lower with the thesis intact, or for a Base that rises on proven margins. At PLN 24.49 even my Bear case returns about 4% a year with dividends, and at today's price it loses about 15% a year; the 13.7% headline yield does not change that arithmetic, because the next PLN 2.94 belongs to someone else.
