Gerdau is a Brazilian steelmaker, and the third quarter of 2026 was a Brazilian disappointment. Brazil earned an EBITDA margin of 10.7%, North America earned 27.7%, and the American half supplied 76% of the group's EBITDA. The stock is priced like a Brazilian cyclical, and the profit looks like an American one.

Valuation route: cyclical steelmaker, valued mainly on a multiple of mid-cycle EBITDA (the average level of profit across a steel cycle) and cross-checked with a ten year discounted free cash flow before interest. The Bear case includes weaker American spreads and a Brazil that stays under import pressure.

## What Gerdau is

Gerdau makes long steel (rebar, wire rod and structural shapes) in the Americas, mostly by melting scrap in electric mills. It reports Brazil, North America and South America. In the third quarter, North America had 56% of net sales and 44% of shipments, Brazil 37% of sales and 45% of shipments. Demand in North America comes from nonresidential construction, data centers and renewable energy projects. Brazil faces a different market: record steel imports of 6.4 million tonnes in 2025 and a high steel supply, which the company expects to fight with trade defense under the new government.

Even so, adjusted net income of R$1.52 billion fell short of the R$1.77 billion analysts expected, and the share price in São Paulo fell 2.75% the next day. Steel earnings look better than steel cash.

## Cash flow: what the owner keeps

Capital spending is the main drag. The plan for 2026 is R$4.7 billion, spending is running nearer R$4.0 billion, and the chief financial officer says a level near R$4.5 billion may be reasonable in coming years, with no formal 2027 guidance yet. Two projects finish around now: the Miguel Burnier iron ore mine in Brazil starts up in the fourth quarter, with a rainy season slowing the ramp, and a Midlothian mill expansion in Texas adds about 150 thousand tonnes of capacity.

I estimate depreciation at R$4.0 billion a year, an estimate I could not check against the filing, and I use the 31% tax rate of the last nine months. At a mid-cycle EBITDA of R$11.9 billion that leaves free cash flow before interest of about $872 million in fiscal 2027 in my Base, which is an enterprise yield of 8.7%.

## Primary valuation: mid-cycle EV/EBITDA

Gerdau's EBITDA was R$10.1 billion in 2025, R$10.8 billion in 2024 and R$12.5 billion over the last twelve months (my sum of the first nine months of 2026 and the fourth quarter of 2025). A multiple on the best twelve months would flatter it, so I use R$11.9 billion as the Base, about 16.5% of R$72 billion of sales: North American margins ease from 27.7% toward the mid 20s, Brazil stays near 11%. The Bear is R$8.8 billion, below 2025, and the Bull R$14.25 billion, about the margin of the last nine months.

I apply 5.0 times in the Base, the bottom of the framework range of 5 to 8 times for commodity producers. The reasons are a return on equity near 10%, capital spending that absorbs about 38% of EBITDA, and results that swing with steel spreads and tariffs. In the Bear I use 4.0 times, below the range, because imports and lower American prices would hit a business that cannot cut its capital spending quickly. The Bull uses 6.0 times. At 5.0 times and a 10% cost of capital the multiple implies free cash flow growing about 2% a year, which is a sober assumption for a steelmaker.

## Reasons to own Gerdau

- North America earns a 27.7% margin, the best in four years, with demand from data centers, construction and renewables.
- Net debt is 0.58 times EBITDA, ratings outlooks are positive and a US$1.125 billion credit line is fully available.
- Capital spending is falling toward R$4.5 billion, and two big projects finish now, which should lift free cash flow in 2027.
- The shares trade at 4.5 times trailing EBITDA and below book value, which the company treats as a reason to buy back stock.

## What could go wrong?

- **Steel spreads:** three quarters of EBITDA comes from North America, where tariffs and demand set the margin.
- **Brazil:** record imports and high interest rates kept Brazil near 10% margins, and a restructuring and the Recife exit add costs.
- **Cash conversion:** receivables and inventories absorbed R$3.77 billion of operating cash in nine months.
- **Currency:** I use a rate of 5.657 reais per dollar, the weakest real in 17 months, and a recovery of the real would change the dollar value of the Brazilian half.
- **Disputes and unchecked items:** the DFESA claim, pension items and the exact depreciation are open.
- **My own numbers:** the mid-cycle EBITDA, the 10% cost of capital, the 5.0 times multiple and the tax rate are my estimates.

## Management execution

Management has delivered where it controls the outcome: leverage fell from 0.81 times a year ago to 0.58 times, capital spending is below plan, and the quarterly dividend is R$0.34, up from R$0.28 a year earlier. It has not delivered in Brazil, where a R$2.0 billion impairment was booked in 2025 and margins remain near 10%. The company now says it will be more selective with capital in Brazil. I score management 7 out of 10.

## Final verdict: hold at $4.39, do not add

Gerdau is a better company than its Brazilian label suggests, and the American half is having a strong year. The price already reflects that: the shares trade 12% below my Base and 1.8 times above my Bear, and the maximum buy price of $3.00 is 32% below the market.

An owner can hold, because the dividend and the Base return of 11.8% a year are acceptable. New money should wait, either for a price near $3.00 or for proof that Brazil earns double digit margins on the new mine.
