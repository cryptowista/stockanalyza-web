Hims & Hers grew revenue 49% in the third quarter to $890.4 million and lost $33.0 million doing it. Gross margin fell from 74% to 57%, and the company ended September with about 103,000 fewer subscribers than it had in June. It also cut its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance for the third time this year, to $257 million to $277 million. The shares fell roughly 12% on November 10 and closed at $19.73 on November 12, and the company is still valued at 22.7 times that guided EBITDA.

My Base value is $10.56 a share, the Bear value $0.00 and the Bull value $25.87. The price sits between the Base and the Bull. I count stock compensation as a cost and the acquisition payments still owed as debt, and on that basis the business produced negative owner free cash flow in 2026. I could not find a company-specific reason for today's decline of about 5%; analyst price target cuts followed the results earlier this week.

Valuation route: consumer telehealth and subscription platform with negative owner cash flow today, valued with an eight year model of free cash flow after stock compensation for 2027 to 2034, cross-checked with a multiple of 2028 EBITDA after stock compensation. Net debt includes the convertible notes, the unpaid acquisition payments and the earn-outs. Cash flows are discounted at 11%.

## What Hims & Hers is

Hims & Hers sells treatments and wellness products online through a subscription telehealth platform. It had about 2.79 million subscribers at the end of September, each paying about $105 a month on average, up from $81 a year earlier. United States revenue was $657.1 million in the quarter, up 21%. International revenue was $233.3 million, up 321%, because the Eucalyptus acquisition closed in June 2026; it is now 26% of sales.

The product mix has changed since 2025. Rules on compounded GLP-1 drugs tightened, the company's arrangement with Novo Nordisk changed more than once, and it now sells branded weight-loss drugs, with Eli Lilly's Zepbound and Foundayo being added next to Novo's, according to TIKR. In the third quarter it also launched testosterone replacement therapy, its first controlled substances, and announced a product called Peptides Plus for late November. Andrew Huberman was appointed to the board in the November 9 filing. Management still targets $6.5 billion of revenue and $1.3 billion of adjusted EBITDA in 2030, a 20% margin. I treat that as the Bull case, not the Base.

## Free cash flow and what it leaves out

The company defines free cash flow as cash from operations less capital spending and capitalized software. For nine months that is $118.8 million less $89.1 million less $18.9 million, or $10.8 million. Three things sit outside it. Stock compensation, $143.5 million in nine months, is a real cost paid in shares. The company sold $434.5 million of receivables to JPMorgan under an uncommitted $400 million program, which pulls cash forward, and the amount by which this flatters the cash flow is not disclosed. Manufacturer rebate receivables on the balance sheet were still $345.3 million. And $130.3 million of deferred acquisition payments went out as cash outside operations.

In the Base, revenue grows 20% in 2027 as Eucalyptus and the new categories annualize, then 15%, 12%, 10%, 8%, 7%, 6% and 5%. ## Reasons to own Hims & Hers

- Revenue growth of 49% in the quarter, the fastest in more than a year, with United States revenue still growing 21% on a large base.
- Revenue per subscriber of $105, up 30%, which shows the customer base is being moved to higher-value products.
- International revenue of $233.3 million a quarter, with management expecting a run rate above $1 billion in 2027.
- $712.2 million of liquidity, an undrawn credit line and zero-coupon convertible notes that do not mature until 2030 and 2032.
- New categories, testosterone therapy and Peptides Plus, plus access to both Novo Nordisk and Eli Lilly drugs.

## What could go wrong?

- **Margin:** gross margin fell 17 points in a year. If branded weight-loss drugs keep margin near 57%, the 20% EBITDA target in 2030 does not happen. One point of margin is worth $2.20 a share.
- **Subscribers and marketing:** subscribers fell in the quarter, and the CFO said earlier that investment will cause variability. The company's own CEO said he would accept subscriber losses to protect economics, according to TIKR.
- **Regulation and supply:** the business depends on drug supply from two manufacturers, on rules for compounded drugs that already changed once, and now on controlled-substance rules for testosterone. A compounded GLP-1 stream made up about 18% of fourth quarter 2025 sales, per one secondary source, and it has since been eroded.
- **Acquisition payments:** $563.1 million of deferred payments and $170.8 million of earn-outs are owed, and the earn-out targets depend on 2026 to 2028 revenue and EBITDA. Paying in shares avoids cash but dilutes.
- **Cash flow quality:** reported free cash flow of $10.8 million over nine months includes proceeds from receivable sales whose effect is not disclosed, and it is before $143.5 million of stock compensation.
- **Guidance record:** three cuts to the profit outlook in 2026, and a fourth quarter revenue guide that implies almost no growth from the third quarter.
- **Valuation of my own numbers:** the Base is a top-down path with my estimates for stock compensation, capital spending and tax. It is not a product-by-product forecast.

## Management execution

Management has grown revenue 49% by adding categories and countries quickly, and it is adding Lilly's drugs next to Novo's. The same record includes three cuts to 2026 profit guidance, a surprise third quarter loss, a 17 point drop in gross margin and a subscriber decline that came after management chose to restrain marketing. The 2030 targets ($6.5 billion and $1.3 billion) have been kept through all of it. I score management 5 out of 10: the speed of execution is real, but forecasts have missed repeatedly and the capital is going into acquisitions whose payments are still owed. The company has two classes of shares (Class A and Class V); I did not verify the voting split.
