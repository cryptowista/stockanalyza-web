On November 5 Rockwell Automation reported adjusted earnings of $3.88 a share against about $3.72 expected, and the stock fell 14% in a single session. It has since recovered about 11%, to $402.46. The drop was not about the quarter. It was about what the shares were already assuming, and after the rebound they still assume a lot.

My Base value is $274 a share, the Bear value $167 and the Bull value $344. The Base blends a mid-cycle EV/EBITDA multiple ($313) with a discounted owner free cash flow ($214). I require a 40% margin of safety, which puts the maximum buy price at $164. This is a verdict on the price and not on the business: Rockwell is one of the better industrial companies I look at, and the Reduce call says that the shares already pay for that.

Valuation route: cyclical industrial automation company with high returns on capital and positive cash flow, valued mainly on a multiple of mid-cycle EBITDA (the average level of profit across an industrial cycle), cross-checked with a ten year discounted cash flow on owner free cash flow, which is free cash flow after stock pay. Because the profit depends on the industrial cycle, the Bear case includes a downturn year.

What Rockwell is

Rockwell makes the controllers, drives, sensors and software that run factories, and its Logix controller line is the heart of the business. The company reports three segments. Intelligent Devices (drives, motion, safety and components) had sales of $4,185 million in fiscal 2026, up 11%, at a 20.0% margin. Software & Control (Logix controllers and software) had $2,818 million, up 18%, at a 33.8% margin, 31% of segment sales. Lifecycle Services (projects and support) had $2,071 million, down 6%, at a 15.1% margin.

Primary valuation: mid-cycle EV/EBITDA

Rockwell is a cyclical company, because its customers cut capital spending quickly in a downturn. A multiple on peak profit would flatter it, so I apply the multiple to mid-cycle EBITDA. I take fiscal 2027 sales of $9,528 million at an enterprise operating margin of 22.5%, which is between this year's 22.2% and the 23% guided, plus 2.1 points of sales for depreciation to get EBITDA, the ratio the company used in fiscal 2026. That gives $2,344 million.

I apply 16 times in the Base. The framework treats 12 to 18 times as the range for a quality industrial. Rockwell sits in the upper half because it earns about 20% on invested capital, almost a third of its segment sales are software at a 34% margin, and it converts profit to cash at about 100%. It does not go higher because growth of 6% to 9% is cyclical and ARR growth is slowing. At 16 times the owner free cash flow yield on enterprise value is about 4.2%, which with a 9.5% cost of capital implies that cash flow keeps growing near 5% a year forever. That is already a generous assumption.

Sales grow 6.0% in fiscal 2027, then 6.5%, 6.0%, 5.5% and fading to 3.5% by fiscal 2036, with an operating margin of 23.0%, 23.5% and then 23.8%, in line with incremental margins of about 35%. Cash flow is 96% of after-tax operating profit less stock pay. In the terminal value, growth is 3.0% and each new dollar of capital earns 15%, so 20% of profit is reinvested.

How the buy price is set

I add nothing for the industrial cycle because the Bear case contains a downturn year, and nothing for the divergence because it already lowered the confidence grade. $274 times 0.60 is $164, 59% below today's price. The multiple and the cash flow give $188 and $128 separately.

Dividend and buybacks

The dividend is $1.45 a quarter, payable December 10 to holders of record on the ex-date of November 16, a yield of 1.4% and about 39% of fiscal 2027 adjusted earnings at the midpoint. Buybacks fall to about $600 million from $1.25 billion as capital spending rises. At 27 times earnings every buyback dollar buys an earnings yield of 3.7%, so repurchases add little to value at this price.

Reasons to own Rockwell

  • Software & Control earns a 34% margin and grew 18% in fiscal 2026, with Logix growth of 25% in the fourth quarter. Rockwell reached its long-term 22% margin target early.
  • The chief executive cites record orders for new U.S. plant capacity, and guidance points to 6% to 9% organic growth in fiscal 2027.
  • Return on invested capital is 20.4% adjusted, and free cash flow of $1.7 billion funds a growing dividend and buybacks with net debt at 1.3 times EBITDA.
  • Semiconductor and discrete industry demand broadens the customer base beyond the classic factory cycle.

What could go wrong?

  • Price: the shares fell 14% in one day on a quarter that beat earnings. They are priced for no disappointment and can fall again.
  • Industrial cycle: customers delay projects quickly when demand softens, and Lifecycle Services is already shrinking.
  • Recurring revenue: ARR growth has slowed four quarters in a row to 5%, which weakens the software story behind the premium multiple.
  • Costs and tariffs: memory chip inflation hits Software & Control hardest, and Section 232 and USMCA tariff uncertainty hangs over auto customers, about 10% of sales.
  • Earnings quality: fourth quarter results included tariff refunds, a customer refund accrual and a charitable contribution. Fiscal 2027 growth is guided against a base that includes them.
  • Unchecked liabilities: I did not verify the pension and asbestos obligations.
  • My own numbers: the ten year path, the 9.5% cost of capital, the 16 times multiple and the share count are my estimates.

Management execution

Management has delivered. Fiscal 2026 adjusted earnings rose 27%, the long-term margin target was reached early, 3.1 million shares were repurchased and the dividend was raised again. The fourth quarter beat consensus by about 4%, yet the shares fell 14%, which shows that investors expected more than a beat. The planned step up in capital spending and the drop in buybacks show management is spending on growth rather than on the share price. I score management 8 out of 10.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Organic ARR growth back above 7%
  • Free cash flow of about 100% of adjusted net income after the step up in capital spending

Yellow

  • Organic growth between 4% and 6% or an operating margin between 22% and 23%
  • ARR growth at 5% for another quarter
  • Lifecycle Services margin below 15%

Red

  • Organic sales falling for a quarter or guidance cut
  • Operating margin below 21% or incremental margins below 25%
  • Free cash flow conversion below 85% without a clear one-off

The first quarter report is due in early 2027. Management has guided to organic growth in the mid-single digits and adjusted earnings of about $3.00, so the first test of the margin and price assumptions comes then.

The three most important thesis breakers

These are the events that would make the Reduce call wrong, or confirm it faster.

  1. Sales growth of 8% to 9% for several years with margins near 25%, led by semiconductors and data centers. That moves the value toward the Bull of $344, and the market keeps paying 20 times EBITDA.
  2. Investors keep paying a 27 times earnings multiple. My value does not move, but the shares can stay above it for years.
  3. A capital spending downturn that cuts sales by 3% or more, which moves the value toward the Bear of $167.

The weakest points are the cost of capital, the multiple, the share count and the unchecked pension and asbestos items. Final verdict: reduce at $402.46

Rockwell is an excellent company in a good phase of its cycle, and the shares have recovered from the November drop. The price is the problem. At $402.46 the shares trade 47% above my Base, 17% above my Bull and at 20 times mid-cycle EBITDA, and the cash flows return 6.5% a year against a 9.5% cost of capital.

The call is Reduce because the price is 32% below my Base value and above my Bull value. It is not a call about the business, which is in good shape. For new money the numbers say wait: I would start looking again near $164, and I would revisit the model sooner if sales growth runs at 8% or more with margins near 25%. A holder with a low cost basis also has to weigh tax before selling.

Verdict: REDUCE at $402.46. Base $273.59, -32.0% against the price, Bear $167.36, Bull $343.74, Confidence C (17/25). Required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $164.15.
