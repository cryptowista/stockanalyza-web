Arista's third quarter revenue was $3.66 billion, up 58.5% from a year earlier, and management told investors to expect about $18.5 billion next year, another 38% to 40%. The stock went the other way: from a closing high of $216.74 on October 9 it slid into the results, jumped 5% the day after, fell 10.8% on November 5 in a broad sell-off of AI infrastructure names and closed at $170.80 on November 6. ## What Arista is

Arista, based in Santa Clara, California, sells the Ethernet switches and the operating system, EOS, that move data between servers in large data centers. Its biggest buyers are the cloud and AI giants; in 2025 two unnamed customers took 26% and 16% of revenue, 42% together, up from 35% a year earlier. In the 2024 annual report the two were named as Microsoft and Meta. The newer products are Etherlink switches for AI clusters, where Arista now counts more than 100 customers, and campus networking for offices.

The interesting detail is what Arista does not do. It does not make the chips inside its switches and it does not sell the GPUs; it wins because its software lets one operating system run everything from a top-of-rack switch to a 100,000-GPU cluster. That is a real advantage, and it shows in a 64% gross margin. It is also a dependence: the competition includes Cisco, Nvidia's own networking, and white-box switches that the largest customers can design themselves.

## Which numbers to trust

- **Adjusted profit leaves out stock pay.** Stock-based compensation was $379.4 million in the first nine months, about 4% of revenue. I count it as a cost, so my operating margin starts at 45.5% in 2027 instead of the guided 49%.
- **Free cash flow trails profit.** Inventory and receivables grow with sales. I charge 12% of each extra dollar of revenue as working capital and 1% of revenue as net capital spending.
- **Interest income is not operating profit.** The $14.36 billion of cash earns interest; I add the cash to the value once and leave its income out of the cash flows.
- **Tax rises.** The rate was 18.5% in the third quarter; management guides about 21% for 2027, and I use 21% throughout.

## History and earnings check

Arista has earned a premium for a decade, and earned it. Revenue went from $7.0 billion in 2024 to about $13.35 billion this year, and free cash flow per share from $2.87 in 2024 to $3.33 in 2025; my Base has $4.57 for 2027. On adjusted earnings of about $4.37 a share for 2026 (nine months plus the fourth quarter guide), the price is 39 times this year's profit and about 28.5 times my rough estimate of $5.99 for 2027. The growth-adjusted price to earnings ratio on 2027 is about 0.8, which looks cheap, but it divides by a single year of 37% growth that comes from a spending boom by a handful of customers; I treat it as a flag, not a value. The free cash flow yield is 2.3% on the last 12 months and 2.7% on my 2027 figure.

## Reasons to own Arista

- Revenue up 58.5% in the third quarter with an adjusted operating margin of 49.1%, among the highest in hardware.
- A 2027 guide of about $18.5 billion, with AI networking doubling to about $7 billion.
- $14.36 billion of net cash, no debt and a share count that has not grown.
- One operating system across data center, AI cluster and campus, which keeps customers on Arista as they scale.
- Management that raised its 2026 revenue outlook every quarter, from about $11.25 billion to about $13.35 billion.

## What could go wrong?

- Concentration. Two customers bought 42% of 2025 revenue, and management expects three or four above 10% this year.
- A spending pause. AI data center budgets set the pace; a digestion year would hit revenue and margins together, as in my Bear.
- Competition. Nvidia sells its own networking, Cisco expects about $7.5 billion of AI infrastructure revenue in its fiscal 2027, and the largest customers can build white-box switches.
- Supply bets. $11.4 billion of purchase commitments and $3.21 billion of inventory are fine in a boom and expensive in a pause.
- Margin. Bigger customers get bigger discounts; the adjusted gross margin is already down from 65.2% to 64.0% in a year.

The options market expects movement. At-the-money implied volatility averaged about 56% in October, mid-range for the past year, which suggests a typical one-month move of about 16%, or $27.60, either way. I could not get a reliable read on how much more downside protection costs than upside, so I draw no tail-risk conclusion. Short interest is small at about 1% of shares. None of this changes the value.

## Management execution

- The 2026 revenue outlook was raised from about $11.25 billion in February to about $13.35 billion now.
- Operating margins held near 49% while revenue grew almost 50%.
- The balance sheet stayed debt-free and the share count flat despite steady stock pay.
- Since the 2025 annual report the largest customers are no longer named, which makes concentration harder to track.
- Buybacks were heavy in 2025 and stopped in 2026, without a stated reason.

## What I would watch from here

The full-year report, expected in February 2027, has to show fourth quarter revenue inside the $3.9 billion to $4.0 billion guide, a confirmed 2027 outlook of about $18.5 billion, an adjusted gross margin of at least 62%, and inventory and purchase commitments growing no faster than revenue.

### Green

- 2027 outlook raised above $19 billion.
- Adjusted gross margin of 63% or more.
- Deferred revenue growing again.

### Yellow

- 2027 outlook held at about $18.5 billion with a lower margin.
- Inventory rising faster than revenue for a second quarter.
- A top customer above 30% of revenue.

### Red

- 2027 outlook cut below $17 billion.
- Adjusted gross margin below 60%.
- A large customer moving AI back-end networking to another vendor or to white-box switches.

Option prices are worth a glance before the February report: a sharp rise in the cost of downside protection would tell me the market expects a guide cut, though it would not change the value on its own.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. A pause in AI data center spending by two or three customers, which leads toward the Bear.
2. Margin pressure from bigger discounts or competition from Nvidia networking and white-box switches.
3. Revenue beating the 2027 guide by $1 billion or more with margins intact, which leads toward the Bull and would make today's price fair.

## Final verdict: hold at $170.80, do not add

Arista is one of the best hardware businesses in the market: 64% gross margins, almost half of revenue left as operating profit, $14 billion of cash and a product that the largest AI builders keep buying. The 21% fall from the October high took out the froth, not the optimism. At $170.80 you are buying my Bull case at full price.

**Verdict: HOLD at $170.80, do not add. Base $129.15, 24.4% below the price, Bear $62.71, Bull $170.38, Confidence C (17.5/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $74.26.**
