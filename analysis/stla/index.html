Stellantis: €10.0 billion of net cash, €4.9 billion of it from hybrid bonds

STLA, NYSE. Published October 11, 2026. Price as of the October 9, 2026 regular session close. Stellantis reports in euros; the share price is in US dollars, converted at 1.1202 dollars per euro.

Hold at $4.48

Hold, do not add. At $4.48 the shares are 34.7% below my Base value of $6.87, and the price already assumes an operating margin of only 2.8%, close to what the company earns today. But the price is above the maximum buy price of $3.09, and the Bear case is 77.7% lower. The buy zone starts at $3.09.

Stellantis reports €10.0 billion of industrial net cash at June 30, 2026. Of that, €4.9 billion came from hybrid bonds sold in March, perpetual debt that accounting treats as equity. Strip them out and net cash is €5.1 billion, against €15.1 billion at the end of 2024. The whole company, 2.91 billion diluted shares at €4.00, is worth €11.6 billion on the stock market. Industrial free cash flow was negative in 2024 (€6 billion), negative in 2025 (€4.5 billion) and negative again in the first half of 2026 (€0.9 billion). The cheap-looking price and the cash line are the same company.

At the October 9, 2026 close of $4.48 (€4.00), the shares trade 34.7% below my triangulated Base value of $6.87. The Bear value is $1.00 and the Bull value is $12.94. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 55%, so the maximum buy price is $3.09, about 31% below today's price. The question is not whether the shares are cheap against the company's own plan. It is whether the plan's margin shows up before the cash runs thinner.

Valuation route: industrial and manufacturing. Subtype: global automaker with a captive finance arm, commodity-like cyclical tier. Economic classification: Standard (2/12). Primary method: enterprise value (EV, equity value plus net debt) as a multiple of mid-cycle adjusted operating income (AOI) after recurring charges, with AOI built from revenue times margin in three scenarios. Secondary method: a scenario cash flow model (DCF) of unlevered owner free cash flow to 2030 with a terminal value. Cross-check: the owner free cash flow multiple implied by the primary value. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

What Stellantis is

Stellantis sells Jeep, Ram, Dodge, Chrysler, Peugeot, Citroën, Fiat, Opel, Maserati and other brands. It shipped 2.96 million vehicles in the first half of 2026 and reports in segments: North America, Enlarged Europe, Middle East and Africa, South America and Asia Pacific, plus a captive finance business (Stellantis Financial Services) that lends to its customers and dealers. Net revenues in 2025 were €153.5 billion. The segments are very unequal. In the first half of 2026, South America (€795 million of AOI) and Middle East and Africa (€611 million) earned more than North America (€547 million), and Enlarged Europe lost €86 million.

Three facts define the setup:

  • A reset. In February 2026 the company took about €22.2 billion of charges in the second half of 2025 (full-year unusual charges €25.4 billion) for its product plan and electric vehicle supply chain, warranty estimates and workforce cuts. About €6.5 billion of that is expected to be paid in cash over four years. The 2025 net loss was €22.3 billion and the 2026 dividend is suspended.
  • A recovery in progress. First half 2026 revenue was €81.6 billion (+9.9%) and AOI was €1,733 million, a 2.1% margin, against €540 million (0.7%) a year earlier. Guidance for 2026 is mid-single-digit revenue growth, a low-single-digit AOI margin and better industrial free cash flow, with the second half improving on the first.
  • A plan. At the May 21 Investor Day, management set targets of an AOI margin of 5% in 2028 and 7% in 2030, revenue of €190 billion in 2030, positive industrial free cash flow in 2027, €3 billion in 2028 and €6 billion a year by 2030, and €6 billion of annual cost savings by 2028, with more than €60 billion of investment. These are management's targets, not mine, and I do not use them as the Base.

The latest quarter

The second quarter looks like a recovery quarter, and the numbers are real, but they start from a low base. Net profit was €293 million against a €1,869 million loss a year earlier. The first half includes a €317 million gain from renegotiated regulatory credit purchase commitments that is excluded from AOI, and €784 million of restructuring, Takata airbag recall and program cancellation charges that are also excluded.

Over the last four quarters AOI adds up to only about €351 million (the second half of 2025 was a €1,382 million loss, my arithmetic from the full-year and first-half figures). Cash is the weaker line. The first half cash flow was a €2,887 million outflow from operations and a €921 million outflow on the industrial measure, with a €1,370 million working capital drag that the company links mainly to the net tariff impact in North America. Why net cash is not what it looks like

The reported industrial net financial position, the industrial cash and securities minus industrial debt, was €10,035 million at June 30, against €6,694 million at December 31, 2025 and €15.1 billion at the end of 2024. It rose in the first half even though industrial free cash flow was negative, because the company issued about €4.9 billion of hybrid perpetual notes in March. Hybrids are classified as equity, so my reading, which the filing extract does not state in so many words, is that they raise reported net cash without being counted as debt. Owners cannot treat them that way: they pay a coupon and rank ahead of the common stock.

Shares, dilution and capital return

Weighted average shares for the first half were 2,898.1 million basic and 2,909.4 million diluted (the 2025 comparison was 2,882.6 million). I use 2,909.4 million diluted shares. Market value on that count is €11.64 billion ($13.03 billion). The 2026 dividend is suspended. The 2025 dividend, paid out of 2024 earnings at €0.68 a share, cost about €2 billion and there is no buyback in the extract. The hybrids carry a coupon I could not read, and the €4.9 billion principal is deducted in full. Stellantis expects capex and research spending of 6.5% to 7% of revenue a year.

Primary valuation: mid-cycle AOI after recurring charges at 3.5x

The formula: enterprise value equals (revenue times AOI margin, minus €1.2 billion of recurring charges) times 3.5. Common equity is that value plus the €0.1 billion of adjusted net cash. Per share equals equity divided by 2,909.4 million shares, converted to dollars at 1.1202.

I use operating income, not EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), because EBITDA overstates what an automaker keeps. Capex and research run at 6.5% to 7% of revenue, and capitalized development costs are amortized: in the first half, €1,273 million was capitalized and €923 million amortized. The framework's commodity tier of 5x to 8x EBITDA is therefore not applicable here. Three assumptions carry the model:

  • Revenue of €160 billion (2025 was €153.5 billion; the 2026 pace is above €160 billion). The plan's €190 billion in 2030 is not assumed.
  • Recurring charges of €1.2 billion a year. AOI excludes restructuring, recalls and program cancellations. In the first half these came to €784 million before the €317 million credit gain, so a full-year rate would be about €1.6 billion; I use €1.2 billion as a mid-cycle level.
  • The multiple of 3.5x is an assumption, not a peer median (I had no verified peer table). It equals about 10.7 times unlevered owner free cash flow in the Base. The multiple is the same in all three scenarios, so the margin alone separates them and the downside is not counted twice.

Secondary valuation: scenario cash flow model

The primary method values a steady state. The cash flow model walks there from today and shows how much of the value depends on the destination. Owner free cash flow here is unlevered: AOI minus recurring charges, minus 25% tax on the result, minus €2.2 billion a year of reinvestment above depreciation, working capital and other items, minus the remaining restructuring cash (€1.8 billion in 2027, €1.3 billion in 2028, €0.8 billion in 2029). The €2.2 billion is my estimate, set so the model reproduces about the plan's €6 billion of free cash flow at a 7% margin and 2030 revenue near €175 billion. Revenue is €160 billion from 2027 and the discount rate is 11%, with 1% terminal growth. The second half of 2026 contributes +€0.5 billion in the Base (-€0.5 billion Bear, +€1.5 billion Bull), my assumption for a seasonally better half after the €0.9 billion first-half outflow.

Classification: cyclical rebound from a low base, with a low-reliability forecast because the plan's free cash flow depends on a margin that has not been seen since 2024. The growth-adjusted price to earnings check (PEG) is not meaningful. Trailing GAAP earnings are a loss of about €7.71 a share, adjusted diluted earnings were €0.32 in the first half, and growth off a loss is not a growth rate. Cyclical distortion: high. It does not set the Base, the MOS or the verdict.

Valuation triangulation

The two Base values differ by 6.7%, well inside the 20% band. The Bull values differ by 29.6%, just inside the warning band, because the cash flow model gives a much larger terminal value when the margin reaches 6%. The Bear cash flow value is zero, so the divergence formula has nothing to measure. I weight the primary method 60% because it is the framework's engine and rests on one margin assumption. I weight the cash flow model 40% because 92% of its Base value is terminal and it leaves out the finance arm. A 50/50 split gives $6.82 and a 70/30 split $6.91, so the weighting changes the Base by about five cents.

How the buy price is set

A Confidence score of 9/25 is grade D, which starts the MOS at 50%. I add 5 points for cyclicality, because the margin swung from 5.5% to -0.5% in a year, and 5 points for accounting opacity, because AOI excluded €25.4 billion of unusual charges in 2025 and €0.8 billion in the first half of 2026. I subtract 5 points for the balance sheet, with €44.1 billion of industrial liquidity and a positive adjusted net cash position. Concentration and the method gap (6.7% in the Base) add nothing. The result is 55%, under the 60% ceiling, so a price is issued.

Maximum buy price: $6.87 times (1 - 0.55) = $3.09. That is the value the primary method gives for a margin of about 2.2%, close to what the company earned in the first half. At the maximum buy price the shares would sit 31% below today's price and below the 52-week low of $4.30. The price today is higher, so the verdict is Hold, even though the shares are 34.7% below the Base.

Dividend and capital return

The 2026 dividend is suspended. The 2025 payment was €0.68 a share, about €2 billion. The plan says it supports shareholder returns, and gives no policy or amount. Neither valuation assumes a dividend or a buyback, and the hybrid coupons are a claim ahead of both.

Reasons to own STLA

  • The price pays for little. It implies a margin of 2.8% at 3.5x, about the level of the first half, and the plan calls for 5% in 2028.
  • The recovery has started. AOI rose from €540 million to €1,733 million in the first half, and North America moved from a €982 million loss to €547 million of profit.
  • The cash drain is shrinking. Industrial free cash flow improved by €2.1 billion in the first half, and the second quarter was positive at about €1 billion according to a summary of the report.
  • Liquidity is large. €44.1 billion of industrial liquidity, 28% of annual revenue, within the company's own 25% to 30% range.
  • A sizeable cost program. €6 billion of annual savings by 2028 is 3.9% of 2025 revenue.

The Bull case of $12.94 includes a margin of 6%, still below the plan's 7% for 2030. It does not need the plan to work in full; it needs about 80% of it.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: the margin stays near 2%. Each point of margin is worth $2.16 a share. At 1.5% the primary method gives $1.66.
  • Tariffs. The net 2026 headwind is €1.0 billion to €1.2 billion, and the first half already shows a €1.37 billion working capital drag linked mainly to them.
  • North America inventory. Shipments rose 38% in the second quarter against a 6% rise in sales, and dealer inventory is 43% above a year ago.
  • Europe. Enlarged Europe lost €94 million in the second quarter, and market share fell 80 basis points to 16%. The plan needs 3% to 5% there.
  • Cash and ratings. About €5 billion of restructuring cash is still to be paid, S&P's outlook is negative at BBB-, and the company has already issued €4.9 billion of hybrids this year. A further downgrade or raise would change the equity.
  • More charges. The company has taken €25.4 billion of unusual charges in one year, and the first half added €784 million before credits.
  • Unseen claims. Pension and lease liabilities and the finance arm's funding are not in my bridge.
  • Currency. The euro reports; the dollar prices. A move from 1.12 to 1.05 changes the Base by $0.44.

I assign no probability to any of these, and the price does not prove they are all discounted.

Management execution

The record is mixed, and the new plan has not yet been tested. In February 2025 the company said it expected positive cash generation in 2025; free cash flow was -€4.5 billion and the year ended with a €22.3 billion net loss and a charge that rewrote its electric vehicle and product plans. Against that, first half 2026 results beat the prior year on every line, the 2026 guidance was reaffirmed in July, and the Investor Day set measurable targets. Capital allocation has been poor: €3 billion of buybacks and €4.7 billion of dividends in 2024 were followed by a year of losses, and net cash fell from €15.1 billion to €6.7 billion. Assessment: 6/20 on the framework's credibility scale, mixed to low. The next test is the third quarter report and a clear second-half free cash flow number.

  • Enlarged Europe AOI margin above 2%
  • Industrial free cash flow positive for the second half of 2026 and for 2027
  • Industrial net cash after hybrids at or above €5 billion

Yellow

  • Quarterly AOI margin between 2% and 4%
  • North America margin between 1% and 4%, inventory between 80 and 100 days
  • Enlarged Europe between -1% and 2%
  • Industrial free cash flow around zero for the second half
  • Industrial net cash after hybrids between €2 billion and €5 billion
  • Net tariff headwind between €0.8 billion and €1.5 billion

Red

  • Quarterly AOI margin below 2%
  • North America margin below 1% or inventory above 100 days
  • Enlarged Europe below -1%

On the evidence I have, two lines are green and the rest are yellow. The first half margin is 2.1% and the second quarter 1.8%, which is yellow and close to red. North America at 1.6% in the second quarter and 92 days of inventory are yellow. Europe at -0.6% is yellow. Industrial net cash after hybrids is about €5.1 billion, at the green threshold. The tariff headwind is €1.0 billion to €1.2 billion, yellow. The next hard date is the third quarter report, which the page listing shows for late October.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The operating margin does not leave the 2% range. At 2.5% the primary method gives $3.81 and at 1.5% $1.66, and each point is worth $2.16 a share.
  2. Cash goes the wrong way again: another negative half of free cash flow with €5 billion of restructuring cash still to pay would push net cash after hybrids toward zero and raise the chance of another hybrid or equity raise at a low price.
  3. On the upside, North America reaches a 4% to 5% margin with inventory under control and industrial free cash flow turns positive in 2027 as guided. That is the path to the Base of $6.87 and above, and it can be seen in two or three quarterly reports.
