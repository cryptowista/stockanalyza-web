## What UGI is

UGI distributes and markets energy in the United States and Europe. Its segments are Utilities (regulated natural gas in Pennsylvania and West Virginia, plus an electric utility that is being sold), Midstream and Marketing (pipelines, storage and energy marketing), AmeriGas Propane (retail propane in the United States) and UGI International (propane and other energy in Europe). In FY26 the reportable segments earned $1,198 million of EBIT. About 77% of the roughly $900 million of capital invested in FY26 went to the natural gas businesses.

Three corporate events set the valuation. The first is the pending sale of the Pennsylvania electric utility for about $470 million, which I include at $425 million after an estimated tax on the gain. The second is the August 18 offer from KKR at $42.50 a share, about $9 billion, reported by the Wall Street Journal on the basis of people familiar with the matter. UGI has not agreed to it. The third is the November 19 announcement that the board unanimously agreed to spin off retail propane. The shares fell from $38.02 to $35.18 that day, according to the Philadelphia Inquirer. I could not confirm where UGI International will sit after the split, so I value the two propane-heavy businesses as one block that does not depend on how they are divided.

## Primary valuation: sum of the parts on earnings

I value Utilities on normalized after-tax earnings of $266 million (pretax $337 million at 21% tax), which equals FY26 reported net income. The multiple is 15.0 times, a regulated gas multiple. It implies low single-digit growth in earnings, a durable franchise with regulated returns, and a heavy reinvestment need that the company funds partly with debt. I do not use a higher multiple because the 10 year Treasury yield is near 5% and the balance sheet is stretched. This is 10.3 times EBITDA.

Midstream and Marketing is $172 million of normalized net income at 12.5 times, or 7.9 times EBITDA. I cut earnings 16% from FY26 reported net income of $204 million, a judgment, because marketing margins and weather swing this segment. Propane and international are $740 million of EBITDA at 7.5 times, which is 7.5 times and equals 5,550 million of enterprise value. The $740 million is my estimate of the combined EBITDA, which the company does not report. The multiple reflects mature volumes, exposure to weather and a European business with gas and electricity price risk.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Holding Utilities, Midstream and Marketing and the other items at Base, the price of $35.56 implies that propane and international are worth 4,890 million of EV, which is 6.6 times my EBITDA estimate against 7.5 times in Base. Holding the propane block and the other items at Base instead, the price implies a blended multiple of 12.5 times earnings for Utilities and Midstream and Marketing, against 14.0 times in Base. In both cases the market is asking for a little less than my central case, not much less, and not as little as my Bear case.

On a one year view, if earnings grow 4.5% from my $2.89 and the multiple stays at 12.3 times, the shares return about 8.7% including the 4.2% dividend. If the multiple moves to the 13.6 times that my Base value implies, the return is about 19%. These are model returns for information, not forecasts.

## Reasons to own

- About 77% of capital spending goes to regulated gas, where investment earns an allowed return.
- The shares are 9.2% below my Base value and 19.5% below a reported offer from a buyer with a stated interest in the whole company.
- A separation can show the value of each business more clearly, and the chief executive has said he is open to a sale of the whole.
- The dividend yield is 4.2% and is 50% of adjusted earnings.

## What could go wrong?

- Debt allocation. Net debt is 3.8 times EBITDA, and the split between the new companies is not disclosed. If the holding company keeps $500 million more, my Base falls to $36.91.
- Rates. A 10 year yield near 5% pressures regulated multiples. At 13.5 times earnings for Utilities, the Base value is $37.51.
- Propane earnings. AmeriGas earned nothing for shareholders in FY26 after interest and tax, and the weather and European energy prices swing the rest. A 6.5 times multiple gives $35.83.
- Refinancing. Dated maturities include AmeriGas 5.75% notes in May 2027, a $400 million term loan in October 2027, AmeriGas 9.375% notes and the convertible senior notes in June 2028. I did not find the full schedule or the principal of each, so the refinancing need in the next 18 months is an open data gap.
- Separation costs. I assume $250 million, and the company has not published a figure.

## Management execution

Adjusted EPS fell 9.6% to $3.00, the FY27 guidance of $2.90 to $3.20 is wide for a utility-weighted group, and the free cash flow does not cover the dividend. In management's favor, the board has approved a plan to separate, an electric utility sale for about $470 million is pending, and the chief executive is open to a sale of the whole company. The record is mixed, not poor. The separation will be the test, because it moves debt, costs and the dividend, and none of the three has been disclosed.

## What I would watch from here

**Green:** the separation filing shows net debt for the propane company at or below about $2.3 billion, a dividend policy that keeps $1.50 in total, and the first-quarter FY27 report tracks toward at least the $3.05 midpoint of guidance.

**Yellow:** separation costs above $250 million, a guidance range that moves lower without a new explanation, or net borrowing above $100 million to cover the dividend.

**Red:** more than $500 million of extra debt left at the holding company, a dividend cut, or FY27 guidance below $2.90.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. A debt allocation that loads the holding company and the new utility business with more than $500 million beyond my assumption.
2. A fall in propane and international earnings that takes the multiple of 7.5 times toward 6.5 times.
3. A rise in long rates that pushes the regulated multiple down to 13.5 times.

## Final verdict: hold at $35.56, watchlist for new money

UGI is being priced as if the offer in August never happened and as if the propane business is worth a little more than a weak year. My sum of the parts says the market is about right, and the cheap parts are mostly not available to a new buyer until the separation shows where the debt goes. My maximum buy price is $22.52.

**Verdict: HOLD at $35.56, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $39.17, upside +10.1%, Confidence C (13.8/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by method $22.16 to $23.06, maximum buy price $22.52.**

The date I would mark is the first-quarter FY27 report, which I expect in early February, when the company has to show whether $3.05 is a floor or a hope, and whether a split that was meant to unlock value has first moved its debt.
