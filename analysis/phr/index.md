## What Phreesia is

Phreesia sells the software a patient meets at check-in: intake forms, insurance and identity capture, consent and the payment screen. The company says it touched more than 180 million visits in calendar 2025, roughly one in six US visits. Three businesses run on that position. Subscription fees from providers were $52.7 million in the second quarter, 40.7% of revenue. Payment processing and, since November 12, 2025, patient financing through AccessOne brought $38.5 million, or 29.7%. Network solutions, which is marketing from pharmaceutical brands delivered to patients and clinicians at the point of care, brought $38.3 million, or 29.6%.

The fiscal year ends January 31, so fiscal 2027 (FY2027) runs from February 2026 to January 2027. The mix matters more than the total. Subscription revenue, the part a software investor would pay up for, fell 1.9% year over year in the second quarter because the company is deliberately moderating prices for providers under payer pressure. Network revenue grew 8.9%, but it depends on pharma brand budgets, and in March 2026 those budgets were the reason fiscal 2027 revenue guidance was cut from $545 to $559 million to $510 to $520 million, 6.7% lower at the midpoint.

AccessOne, bought for $153.2 million in cash, adds a financing layer that a pure software company does not have. On July 31 it carried $88.9 million of cardholder receivables at fair value against $147.3 million of unpaid principal, $89.5 million owed to providers, a $21.5 million deferred purchase price receivable and an off-balance-sheet securitization with PNC whose limit was raised to $300 million. Patient credit risk sits with providers, which have full recourse, and Phreesia is a joint and several co-guarantor of the servicer's obligations. AccessOne is expected to bring about $37 million of revenue in fiscal 2027, roughly 7% of the total.

## Why EBITDA after stock compensation is the metric

The company's adjusted EBITDA adds back stock compensation, acquisition and restructuring costs and everything in other expense. Each add-back has a stated reason. Together they explain how $12.5 million of trailing GAAP operating income becomes $122.0 million of adjusted EBITDA. Depreciation and amortization account for part of that gap. Stock pay accounts for $59.4 million of it, and stock pay is not a one-off here: it was between $58.8 million and $71.6 million in every fiscal year from 2023 to 2026.

The table is the reason the company definition of FCF is not the value anchor. On a $671.0 million market value, the headline FCF yield is 10.1%. On the last line it is 0.3%. So I value Phreesia on EBITDA minus the full SBC charge, and on owner cash flow after SBC, leases, capital spending and cash taxes.

One item sits below operating income and inside the add-backs: the $2.8 million fair value loss on AccessOne instruments in the second quarter, against about zero in the first. The filing does not split it into interest rate and credit effects. Economically it is the cost of the receivable book, carried at $88.9 million against $147.3 million of principal. One quarter is not a run rate, so it is not deducted in the Base, but it is tested below and it is on the dashboard as red.

## Shares, dilution and the balance sheet

The diluted count I use is 66.456 million: 62.071 million shares outstanding on August 27, 2026, 2.787 million restricted stock units, 1.423 million performance stock units at target and 0.175 million shares from in-the-money options under the treasury method. Weighted basic shares rose from 49.89 million in fiscal 2022 to 59.74 million in fiscal 2026, 4.6% a year, and cover-page shares rose 3.6% in the last twelve months. A buyback of up to 2.5 million shares was authorized in March 2025. None has been executed. An automatic shelf registration was filed on September 3, 2026, with no announced use.

Stock compensation is falling. It was $67.5 million in fiscal 2026, $25.4 million in the first half of fiscal 2027 against $33.5 million a year earlier, and $11.9 million in the second quarter. The second quarter was probably helped by forfeitures in the restructuring (377,299 restricted stock units forfeited in the first half against 1,067,797 granted), so the first half average of $12.7 million a quarter is the better signal. Unrecognized compensation of $46.9 million on restricted stock units and $19.7 million on performance units supports a run rate of $45 to $50 million, not a return to $59 million.

Because the full SBC charge is deducted and the diluted count already includes unvested units, the multiple carries no further dilution penalty. Using basic shares instead would give $11.65, which shows the size of that overlap.

The balance sheet is not the problem. On July 31 the company held $72.9 million of cash against $60.7 million drawn on a $275 million Capital One revolver at 6.4% and $4.6 million of finance leases. With $7.2 million of remaining restructuring charges added, adjusted net debt is -$0.40 million, a small net cash position. The $4.95 million ConnectOnCall data breach settlement is matched by a recorded insurance receivable of the same amount, so it nets to zero. Debt is 0.54 times trailing adjusted EBITDA. The federal net operating loss carryforward (NOL), past losses that can offset future taxable profit, was $587.2 million at January 31, 2026.

Ownership has one notable feature. Pale Fire Capital, a Czech investor, disclosed a 14.7% stake (8,924,329 shares, at an aggregate cost of about $103.5 million, roughly $11.60 a share) in its latest amendment, for an event dated March 31, 2026. Its filing states that it considers the shares undervalued and may engage with the board and management on capitalization, board composition and operations. No later amendment was found, so the current stake may differ. AQR Capital Management reported 5.1%.

The 52 week range of $7.77 to $24.25, and a 53.5% fall over the year, describe how far sentiment moved, not a fair multiple. The historical layer gets no weight.

On reported earnings the stock is 64.9 times trailing GAAP earnings of $0.17 a share, which makes a price to earnings to growth (PEG) ratio meaningless. The non-GAAP PEG of 0.33 adds back SBC, which is 38% of next year's adjusted EBITDA, so it compares numbers on different bases and is not used. Growth durability is normalizing and acquisition affected: it comes from cost cuts, lower SBC and AccessOne, not from organic demand.

Free cash flow moved from -$116.3 million in fiscal 2023 to $54.4 million in fiscal 2026, so a growth rate on it is not meaningful. None of these diagnostics sets the Base, the MOS or the verdict.

## Dividend and capital return

Phreesia has never paid a dividend, and its revolver restricts dividends. The 2.5 million share buyback authorized in March 2025 has not been used while the share count grew 3.6% to 4.6% a year. ## Reasons to own PHR

- **A hard position to replicate.** Phreesia sits in the check-in workflow of roughly one in six US visits. Average healthcare services clients grew 6% to 4,744 and revenue per client 4%, and that reach is what pharma marketers pay for.
- **Operating leverage has shown up.** Adjusted EBITDA went from $36.8 million in fiscal 2025 to $122.0 million over the trailing twelve months, and quarterly SBC fell from $16.2 million to $11.9 million.
- **A clean balance sheet.** Debt fell from $90.6 million to $60.7 million in six months, the AccessOne bridge was refinanced into a $275 million revolver at 6.4%, and the ConnectOnCall settlement is covered by insurance.
- **A large tax shield.** $587.2 million of federal NOLs shelter federal tax for years, worth about $0.41 a share in the Base.
- **A 14.7% holder with a stated view.** Pale Fire Capital's filing calls the shares undervalued and reserves the right to engage on capitalization and the board. A control transaction at 8x adjusted EBITDA before SBC would be worth $15.89 a share, and it is not in the Base.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: stock pay does not fall.** At the trailing $59.4 million the Base is $9.39, inside HOLD by $0.20. At $60.7 million or more it becomes Slightly Overvalued.
- **AccessOne credit cost.** The $2.8 million second quarter fair value loss is excluded from adjusted EBITDA. Recurring at the second quarter pace of $11.2 million a year, the Base is $9.11. Combined with SBC at the trailing level, even $5.6 million a year gives $8.50.
- **Pharma budgets and payer pricing.** Brand-specific and regulatory dynamics in pharma already forced one guidance cut. Payers squeeze provider budgets, and subscription revenue is down 1.9%.
- **The off-balance-sheet layer.** Phreesia co-guarantees the servicer obligations in a $300 million PNC securitization and must keep $50 million of minimum liquidity. A servicing failure or provider defaults would hit cash.
- **Legal and dilution.** Two securities class actions filed in May and July 2026 have no loss estimate. Dilution runs at about 4% a year with an unused buyback and a new automatic shelf in place.

## Management execution

The record has both directions in it. Fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance was raised from $87 to $92 million to $99 to $101 million, and the company delivered $101.5 million. The fiscal 2027 adjusted EBITDA range of $125 to $135 million has been held through three updates. Against that, fiscal 2027 revenue guidance was introduced in December 2025 at $545 to $559 million and cut to $510 to $520 million four months later. The adjusted EBITDA definition widened to add back acquisition costs from the third quarter of fiscal 2026 without restating earlier periods, the buyback has not been used while the share count rose, and two securities class actions allege misleading statements about the business and outlook. The allegations are unproven. **Assessment: 8/20, mixed. The next test is the third quarter of fiscal 2027, reported in early December 2026, with the second half EBITDA run rate, SBC and any further fair value loss.**
