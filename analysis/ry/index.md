# Royal Bank earned 18.1% on equity against a 17% objective, and the price still needs more

Royal Bank of Canada earned an adjusted return on equity of 18.1% in the quarter to July 31, 2026, a point above its objective of 17% or more. At C$263.05 the shares trade at 2.72 times book value and 16.3 times trailing adjusted earnings. My model says that price needs a return on equity of about 22% held for ten years, four points above what the bank is earning now.

The quarter was a record. Net income was C$6,024 million, up 11%, and adjusted EPS was C$4.28, up 11%. Revenue rose 9%, and trading revenue rose 40%. Personal Banking earned C$1,923 million, Wealth Management C$1,442 million and Capital Markets C$1,544 million. Credit stayed manageable: the provision for credit losses was C$1,000 million, or 35 basis points on impaired loans. The shares closed at C$263.05 on November 20 after falling 2.4% that day and about 9% over the week. They are still about 30% above where they stood a year ago and about 14% below the 52-week high of C$306.38.

My Base value is **C$227.09**, which is 15.8% below the price. The Bear value of C$155.13 is 41.0% below the price and the Bull value of C$260.19 is 1.1% below it too, so the price already sits above my optimistic case. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is C$217.38. At Confidence B my rules require a 30% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of **C$158.96**, or C$147.99 to C$175.42 depending on the method. The verdict is Hold for existing holders. New money should wait.

Valuation route: a large Canadian bank with big wealth, capital markets and insurance businesses, so I value it on residual income (earnings above the cost of equity, built on book value) as the primary method and on forward earnings as the second. Economic classification: Compounder (9/12). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

## What Royal Bank is

The bank has C$2,499 billion of assets, C$1,109 billion of loans and C$1,645 billion of deposits. Of the C$6,024 million earned in the third quarter, Personal Banking contributed 32%, Capital Markets 26%, Wealth Management 24%, Commercial Banking 16% (C$936 million) and Insurance 3% (C$197 million), with Corporate Support losing C$18 million. A large share of its earnings comes from fees, which helps its return on equity and also means about a quarter of earnings, from Capital Markets, follows markets.

Two corporate items matter. The HSBC Bank Canada acquisition is now fully in the numbers, and fiscal 2025 included five extra months of it. On August 10, 2026, Royal Bank and BMO agreed to sell their jointly owned payments business Moneris to Francisco Partners. Royal Bank expects about C$2 billion of cash for its 50% share and a gain of about C$475 million after tax, with closing expected by the end of the first quarter of FY27. I leave that gain out of the Base case.

## Primary valuation: residual income

Residual income values a bank as book value plus the present value of earnings above the cost of equity. I use a 9.0% cost of equity, which is my estimate: a Canadian long bond yield near 3.5% plus a 5.5% premium for equity risk at a beta close to 1. The stock's beta is lower, near 0.9, and I used 9.0% anyway so that this bank is valued on the same terms as the other banks I cover. Base return on equity starts at 17.6%, a little below the last quarter, and eases to 17.2% by year five, which is still above the 17% objective. It stays there for ten years. Terminal return is 13.0% and terminal growth 3.0%.

The market is using a lower cost of equity than I am, and I cannot verify mine from a filing.

## Secondary valuation: forward earnings

Adjusted earnings per share over the last four quarters were C$16.11: C$12.26 for nine months of FY26 plus C$3.85 in the fourth quarter of FY25. For FY27 my Base estimate is C$17.90, about 8% above my FY26 estimate of roughly C$16.60. I apply 14.0 times, about two turns above the 12 to 12.5 times I use for the other large Canadian banks, because this bank earns the highest return on equity and has the most diversified revenue. The result is **C$250.60**. The EPS estimates and the multiple are mine and are not company guidance.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give C$211.41 and C$250.60, a divergence of 17%, which is below the 20% warning level but wide. The gap comes from the multiple: 14.0 times earnings pays for a return on equity above 17% for longer than my cost of equity of 9.0% will. Both methods rest on the same earnings path, so agreement is not independent evidence. I weight residual income 60% and forward earnings 40%, because the first charges for the cost of equity explicitly and the second reflects what the market has paid. The weighted value is C$227.09. The price is 24.4% above the first method and 5.0% above the second. Only the multiple method comes close to the price, so the choice of method matters for how cheap or expensive the stock looks, though not for the decision to buy.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Holding the cost of equity at 9.0% and the terminal return at 13.0%, the price of C$263.05 requires a return on equity of about 22%, held flat for ten years. The bank earned 18.1% in the third quarter and 17.8% over nine months, and management calls it a premium ROE quarter after quarter. The premium is the 18.1%, and it is also what 2.72 times book value pays for. My Base case keeps the premium for ten years and still lands below the price.

## How the buy price is set

The base margin of safety is 30% at Confidence B. I did not add or subtract points: the market-sensitive fees and the credit cycle are carried in the Bear scenario, and the divergence of 17% between the methods is below my 20% warning level. The maximum buy price is C$227.09 times (1 - 30%), which is C$158.96, or C$147.99 to C$175.42 by method. The price is 65% above it, and the stock would need to fall 40% to reach it.

## Dividend

The dividend is C$7.04 a year, a 2.7% yield, about 41% of earnings in the quarter. In my Bear case EPS of C$13.80 covers it 2.0 times. With buybacks of C$1.6 billion a quarter, the total yield to shareholders is higher than the dividend alone, but the buyback is paid at 2.72 times book value.

## Reasons to own

- An adjusted return on equity of 18.1%, above the 17% objective and the highest of the large Canadian banks I have valued.
- Diversified earnings: no single segment is more than a third of net income.
- A capital ratio of 13.5% after C$4.0 billion returned to shareholders in the quarter.
- A C$475 million after-tax gain from the Moneris sale expected by early FY27, not included in my Base case.

## What could go wrong?

- Credit: provisions were up C$119 million from a year ago, mostly in Capital Markets, and the bank is exposed to Canadian household debt and to U.S. trade policy.
- Earnings quality: trading revenue up 40% and record wealth earnings depend on markets staying strong.
- Capital: a ratio of 13.5% is already near the top of the 12.5% to 13.5% range, so the buyback pace has to rise to bring it down.
- Valuation: the price already needs a cost of equity near 8% or a return on equity near 22%.

## Management execution

Management raised its return on equity objective to 17% or more for fiscal 2026 and then beat it: 18.1% adjusted in the quarter and 17.8% over nine months. Operating leverage was a positive 2.4% and capital return was steady. The acquisition of HSBC Bank Canada is integrated, and the bank is selling its stake in Moneris at a gain. The weak point is the limited room to disappoint: an objective that is already beaten by a point cannot be raised much further without more risk or more leverage, and the capital ratio leaves little spare.

## What I would watch from here

**Green:** fourth-quarter results on December 3 show adjusted ROE at or above 17.5%, impaired provisions at or below 35 basis points of loans, and a capital ratio at or above 13.0% with buybacks continuing.

**Yellow:** adjusted ROE between 16.0% and 17.5%, or impaired provisions above 40 basis points, or trading revenue falling back sharply.

**Red:** adjusted ROE below 15.0%, impaired provisions above 50 basis points, or a capital ratio below 12.5%.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. A rise in credit losses that lifts impaired provisions above 50 basis points.
2. A fall in trading and wealth fees that takes the return on equity down toward 15%.
3. A cost of equity of 10%, which on my Base path lowers the value to C$184.78 at an 18.0% starting return.
