Micron's fiscal 2026 net income of $85.0 billion was more than twice its fiscal 2025 revenue of $37.4 billion, and the shares are still 45% below their 52-week high of $1,255. The market is not arguing about last year. It is arguing about how long the shortage lasts.

Micron is the most cyclical large business I have valued, and the cycle sits at a record. Management guides the first quarter of fiscal 2027 to $61.5 billion of revenue and an adjusted gross margin near 86%. At $693.60 the shares trade at about 4.3 times my fiscal 2027 earnings estimate of $161.72 a share. That low multiple is the market's way of saying it does not trust peak earnings to last.

What Micron is now

Micron makes DRAM and NAND memory. The fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 (ended September 3, a 14-week quarter) split into four units: Core Data Center $18.0 billion of revenue at a 90% gross margin, Cloud Memory $16.3 billion at 83%, Mobile and Client $13.1 billion at 90%, and Automotive and Embedded $6.8 billion at 84%. A year earlier Core Data Center revenue was $1.6 billion.

Management says supply and demand will be much tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in fiscal 2026, and that revenue should rise every quarter of fiscal 2027. The company has signed 26 long-term strategic customer agreements, up from 16 in June, with customer commitments of $32 billion, mostly cash deposits, and a remaining performance obligation (contracted revenue not yet recognized) of about $150 billion. It says these agreements should cover more than 35% of revenue through 2030, with about three quarters of that carrying a price framework with a floor and a ceiling.

Which earnings to value

Fiscal 2026 free cash flow was $62.3 billion and GAAP net income $85.0 billion, both set at the top of a memory cycle that began with revenue of $37.4 billion in fiscal 2025. I do not capitalize any of it. The model forecasts five fiscal years explicitly (fiscal 2027 to 2031), fades revenue from $270 billion to $175 billion, fades the operating margin from 82% to 42%, and then values a mid-cycle year of $170 billion of revenue at a 38% operating margin. All of these are my estimates, set against management's statement that fiscal 2027 will be a record.

Cash flow is operating profit after the 16% tax rate (fiscal 2026 was about 15%), less net capital spending after depreciation and a working capital charge of 12% of each year's revenue change. Capital spending is $55 billion in fiscal 2027 and falls to $45 billion by 2031. Stock pay is a cost inside operating profit. Customer deposits are financing, not revenue, and I count 90% of them as a claim in the Base.

Secondary valuation: peak earnings multiple

A memory maker's earnings at the top of a cycle are usually capitalized at a very low multiple, because the market expects them to fall. My fiscal 2027 earnings estimate in the Base is $161.72 a share (revenue $270 billion, 82% operating margin, 16% tax, no interest income), and I apply 4.5 times, a low multiple that reflects how the market values peak memory earnings (my judgment), plus $42 a share of net cash. That gives $770.08. This method shares the same fiscal 2027 estimate as the first, so its agreement is partial confirmation only: it tests the later fade, not the earnings level.

Growth check and history

Quarterly revenue went from $11.3 billion to $54.2 billion in a year, and the company guides to $61.5 billion for the next quarter. A growth-adjusted check is not meaningful for earnings that rose 11-fold. I did not verify a long history of valuation multiples from one consistent source, so history carries no weight. The 52-week range of $192.59 to $1,255 shows how much the market's view has moved: the price was above $1,050 after the September 30 results and has fallen about 34% since.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $693.60, holding everything else at Base, the market needs mid-cycle revenue of about $137 billion at a 38% operating margin, or a mid-cycle operating margin of about 31% on $170 billion, or a discount rate of about 11.1%. Mid-cycle revenue of $137 billion is close to the $133 billion Micron actually booked in fiscal 2026. So the market already assumes the shortage fades, but that the post-shortage business is about as large as fiscal 2026 was. If memory returns to its old pattern of long gluts, my Bear applies, and if the long-term agreements do what management says, my Bull applies. I cannot say which from the filings.

How the buy price is set

Confidence of 12 is grade D, which carries a 55% margin of safety. I add nothing on top, because the cycle risk is already in the Bear case and in the Confidence grade. Dividend and capital return

Micron pays $0.15 a quarter, or $0.60 a year, a yield of about 0.09% at $693.60. Fiscal 2026 dividends were $0.6 billion and buybacks $1.8 billion including employee withholding, small against $62.3 billion of free cash flow. Management has put the cash into capacity: debt repayments were $10.0 billion in the year, and net capital spending is set to more than double.

Reasons to own Micron

  • Contracted demand. 26 strategic customer agreements, $32 billion of customer commitments and a remaining performance obligation of about $150 billion, with more than 75% of fiscal 2027 output reported as secured.
  • Extraordinary unit economics. A gross margin of 86.8% and an operating margin of 80.7% in the fourth quarter, with the Core Data Center unit at 90% and 85%.
  • Balance sheet. $73.5 billion of cash and investments against $5.2 billion of debt.
  • Low multiple on current earnings. About {pe_now:.1f} times my fiscal 2027 estimate and about {px/(38.15*4):.1f} times the first-quarter adjusted EPS guide annualized.
  • Tight supply. Management expects new cleanroom capacity to arrive late in calendar 2028 and beyond, which limits supply growth in the meantime.

These support the Base. They are why I do not call it a reduce. They do not create a margin of safety.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: the cycle. Memory earnings have turned sharply lower in past downturns. A mid-cycle operating margin of 30% instead of 38% takes the Base to $691, below the price.
  • Capacity response. Micron, SK Hynix and Samsung are all adding capacity. A supply wave in fiscal 2029 would hit prices and margins together.
  • AI spending. Demand comes from a small set of AI buyers. A pause in their spending would hit orders, and the agreements protect only the minimums.
  • Capital intensity. Net capital spending goes from $27.4 billion to more than $50 billion in fiscal 2027, and much of it buys capacity that arrives after the shortage may have ended.
  • Working capital. Receivables of $36.2 billion and inventory of 129 days, up nine days, tie up cash and reverse sharply in a downturn.
  • Earnings quality. The fourth quarter had 14 weeks, deposits are generally refundable under conditions tied to minimum purchases under the agreements, and a large part of reported earnings sits at an unusual peak.

Management execution

Management beat its own guidance by about $4 billion in the quarter, signed 10 more strategic agreements in three months, built a net cash position and repaid $10.0 billion of debt in a year. The test ahead is capital discipline: the company is more than doubling capital spending into a market where it itself says supply is tight until at least fiscal 2028. Assessment: 15/20, strong execution and a cycle-sensitive capital plan. The next tests are the first quarter report in December, gross margin against the 86.25% guide, and the full-year capital spending figure.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • First quarter revenue at $60 billion or more with adjusted gross margin at or above 86%
  • More strategic agreements, with commitments above $32 billion and deposits growing
  • Full-year capital spending kept near $50 billion with output starting in mid-2027
  • Inventory days falling from 129
  • Contract pricing holding at or above the floors when spot prices soften

Yellow

  • Sequential revenue growth slowing below 5%
  • Gross margin falling below 85% before the end of fiscal 2027
  • Full-year capital spending guided above $55 billion
  • Receivables growing faster than revenue

Red

  • A quarter of falling revenue while capital spending is still rising
  • Customers invoking minimum-volume relief or seeking deposit refunds
  • Spot DRAM or HBM prices falling for two quarters in a row
  • A competitor announcing a large capacity increase for 2027 or 2028
  • Inventory days rising above 150

Today's readings: management guidance for a record fiscal 2027 and the guided first-quarter gross margin are green, the size of the capital plan is yellow, and the 34% fall in the shares since September 30 is a market signal I treat as information, not as a red flag by itself. The next point is the first quarter report, which stockanalysis.com estimates for December 16.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Mid-cycle operating margin proves to be below about 30%, which puts the Base below the current price.
  2. Industry supply grows faster than AI demand in fiscal 2028 and 2029, ending the shortage early.
  3. A large customer uses its minimum-volume rights to cut purchases and takes back deposits.

The mid-cycle margin and the mid-cycle revenue move the value most: from about the price to 13% above it with each step I tested, and a slower fade adds more. That is why I give a margin of safety and no precise entry price. A $10 billion error in net cash is worth $8.70 a share.

The strongest case against my Hold: the long-term agreements really change the industry, and a mid-cycle revenue of $170 billion is too low for a market where AI demand exceeds supply for several years. At the Bull assumptions the value is $1,458.46, more than double the price. My answer is that I cannot verify the agreements' terms and minimums, and that memory has repeatedly looked structural at the top. The strongest case against my Base: memory earnings have fallen sharply, and even turned to losses, in past downturns, and a mid-cycle margin of 38% is a very high bar. At 30% the value is $690.71.

Main data limits: no 10-K or note on the strategic agreements, no verified history of multiples, no exact fiscal 2027 capital spending, no view of competitors' capacity plans, and call content only from press summaries. The revenue and margin fade, mid-cycle level, tax rate, discount rate, working capital charge, the cash held for operations and the share of deposits counted as a claim are my estimates.

Final verdict: hold at $693.60

Micron has the best unit economics I have seen in a large chip company, a net cash balance of about $68 billion and contracts that run to 2030. At $693.60 the price is 6% below my Base and 109% above the maximum buy price, which is the right place to hold, not to add.

Verdict: HOLD at $693.60, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $735.81, upside 6.1%, Confidence D (12/25), required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $331.11.

The market needs mid-cycle revenue of about $137 billion, close to what Micron booked in fiscal 2026. The question for the next two years is whether 2026 was the top of a cycle or the floor of a new one.
