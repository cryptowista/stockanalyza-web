## What Credo is

Credo designs the chips and cables that move data between servers, switches and memory inside AI data centers. Its main product is the active electrical cable (AEC): a copper cable with a Credo chip in each connector that cleans and boosts the signal. It also sells retimers, DSPs (digital signal processors) and licenses its SerDes technology (the circuitry that turns data into a high speed signal). Credo does not make its own chips; it hires foundries and assemblers. In the latest quarter AECs drove more than 90% of the revenue increase, and management says optical revenue should pass $600 million in fiscal 2027.

Optical is the reason for the largest decision of the year. On May 28, 2026 Credo closed the purchase of DustPhotonics, a silicon photonics chip designer. The 10-Q puts the total consideration at $1.25 billion: $769.6 million in cash, $169.1 million in shares (about 0.76 million), and $310.0 million of contingent consideration payable in up to about 3.21 million more shares if financial milestones are met. Goodwill and intangibles now total $1.37 billion, 45% of total assets and 50% of shareholders' equity.

Concentration is the other side. By end customer, the three largest were 28%, 33% and 13% of last quarter's revenue and a fourth was 10%, as reported in the 10-Q. By contracting party, Customer A was 43% of revenue and 57% of receivables. Contracted but unsatisfied performance obligations were $4.2 million at quarter end, against $479.0 million of quarterly revenue, so orders carry almost no commitment.

## Why free cash flow needs the stock compensation line next to it

Fiscal 2026 free cash flow was **$407.0 million**: operating cash flow of $464.3 million minus capital spending of $57.3 million. After $182.6 million of stock-based compensation (SBC), a real cost paid in shares, the hard figure is **$224.4 million, or 55.1% of the headline**. In the latest quarter the hard figure is negative, at -$5.1 million.

My valuation uses the headline figure, as consensus does, and charges SBC through the multiple and the discount rate. The adversarial review shows what happens if the full cost is deducted instead.

Fiscal 2027 consensus free cash flow is about **$827 million** (third-party datasets, which I could not reproduce). The first quarter delivered $82.9 million, so the remaining three quarters have to average about $248 million each. The best quarter on record is $177.5 million, in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. That is possible, since net income will be rising and the first quarter was held back by a $61.5 million inventory build and $25.3 million of refundable capacity deposits, but Credo has also agreed to pay a further $102.4 million of such deposits within the year.

## Balance sheet and shares

Credo has no financial debt. Cash and short-term investments were **$764.3 million** on August 1, down from $1,443.3 million on May 2 after the DustPhotonics payment. I use $764.3 million as net cash and leave out $26.2 million of operating lease liabilities, because rent is already paid out of the cash flow. The first draft deducted them and used $738 million, a difference of $0.13 a share.

Shares are where the problem sits. Shares outstanding went from 171.2 million at the end of fiscal 2025 to 185.4 million a year later (+8.3%) and 187.8 million on August 1. In fiscal 2026 the company raised $736.3 million through an at-the-market stock offering, almost twice the free cash flow of the year. First quarter weighted diluted shares were 194.4 million against 184.6 million a year earlier (+5.3%), and management guides to about 200 million for the second quarter. Unvested employee awards are 8.3 million restricted units, 3.1 million performance units and 1.7 million options, and the new fiscal year's grants were made at $226.57 and $155.39 a share. I use 198 million forward diluted shares for all per share values, and test 201.2 million, which adds the maximum contingent DustPhotonics shares.

## Primary valuation: a justified multiple of free cash flow per share

Fiscal 2027 consensus free cash flow of $826.9 million divided by 198 million shares is **$4.18 a share**. A multiple of 33x to 36x, which is meant to reflect growth and quality net of concentration, dilution and cycle risk, gives $137.82 to $150.35. The midpoint of 34.5x gives **$144.08**. The first draft showed the central value as $145; I use the unrounded figure. The multiple is my assumption, not the output of a build, so the grid below tests it.

At $218.64 and 198 million diluted shares, market value is about $43.3 billion and enterprise value about **$42.5 billion**. That is 51.4x fiscal 2027 consensus free cash flow, 17.0x consensus revenue of $2.5 billion and 31.9x fiscal 2026 revenue of $1.34 billion. At my 34.5x multiple the price needs fiscal 2027 free cash flow of about **$1.25 billion**, 52% above consensus. In the model, the price equals the Base path scaled up by about 1.43 times (fiscal 2027 free cash flow near $1.18 billion), or the Base path at a discount rate of about 8.1%.

A buyer today needs several things to be true: growth stays above 25% for years, the optical business is integrated and scales, the largest customers keep buying without long term commitments, margins hold near 68% non-GAAP, stock compensation stops growing faster than free cash flow, and the share count stabilizes. The price is not asking for a good outcome. It is asking for something close to the Bull.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence C score carries a base MOS of 35%. I add 5 points for customer concentration and model risk, and nothing for the balance sheet, because cash is under 2% of the company's value. The required MOS is **40%**. Applied to the $149.66 Base, the **maximum buy price is $90** ($89.80), and a stronger 50% margin marks a strong-buy reference near $75. Charging SBC explicitly, as the stress test in the review does, would justify 45% and a maximum buy price near $82. The first draft also listed a practical buy zone of $90 to $105. A price above the maximum buy price does not carry the required margin of safety, so I drop it. The buy zone is $75 to $90, and the shares would need to fall about 59% from $218.64 to reach $90.

## Dividend and capital return

Credo pays no dividend and does not buy back shares, so the income check does not apply. Its capital activity has gone the other way: it sold $736.3 million of stock in fiscal 2026.

## Reasons to own CRDO

- **AI clusters need more bandwidth per rack.** Credo's cables and chips address the short links where copper and optical choices are being made now.
- **Proprietary SerDes and DSP technology.** Customers qualify these designs for years, which creates switching friction.
- **Exceptional operating leverage.** Revenue is up 115%, non-GAAP gross margin is 68% and non-GAAP operating margin is 48.2%.
- **Optical option.** DustPhotonics adds silicon photonics and design wins with hyperscale customers.
- **No financial debt.** $764.3 million of cash funds research and integration.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: a major customer or design win lost while the valuation stays at a premium.** Four end customers are 84% of revenue, and one contracting party is 57% of receivables.
- **Valuation.** At 51 times fiscal 2027 free cash flow, a smaller multiple can outweigh years of good growth. The 52 week range is $86.49 to $308.67, and the shares are 29% below the high.
- **SBC and dilution.** Stock compensation was larger than free cash flow in the latest quarter, the share count is up 8.6% in a year, and up to 3.21 million contingent shares are still to come.
- **Working capital and supply.** Inventory rose 24.8% in one quarter against revenue growth of 9.6%. Credo has $222.2 million of purchase commitments and depends on third-party foundries and assemblers.
- **Acquisition execution.** $1.37 billion of goodwill and intangibles now sit on the balance sheet, with $58.2 million a year of amortization to come from fiscal 2028.
- **Hyperscaler spending cycle.** Orders carry no long term minimums and only $4.2 million of performance obligations were contracted.

## Management execution

Management has beaten its revenue guidance, scaled revenue more than threefold in fiscal 2026 and closed a large acquisition in the same year. The offsets are a rising share count that has not matched the growth in the business, a 10-K that describes special performance awards with six revenue and stock price hurdles (the first at $244.70), and an acquisition that has to be integrated while the largest customers decide where to buy optics. **Assessment: 15/20, good. The next test is whether free cash flow per share grows faster than SBC.**

## Final verdict: reduce at $218.64

Credo is a high-quality connectivity business growing at a rate few companies of its size have matched, and it ended the quarter with $764 million of cash and no debt. The shares, though, now trade 46% above my Base value and within 9% of my Bull, while the quarter's stock compensation was larger than its free cash flow. The growth is real. So is the bill.

**Verdict: REDUCE at $218.64. Triangulated Base value $149.66, downside 31.5%, Confidence C (16/25). Required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $90.**

The case changes if free cash flow per share starts growing faster than stock compensation and the share count, or if the price falls toward the buy zone with the business still executing.
