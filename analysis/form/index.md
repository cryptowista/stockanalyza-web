## What FormFactor is

FormFactor makes probe cards and test systems. A probe card is a precision board with thousands of microscopic needles that touches a chip while it is still on the silicon wafer and checks that it works before it is cut and packaged. Advanced chips for artificial intelligence, high bandwidth memory (HBM, the stacked memory next to AI processors) and advanced packaging need more test steps and more complex cards, which is why revenue is growing. Probe Cards were $209.7 million of the $258.2 million second-quarter revenue and Systems were $48.5 million.

At its May 2026 investor day management set a target model for 2030: $1.6 billion of revenue, a 55% non-GAAP gross margin, operating expenses of 23% of revenue and $5.00 of non-GAAP earnings per share. It estimates the advanced probe card market will grow from about $2.8 billion to $4 billion by 2030. Co-packaged optics (CPO), which puts light-based connections next to the chip, is a new product line that management now expects to exceed $20 million of revenue in 2026.

Concentration is material. According to the company's filings as summarized in the framework analysis, one customer was 22.9% of 2025 revenue, and three customers made up 17.1%, 16.9% and 12.9% of receivables at the end of the second quarter.

## Why earnings and free cash flow cannot simply be annualized

FormFactor is a cyclical manufacturer, so I value it on mid-cycle EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) and on free cash flow after stock-based compensation (SBC), not on the best quarter multiplied by four. First half free cash flow was $82.0 million by my simple measure (operating cash flow minus capital spending). After deducting $16.7 million of SBC, a real cost paid in shares, the hard figure is **$65.3 million, or 79.6% of the headline**.

That first-half figure cannot be doubled. Management expects 2026 cash capital spending of $140 million to $170 million for the Farmers Branch, Texas factory, of which only $24.8 million was spent in the first half. The second half therefore carries roughly $115 million to $145 million of capital spending, before a $24.2 million state grant that partly offsets it.

My normalized scenarios are revenue of $1.00 billion, $1.10 billion and $1.20 billion with EBITDA margins of 23%, 27% and 30%. That gives EBITDA of **$230 million** in the Bear case, **$297 million** in the Base case and **$360 million** in the Bull case, and hard free cash flow of $145 million, $200 million and $260 million. For perspective, my estimate of second-quarter EBITDA after SBC and before restructuring is about $71.5 million, or $286 million annualized, so the Base case asks for little beyond today's run rate. Trailing twelve month EBITDA was $187 million (S&P Global). The problem is not the Base earnings. It is the multiple they must carry.

## Balance sheet and shares

The share count is moving the wrong way. Diluted shares were 79.6 million in the second quarter against 77.5 million a year earlier, an increase of 2.7%. About $70.9 million remains under the buyback authorization. I use 79.6 million diluted shares for all per share values.

## Primary valuation: growth-adjusted EV/EBITDA

Enterprise value (EV) is market value plus debt minus cash. The primary method builds a justified EV/EBITDA multiple: a standard industrial anchor of 16x, plus 6x for structural growth, 2x for the balance sheet and 2x for the moat, minus 2x for cyclicality and customer concentration and 1x for dilution and execution risk. The result is a Base multiple of about **23x**.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence B score carries a base margin of safety (MOS) of 30%. I add 5 points for semiconductor cyclicality and 5 points for customer concentration, and subtract 5 points for the net cash balance sheet, which gives a required MOS of **35%**. Applied to the $97.20 Base, the **maximum buy price is $63.18**, and a stronger 45% margin marks a strong-buy reference near $53.46. The net cash credit is generous, since cash is under 3% of the company's value; without it the required margin would be 40% and the maximum buy price $58.32. The shares would need to fall about 57% from $146.88 to reach $63.18.

## Reasons to own FORM

- **Genuine test intensity growth.** HBM, advanced packaging and custom AI chips raise the number and complexity of test steps, and FormFactor has a place in each.
- **Operating leverage is real.** Revenue is up 32% year over year, non-GAAP gross margin is up 14.8 points and non-GAAP earnings per share tripled to $0.82.
- **A real moat.** Qualification barriers, precision manufacturing, patents and customer co-development make probe cards difficult to switch.
- **A net cash balance sheet.** About $334 million of net liquidity funds the Texas expansion without borrowing.
- **A consistent record of beating guidance.** Q2 revenue came in $18.2 million above the midpoint of the outlook.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: valuation.** Strong execution can still produce poor stock returns if a premium multiple normalizes. The stock is up 28% since its September 11 close of $114.95.
- **Customer concentration.** A design loss, a second-source decision or an in-house test program at a large customer could take a large share of revenue.
- **Semiconductor cyclicality.** Strength in HBM and high-performance computing does not remove inventory, utilization and capital spending cycles. FormFactor's revenue was nearly flat in 2025 ($785.0 million against $763.6 million) before this surge.
- **Capacity ramp.** Farmers Branch starts production late in the fourth quarter, ramps through 2027 and is expected to reach initial target capacity by early 2028. Cash capital spending of $140 million to $170 million in 2026 and pre-production costs of $25 million to $30 million come first.
- **Gross margin may have peaked for now.** Management calls about 51% the sustainable baseline at current volumes, and the 2030 target of 55% depends on the new factory.
- **Dilution.** Diluted shares rose 2.7% in a year and no buybacks were made in the first half.

## Management execution

Management has beaten its outlook on revenue, gross margin and earnings per share in each recent quarter, posted three consecutive record quarters, set measurable 2030 targets and restructured manufacturing to lower cost. The offsets are a capacity ramp that must be delivered on time, long-dated targets and a rising share count. **Assessment: 16/20, good. The next test is whether gross margin holds above the 51% baseline once volumes normalize.**

FormFactor is a high-quality manufacturer whose growth inflection is real. Its shares, though, have risen 286% in a year and now trade above my Bear and Base values and within 5% of my Bull value. At $146.88 a buyer is paying today for the company management hopes to be in 2030, with the $5.00 earnings target already priced at 29 times.
