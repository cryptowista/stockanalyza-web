# Cucinelli trades at 15 times 2026 EBITDA, but only 18% of that EBITDA becomes free cash flow

BC.IT, Borsa Italiana. Published November 15, 2026. Price as of the November 13, 2026 regular session close. Values in euros. Latest full results: first half 2026. Latest revenue update: nine months to September 30, 2026.

## Verdict: REDUCE at €87.80

Cucinelli is an excellent business, but after rent, shop investment and tax only 18% of its 2026 EBITDA becomes free cash flow, a yield of 1.4%. At €87.80 the shares trade 54% above my Base value of €56.93 and 22% above my Bull. The two methods differ by 71%: the cash method says Reduce, the multiple method alone says Hold. Neither says buy. New money waits for €32.74 or less.

In 2025 Brunello Cucinelli earned €142 million, paid €69 million of dividends and still ended the year with €95 million more net debt than it began with. Revenue grew 10% and the operating margin was 16.2%, so the business is not the problem. The problem is cash: after rent on its boutiques, €142 million of capital spending and tax, free cash flow in 2025 was minus €12 million. In 2026 it recovers to about €85 million on my estimate, 18% of €464 million of EBITDA. The shares closed at €87.80 on November 13.

My Base value is €56.93 a share, the Bear value €37.86 and the Bull value €71.78, so the price sits above the whole range. The Base blends two methods that disagree sharply: a ten year cash flow model after rent gives 39.63 euros, and a multiple of 2027 EBITDA gives 82.89 euros. The first value is 55% below the price, the second 6% below it. I count rent as a real cost, so the cash model carries the larger weight. Whichever method you prefer, neither produces a buy.

Valuation route: listed luxury brand with high margins, fast growth and heavy boutique investment, valued with a ten year model of free cash flow to shareholders after lease payments for 2027 to 2036, discounted at 9%, and cross-checked with a multiple of 2027 EBITDA before rent. The cash model charges lease payments directly. The multiple method deducts lease liabilities, net debt and minorities, so rent is counted once in each.

## What Cucinelli is

Brunello Cucinelli is an Italian luxury house from Solomeo in Umbria, known for cashmere and understated clothing without logos. The founder is still Executive Chairman and Creative Director. It sells through its own boutiques and online (retail) and through multi-brand stores (wholesale). Retail was €741.0 million of the €1,123.6 million of revenue for the first nine months of 2026, 66%, and grew 19% at constant exchange rates. Wholesale was €382.6 million and grew 4%. The company reported 141 directly operated boutiques at the end of June, and the first margin (sales less the cost of goods) was 75.0% in the first half.

Third quarter revenue was €374.2 million, up 13.5% at constant rates and slightly above what management expected. In October the company raised its 2026 growth outlook to about 12% at constant rates, from 10% to 11%, and said currency would cut reported growth by about 2.5 points. For 2027 it expects growth of about 10%, with retail above that and wholesale at mid single digits.

## Free cash flow and what it leaves out

Under IFRS 16, the lease accounting standard used in Europe, rent on boutiques is left out of EBITDA and shows up as depreciation and interest. For a company that keeps adding boutiques that matters. In 2025 EBITDA before rent was €408.4 million (EBIT of €227.8 million plus €180.6 million of depreciation). Cucinelli paid €113.1 million of lease principal and €27.1 million of lease interest, 34% of that EBITDA, and spent €141.7 million on capital investment, 10.1% of revenue. Operating cash flow was €242.6 million. Free cash flow after lease principal and capital spending was minus €12.2 million.

My 2026 estimate is positive because capital spending falls to about 6% of revenue, in line with what management has said, and because the business keeps growing at a 17% margin. My estimate also lands where management points on debt: net debt before leases of €177 million at the end of 2026, 11.5% of revenue, against the company's 11% to 12%. Even so, conversion reaches only 25% of EBITDA by 2030 in my Base, as margins widen and capital spending falls to 5.5%. At €87.80 the free cash flow yield on my 2026 estimate is 1.4%, and the trailing price to earnings ratio is about 42.

The growth-adjusted check is a diagnostic only and does not feed the value. On my 2026E earnings of about €2.30 a share the price to earnings ratio is 38. My Base grows EBIT about 10% a year to 2030, so a growth-adjusted ratio near 4 says the market is paying for growth that lasts well past that horizon, not just for the next five years.

## Primary valuation: free cash flow to shareholders after lease payments

I project revenue, EBIT margin, capital spending, tax and working capital for ten years, 2027 to 2036, and discount free cash flow after rent at 9%, with terminal growth of 2.5%. In the Base, revenue grows 9.5% in 2027, 9.0% in 2028, 8.0% in 2029 and 7.0% in 2030, then slows to 3.0% in 2036, reaching €2,121 million in 2030. The EBIT margin rises from 17.2% in 2026 to 18.0% in 2030. Capital spending is 6.0% of revenue, falling to 5.2%. Lease principal is 8.1% of revenue. Tax is 28.8% of operating profit after lease interest and other financing costs, working capital absorbs 22% of each added euro of revenue, and 3% of free cash flow goes to minorities. An allowance for other cash outflows of 2.0% of revenue fades to 0.5%; it stands for items I cannot see in the statements, such as key money for new boutiques and cash taxes above the charge, and it is the least certain line in the model.

The 9% discount rate is a judgment, not a beta calculation. Cucinelli has modest debt before leases and a strong brand with a loyal local clientele, but it sells discretionary goods at the top of the market, a large share of growth comes from the US and China, and lease commitments are big, so I did not use a low rate.

## Secondary valuation: multiple of EBITDA before rent

The second method applies a multiple to 2027E EBITDA before rent, subtracts lease liabilities, net debt and minorities. The multiples are 10.5 times in the Bear, 13.0 times in the Base and 16.0 times in the Bull. At €87.80 the market pays 13.7 times 2027E and 15.2 times 2026E. I set the Base below the market because the multiple has to be earned by growth that lasts, and my Base has revenue growth of 9% to 10% a year to 2028 and 6% a year over the decade. It assumes a steady EBIT margin near 18%, capital spending near 6% of revenue and conversion of EBITDA into cash that rises from 18% to 25%. The Bear, with growth of 3% to 6% and a margin sliding to 16%, justifies a multiple below the current one, and the Bull, with 11% growth and a 19% margin, a premium. I did not verify peer multiples and did not use them.

## Dividend and buyback

Cucinelli paid €1.04 a share for 2025, a 50% payout and a 1.2% yield at €87.80. It was not covered by 2025 free cash flow, which was negative, and is covered 1.2 times by my 2026 estimate. The company also repurchased €8.0 million of shares in 2025; I did not verify a continuing program.

## Reasons to own Cucinelli

- Revenue grew 13.3% at constant rates in nine months with 19% in retail and double digit growth in every region, and management raised its 2026 outlook twice.
- The EBIT margin has risen to 17.1% in the first half, and the first margin is 75%, which signals pricing power.
- Management expects net debt before leases to fall to 11% to 12% of revenue by the end of 2026, from 14%, as capital spending drops to about 6% of revenue.
- The multiple method alone puts its Base only 6% below the price, and a market that keeps paying 15 times EBITDA would deliver 11% a year to 2030.
- A founder-led house with a stated plan, including a target of about €1.8 billion of revenue by 2028, according to a press article I could not verify.

## What could go wrong?

- **Price against cash:** at 15.2 times EBITDA and a 1.4% free cash flow yield, a slowdown in growth from 12% to 8% could reduce the multiple more than the profit.
- **Cash conversion:** 2025 free cash flow was negative. If capital spending stays near 7% to 8% of revenue, as in the first half, free cash flow stays low; half a point of capital spending is worth €2.38 a share.
- **Leases:** €822 million of lease liabilities and €140 million of annual lease cash, which grow with every new boutique.
- **Regional concentration:** the Americas and Asia are growing fastest and make up 65% of revenue; a US consumer slowdown, tariffs or weaker Chinese spending would hit growth.
- **Wholesale and customers:** wholesale grew only 4%, and an €8.1 million provision was booked in 2025 for the US retailer Saks.
- **Currency:** the company expects exchange rates to cut reported 2026 growth by about 2.5 points.
- **My own numbers:** the second half of 2026, the margin path, the other outflows and the share count are my estimates.

## Management execution

Management has raised its revenue outlook twice in 2026 and delivered a half year margin above last year while paying for more boutiques. It guided to roughly 10% growth for 2026 and 2027 at the start and now expects about 12% for 2026. Against that, 2025 operating cash flow of €242.6 million was fully absorbed by capital spending and leases, net debt before leases roughly doubled, and the 2026 improvement relies on a capital spending cut to about 6% of revenue. I score management 8 out of 10: growth and margin delivery is strong and it is candid about investment, but cash discipline has not been proven through a full year yet.

## The three most important thesis breakers

These are the events that would make the Reduce call wrong, in either direction.

1. Capital spending stays near 5% of revenue and the other outflows I assume prove too high, so free cash flow reaches 25% of EBITDA by 2027; this lifts the DCF toward €47 and narrows the gap.
2. Growth stays near 10% a year beyond 2030 with a margin near 19%, which supports the Bull value of €71.78 and a lasting multiple above 15 times. At that point the price is fair, not cheap.
3. Retail growth slips below 10% and the multiple falls toward 11 times EBITDA, the DCF is right, and the price moves toward €56.93. The other way out is a price of €32.74 or less, where the margin of safety is met with the Base unchanged.

| Minority interests of €17.3 million | Book value; the true claim may differ |

The weakest points are the second half of 2026, the 2% allowance for other outflows and the share count. The generous choices are a margin that rises every year to 18%, terminal growth of 2.5% and the 2025 calibration gap. The conservative choices are rent counted in full, a 9% discount rate, and a 22% working capital charge on each added euro of revenue. The value is struck at the end of 2026 and not rolled back by six weeks, which is worth about 1%. I did not use analyst price targets or consensus estimates in the model.

My Base DCF is 55% below the price, so I tested whether I am too harsh on management's own outlook.

Even management's growth outlook followed by a 19% margin gives €43.62 on the DCF, and a twenty year runway at an 8% discount rate gives €58.06. Only the multiple method reaches the price range. The market is either paying the multiple method's view, in which EBITDA converts into cash far better than I model, or a margin and runway that the group has not shown. I found evidence for neither: the multiple view rests on the market's own pricing, not on a peer set I could verify, and a 33% margin has no precedent in these filings.

## Final verdict: reduce at €87.80

Cucinelli is one of the better businesses I have looked at: growing double digits, widening margin, 75% first margin and a clientele that keeps spending. What the shares ask you to believe is the problem. At €87.80 the enterprise value including leases is €7.03 billion, 15.2 times 2026E EBITDA before rent, for a company that turns 18% of that EBITDA into free cash flow. The Base earns 5.7% a year at this price, below the 9% I discount at, and a 9% return needs a purchase at 39.63 euros.

The call is Reduce, not Sell, because the multiple method puts its Base only 6% below the price and the business is still improving. It is not a buy at any price I can defend above €47.66. The shares do not need bad news to disappoint, only a year in which growth slows while free cash flow stays near €85 million.

Admiring the company and paying €87.80 for it are two separate decisions, and only the second has a price.

**Verdict: REDUCE at €87.80. Base €56.93, -35.2% against the price, Bear €37.86, Bull €71.78, Confidence C (16/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price €32.74, range €22.78 to €47.66 under the two methods.**
