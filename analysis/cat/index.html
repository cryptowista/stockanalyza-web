Caterpillar reported record sales of $20.7 billion in the third quarter, a record backlog of $78.1 billion and adjusted profit per share up 45%, and the shares fell 9.3% that day. They are now about 47% below their June 30 high of $1,073.46.

The market is not arguing with the quarter. It is arguing about what part of it lasts. Machine dealers added inventory, tariff recoveries of $128 million lifted the margin, power generation sales for data centers are growing 30% and the company itself guides fourth quarter sales to only moderate growth.

My triangulated Base value is $430.37, which is 23.8% below the $564.50 reference price. The Bear value is $232.19 and the Bull value is $713.07. Confidence is grade C (15/25), which requires a 42.5% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at $247.46. The verdict is a hold, and I would not add: Caterpillar is a first-rate business, but the price already pays for a record cycle to continue.

Valuation route: heavy equipment and engine maker with a captive finance arm. Primary method: a five-year free cash flow model of the machinery business, with a terminal value on a mid-cycle level, plus the finance arm at an earnings multiple, discounted at 9.5%. Secondary method: a price-to-earnings multiple on 2027 earnings. Classification: quality growth company, 8 of 12 on the generic scorecard. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets. The reference price is an intraday quote, see the stress test section.

What Caterpillar is now

Caterpillar sells construction equipment, mining and rail equipment, and engines and turbines, through a dealer network, and finances customers through Financial Products. In the third quarter Power and Energy sold $8.8 billion (up 18%), Construction Industries $8.0 billion (up 18%) and Resource Industries $4.6 billion (up 15%). Power generation sales rose 30% to $3.4 billion on turbines and large engines for data centers, and retail sales of those products to users rose 75%.

Backlog was a record $78.1 billion, up $6.0 billion in the quarter and $38.2 billion in a year, and customer advances were $5.3 billion against $3.3 billion at the end of 2025. The release points to factory capacity as the constraint for power generation, with major new engine plants arriving in 2027.

Which cash to value

Caterpillar has two businesses with different value. The machinery business (called Machinery, Power and Energy in the release) generated $11.5 billion of operating cash flow in nine months, and I value it on free cash flow, which was $9.5 billion in 2025. Financial Products lends to customers and carries $34.6 billion of its own debt, so I value it on earnings and keep its debt out of the machinery claims.

The Base assumes machinery free cash flow of about $11 billion for 2026 (the guide says top half of $6 billion to $15 billion; this is my estimate), $12.0 billion in 2027, $12.5 billion in 2028, then a slow fade to $11.5 billion in 2030 and $12.0 billion in 2031, as dealers stop restocking and tariff recoveries end. Mid-cycle free cash flow is $11 billion. The finance arm is worth $8.9 billion, about 11 times its annualized segment profit of $1.05 billion after a 23% tax rate. All of these are my estimates.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $564.50, holding everything else at Base, the market needs free cash flow and earnings about 32% above my Base in every year (2027 earnings near $40 a share and mid-cycle machinery free cash flow near $14 billion), or a discount rate of about 7.1%. The market is paying for backlog conversion and for data center demand to keep growing, and I cannot verify either beyond what management says.

Dividend and capital return

Caterpillar pays $6.52 a share a year, a yield of about 1.2% at $564.50. In nine months it paid $2.1 billion of dividends and bought back $7.7 billion of shares, mostly in the first quarter when the price was higher. The dividend is covered about 4 times by machinery free cash flow in my Base.

Reasons to own Caterpillar

  • Record backlog. $78.1 billion, up 96% in a year, with customer advances of $5.3 billion.
  • Data center demand. Power generation sales grew 30% and retail sales to users 75%, with new capacity arriving in 2027.
  • Margins. An adjusted operating margin of 20.6%, with Power and Energy at 24.4% and Construction at 22.9%.
  • Cash returns. $2.0 billion returned in the quarter and a share count down 2.1% in a year.
  • Pricing. Price realization added $663 million to third quarter sales.

These support the Base. They do not support the price.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: the cycle. Construction and mining are cyclical, and the company says Resource Industries growth came mainly from dealer inventory.
  • Data center concentration. A pause in AI spending would hit power generation, the fastest-growing unit.
  • Tariffs. About $2.2 billion of cost in 2026, and the recoveries (about $520 million) are not assumed to continue.
  • Margin. The company guides the adjusted margin near the bottom of its target range, and costs for manufacturing, freight and research are rising.
  • Inventory. $22.2 billion of inventory, up 17% in a year.
  • Finance arm. $34.6 billion of Financial Products debt backs customer loans and leases.

Management execution

Management has delivered record sales and backlog, raised prices, kept returning cash and is adding engine capacity. It also guided the fourth quarter to moderate growth, which coincided with a 9.3% fall in the shares, and the margin is helped by tariff recoveries that fade. Assessment: 15/20, strong operations with a margin that is still below target and a cycle risk the guidance does not address. The next tests are the fourth quarter report in late January, the margin against the target range, and the start of the new engine plants in 2027.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Backlog above $78 billion with customer advances growing
  • Adjusted operating margin above 20% without tariff recoveries
  • Resource Industries retail sales to users growing as fast as sales to dealers
  • New engine capacity starting on schedule in 2027
  • Machinery free cash flow above $11 billion for 2026

Yellow

  • Fourth quarter sales growth below 10%
  • Dealer inventory reductions larger than expected
  • Tariff costs above $2.2 billion
  • Inventory continuing to grow faster than sales

Red

  • Backlog falling for two quarters
  • Power generation orders slowing
  • Adjusted margin below the target range
  • Cancellations or deferrals of large data center engine orders
  • Financial Products credit losses rising

Today's readings: the backlog and power generation growth are green, the guided fourth quarter growth and the dealer inventory are yellow, and the margin near the bottom of the target range is also yellow. The next point is the fourth quarter report in late January.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Data center spending slows and power generation orders fall, removing the fastest-growing profit pool.
  2. Dealers cut inventory while end-user demand softens, turning record sales into a decline.
  3. Tariff costs rise further without recoveries, keeping the margin below the target range.

The strongest case against my Hold: the backlog of $78 billion is about a year of sales, power generation is supply-constrained, and a company with such visibility deserves a higher multiple and a higher mid-cycle level than I use. At mid-cycle free cash flow of $13 billion and 20 times earnings the value is $494.71. My answer is that backlog is not margin, the company itself guides fourth quarter sales to moderate growth and the margin to the bottom of its target range, and the stock fell 9.3% when management said so. The strongest case against my Base: it fades free cash flow after 2028, while a record backlog and new engine plants point to higher volumes in 2027.

Main data limits: no 10-Q, no pension and retiree obligations, no Financial Products equity, no verified history of multiples, no view of the call or of the segment backlog split. The free cash flow path, mid-cycle level, finance arm value, 2027 EPS, multiple, discount rate and the use of all enterprise cash against machinery debt are my estimates.

Final verdict: hold at $564.50, do not add

Caterpillar has the best backlog and margin in its history, and a power business that is growing faster than the company can build. At $564.50 the price is 31% above my Base and 128% above the maximum buy price, so I hold and do not add.

Verdict: HOLD at $564.50, do not add. Triangulated Base value $430.37, downside 23.8%, Confidence C (15/25), required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $247.46.
