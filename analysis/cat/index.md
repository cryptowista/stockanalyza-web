## What Caterpillar is now

Caterpillar sells construction equipment, mining and rail equipment, and engines and turbines, through a dealer network, and finances customers through Financial Products. In the third quarter Power and Energy sold $8.8 billion (up 18%), Construction Industries $8.0 billion (up 18%) and Resource Industries $4.6 billion (up 15%). Power generation sales rose 30% to $3.4 billion on turbines and large engines for data centers, and retail sales of those products to users rose 75%.

Backlog was a record $78.1 billion, up $6.0 billion in the quarter and $38.2 billion in a year, and customer advances were $5.3 billion against $3.3 billion at the end of 2025. The release points to factory capacity as the constraint for power generation, with major new engine plants arriving in 2027.

## Which cash to value

Caterpillar has two businesses with different value. The machinery business (called Machinery, Power and Energy in the release) generated $11.5 billion of operating cash flow in nine months, and I value it on free cash flow, which was $9.5 billion in 2025. Financial Products lends to customers and carries $34.6 billion of its own debt, so I value it on earnings and keep its debt out of the machinery claims.

The Base assumes machinery free cash flow of about $11 billion for 2026 (the guide says top half of $6 billion to $15 billion; this is my estimate), $12.0 billion in 2027, $12.5 billion in 2028, then a slow fade to $11.5 billion in 2030 and $12.0 billion in 2031, as dealers stop restocking and tariff recoveries end. Mid-cycle free cash flow is $11 billion. The finance arm is worth $8.9 billion, about 11 times its annualized segment profit of $1.05 billion after a 23% tax rate. All of these are my estimates.

In the Base the two methods are 30% apart, just inside the line where I would flag a divergence, and well above the 20% warning level. The gap comes from the multiple method treating $30 of 2027 earnings as a lasting level at 17 times, while the cash flow model fades free cash flow to a mid-cycle $11 billion and discounts it. I weight the cash flow model at 60% because it follows the fade, and the multiple at 40%. Unweighted Base values are $377.28 and $510.00, and the weighted value is a point inside that range, not a narrowing of it. The maximum buy price would be $216.94 on the first method and $293.25 on the second. Which method is right does not change the decision at $564.50.

- **Record backlog.** $78.1 billion, up 96% in a year, with customer advances of $5.3 billion.

- **Margins.** An adjusted operating margin of 20.6%, with Power and Energy at 24.4% and Construction at 22.9%.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: the cycle.** Construction and mining are cyclical, and the company says Resource Industries growth came mainly from dealer inventory.
- **Data center concentration.** A pause in AI spending would hit power generation, the fastest-growing unit.
- **Tariffs.** About $2.2 billion of cost in 2026, and the recoveries (about $520 million) are not assumed to continue.
- **Margin.** The company guides the adjusted margin near the bottom of its target range, and costs for manufacturing, freight and research are rising.
- **Inventory.** $22.2 billion of inventory, up 17% in a year.
- **Finance arm.** $34.6 billion of Financial Products debt backs customer loans and leases.

## Management execution

Management has delivered record sales and backlog, raised prices, kept returning cash and is adding engine capacity. It also guided the fourth quarter to moderate growth, which coincided with a 9.3% fall in the shares, and the margin is helped by tariff recoveries that fade. **Assessment: 15/20, strong operations with a margin that is still below target and a cycle risk the guidance does not address. The next tests are the fourth quarter report in late January, the margin against the target range, and the start of the new engine plants in 2027.**

- Backlog falling for two quarters

Today's readings: the backlog and power generation growth are green, the guided fourth quarter growth and the dealer inventory are yellow, and the margin near the bottom of the target range is also yellow. The next point is the fourth quarter report in late January.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Data center spending slows and power generation orders fall, removing the fastest-growing profit pool.
2. Dealers cut inventory while end-user demand softens, turning record sales into a decline.
3. Tariff costs rise further without recoveries, keeping the margin below the target range.
