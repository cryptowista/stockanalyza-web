Universal Corporation has raised its dividend for 56 consecutive years. In the five fiscal years to March 2026 it paid $393 million of dividends and generated $130 million of free cash flow, and in the first half of fiscal 2027 operating cash flow was an outflow of $213.8 million. On October 9, ten months after it refinanced its bank facilities, Universal amended the credit agreement to lift its leverage cap from 3.00 times to 3.75 times. At $33.02 the dividend of $3.32 yields 10.1%, and the shares are 44% below their February high of $59.38.

Universal buys, processes and sells leaf tobacco to the large cigarette makers, and runs a small ingredients business. In the second quarter the tobacco side was still waiting for customers to ship. Management says it is "encouraged by where our tobacco business stands", and in the first half tobacco operating income was $47.4 million, down 53%. The company calls the shortfall timing, and says shipments and profit will fall in the second half. Shipments do move between quarters. A covenant amendment is a different kind of item.

My Base value is $23.45, which puts the price 40.8% above it. The Bear value of $5.14 is 84.4% below the price and the Bull value of $42.07 is 27.4% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is $23.53. At Confidence C my rules require a 47.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of $12.31, or $12.04 to $12.72 depending on the method. The verdict is Reduce for existing holders, and new money should wait.

Valuation route: a global leaf tobacco merchant with thin margins and a large working capital need, plus a small ingredients business, so I value it on enterprise value to forward EBITDA, with a ten-year cash flow model that charges for working capital as the second method. Economic classification: Standard (4/12). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

What five years of cash flow show

Leaf merchants swallow cash when crops are short and customers want tobacco, because inventory and advances to growers rise, and release it when the cycle turns. The cycle explains the swings. It does not explain the total: over five fiscal years free cash flow of $130 million covered one third of the dividends.

What Universal is

Universal sources flue-cured, burley and dark air-cured tobacco from farmers in more than 30 countries, grades, processes and ships it to makers of consumer tobacco products. Tobacco Operations earned $211.5 million of operating income on $2,576.4 million of revenue in fiscal 2026, an 8.2% margin. Ingredients, which makes plant-based ingredients including fruit and vegetable products, earned $3.2 million on $348.1 million of revenue, down from $12.3 million a year earlier, and had a $41.1 million goodwill impairment at its Shank's operation in the fourth quarter.

The business is a service to a small group of large cigarette makers and a working capital machine. Customers commit to volumes, and the company holds the leaf in between. The risk is leaf it has bought and nobody has ordered: uncommitted inventory was 27% of tobacco inventory at March 31, 2026, above the 10% to 20% range management targets, and tobacco inventory write-downs were $43.4 million in fiscal 2026, against $18.7 million the year before and a five-year average the CFO put at about $14 million.

The primary Base value is $22.94. The multiple needs a reason. Setting enterprise value equal to after-tax operating profit times one minus growth divided by the return on new capital, all over the 8% discount rate minus growth, 7.0x implies growth of 2.8% a year forever at an 8.5% return on new capital. The Bear multiple of 6.0x implies 1.2% (at 6%) and the Bull multiple of 8.5x implies 3.8% (at 10.5%). For a company whose main product faces falling cigarette volumes these are not conservative growth rates, and that is why I do not pay more. Returns on capital here are thin: fiscal 2026 adjusted operating profit after tax of about $150 million on roughly $2.4 billion of equity and net debt is a return near 6%, below my 8% discount rate. A merchant that earns less than its cost of capital is worth about its after-tax operating profit divided by the discount rate, which is close to 7 times EBITDA.

My Base EBITDA path is $212 million in fiscal 2027, $245 million in 2028, $252 million in 2029, $257 million in 2030 and $260 million in 2031. The recovery in 2028 comes from uncommitted inventory normalizing, smaller crops that management expects, and an Ingredients restructuring that management says runs through fiscal 2028. After that it grows about 1% to 3% a year. The Bear path stays near $190 million to $205 million and the Bull path reaches $295 million in 2030, still below fiscal 2025's $303 million.

How good is the earnings power?

Revenue grew from $2,104 million to $2,924 million between fiscal 2022 and 2026, but operating margin and cash did not follow: EBITDA rose 19% over the period while interest expense stayed high and free cash flow was $130 million in total. The first half of fiscal 2027 took revenue down 15% and operating income down 56%. Return on invested capital near 6% is below any cost of capital I would use. The business is not broken, but it is a low-return, high-working-capital merchant with a thin margin for error, and the first-half loss shows how little a bad six months needs to wipe out.

Dividend

The quarterly dividend is $0.83, or $3.32 a year, a 10.1% yield at $33.02. In May 2026 the board raised it from $0.82 for the 56th consecutive year, an increase of one cent. It costs about $83 million a year on 24.9 million shares, and first-half dividends were $41.4 million. Free cash flow covered it once in the last five fiscal years, in the exceptional fiscal 2025, and fell $1.0 million short in fiscal 2026. On my Base path cash flow after dividends is negative every year to 2032. The record is an asset a board protects, and protecting it with borrowing has a limit that the covenant sets. A yield above 10% usually means the market doubts the payout, and I give the dividend no weight in the valuation beyond the cash it returns in the model.

Reasons to own UVV

  • The cycle may be turning. Worldwide unsold flue-cured and burley stocks fell by about 45 million kilograms in the quarter, and management expects smaller crops next season and says El Niño worries are drawing in new demand.
  • Customers have committed. Management says commitments are in line with its full-year sales plan, and that 81% of tobacco inventory is committed.
  • The equity is a leveraged bet on recovery. My Bull value of $42.07 is 27% above the price, and at 8.0x the multiple method alone gives $32.22.
  • The dividend history is long and the yield is high. A record of 56 increases and a 10.1% yield mean income investors have reasons to hold, and a $100 million buyback authorization runs to November 2028.
  • Ingredients could stop losing money. The restructuring is expected to run through fiscal 2028, and goodwill is down to $75.0 million after the impairment.

What could go wrong?

  • The covenant gets tight. The cap is 3.75 times for September and December 2026, 3.25 times for March 2027 and 3.00 times from June 2027. The first half cash outflow was $213.8 million, and failing a test could put access to the revolver at risk.
  • The dividend is cut or funded by debt. On my Base path leverage stays near 4.3 times because cash after dividends is negative, and the board has to choose between the record and the balance sheet.
  • Oversupply lasts. If EBITDA stays near $200 million my Bear value is $5.14, and with EBITDA 10% below Base the triangulated value is $15.81.
  • More write-downs. Tobacco inventory write-downs were $43.4 million in fiscal 2026 against a five-year average of about $14 million, and uncommitted inventory was still $229.5 million in September.
  • Higher taxes and interest. The tax rate was 34.3% in the first half against 25.2%, and at a 35% rate in the model the Base value is $21.03.
  • The core category shrinks. Cigarette volumes fall over time, and the customers are a small group of large cigarette makers.

Management execution

Management delivered a miss in the third quarter of fiscal 2026 (the stock fell about 10%), a goodwill impairment in the fourth, a weak first quarter of fiscal 2027 and a second quarter that cut first-half adjusted earnings per share to $0.19 from $1.74 a year earlier. It refinanced its bank facilities in December 2025 and amended the leverage covenant ten months later. Credit goes to its stated aim of cutting debt and interest expense, the candid view that shipments are weighted to the second half and a 56-year dividend record. The remaining question is whether a company that borrows to keep raising the dividend by a cent can keep doing so. Management credibility scores 9/20.
