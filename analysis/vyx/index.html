What NCR Voyix is

NCR Voyix sells the software, services and payments that run checkout and the restaurant floor: Aloha for restaurants, a retail platform for grocers and convenience stores, self-checkout and payments. It has about 85,000 platform sites (up 10%) and more than 8,500 payment sites (up 2%). Revenue was $2,687 million in 2025. Recurring revenue was $1,676 million, 62% of the total, and in the second quarter it was $435 million, 83% of revenue, because hardware sales are now out of the base. The company was split in 2023 (NCR Atleos was spun off) and sold its Digital Banking business in September 2024, which paid down debt.

Three facts define the setup:

  • Hardware is now outsourced. From April 1, 2026, Ennoconn designs, builds and ships the hardware, and NCR Voyix books a net commission in service revenue. Second quarter product revenue fell from $185 million to $27 million, and total revenue fell 21%, but pro forma for the change revenue rose 1% ($523 million against $520 million). Margins look better because low-margin hardware left: adjusted EBITDA margin was 18.7% against 14.1%.
  • EBITDA is growing, slowly. Adjusted EBITDA was $425 million in 2025 (+22%) and the 2026 guide is $432 to $447 million (+3% to +7%). The February outlook was $440 to $455 million, so the range has come down by $8 million.
  • The stock has fallen. The shares started 2026 at $10.19 and the 52-week range is $6.02 to $12.09. At $7.15 they are 41% below the high and 19% above the low.

Primary valuation: normalized owner free cash flow times a multiple

The framework classifies NCR Voyix as a Standard company (score 2/12) and routes it to the free cash flow method. That method multiplies normalized free cash flow per share by a multiple chosen from three tiers: 8 to 10 times for cyclical or risky cash flows, 10 to 12 times for average stable ones, 12 to 15 times for very stable growing ones. For leveraged companies the framework says to anchor on owner earnings after maintenance capital expenditure, not on adjusted free cash flow. I use the 2028E owner free cash flow as the normalized figure, because by then the restructuring cash has halved, and I take multiples of 9, 10 and 11 times for the Bear, Base and Bull. The beta of 1.57 and cash flow variance above 30% point to the first tier; leverage of 2.0 times and a reasonable competitive position point to the second, so 10 times, the boundary, is the Base. The multiple rises one turn in the Bull because cash flow would then grow more than 10% a year, as the framework allows.

The formula: equity value equals 2028E owner free cash flow times the multiple, minus $207 million of preferred stock. Debt is already in the cash flow through interest, so net debt is not subtracted again. Per share equals equity divided by 138.07 million shares.

Reasons to own VYX

  • EBITDA is rising. Adjusted EBITDA grew 22% in 2025 and the first half of 2026 is up 5%.
  • It is cheap on the adjusted lines. Enterprise value is 4.7 times 2026E adjusted EBITDA and the company guides to a 21% free cash flow yield, before restructuring.
  • The cash drag may be ending. The Digital Banking tax of $284 million is paid, and operating cash flow turned positive in the first half ($59 million against minus $284 million).
  • The installed base is large. About 85,000 platform sites, up 10%, and new wins such as a platform contract with more than 200 Pizza Ranch restaurants and a multi-year agreement with Rutter's.
  • Recurring software is growing. Recurring software revenue grew 6% in the second quarter, and remaining contract value is up 65% to $286 million.
  • The hardware burden is gone. The Ennoconn arrangement removes inventory and about $41 million of receivables are still to be collected.

The Bull value of $12.58 assumes EBITDA of $505 million and restructuring cash of $30 million by 2028. It needs the cost roll-off and some growth together.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: the cash costs do not roll off. If restructuring cash stays at $80 million in 2028, the Base primary value falls to $3.57.
  • EBITDA misses. The 2026 range has already come down by $8 million since February. At 2028E EBITDA of $440 million the Base primary value is $3.57.
  • Restaurants profit is shrinking. Restaurants adjusted EBITDA fell 15% in the second quarter and 12% in the half.
  • Debt and preferred stock rank ahead. $864 million of net debt and about $207 million of preferred stock, with $650 million of notes due in 2028. The covenant terms and the revolver size are UNAVAILABLE; the company borrowed and repaid $103 million on the revolver in the first half.
  • Operational and control events. A ransomware incident has cost $47 million in total ($37 million recovered from insurance), and fraudulent bank transfers have totaled $34 million (about $16 million recovered so far), according to the quarterly filing.
  • Reporting changes. The company now reports remaining contract value instead of annual recurring revenue, and its adjusted free cash flow guide has no reconciliation.
  • Reliance on a partner. Ennoconn now designs, builds and ships the hardware, and the company is an agent for those sales.
  • Volatility. The shares fell 29.8% from the start of the year. A market value of under $1 billion with a beta of 1.57 can move a long way on one report.

I assign no probability to any of these, and the price does not prove they are all discounted.

Management execution

The record is mixed. Management completed the spin-off, sold Digital Banking for about $2.5 billion and repaid $2.1 billion of debt in 2024, and has grown adjusted EBITDA from $348 million to $425 million in a year. Against that, it has booked restructuring and transformation costs every year, free cash flow has been negative for two years, guidance for 2026 EBITDA has been lowered once, the company has had a ransomware incident and fraudulent bank transfers, and it bought back stock while owner cash flow was negative. Insiders hold about 0.5% of the shares (data site). A longer guidance history is UNAVAILABLE. Assessment: 8/20 on the framework's credibility scale, mixed to low. The next test is the third quarter report, estimated for November 5, and a clear second-half free cash flow figure that reconciles to the cash flow statement.

Character change: the hardware transition is a real change in the business model, from a hardware seller to a software, services and commission business. It lifts margins and removes working capital, and I do not treat it as negative, so the verdict is not lowered. It also makes year-over-year revenue comparisons less useful until the second quarter of 2027.
