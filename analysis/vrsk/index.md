## What Verisk is

Verisk sells data, models and analytics to insurers. Its underwriting segment (about 70% of revenue) supplies the industry's standard forms, rules, loss costs, catastrophe models and risk data. Its claims segment supplies anti-fraud analytics, estimating tools and property restoration solutions.

- Subscriptions are 83% of revenue and grew 8% organically in the second quarter. Transactional revenue is 17% and fell 4.2%.
- Insurers rely on Verisk's loss data and standard forms. That dependence is the moat, and it is why management sees AI as additive.
- Management retired 8.5 million shares in the first half, so the share count fell 3.6% in a year, and shareholders' equity is now negative because buybacks exceeded retained earnings.

## Reasons to own VRSK

- **Subscription base.** 83% of revenue is subscriptions that grew 8% organically, with strong price realization in renewals.
- **Margins and returns.** Adjusted EBITDA margin of 56% to 57% and ROIC of about 32%.
- **Growth is re-accelerating.** Organic constant-currency growth was 5.8% in the second quarter and management expects gradual improvement.
- **Cash conversion.** Free cash flow of $1.24 billion over twelve months is 37.6% of revenue after stock pay.
- **AI as a product.** XactAI licensees rose nearly tenfold since March to about 7,000, though management says AI monetization is early and hard to quantify.

## What could go wrong?

- **AI and in-house models.** Carriers building their own large language models could pay less for third-party data, which is the main long-run risk.
- **Soft commercial property market.** Clients quote fewer opportunities and spend less on underwriting data, which hurts transactional revenue.
- **Leverage and buybacks.** Net debt rose by $1.4 billion in six months and equity is negative. Buying back $1.7 billion at about $200 a share has not helped returns at today's price.
- **Litigation.** Legal fees tied to ongoing litigation reduced net income, and I have no estimate of the outcome.
- **Execution.** The chief information officer is leaving and the chief technology officer is interim CIO.

## Management execution

Management has delivered steady margins and organic growth near 6%, and it keeps leverage inside a stated target of 2 to 3 times. The capital allocation record is mixed: large buybacks at an average price above today's, a leverage increase to fund them, and an unnamed divestiture at the end of 2025. Management credibility scores **14/20**.

## Final verdict: hold at $177.63

Verisk is a very good business at a fair price. The shares are 6.7% above my Base value, within 1% of my primary value, and the price has already absorbed the 28% drop. I would want a price between about $108 and $92, or clear evidence that growth is above 6%, before treating it as a buy.
