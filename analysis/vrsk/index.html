Verisk is the kind of business that rarely goes on sale: 83% of its revenue is subscriptions, its adjusted EBITDA margin is 57% and its return on capital is about 32%. It is down 28% from its high of $248.22. But the company also spent $1.7 billion on buybacks in the first half at an average price near $200, using cash and debt, and the stock still trades about where my Base case says it should.

At the October 9, 2026 close of $177.63, the shares trade 6.7% above my triangulated Base value of $166.43. The Bear value is $103.72 and the Bull value is $209.49. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 45%, so the maximum buy price is $91.54, 48% below the price.

Valuation route: standard value. The sector-specific method does not apply: Verisk is a profitable, cash-generating data and analytics company, not an ARR software story. Economic classification: Quality Growth, 7/12 on the sector scorecard, so the engine is GROWTH_ADJUSTED_MULTIPLE with an explicit multiple build. Primary method: normalized owner free cash flow times a multiple. Secondary method: a six-year DCF at 10%. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets. Owner free cash flow here is cash after interest, taxes, capex (including capitalized software) and stock pay.

What Verisk is

Verisk sells data, models and analytics to insurers. Its underwriting segment (about 70% of revenue) supplies the industry's standard forms, rules, loss costs, catastrophe models and risk data. Its claims segment supplies anti-fraud analytics, estimating tools and property restoration solutions.

  • Subscriptions are 83% of revenue and grew 8% organically in the second quarter. Transactional revenue is 17% and fell 4.2%.
  • Insurers rely on Verisk's loss data and standard forms. That dependence is the moat, and it is why management sees AI as additive.
  • Management retired 8.5 million shares in the first half, so the share count fell 3.6% in a year, and shareholders' equity is now negative because buybacks exceeded retained earnings.

History and growth diagnostics

Valuation history. The enterprise value is 15.0 times 2026E adjusted EBITDA and the price is 18.8 times trailing free cash flow (19.7 times after stock pay). Verisk has traded at higher multiples in recent years (ESTIMATED from public data, not verified here), so today's price is below its own history, but the history belonged to a period of lower rates and faster per-share growth.

PEG (diagnostic only). The price is 21.7 times forward adjusted earnings and adjusted EPS grew 5.0% in the first half, so the PEG is about 4, high on its face. I treat it as a diagnostic only. Growth type: STRUCTURAL for subscriptions, with ACQUISITION_AFFECTED flags from a divestiture at the end of 2025 that the sources do not name.

Free cash flow growth quality (diagnostic only). Free cash flow rose from $887 million in 2021 to $1,192 million in 2025, a compound growth of 7.7% a year, and $1,237 million in the last twelve months. Per share it grew 11.9% a year because the share count fell 14% over four years. FCF margin rose from 36.0% to 38.8%. Dividend and capital return

The quarterly dividend is $0.50, up from $0.45, a 1.1% yield with a payout of about 31% of earnings. Buybacks are much larger, $2.2 billion in the last twelve months, funded with cash and new debt. I do not count future buybacks in value per unit.

Reasons to own VRSK

  • Subscription base. 83% of revenue is subscriptions that grew 8% organically, with strong price realization in renewals.
  • Margins and returns. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 56% to 57% and ROIC of about 32%.
  • Growth is re-accelerating. Organic constant-currency growth was 5.8% in the second quarter and management expects gradual improvement.
  • Cash conversion. Free cash flow of $1.24 billion over twelve months is 37.6% of revenue after stock pay.
  • AI as a product. XactAI licensees rose nearly tenfold since March to about 7,000, though management says AI monetization is early and hard to quantify.

What could go wrong?

  • AI and in-house models. Carriers building their own large language models could pay less for third-party data, which is the main long-run risk.
  • Soft commercial property market. Clients quote fewer opportunities and spend less on underwriting data, which hurts transactional revenue.
  • Leverage and buybacks. Net debt rose by $1.4 billion in six months and equity is negative. Buying back $1.7 billion at about $200 a share has not helped returns at today's price.
  • Litigation. Legal fees tied to ongoing litigation reduced net income, and I have no estimate of the outcome.
  • Execution. The chief information officer is leaving and the chief technology officer is interim CIO.

Management execution

Management has delivered steady margins and organic growth near 6%, and it keeps leverage inside a stated target of 2 to 3 times. The capital allocation record is mixed: large buybacks at an average price above today's, a leverage increase to fund them, and an unnamed divestiture at the end of 2025. Final verdict: hold at $177.63

Verisk is a very good business at a fair price. The shares are 6.7% above my Base value, within 1% of my primary value, and the price has already absorbed the 28% drop. I would want a price between about $108 and $92, or clear evidence that growth is above 6%, before treating it as a buy.
