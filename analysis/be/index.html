Bloom Energy reported $250 million of net income for the third quarter and then spent more cash than it made before buying a single machine: operating cash flow was negative $27 million, and capital spending of $153 million took free cash flow to negative $181 million. Sales were up 138% to $1.24 billion and the guide for 2026 was raised for the third time. Over nine months the company booked $480 million of operating income and $273 million of operating cash flow, then spent $231 million on factories. That is the gap I value.

At $175.45 the shares trade 243% above my weighted Base value of $51.15. The Bull value of $108.26 is 38% below the price and the Bear value of $13.52 is 92% below it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade C, which gives a required margin of safety of 42.5% and a maximum buy price of $29.41. My rules say reduce when the price is more than 30% above the Base, which is $66 here, and the shares are about 2.6 times that level.

Valuation route: an industrial equipment maker with a few very large customers, valued on owner free cash flow: non-GAAP operating profit less stock-based pay, taxed at 21%, plus depreciation, less capital spending, less the working capital that growth consumes. Primary method: a ten-year path with a terminal value, discounted at 10%. Cross-check: a multiple of owner free cash flow at the end of 2031, discounted back. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Bloom Energy is

Bloom Energy makes solid oxide fuel cell generators that produce electricity on site from natural gas or biogas, without waiting for the grid. Data center builders have become its main customers. Product revenue was $1,073.6 million of the $1,235.8 million in the third quarter, installation $84.9 million, service $67.6 million and electricity $9.8 million. The 10-Q shows two customers at 43% and 26% of third-quarter revenue (the second is a related party), and 97% of sales were in the United States.

The named projects are large and spread over years. According to Stock Titan, Oracle has contracted 1.2 gigawatts of up to 2.8 gigawatts, deploying into 2027, and AEP is building a 702 megawatt plant for Google that should be fully running by the end of 2028. The 10-K describes a Brookfield framework of up to $5.0 billion over five years. Bloom plans to double factory capacity from 1 gigawatt to 2 gigawatts a year by the end of 2026.

What the filings do not give is a backlog. Remaining performance obligations were $661.2 million at September 30 (the FourWeekMBA summary of the 10-Q puts the nine-month rise at 67.6%), to be recognized within one to two years. That is about six weeks of fourth-quarter sales. Management says the backlog is growing faster than revenue and splits its outlook into backlog and a probability-weighted pipeline, but I could not find an amount for either.

Reasons to own Bloom Energy

  • Demand is large and visible in names: Oracle, AEP with Google, Brookfield, and a Nebius project in New Jersey, with the first Oracle site powered in under two months.
  • Margins are widening: the non-GAAP operating margin was 23.1% in the third quarter against 8.9% a year earlier, and gross margin was 33.8%.
  • Management has raised the 2026 sales guide three times, to $4.4 billion to $4.6 billion, and capacity is doubling to 2 gigawatts a year.
  • Cash is $2.49 billion, nearly equal to the $2.52 billion of convertible notes, which carry no coupon for the largest part.
  • The shares are 50% below their 52-week high.

What could go wrong?

  • Customer concentration. Two customers were 43% and 26% of third-quarter revenue, and one is a related party; a pause by either would hit 2027 sales directly.
  • Cash conversion. Nine-month free cash flow is $42 million on $480 million of operating income, and negative after stock pay. If working capital keeps absorbing 20 cents per dollar of growth, the Base holds; at 30 cents it is $47.41.
  • Thin disclosed backlog. Remaining performance obligations of $661.2 million cover about six weeks of sales, and no amount is given for the rest of the pipeline.
  • Capital intensity. Capital spending jumped to $153.5 million in one quarter; 2 percentage points more of sales every year takes the Base to $41.99.
  • Financing. The $2.5 billion 0% notes mature in November 2030 and can be called from November 2028; the share price must stay above $194.97 for them to convert, and below it they are a debt to repay or refinance.
  • Dilution. Shares outstanding are up 5.5% in nine months, and the weighted basic count is 25% above a year ago.
  • Competition. A June sell-off came on a sign that Bloom will not monopolize data center power supply (Motley Fool), and a lead in hardware tends to erode as rivals copy it.

Management execution

The founder, chairman and chief executive is KR Sridhar and the chief financial officer is Simon Edwards. Management has raised guidance at every report this year, converted or redeemed the 2028 notes, and delivered power to a new Oracle site within two months. The offsets are the size of the dilution from converting debt, a related-party customer among the two largest, and capital spending that has run ahead of cash flow. Assessment: 14/20, good on delivery and weaker on cash. The next test is whether the fourth quarter shows positive free cash flow after the capacity build.

What I would watch from here

The next results are for the fourth quarter and the full year, expected in February. They need to show sales inside the implied $1,348 million to $1,548 million, a non-GAAP margin near 21.5% or better, and, more important for me, positive free cash flow after capital spending.

Green: moves me toward the buy zone

  • Fourth-quarter operating cash flow above capital spending, and a 2027 guide that implies sales of $6 billion or more.
  • A disclosed backlog or remaining obligations above $1.5 billion, or a signed multi-gigawatt order with prepayments.
  • No customer above 35% of revenue.
  • A price at or below $29.41 with the business executing.

Yellow

  • Fourth-quarter sales in the lower half of the implied range ($1.35 billion to $1.45 billion).
  • Free cash flow between negative $100 million and zero for the quarter.
  • A 2027 sales guide of $5.0 billion to $6.0 billion, or capital spending above 9% of sales.

Red

  • Fourth-quarter sales below $1,348 million or a cut to the 2026 guide.
  • A second quarter of negative free cash flow worse than negative $100 million.
  • A 2027 sales guide below $5.0 billion, a large customer pausing orders, or notes settled in a way that costs more than $1 billion of cash.

Today one of my red triggers is already hit in part: third-quarter free cash flow was negative $181 million, worse than the negative $100 million line, though the second quarter had been positive ($175 million). The three most important thesis breakers

  1. A pause by one of the two large customers. The Bear has 2028 sales falling 5% and a value of $13.52.
  2. Working capital and capital spending that keep absorbing the profit. Working capital at 30 cents takes the Base to $47.41; capital spending 2 points higher to $41.99.
