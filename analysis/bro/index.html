What Brown & Brown is

Brown & Brown is an insurance broker. It places property, casualty and employee-benefit coverage for clients and earns commissions and fees, with no underwriting risk on its own balance sheet. It has two main parts, a retail brokerage and a specialty distribution business, and it has historically grown by buying small brokers.

  • In 2025 it spent $7.9 billion of cash on acquisitions, chiefly Accession (RSC Topco), funded with debt and new equity. Shares outstanding rose from about 286 million to 336 million and debt from $4.1 billion to $7.9 billion.
  • Acquisitions contributed $393 million of second-quarter revenue and $829 million for the half. Revenue is not growing without them.
  • The business model is attractive: commissions renew every year, capex is about 1% of revenue, and EBITDAC margins are in the mid-30s.

Brown & Brown has traded much higher in past years (ESTIMATED from public data, not verified here), so today's price is low against its own history, but that history belonged to a company growing organically at high single digits.

Dividend and capital return

The dividend is $0.66 a share, a yield of 1.0% and a payout of about 21% of earnings, covered several times by free cash flow. The company repurchased $500 million of stock in the first half of 2026, which, with other changes, left shares outstanding about 6 million lower, and $628 million over the last twelve months. Buying back stock while net debt is still 2.8 times EBITDAC competes with debt paydown, but it is small against $1.4 billion of annual free cash flow.

Reasons to own BRO

  • Recurring, asset-light cash flow. Capex is about 1% of revenue, and free cash flow has exceeded net income in every year shown.
  • Free cash flow yield. Owner cash flow yield on market value is 6.7% for 2026E and 7.3% for 2027E in my Base.
  • Organic growth can recover. Retail organic growth was still 1.5% in the quarter, and the pressure in specialty distribution is mainly a cycle in catastrophe property rates.
  • Margin discipline. The adjusted EBITDAC margin has stayed between 35% and 37% through the integration.
  • Debt can fall. Debt of $7.8 billion is under 3 times EBITDAC, and my Base free cash flow is enough to repay it over time or fund buybacks and dividends.

What could go wrong?

  • Organic growth stays at zero. Organic revenue fell in the second quarter and grew 0.3% or less in the first half. If property rates keep falling, growth could be negative in 2027.
  • Integration. Accession is a large acquisition that is not fully integrated. Acquisition and integration costs were $57 million in the half.
  • Interest and amortization. Interest expense doubled to $100 million a quarter and amortization to $110 million, which reduces reported earnings.
  • Earnings quality. First-half results include gains from changes in earn-out liabilities ($34 million) and an escrow mark-to-market ($69 million) that I exclude.
  • Earn-outs. The size of unpaid earn-outs is not stated in the release and is not in my numbers.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Organic revenue growth stays at or below zero through 2027, which removes the recovery that the price already assumes.

  3. Large unrecorded earn-outs or a further debt-funded deal push net debt above 3 times EBITDAC.

Final verdict: hold at $62.88

Brown & Brown is a good business that fell because growth stopped, and the price already assumes it comes back. At $62.88 the shares are within 1% of my primary value and 11% below my triangulated Base, which is a fair price with no margin of safety. I would want a price between about $35 and $46, or two quarters of organic growth above 3% that justify a lower margin of safety, before treating it as a buy.

VERDICT: HOLD at $62.88; Triangulated Base $70.70; upside 12.4%; required MOS 50%; maximum buy price $35.35. The main thesis breaker is organic revenue that stays flat while debt and interest remain high.
