Besi's third quarter orders were €342.6 million, up 96% from a year earlier and well above what analysts expected. Hybrid bonding orders in the first nine months were more than double the whole of 2025. The share price did the opposite: from a high of €328.40 in June it fell to €145.10 the day after the results, and after a sharp chip sell-off on November 5 it closed at €143.95 on November 6.

Even at that price Besi trades 19% above my Base value of €120.72. The Bear value is €49.43, 65.7% below the price, and the Bull value is €185.73, 29.0% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The verdict is Hold, with new money waiting for €69.41 or less.

Valuation route: a high-margin, cyclical maker of chip assembly equipment with net cash. The primary method is a ten-year free cash flow model; the cross-check is a forward multiple of enterprise value to 2028 operating profit. Both use the same net cash and share count. Values are present fair-value estimates in euros, not 12-month price targets.

## What Besi is

Besi, based in Duiven in the Netherlands, builds the machines that place and bond chips onto substrates and onto each other at the end of the manufacturing process. Its most important product today is hybrid bonding equipment, which joins chips directly copper to copper without solder bumps. That allows denser stacks of memory and logic, which is exactly what AI accelerators need. Besi says it has 23 customers for the technology and cumulative demand above 215 systems.

Applied Materials, the world's largest chip equipment maker, has worked with Besi on hybrid bonding since 2020, first bought shares in April 2025 and on October 26, 2026 agreed to raise its stake to about 20%, without seeking a board seat. In the fourth quarter Besi expects higher costs for "strategic consulting". Neither tells you whether a bid is coming, and I put no takeover premium in the value.

The worry in the release was timing. Some customers asked to move deliveries to fit their capacity plans for the first half of 2027, and severe memory shortages are keeping lower memory stacks in production longer, which delays demand for the taller stacks where hybrid bonding is needed most. Management called it production-line readiness, not postponement. The fourth quarter revenue guide was flat, slightly below what analysts expected, and the stock fell 7.7% the next day.

For 2026 as a whole, Besi is heading for about €1.0 billion of revenue, up from €591.3 million in 2025, and net income of €247.8 million in the first nine months alone, against €131.6 million for all of 2025.

## Which numbers to trust

- **Profit is clean.** Besi's operating income includes its share-based pay, and it does not publish an adjusted version. Net margin was 37.8% in the third quarter.
- **Some development spending is capitalized.** About €26 million in 2025. I count it as capital spending, together with property and equipment, about 5.5% of revenue.
- **Equipment sales tie up working capital.** Receivables and inventory grow with revenue. I let them take 20% of each extra euro of sales.
- **The cycle is real.** Revenue fell 2.7% in 2025 before doubling in 2026. My Bear case includes a downturn in 2028.

## Cash, shares and capital returns

Net cash was €246.6 million at the end of September, after Besi called its €175 million 2029 convertible bond earlier this year; the bond converted at €115.50, well below the €244 share price at the time. I value about 81.0 million shares (my estimate, including those conversions and net of buybacks). Besi paid a dividend of €1.58 a share in May, 95% of 2025 earnings, and has started a new €100 million buyback after completing a €60 million one; in the third quarter it bought shares at an average of €210.67, well above today's price.

## Primary valuation: ten years of free cash flow

The Base assumes revenue of €1.25 billion in 2027, €1.5 billion in 2028 and €1.9 billion in 2030, inside the company's long-term target of €1.7 to €2.2 billion. Growth then slows to 4%. The operating margin rises from 44% to 48% at the peak and settles at 46% over the cycle, in the middle of the company's 45% to 55% target. Tax is 18%. I discount at 10% a year with 3% growth after 2036.

## History and earnings check

Besi's stock has always moved more than its business. Over the past year it ranged from €120.15 to €328.40. On my rough estimate of 2026 earnings, about €4.3 a share (nine months of €247.8 million plus a fourth quarter similar to the third, my arithmetic), the price is about 33 times this year's profit. That is high for an equipment maker even at the start of an upturn. The growth-adjusted price to earnings ratio is around 1, but in a cyclical industry I treat it only as a flag.

## How the buy price is set

Confidence is C, with a margin of safety of 42.5%. The business itself is excellent; the grade is lower because the forecast depends on the timing of a technology change at a few memory and logic makers, my share count is an estimate, and I did not have a dated peer set. That gives a maximum buy price of **€69.41**.

15% more or less revenue in every year moves the Base by €17.38, the largest single effect at 14.4% of the value. A point of discount rate moves it by €9.83 to €12.99, four points of operating margin by €10.64, three turns of multiple by €9.16, and €100 million less net cash by €1.23.

For scale, the expected return under my Base is about 15.3% a year at €69.41, 10.1% at €120.72, 9.0% at €143.95 and 8.1% at €170.

## Dividend

Besi paid €1.58 a share in May 2026, 95% of 2025 earnings, a yield of 1.1% at today's price. With 2026 profit far higher, a high payout would mean a much larger dividend next spring, but the amount is the board's decision and I do not model it. The new €100 million buyback runs to October 2027.

## Reasons to own Besi

- Third quarter orders of €342.6 million, up 96%, and hybrid bonding orders more than doubled.
- A 65% gross margin and a net margin near 38% at the start of an upturn.
- Net cash, a 95% payout and a running buyback.
- 23 hybrid bonding customers and Applied Materials as a long-term partner and soon a roughly 20% shareholder.
- Long-term targets of €1.7 to €2.2 billion of revenue at a 45% to 55% operating margin.

## What could go wrong?

- Timing. Customers already moved deliveries into 2027, and memory shortages keep older stack heights in production longer.
- The cycle. Equipment orders can drop sharply in a downturn, as in my Bear.
- Concentration. A few memory and foundry customers decide when hybrid bonding goes into volume.
- Competition. Other bonding equipment makers, and the question of what Applied Materials ultimately wants.
- Valuation. The price already sits above the top of the company's own long-term revenue target.

## Management execution

- Orders beat expectations by about 15% in the third quarter, and revenue more than doubled.
- Hybrid bonding was developed over years with Applied Materials and now has 23 customers.
- Capital returns are generous: a 95% dividend payout and continuous buybacks.
- Buybacks in the third quarter were done at an average of €210.67, far above today's price.
- The fourth quarter guide came in below expectations as customers shifted deliveries.

On my ten-point management check (two points each), Besi scores 17 of 20.

## What I would watch from here

The full-year report in February has to show fourth quarter revenue inside the guided range, orders of at least €300 million, a gross margin of 63% or more and confirmation that the shifted deliveries land in the first half of 2027.

### Green

- Quarterly orders above €300 million.
- A memory maker announcing hybrid bonding for volume HBM.
- Gross margin of 64% or more.

### Yellow

- Orders between €220 and €300 million.
- More delivery shifts without cancellations.
- A change in Applied Materials' intentions.

### Red

- Orders below €220 million for two quarters.
- Gross margin below 60%.
- Order cancellations at a large hybrid bonding customer.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Memory makers staying with older bonding methods beyond 2028, which leads toward the Bear.
2. A semiconductor equipment downturn in 2027 or 2028, which hits revenue and margins at once.
3. Revenue tracking toward €2.5 billion by 2030 with margins above 50%, which leads toward the Bull.

## Final verdict: hold at €143.95

Besi is one of the best businesses in European technology: a clear technology lead, a 65% gross margin, net cash and owners who get almost all the profit back. After a fall of more than half, the stock is no longer priced for perfection. It is still priced for something close to it.

**Verdict: HOLD at €143.95, watchlist for new money. Base €120.72, 16.1% below the price, Bear €49.43, Bull €185.73, Confidence C (16.25/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price €69.41.**

New money waits for €69.41 or less. For an existing holder the original cost is irrelevant; the question is whether hybrid bonding takes over memory stacking on schedule, because the price already assumes it does. A takeover is possible with Applied Materials at about 20%, but I would not pay for that possibility in advance.
