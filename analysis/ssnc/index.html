What SS&C is

SS&C is based in Windsor, Connecticut and run by its founder William Stone, who is chairman and chief executive. It sells software and, more importantly, runs back-office work for financial services and healthcare clients: fund administration for hedge funds and private markets, investor servicing and distribution, data rooms (Intralinks), automation software (Blue Prism) and, since October 2025, Calastone, a network that routes orders for funds. In the second quarter 83% of revenue was technology-enabled services, work SS&C does for the client, and 17% was license, maintenance and related revenue.

Calastone cost about £766 million ($1.03 billion), closed on October 14, 2025 and was financed with a $1,050 million increase in the term loan B at SOFR plus 2.00%, maturing in 2031. The company gave no revenue figure for it. Management now says acquisitions added about $36 million to second quarter revenue (call transcript, an automated version, so treat it as approximate) and that Calastone is ahead of plan.

Organic growth is the number to watch, because acquisitions have always been a large part of the SS&C story. It was 4.8% in 2025, 5.3% in the fourth quarter and 7.6% in the second quarter of 2026. About 200 basis points of the latest figure came from a few large license renewals, which are recognized up front under ASC 606, the revenue accounting standard. That is why third quarter guidance is only about 5% organic at the midpoint.

Primary valuation: present value of owner free cash flow

SS&C sits in the standard value branch of my method, a technology company with a stable cash flow, and scores 4 of 12 on the generic scorecard, one point short of Quality Growth. A standard company is valued as a normalized cash flow times a justified multiple. Because this cash flow is growing, I build the multiple from a growth path and a discount rate instead of picking a tier. The table tier for an average stable company, 11 times owner free cash flow, gives $53.88 and I treat it as a sanity check close to my Bear value.

Years 1 to 5 grow at a stated rate, years 6 to 10 fade in a straight line to terminal growth, and a Gordon growth terminal value follows. The discount rate is my 10% default, tested at 9% and 11%. Interest is already deducted from the cash flow, so debt stays flat, and buybacks and dividends are not subtracted because they are how the cash reaches owners. Equity value is divided by 240.2 million diluted shares: 234.7 million outstanding on July 23 plus 5.5 million for options and units (my estimate).

Base growth of 6.5% comes from revenue growth of 5.0% a year (organic growth was 4.8% in 2025 and guidance implies about 5.5% for 2026) and an owner margin that rises 0.3 points a year. That is slower than the roughly 0.5 point a year that management targets for EBITDA margin, because interest, capital spending and stock pay are growing too.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $84.19 and 240.2 million diluted shares the market value is $20,219 million and the enterprise value, with net debt and minorities, is $27,467 million, 11.4 times my Base EBITDA after stock pay against my 12.0 times. A reverse calculation with my method says the price needs owner free cash flow to grow 4.0% a year for five years at a 9% discount rate, 6.7% at 10% and 9.1% at 11%. Put another way, if growth is my 6.5%, the price needs 2026 owner free cash flow of $1,187 million, 0.9% above my $1,176 million.

So the price assumes that SS&C hits its guidance and keeps growing owner cash flow near its revenue growth plus a little margin, which it has not done since 2021. As earnings, the shares trade at 11.9 times adjusted EPS of $7.09 and 24.2 times trailing GAAP EPS of $3.48 (aggregator). The gap between those two is the amortization and the stock pay.

Dividend and buybacks

The aggregator lists a dividend of $1.20 a year ($0.30 a quarter), a 1.43% yield at $84.19. The 10-Q shows $64.0 million paid in the second quarter, about $0.27 a share, so I read the $0.30 as a raise that took effect with the September 1 ex-dividend date (my inference). Dividends paid rose from $174.0 million in 2021 to $253.8 million in 2025, 9.9% a year, and the 2025 payout was 21% of owner free cash flow. The dividend is well covered. Buybacks are the larger return: in 2025 SS&C bought 12.3 million shares at an average $84.12, within 0.1% of today's price, and in the second quarter it bought 6.4 million for $435.2 million. Dividends paid to non-U.S. holders face U.S. withholding tax, and the rate depends on your tax treaty and your broker.

Reasons to own SS&C

  • The revenue is sticky. Technology-enabled services are 83% of revenue, the work runs inside client operations, and the adjusted EBITDA margin was 39.2% in 2025 and 39.5% in the second quarter.
  • Organic growth has picked up. It rose from 4.8% in 2025 to 7.6% in the second quarter, with about 2 points from renewal timing, and guidance for the year is about 5.5%.
  • It generates cash. 2026 cash from operations is guided to $1,717 to $1,817 million, and capital spending of about $310 million (4.6% of revenue) still leaves a free cash flow of about $1.46 billion before stock pay (my arithmetic).
  • The share count is falling. Diluted shares were 253.1 million in 2025 and 242.0 million in the second quarter, and the third quarter guide is 237.6 to 240.6 million.
  • The price is below its own history. 16.4 times owner free cash flow compares with a year-end median of 18.0, worth $92.65 a share.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: owner cash flow stays flat while revenue grows. It did exactly that from 2021 to 2025. If capitalized software ($248 million over the last 12 months) and stock pay ($268.5 million) keep growing faster than revenue, a business at 16.4 times is paying for growth it does not deliver.
  • Leverage and refinancing. Gross debt is $7.6 billion, interest is guided at $406 to $416 million, notes mature in 2027, and buybacks ran $164 million ahead of cash flow in the first half.
  • AI. Analysts have cited AI risk to the stock since at least February 2026. Management says it sees no sign of clients using AI to push on price and points to AI as a driver of its own margin. A Bear case with 2.5% growth is how I price the possibility.
  • License renewals are lumpy. About 2 points of second quarter organic growth came from a few large renewals, and the third quarter guide of about 5% shows the reversal.
  • Healthcare policy. Management described the Medicare GLP-1 program as a six-month government trial with about even odds of continuing (automated transcript). I could not size its revenue.
  • Acquisition accounting. Goodwill ($9,930.0 million) and intangibles ($3,873.8 million) add up to $13,803.8 million against equity of $6,710.5 million. Return on invested capital is 8.7% on the aggregator's measure, close to a cost of capital.
  • Data quirk. One aggregator shows net debt of negative $7.33 billion and labels it net cash. It is net debt, not cash, and I use my own $7,178.7 million.

Management execution

William Stone has run the company since he founded it and is both chairman and chief executive. Insiders own 14.4% of the shares (aggregator). The record is good on the measurable items: full year EPS guidance was raised from $6.70 to $7.02 to $6.93 to $7.25, net leverage drifted from 2.80 times at year end to 2.75 times, margin guidance is on track, and the second quarter buyback was the largest in the company's history. Against that, SS&C has spent about $3.3 billion in cash on acquisitions since 2022 (aggregator), adjusted figures leave out amortization and stock pay, stock pay has grown 22.6% a year, and one person holds both top jobs. Assessment: 13/20, strong operators, heavy adjustments.
