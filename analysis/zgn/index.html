Zegna's shares trade at 9.9 times my 2026 estimate of EBITDA before rent, which looks unremarkable for a luxury brand. What the multiple hides is the cash. After rent on its shops, capital spending, tax and working capital, about €59 million of free cash flow is left on €446 million of EBITDA, 13% of it, against 20% in 2025. Revenue is growing 7%, the ZEGNA brand grew 13.8% in the third quarter and the company holds €59.6 million of net cash before leases. The shares closed at $13.79 on November 13.

My Base value is $10.15 a share, the Bear value $6.32 and the Bull value $13.91, so the price sits at the top of my range. The Base blends two methods that disagree: a ten year cash flow model after rent gives $8.40, and a multiple of 2027 EBITDA gives $12.77. The first says the stock is 39% too expensive, the second 7%. I count rent as a real cost, which is why the cash model carries the larger weight. The price comes from a single quote page, so treat the exact dollar figure as unconfirmed.

Valuation route: listed luxury brand with franchise economics but low cash conversion, valued with a ten year model of free cash flow to shareholders after lease payments for 2027 to 2036, discounted at 9%, and cross-checked with a multiple of 2027 EBITDA before rent. The cash model charges lease payments directly. The multiple method deducts lease liabilities, put options on minority stakes and the minorities themselves, so rent is counted once in each.

What Zegna is

Ermenegildo Zegna Group is an Italian luxury menswear company with three businesses. The Zegna segment (the ZEGNA brand, a textile division and some third-party brands) earned an adjusted EBIT margin of 14.4% in 2025 and 14.8% in the first half of 2026. Thom Browne earned 0.4% in 2025 and lost €8.3 million in the first half of 2026. Tom Ford Fashion lost €12 million in the first half, an improvement on a €19 million loss a year earlier. The ZEGNA brand was 62% of 2025 revenue, and 88% of its sales went through the company's own stores and websites, according to a third-party summary of the corporate presentation.

Growth has turned up. Revenue for the nine months to September 30 was €1,421 million, up 7.2% (8.9% organic), after a third quarter of €433.8 million, up 8.9%. Direct-to-consumer sales are 85% of branded revenue, up from 82%, while wholesale fell 11.8%. The Americas grew 14.7% and Greater China 6.6% over the nine months. Management's 2027 targets, as summarized by Quartr, are revenue of €2.2 billion to €2.4 billion and adjusted EBIT of €250 million to €300 million, and the call after the first half confirmed 2027 at the lower end of that range. My Base has €206 million of adjusted EBIT in 2027 and my Bull €222 million, so I treat the target as above both cases and test it separately in the stress section.

Free cash flow and what it leaves out

Under IFRS 16, the lease accounting standard used in Europe, rent on shops is left out of EBITDA and shows up as depreciation and interest. For a company that runs 279 ZEGNA stores of its own, plus Thom Browne and Tom Ford Fashion stores, that matters. In 2025 EBITDA before rent was €407.9 million (adjusted EBIT of €163.0 million plus €244.9 million of depreciation). Zegna paid €147.7 million of lease cash, 36% of that EBITDA, and spent €102.9 million on capital investment. Free cash flow after all of that was €82.1 million, 20% of EBITDA.

Debt, shares and capital allocation

Zegna reports net cash of €59.6 million at June 30, up from €52.1 million in December. That figure excludes lease liabilities of €927.2 million (taken from a data aggregator), which is 2.1 times 2026E EBITDA before rent. It also excludes put options that let minority holders sell stakes in subsidiaries to Zegna, and the minority interests themselves. I deduct €105.6 million for the put options (the December balance of other non-current financial liabilities, a proxy) and €73.2 million for minority interests at book value.

The board proposed a dividend of €0.12 a share for 2025, about €32 million, paid after June 30; I deduct it from the equity value. There are 268.9 million basic shares and I use 270 million diluted, an estimate. The company is controlled by the founding family through a holding company; I did not verify the current stake.

Primary valuation: free cash flow to shareholders after lease payments

I project revenue, adjusted EBIT margin, capital spending, tax and working capital for ten years, 2027 to 2036, and discount free cash flow after rent at 9%, with terminal growth of 2.5%. In the Base, revenue grows 6.5% in 2027, slows to 5.0% in 2030 and 2.5% in 2036, and reaches €2,568 million in 2030. The adjusted EBIT margin rises from 8.8% in 2026 to 9.4% in 2027 and 12.0% from 2031. Capital spending is 5.8% of revenue, falling to 5.0%. Tax is 30% of operating profit after lease interest (the company guides 28% to 30% for 2026), working capital absorbs 21% of each added euro of revenue, and 7% of free cash flow leaks to minorities. I rebuilt 2025 with the same formulas and got €83.2 million against €82.1 million reported.

The 9% discount rate is a judgment, not a beta calculation. Zegna has net cash before leases and a strong flagship brand, but it sells discretionary luxury goods, two of its three brands earn little, and its sales and costs are in several currencies, so I did not use a low, utility-like rate.

Secondary valuation: multiple of EBITDA before rent

The second method applies a multiple to 2027E EBITDA before rent, subtracts lease liabilities, put options and minorities, and adds net cash. The multiples are 7.0 times in the Bear, 8.5 times in the Base and 10.0 times in the Bull. At $13.79 the market pays 9.0 times 2027E and 9.9 times 2026E. I set the Base below that because Zegna turns less EBITDA into cash than the peers I looked at, and because lease liabilities are 2.1 times its EBITDA. The 8.5 times assumes EBITDA grows 8.7% a year to 2030, steady demand for the ZEGNA brand, capital spending near 6% of revenue and no net debt before leases. In the Bear, revenue grows 3% and the margin stays near 9%, which justifies a multiple below peers. Screener readings put Moncler at 9 to 10 times 2026E; I did not verify that.

Dividend and buyback

Zegna proposed €0.12 a share for 2025, about €32 million and a 1.0% yield at $13.79. It was covered 2.6 times by 2025 free cash flow and is covered 1.9 times by my 2026 estimate. I did not verify a share repurchase program.

Reasons to own Zegna

  • The ZEGNA brand grew 13.8% in the third quarter, and its segment margin has risen from 13.9% in 2024 to 14.8% in the first half of 2026.
  • Group reported revenue growth went from 2.5% in the first quarter to 8.9% in the third, with the Americas up 14.7% over nine months.
  • Net cash of €59.6 million before leases, and free cash flow turned positive in the first half.
  • Tom Ford Fashion's loss narrowed to €12 million from €19 million, and management expects it and Thom Browne to be positive in the second half, according to the call summary.
  • If the €250 million profit target for 2027 is met, the multiple method alone is worth about $14.35, level with the price.

What could go wrong?

  • Cash conversion: only 13% of EBITDA reaches free cash flow in 2026. Capital spending of 7% of revenue, as management has indicated for 2026, would cut that year's cash by about €19 million, and every extra half point of capital spending in later years costs $0.74 a share.
  • Margin target: the 2027 target needs the margin to rise from my 2026 estimate of 8.8% to 11.4% in one year, after it fell a full point in 2025.
  • Thom Browne: revenue fell 5.9% in the third quarter and adjusted EBIT swung from a €4 million profit to an €8 million loss in the first half.
  • Leases and claims: €927 million of lease liabilities from an aggregator, and put options and minorities that I can only proxy. The lease balance is large against about €148 million of annual lease cash.
  • Customers and currency: a €10 million provision was booked in 2025 for a bankrupt US retail customer, and exchange rates cut first half revenue growth by about 3 points.
  • Valuation of my own numbers: the second half of 2026, the margin path, the share count and the exchange rate are my estimates, and the price is from one source.

Management execution

Management moved the group to direct sales (85% of branded revenue), cut wholesale and kept growing while it did so. The Zegna segment margin has risen two years in a row, from 13.9% in 2024 to 14.4% in 2025 and 14.8% in the first half of 2026. Against that, group adjusted EBIT fell from €184 million to €163 million in 2025, the Thom Browne margin fell from 8.7% to 0.4%, Tom Ford Fashion still loses money, and the 2027 profit target is far from the current run rate. I score management 7.5 out of 10: the repositioning of the core brand is working, but group profit went backwards in 2025 and the target needs a step the record has not shown yet.

The three most important thesis breakers

These are the events that would make the Hold call wrong, in either direction.

  1. Capital spending falls back toward 5% of revenue and free cash flow reaches 20% of EBITDA by 2027, which lifts the DCF toward $10 and moves the Base toward the multiple method.
  2. Management delivers €250 million of adjusted EBIT in 2027 and the margin keeps rising toward 13% by 2029, which shifts weight to the Bull of $13.91. At that point the price is fair, not cheap.
  3. Free cash flow stays near €60 million while EBITDA grows, the DCF is right, and the call becomes Reduce. The other way out is a price of $5.83 or less, where the margin of safety is met with the Base unchanged.

Final verdict: hold at $13.79, do not add

Zegna is a good brand in a better year than the last one: the ZEGNA brand is growing in double digits, its segment margin is rising and the balance sheet has net cash before leases. The problem is what the shares ask you to believe. At $13.79 the enterprise value including leases is €4.40 billion, 9.9 times 2026E EBITDA before rent, for a company that turns 13% of that EBITDA into free cash flow. The Base earns 6.6% a year at this price, below the 9% I discount at, and a 9% return needs a purchase at $8.40.

The call is Hold rather than Reduce because the multiple method puts the shares only 7% above its Base and the price is level with my Bull. It is not a Buy at any price I can defend above $7.34.
