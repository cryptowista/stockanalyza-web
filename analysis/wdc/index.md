# Western Digital: 11 times earnings sounds cheap until you ask which earnings

WDC, NASDAQ. Published November 20, 2026. Price is the November 20, 2026 intraday quote (3:33 PM ET), down 12.14% on the day. It is not a closing price. Values in U.S. dollars. Fiscal years end in early July.

## Verdict: HOLD at $260.72

Do not add at this price. The stock looks cheap at about 11 times annualized guided earnings, but those earnings carry an operating margin near 50% in a business that earned 24% two years ago. The price is 14.9% above my Base value of $226.97 and 110% above the conservative buy limit of $123.88.

Western Digital reported earnings of $24.28 a share for fiscal 2026. The hard drive business earned $10.22. The difference is largely gains on its retained stake in Sandisk, the flash business it spun off in February 2025. This analysis values the drives and not the gains, and on that basis the stock at $260.72 is 14.9% above my Base value of $226.97.

The quarter itself was excellent. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (ended October 2) revenue rose 55% to $4.38 billion, the non-GAAP gross margin reached 58.1%, the operating margin 49.1%, and free cash flow $1.38 billion. Management guides the second quarter to $5.1 billion of revenue and earnings of $5.80 a share. Annualized, that puts the stock at about 11 times earnings. The market is not paying for those earnings, and it is right not to: in fiscal 2025 the same business had a 24.4% operating margin, and a hard drive is a product that two competitors can make.

My triangulated Base value is **$226.97**. The Bear value of $80.68 is 69.1% below the price and the Bull value of $325.40 is 24.8% above it. At Confidence C the framework requires a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives an entry range of **$123.88 to $137.13**. The lower end, $123.88, is the conservative buy limit. The stock would have to fall another 47.4% to 52.5% to get there. This is a hold, and new money should not add.

Valuation route: industrials and manufacturing, a cyclical hard disk drive maker selling mainly to cloud data centers. Economic classification: Quality Growth (8/12), with the growth scored as a cyclical rebound. Primary method: mid-cycle EBITDA after stock compensation at a normal multiple, valued at mid 2029 and discounted to today with the cash flow in between. Secondary method: an explicit six-year cash flow model whose margins fade to the mid-cycle level. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

## Why the earnings number is not the earnings

Fiscal 2026 revenue was $12.92 billion, up 36%. GAAP net income per share was $24.28 and non-GAAP net income per share was $10.22. The fourth quarter shows the gap clearly: GAAP earnings of $8.21 a share against non-GAAP earnings of $3.56. Western Digital has been selling and exchanging the Sandisk shares it kept after the spin-off, and those gains flow through GAAP earnings but not through the drive business. I use non-GAAP operating income and subtract stock compensation myself, which was $79 million in the first quarter. Anyone comparing the stock with a GAAP price to earnings ratio is comparing it with a number that includes a one-time asset sale.

## What Western Digital is

Since the separation on February 21, 2025, Western Digital is a hard disk drive company. It sells high-capacity nearline drives to cloud providers, which accounted for 89% of revenue in the first quarter of fiscal 2026, and smaller volumes to client and consumer customers. Its two main competitors are Seagate and Toshiba. Toshiba, the smallest, has said it wants 30% of the market by capacity and plans to double output.

The business is a price and volume story. Revenue per exabyte shipped was $17.7 million in the latest quarter, up 28% from a year earlier and 8.8% from the prior quarter, according to Blocks and Files. Management says expiring long-term agreements are being renewed at higher prices and expects more increases over the next three to four quarters. The technology roadmap matters as much: 40-terabyte ePMR drives are expected to be 30% of nearline shipments in the second quarter and 50% in the third, and HAMR drives are expected in volume in the first half of calendar 2027.

The gross margin tells the same story: 37.7% in the December 2024 quarter and 58.1% in the September 2026 quarter, and it has risen every quarter for two years. A business whose margin doubles in two years is either changing structurally or sitting at the top of a cycle, and the honest answer is a mixture. Long-term agreements, hyperscaler concentration and three suppliers instead of many argue for a higher floor than before. Toshiba's expansion and reports of paused data center construction argue against assuming the peak is permanent. My Base case lands between the two: a mid-cycle operating margin of 35%, about the fiscal 2026 level, and well above fiscal 2025.

## Cash, debt and shares

At October 2 Western Digital held $1,314 million in cash and $789 million of current debt, a net cash position of about $525.00 million, or $1.38 a share. On November 16 it redeemed the last $109.5 million of its 3% convertible notes. Another $799 million of other liabilities is not separately identified in the release and is not in my net cash figure, which I flag as an open data gap. It is under 1% of the enterprise value. I use 380 million diluted shares, the company's own guidance for the second quarter. The company returned $3.1 billion to shareholders in fiscal 2026 and added $4.0 billion to its repurchase authorization in February.

## Primary valuation: mid-cycle EBITDA after stock compensation

Enterprise value (EV) is market value plus debt minus cash. Today's earnings are far above the level I consider normal, so a multiple on current EBITDA would value the peak. The transition model instead adds the cash flow from now to mid 2029 (the end of fiscal 2029), and values the business at that date as a multiple of mid-cycle EBITDA after stock compensation, discounted at 11%. Values are in $ million unless stated.

The primary Base value is **$215.45**. The 10x multiple sits in the standard industrial band of 8x to 12x. It rests on a market with three suppliers, customers who sign multi-year commitments, capex near 4% of revenue and no net debt, and it stops short of a premium because the product is priced per terabyte and prices have moved a lot before. At 10x on 8,100 of EBITDA, the business is worth 81.0 billion at mid 2029. The Bear multiple of 6.5x is the commodity cyclical band, for a case where revenue falls to $13.6 billion and the operating margin returns to 24%. The Bull multiple of 12.5x assumes margins settle at 42%.

The exit value is 76% of today's enterprise value in the Base case, over 2.6 years, below the level that calls for extra scrutiny. The cash flow before then is large: about 22.4 billion over the next three fiscal years, which is 23% of today's market value.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $260.72 the market value is about $99.1 billion and the enterprise value about $98.5 billion. That is 9.3x my fiscal 2027 EBITDA after stock compensation, 8.3x fiscal 2028, and 12.2x the mid-cycle EBITDA of 8,100 million. The Q1 free cash flow annualizes to a 5.6% yield. Solving backwards with the rest of the Base case fixed, the price needs an exit multiple of 12.8x mid-cycle EBITDA (against my 10x), or a mid-cycle operating margin of 45% (against my 35%, the fiscal 2026 level of 37.3%, and the current quarter's 49%). It is also the price at which the Base case earns about 2% a year. The price sits between my Base and Bull cases.

If I am right on the business and the stock is worth 10x mid-cycle EBITDA in mid 2029, the equity is worth about $273.42 a share with all the cash kept, +4.9% from today, or 1.8% a year. If the market keeps paying today's 12.2x, it is worth about $320.31, +23%, or 8% a year. A buyer at $260.72 is being paid for the multiple holding, not for the business improving.

## Dividend and buybacks

The quarterly dividend is $0.175 a share, or $0.70 a year, a yield of 0.27%. It is raised 17% and costs about $56 million a quarter. The larger return of capital is the buyback, $1.0 billion in the first quarter alone. Buying shares at $400 or $500 and holding them while the stock falls to $260.72 is a poor use of cash in hindsight, and the next quarters will show whether management keeps buying at lower prices or builds cash.

## Reasons to own WDC

- **Pricing is still rising.** Revenue per exabyte is up 28% in a year, and management expects further increases for three to four quarters.
- **Demand is committed.** Five hyperscale customers had signed firm purchase orders covering 2027 by last fall, and management cites a long-term view from customers who plan into the next decade.
- **Cash generation is huge.** Free cash flow was $1.38 billion in one quarter, about 5.6% of today's market value annualized.
- **The technology roadmap is moving.** 40-terabyte drives are ramping and HAMR is due in volume in the first half of calendar 2027.
- **The stock has already fallen by half.** It traded near $556 in early July, so a lot of optimism has left the price.

## What could go wrong?

- **Margin mean reversion.** An operating margin of 49% is double the fiscal 2025 level. If it returns to 24%, as in my Bear case, the stock is worth about $80.68.
- **Competing capacity.** Toshiba announced plans to double hard disk output on October 2, and the stock fell about 7% that day.
- **AI spending.** Storage stocks fell 5% to 8% in the days after the November midterm elections amid reports of data center construction pauses, and Western Digital fell 13.5% on November 5. Cloud customers are almost 90% of revenue.
- **Customer concentration.** A small number of hyperscalers decide volumes and prices.
- **Execution.** A late HAMR qualification, which management expects with two hyperscalers in the first calendar quarter of 2027, would hand share to a rival.

## Management execution

Management beat its own first-quarter guidance on revenue, margin and earnings, simplified the capital structure by redeeming the convertible notes, raised the dividend and expanded the buyback. Against that, GAAP earnings are inflated by asset gains, capex is rising quickly, and the buyback has been executed at much higher prices than today. Management credibility scores 15/20, good.

## Final verdict: hold at $260.72, do not add

Western Digital is a well-run supplier in a market that has become unusually favorable, with margins that doubled in two years and a stock that has already fallen by about half. At $260.72 it is 14.9% above my Base value and 110% above the conservative buy limit. The cash flow is real and the balance sheet is clean, so there is no reason to sell on the numbers. There is also no margin of safety against a normal year for hard drives.

**Verdict: HOLD at $260.72. Triangulated Base value $226.97, downside 12.9%, Confidence C (15.0/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range $123.88 to $137.13, maximum buy price $123.88.**

The market is paying 11 times this year's earnings for a margin that was 24% two years ago. I am paying attention to the margin.
