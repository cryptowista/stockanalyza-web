What Netskope is

Netskope sells security and networking delivered from the cloud: it sits between employees and the web, cloud apps and AI tools, and decides what data may pass. The category is called secure service edge (SSE) or SASE, and Netskope competes in it with Zscaler and Palo Alto Networks, among others. Customers run their traffic through the company's own network, NewEdge, which has 80 regions. Sanjay Beri is chief executive. The company was founded as Skope, Inc., changed its name in November 2012, listed on Nasdaq in September 2025 and had 3,281 employees at January 31, 2026, before a June 2026 plan to cut about 5% of the workforce.

The numbers show a company that is growing quickly and is still paying for it. Revenue was $709 million in fiscal 2026 and the guide for fiscal 2027 is $888 million to $892 million, up about 26%. Annual recurring revenue (ARR) is $899 million, up 27%, with the Americas up 25%, EMEA up 37% and Asia-Pacific and Japan up 31%. Remaining performance obligations (RPO, contracted revenue not yet recognized) are about $1.4 billion, up 36%, with 53% due within 12 months. Dollar-based net retention is 114%, down from 118% a year ago, and 59% of customers use four or more products.

The cost side is heavy. On a GAAP basis, research and development alone is 46% of sales (Shashi Bellamkonda), and the GAAP operating loss was $89.8 million in the quarter, 41% of sales. Gross margin is improving as the network build ends: 74% GAAP and 77% non-GAAP against 72% and 75% a year earlier.

Reasons to own Netskope

  • Demand is real: ARR up 27% to $899 million, RPO up 36% and revenue growth of 29% in the quarter.
  • Margins are moving the right way: non-GAAP operating margin improved to -9% from -20% in a year and gross margin to 77%.
  • The network build is finished (80 regions), so capital needs should fall.
  • There is $1.07 billion of cash, and the guide implies positive free cash flow in the second half of fiscal 2027.
  • Customers are buying more: 59% use four or more products and EMEA grew 37%.

What could go wrong?

  • Valuation. The price is 5.3 times my Base and 1.6 times my Bull; it leaves no room for a slower path.
  • Stock pay. At 28% of sales it is larger than the whole free cash flow guide and was $139 million in six months. A reduction to 13.5% by fiscal 2032 is in my Base, and 2 points of sales higher in every year takes the Base to $3.45.
  • The notes. The holders can demand $1,058 million in 2028 and 2029 or convert into 24.6 million shares; the filing says additional financing may be needed.
  • Slowing growth. Net retention fell to 114% from 118%, ARR growth to 27% from 34%, and the guide implies growth near 22% in the fourth quarter.
  • Competition and AI. Larger security vendors sell similar services and AI traffic may add spending or shift it to others.
  • Control and reporting. The company is an emerging growth company, exempt from auditor attestation of internal controls, and its Class B shares carry different voting rights from Class A.
  • Data gaps. Cash taxes paid, customers above $100,000, a peer table and the reason for today's move were not available to me.

The three things that would make this verdict wrong

  1. Growth that stays at 25% or more and a margin that reaches 25%. The Bull case has 28% fading to 7% and a value of $16.34, still below the price.

  3. A market that keeps paying 60 times or more for owner cash flow. At 60 times the shares return -13.7% a year; the price needs about 205 times on my Base.

All three at once is my Bull case, and it is 37% below the price.
