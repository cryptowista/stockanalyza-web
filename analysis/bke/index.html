What Buckle is

Buckle runs 450 mall and outlet stores in 42 states, with two more opened in November, and sells denim, tops and accessories to teens and young adults, with a growing women's business. In the third quarter women were 52% of sales and men 48%, the company's own private label brands were 48% of sales (47.5% a year earlier), and online was about 16% of sales. Management does not give financial guidance, so there is no target to hold it to.

The business model is unusual for apparel: no debt, a modest capital budget, and a habit of paying out most of what it earns. In fiscal 2025, which ended January 31, 2026, sales were $1,298 million, operating margin 20.2% and net income $209.7 million. The company paid $3.35 a share on January 29, 2026, made up of a $0.35 regular dividend and a $3.00 special, and the regular dividend has stayed at $0.35 a quarter.

Dennis Nelson is president and chief executive and Tom Heacock is chief financial officer. The company has also announced the retirement of its executive vice president of stores and its senior vice president of sales, which matters because store execution is the whole business.

Balance sheet, dividends and shares

At October 31 Buckle had $278.7 million of cash, $25.7 million of short-term investments and $27.8 million of long-term investments, a total of $332.2 million, and no bank debt. It has an unused $25 million credit line. Operating lease liabilities are $434.2 million ($81.5 million current), with a weighted remaining term of 6.6 years at the August 1 filing. Equity is $522.6 million. I count $304.4 million of cash and short-term investments and set the long-term investments against the deferred compensation liability they fund. From that I hold back $120 million as working liquidity, my estimate, because inventory runs $40 million higher at the third quarter than at year end. That leaves $184 million of excess cash, or $3.58 a share, which I add to value once.

The dividend is the payout policy. The regular dividend costs about $72 million a year. Special dividends were $2.50 in January 2024, $2.50 in January 2025 and $3.00 in January 2026, and the board declared the last one on December 8. In fiscal 2025 the total paid was about $4.40 a share, roughly $224 million, which was 114% of my owner free cash flow of $196 million, and cash and investments still ended the year near where they started ($306.6 million against $318.8 million). A special dividend for January 2027 is possible and has not been announced, and I model none: the cash it would use is already counted in the value.

Shares outstanding are 51.51 million at October 31, up from 51.16 million a year earlier, so stock awards are adding about 0.7% to the share count a year and the company has not bought back stock this year. I use 51.5 million shares, which also includes unvested restricted stock, a conservative choice since the diluted weighted count was 51.0 million.

Primary valuation: ten years of owner free cash flow

The primary method discounts ten years of owner free cash flow at 10%, the framework default for an established company with no debt, and I test 9% and 11%. Base grows cash flow 2.0% a year for five years and 1.5% for the next five, reaching $210 million in 2031 and $226 million in 2036, with terminal growth of 1.0%. That is below inflation plus store growth: management says it will open about 8 stores in fiscal 2027, and I assume flat margins and comparable sales near zero to 2%. The terminal value is 11.2 times 2036 cash flow. I add the $184 million of excess cash once. The primary value is $47.35. Terminal value share: 40% of the value at a ten-year horizon, well under the 75% level at which I would check it.

Secondary valuation: five years of cash flow and an exit at 8 times operating profit

The cross-check takes the first five years of cash flow and values the business at the end of 2031 at a multiple of operating profit. Operating profit starts at $262 million (fiscal 2025's $261.4 million, rounded) and grows 1.5% a year to $282 million in 2031. At the Base exit of 8.0 times, the business is worth $2,258 million in 2031, and I add the excess cash. The secondary value is $44.23.

Why 8.0 times. Today's enterprise value, the market value minus cash and short-term investments of $304 million, is $1.92 billion, or 7.0 times trailing operating profit of $275 million. Owner free cash flow is about 73% of operating profit, and at a 10% required return a multiple of 8.0 times implies growth of 0.9% a year forever. That is a modest rate for a business that has a fixed store base and a fashion cycle. Bear uses 6.0 times, implying -1.9% a year, a shrinking business, and Bull uses 10.0 times, implying 2.6%. Exit value share: 62% of the secondary value comes from the exit at a five-year horizon, under the 85% level at which I would test it.

What is the market already pricing in?

Run backward on the Base case, $43.24 needs one of four things: cash flow and operating profit growing 1.1 percentage points a year less than my Base, an exit multiple of 6.5 times instead of 8.0, a discount rate of 10.8% instead of 10%, or a starting owner free cash flow of $173 million, 9% below mine. The market is therefore pricing roughly no growth, which is close to what the stock's operating multiple of 7.0 times says. That makes sense for a company whose comparable sales have just fallen to zero.

As a return, buying at $43.24 and holding the Base case for five years gives about 10.6% a year, close to the 10% discount rate, from cash flow and an exit at 8.0 times. The Bear case gives 0.4% and the Bull case 17.5%.

On earnings the shares trade at 10.0 times trailing diluted EPS. The growth-adjusted ratio (PEG) is not meaningful, because earnings growth is close to zero over three years, and I use it as a diagnostic only. Reasons to own BKE

  • No debt and real cash. $332 million of cash and investments, a $25 million unused credit line and no borrowings in the first half of fiscal 2026.
  • Cash returned. A $1.40 regular dividend and a special dividend each January ($2.50, $2.50 and $3.00), with the nine-month profit up 8.1%.
  • High returns on capital. Operating margin was 20.2% in fiscal 2025 and 18.6% in the first nine months of fiscal 2026, with a return on capital near 40% when lease liabilities are counted.
  • Growth where it is working. Women's sales were up 4% and kids up about 14% in the third quarter, and private label is 48% of sales.
  • A fair price. At about 10 times trailing earnings and an 8.5% owner cash flow yield, the stock prices roughly no growth.

What could go wrong?

  • Sales have stalled. Comparable sales were +0.6% in the quarter and -0.5% in October, after +8.3% a year earlier. Men's denim fell about 5%.
  • Fashion and the customer. A trend shift away from the styles that drove the last two years would reverse the margin gains. Comparable sales fell 8.0% in fiscal 2023 and 2.7% in fiscal 2024.
  • Costs are rising faster than sales. Selling, general and administrative expense rose 80 basis points of sales in the quarter, and occupancy costs rose 60 as new stores opened. Operating margin fell 100 basis points to 18.0%.
  • Inventory. Inventory is up 8.9% on sales up 2.6%.
  • Online is shrinking. Online sales fell 1.4% in the quarter, which matters for a company that depends on mall traffic.
  • Payout is not guaranteed. The special dividend is discretionary and total payout last year exceeded free cash flow.
  • Leases. $434 million of operating lease liabilities with a weighted remaining term of 6.6 years make the cost base fixed in a downturn.

Management execution

There is no guidance to grade, so I look at the results. Management took operating margin from 19.8% to 20.2% in fiscal 2025 and kept it near 18.6% over nine months in fiscal 2026 while opening 14 stores and remodeling 14 more. It has paid out most of its cash without issuing debt. Against that, comparable sales have slowed sharply, selling, general and administrative expense is rising faster than sales, a new aircraft was bought in the first half, and the heads of stores and sales have announced their retirement. Management credibility is 13/20: reliable on margin control and payout, weaker on forward visibility, which the company does not offer.

Green

Red

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. A turn in the fashion cycle that returns comparable sales to the declines of fiscal 2023 and 2024 and cuts operating margin toward 15%.
  2. Cost growth, in selling and occupancy expense, that continues to outrun sales and erases the margin gain of the last two years.
  3. A cut in the payout policy, which would remove the main reason many investors own the stock.

Final verdict: hold at $43.24, fairly valued

Buckle is a debt-free, cash-rich apparel retailer that pays out most of what it earns, and at $43.24 it trades 5.6% below my Base value of $45.79, a discount too small to count when comparable sales have fallen to zero. That is a reason to hold what you own, not to add. With Confidence C, the framework asks for a 42.5% margin of safety, which sets the buy price at $26.33.

VERDICT: HOLD (FAIRLY VALUED, WATCHLIST) at $43.24; Triangulated Base $45.79; upside +5.9%; required MOS 42.5%; maximum buy price $26.33. The main thesis breaker is a fashion-cycle turn that cuts comparable sales and margin.
