# Saab's sales grew 27.5% and customer prepayments released SEK 5.7 billion of working capital. The price now assumes 3.4 times 2025 sales by 2030

SAAB B, Nasdaq Stockholm. Published November 24, 2026. Price as of the Monday, November 23, 2026 regular session close (SEK 599.60). Values in Swedish kronor (SEK). Saab is a Swedish maker of fighter aircraft, radar and airborne surveillance systems, missiles and ground weapons, submarines and warship systems. Operating income (EBIT) is profit before interest and tax. EBITDA is operating income plus depreciation and amortisation. Free cash flow to the firm is the cash left after operating costs, taxes, capital spending and working capital, before paying lenders and owners. Working capital is the money tied up in inventory and receivables, net of customer prepayments. Operational cash flow is Saab's own cash flow measure before financing. Net liquidity is cash and investments minus borrowings, leases and pension liabilities. Backlog is signed orders not yet delivered. Cash conversion is operational cash flow as a share of operating income after tax.

## What Saab is

Saab reports five segments. Aeronautics builds the Gripen fighter. Dynamics makes missiles and ground combat weapons and earns the highest margin in the group. Surveillance makes radars and airborne early warning aircraft, including GlobalEye. Naval builds submarines and ship systems. Combitech is a technology consultancy.

Saab has 543,383,388 shares, of which 9,535,612 are unlisted A shares owned by Investor AB, each with ten votes. The remaining 533,847,776 are B shares with one vote each. Excluding treasury shares there were 539,048,711 shares outstanding on September 30, and the average diluted count in Q3 was 541.5 million, which is the number I use. In the fourth quarter of 2025 Saab sold its subsidiary TransponderTech and booked a SEK 336 million gain, so the SEK 8.1 billion of 2025 operating income, a 10.2% margin, was 9.8% on an adjusted basis.

## Primary valuation: discounted free cash flow to the firm

Sales. Saab gives no 2026 outlook. I assume fourth quarter sales of SEK 34 billion, which makes 2026 about SEK 99 billion, up 25%. Then I take SEK 114 billion in FY27, which is 15% growth and matches the roughly 20% a year for 2026 and 2027 that the company's own medium-term target implies from 2025, then SEK 130 billion, SEK 143 billion and SEK 154 billion, reaching SEK 163 billion in FY31. After that growth fades from 5% to 3%. The SEK 102.1 billion already scheduled for delivery in 2027 covers 90% of my FY27 figure.

Margin and cash. The operating margin is 11.0% in FY27 and 12.0% from FY29, against 10.2% for the first nine months of 2026. The company's target is for operating income to grow faster than sales, and my path does that. Taxes are 22% of operating income, close to the 21.6% effective rate in the first nine months. Depreciation is 4.2% to 4.5% of sales. Capital spending is 9.0% of sales in FY27, close to the nine-month level, and falls to 5.0% by FY32 as the new capacity is built. Working capital absorbs 8% of each extra krona of sales, because prepayments offset part of the inventory build. Q4 2026 free cash flow is SEK 2.5 billion, well below the SEK 6.5 billion of Q4 2025. I discount at 9%.

## Secondary valuation: exit multiple on FY31 EBITDA

The second method keeps the cash flows for five years and then values the group at 10 times FY31 EBITDA of SEK 26.9 billion. By FY31 growth has slowed to 6%, capital spending still takes 33% of EBITDA, and the return on new capital is 14%. A price of 10 times EBITDA against FY31 free cash flow of SEK 12.9 billion is equivalent to growing at about 4.0% a year forever at a 9% discount rate, which is above my 2% terminal growth, so it is not a harsh multiple. The exit value is 84% of the five year enterprise value, just under the 85% level that needs close checking, because the five-year cash flows are small while capital spending is high. The result is **SEK 397**.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give SEK 336 and SEK 397, a divergence of 16.6%, under the 20% warning level. Both use the same sales, margins, capital spending and net cash bridge, so they do not test those inputs against each other. ## Reasons to own

- The backlog is SEK 364.2 billion, 4.6 times 2025 sales, and SEK 102.1 billion is scheduled for delivery in 2027.
- Sales grew 27.5% organically in nine months and operating income grew 39%, faster than sales, which is the group's own target.
- Saab has net liquidity of SEK 8.7 billion, a SEK 6.0 billion unused credit line and a small net pension asset.
- Order bookings were SEK 152.5 billion in nine months, so the growth is funded by orders already signed.
- The price is 20% below the 52-week high of SEK 748.80.

## What could go wrong?

- Prepayments unwind. Contract liabilities are SEK 46.6 billion, and the nine-month free cash flow came from a SEK 5.7 billion working capital inflow. As projects deliver, that cash flow reverses.
- Margins. Aeronautics earned 6.3% and Naval 6.0% over nine months, and these are the segments with the largest delivery volumes.
- Capacity. Saab says it is increasing investments to expand capacity, and tangible capital spending was SEK 5.9 billion in nine months, up 47%.
- Peace talks. A ceasefire in Ukraine would test how much of the order book and the sentiment around European defence shares is tied to the war.
- Customer concentration. Large single orders, such as the SEK 24.6 billion Gripen E order for Ukraine, drive the quarterly order figures, and a delay in one contract moves the whole year.
- Valuation. The price needs sales of SEK 268 billion in 2030 or margins near 20%, and there is little room for a miss.

## Management execution

Management has delivered growth and margin. Organic growth was 25.6% in 2025 and 27.5% in the first nine months of 2026, against a medium-term target of 22% a year, which it raised from 18%. Operating income has grown faster than sales, and cash conversion was 68% in 2025. It has also chosen not to give an outlook for 2026, which takes away the main check on its own numbers. For Q3 it reported significantly increased cash flow, but a large part of it came from customer prepayments, not from earnings.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Sales run far ahead of my path. If out-year sales are 74% above my Base, at about SEK 268 billion in 2030, the value rises to today's price. With sales 15% higher alone, my Base becomes SEK 409.
2. Margins double. If margins settle near 20% instead of 12%, the Base value is the price. With margins 2 points higher, the Base is SEK 419.
3. The market keeps paying a high multiple. At an exit multiple of about 29 times EBITDA my Base cash flows reach the price, against the 10 times I use and the 12 times that gives SEK 386.

## Final verdict: reduce at SEK 599.60

Saab is a real business with a SEK 364 billion backlog, net cash and sales growing at 27.5%. At SEK 599.60 the market is paying for about 3.4 times 2025 sales by 2030, and even my Bull case does not reach that price on a discounted cash flow basis. I would not buy here, and I would reduce existing holdings, because the margin of safety is negative against every value I can build.

**Verdict: REDUCE at SEK 599.60. Triangulated Base value SEK 360, downside -39.9%, Confidence C (16.25/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by method SEK 193 to SEK 228, maximum buy price SEK 207.**
