Yum China's same-store transactions rose 5% in the second quarter, the 14th straight quarter of growth, and its same-store sales rose 1%. The gap means the average check fell: customers came more often and spent less each time. Operating profit still grew 14%, and the company has promised to return about 10% of its market value to shareholders this year. The third quarter report arrives on November 3.

At $45.78 on October 30, 2026, Yum China trades 1.9% above my weighted Base of $44.94. The Bear value of $28.86 is 37.0% below the price and the Bull value of $60.04 is 31.1% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. A required margin of safety of 30% gives a buy range of $31.38 to $31.53. The verdict is Hold, and no new money at this price.

Valuation route: a restaurant operator valued on the free cash flow it returns to shareholders over five years, with the Pizza Hut brand purchase included, and cross-checked on 2027 earnings. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What Yum China is

Yum China runs KFC and Pizza Hut in mainland China. At June 30, 2026 it had 19,297 stores: 13,789 KFC and 4,549 Pizza Hut, plus smaller formats. KFC is 17% franchised and Pizza Hut 11%. Delivery is now 54% of company sales, against 45% a year earlier.

Yum China pays Yum! Brands a license fee of 3% of sales. On August 7 it completed the purchase of the Pizza Hut brand in mainland China for $1.2 billion, which ends that fee for Pizza Hut. Yum! Brands sold Pizza Hut outside mainland China separately, for about $1.5 billion to another buyer.

## What the cash flow is made of

- **Free cash flow is already after leases.** In the trailing twelve months to June it was $940 million: operating cash flow of $1,578 million less capital spending of $638 million. US accounting puts operating lease payments inside operating cash flow.
- **Shareholder returns exceed free cash flow today.** The company repurchased $1,306 million of shares and paid $376 million of dividends over the same twelve months, $1,682 million in total against $940 million of free cash flow. The balance came from its cash pile, which fell from $2.6 billion in 2023 to $1.4 billion.
- **The promise changes in 2027.** The company plans to return about 100% of free cash flow after distributions to minority holders, which it expects to be $900 million to over $1 billion a year in 2027 and 2028.
- **Profit is not the problem.** Net income was $929 million in 2025 and $975 million trailing, with earnings per share of $2.51 and $2.72.

## Primary valuation: five-year free cash flow to shareholders

I project free cash flow after minority holders (3% haircut, my estimate) for 2027 to 2031, treat it as distributed to shareholders, add an exit value of 14 times 2031 free cash flow and add net cash, all discounted at 10%. The Base value is **$44.83**.

- **Discount rate:** 10%, one point above my usual 9%, for China consumer and regulatory risk.
- **Terminal share:** the exit value is about 70% of the Base value over a five-year horizon, because I assume the cash is distributed along the way and not retained.

## Growth diagnostic

Revenue grew 13% in the quarter and system sales 6% excluding currency, and the company says a favorable currency effect helped earnings. The growth is structural in store count (560 net new stores, a record, with 41% opened by franchisees) and cyclical in spending per visit. Same-store sales rose 1% while transactions rose 5%, which means the average check fell. I classify the growth as moderate and volume-led.

## Reasons to own Yum China

- Operating profit grew 14% in the second quarter and earnings per share 21%, with a record 560 net new stores.
- The Pizza Hut purchase saves a 3% fee and adds about 2.8 points to that brand's margin, with mid-single-digit earnings accretion promised for 2027 and 2028.
- A free cash flow yield of 6.0% and a 9.6% targeted capital return yield are high for a company still adding 1,900 stores a year.
- Transactions have grown for 14 quarters in a row.
- The balance sheet has net cash even after paying $1.2 billion for the brand.

## What could go wrong?

- Consumer spending in China. Same-store sales rose only 1% and the average check is falling.
- Delivery economics. Delivery is 54% of company sales and rider costs rise with it; the subsidy war cost about 140 basis points of margin last year.
- Tough comparisons. Management said the second half faces last year's platform subsidies, so the third quarter due November 3 may be weaker.
- KFC license. KFC remains licensed from Yum! Brands, and KFC is the larger part of the business, with operating profit of $332 million against $51 million for Pizza Hut.
- Capital returns above free cash flow. Returns of $1.7 billion against $940 million of free cash flow have drained cash to $1.4 billion.
- Regulation and geopolitics. The shares are listed in New York and Hong Kong and the business is entirely in mainland China.

## Management execution

- Store openings are accelerating: 560 in the quarter, above the 2026 target pace, and 2027 and 2028 targets were raised to more than 800 a year.
- The company closed the Pizza Hut purchase quickly, within two months of announcing it on June 16.
- It disclosed a clear plan for returns: $1.5 billion in 2026 and about 100% of free cash flow from 2027.
- The Pizza Hut earnings accretion figures come from management and, as summarized, leave out some costs.

### Yellow

2. Delivery economics reduce margins by more than 2 points.
