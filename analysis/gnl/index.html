What Global Net Lease is

Global Net Lease owns about 800 properties, roughly 40 million square feet, and leases them to single tenants on long contracts where the tenant pays most property costs. On June 30, 2026 the portfolio was 47% industrial, 28% retail and 25% office by rent, 74% in the United States and Canada and 26% in Europe, 97% leased with a weighted average remaining lease term of 5.7 years. Of its rent, 63% comes from investment grade or implied investment grade tenants, but only 38.0% is actually rated; the rest relies on a parent's rating.

The company has changed shape three times: the 2023 merger and internalization, the 2025 sale of its multi-tenant portfolio, and the acquisition of Modiv Industrial, which closed on August 12, 2026. Modiv adds about $535 million of mostly industrial property with long leases and raises industrial exposure to about 50% of rent and the average lease term to 6.6 years. Those changes simplified the company and reduced debt, but selling rent-producing assets to repay debt is not organic growth, and the per-share numbers show it: AFFO per share was $1.40 in 2023, $1.32 in 2024 and $0.99 in 2025. Common stock sits behind four series of preferred shares with a liquidation claim of $600.6 million.

Which cash to value

But it does not subtract property capital spending or leasing costs, and it adds back stock pay and some financing costs. I use hard AFFO: common AFFO after property capital spending, leasing cash, the economic cost of stock pay and a small reserve for recurring deal and financing costs. Preferred dividends are already out of common AFFO and are not subtracted again.

Reasons to own

  • Contracted rent. Long leases, 97% occupancy and a +5.6% renewal spread support the existing rent base.
  • Better duration. Modiv's 15 year average lease term lifts the portfolio average to 6.6 years and raises industrial exposure to about 50%.
  • Execution on the balance sheet. Debt reduction, simplification and investment grade progress are real, and 2024 and 2025 results met or beat initial guidance.
  • A lower price could pay. A 10% yield bought at a real discount to a conservative value, with a verified hard cash budget, would be a different decision.

Main risks

  • Refinancing. Cheap old coupons reset over several years, and the average maturity was only 2.7 years in June. The 92% fixed share was measured before the revolver draw for Modiv.
  • Renewal costs. Leases covering 34.7% of rent expire from 2027 to 2029. Tenant improvements, commissions and downtime can make real coverage weaker than AFFO shows.
  • Senior claims. Debt, preferred stock and overhead leave common equity sensitive to small changes in capitalization rates: 50 bp moves the NAV by about $1.4 to $1.6 per share.
  • Portfolio change hides per-share results. Sales, acquisitions, stock awards and operating partnership units can improve leverage without improving the cash per share.

Final verdict: reduce at $7.31

Global Net Lease has improved its portfolio and balance sheet, and a 10.4% yield is attractive on paper. But at $7.31 you pay more than my Base value for a dividend that exceeds recurring cash after reinvestment, with a refinancing wall still ahead and a preferred stack in front of the common shares.

Verdict: REDUCE at $7.31, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $6.31, downside 13.7%, Confidence C (13/25), required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $3.79, one-input range $3.40 to $4.20.

For current holders this means reducing, not panicking: the Bull case at $10.64 is real, and I show what it requires. For new money it means waiting for a much lower price or a verified improvement in hard cash.
