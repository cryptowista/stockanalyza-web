What Tenable is

Tenable sells software that finds and ranks the weak points in a company's computers, cloud accounts and identities, the business it now calls exposure management. It began with the Nessus vulnerability scanner and now sells the Tenable One platform on subscription. Recurring revenue is 95% of the total.

Tenable One was a record 59% of new business in the third quarter, up from 50% in the second quarter and 39% a year earlier, and the platform has more than 5,000 customers. Net dollar expansion, which measures how much more existing customers spend, rose to 107% from 106%, and current calculated billings grew 9% (Mitrade summary of the call). These are the numbers behind the story that customers are moving to the platform.

The sales channel is concentrated. The 10-Q shows that channel partners handle 95% of revenue and that one distributor alone was 29% of third-quarter revenue and 23% of accounts receivable. U.S. customers are 52% of revenue. Remaining performance obligations, the contracted revenue not yet recognized, were $1,063.0 million, of which $747.2 million falls due within twelve months, roughly 2.7 quarters of sales.

Dividend and capital return

Tenable pays no dividend. It spends its cash on buybacks: $223.8 million in the third quarter alone, $170.5 million of it financed by the new notes, and in February 2026 the board added $150 million to the authorization (fourth-quarter release). Buying back stock at about $33 with borrowed money was a better trade than the same purchase at $46.75, and the notes' conversion price of $44.84 means that part of the benefit goes to the note holders if the stock stays above it.

Reasons to own Tenable

  • Cash generation is steady: unlevered free cash flow of $277.0 million in 2025 and a guide of $289.0 million to $295.0 million for 2026.
  • Margins are widening: the non-GAAP operating margin was 27.1% in the third quarter against 23.3% a year earlier, and management has beaten every guided metric and raised the full-year outlook in each report this year.
  • Customers are moving to the platform: Tenable One was 59% of new business, net dollar expansion is 107% and has risen two quarters in a row.
  • Revenue is 95% recurring, and remaining performance obligations of $1,063.0 million are 3.8 quarters of sales.
  • The company repurchases shares: 6.8 million in the third quarter, and the guided diluted count falls to 114.0 million.

What could go wrong?

  • Stock pay. At 17.5% of sales it is 70% of non-GAAP operating profit. Stock pay 2 points of sales higher in every year takes the Base to $17.87.
  • Slow growth. Sales have grown between 8.6% and 11% for five quarters, and the fourth-quarter guide is flat on the third quarter.
  • Distributor concentration. One distributor was 29% of third-quarter revenue and 23% of receivables, and channel partners handle 95% of revenue.
  • Financing. The $800 million of 0.25% notes mature in September 2031 and are freely convertible from June 2031; below $44.84 they are a debt to repay or refinance, and above it they dilute, partly offset by a capped call whose cap I could not find. The debt now exceeds cash and investments by $305 million.
  • Falling deferred revenue. Deferred revenue was $863.4 million at September 30, 4.0% below the end of 2025, which does not fit an upbeat billing story.
  • Margin pressure. The fourth-quarter guide implies a non-GAAP margin of 24.2% to 25.0% against 27.1%; the cause was not in what I read.
  • Technology change. Management said it was too early to quantify the commercial impact of the PHP to Shell vulnerability, and the company bets on AI-driven products (Hexa AI) to speed up growth.

Management execution

Tenable is led by co-CEO Steve Vintz, and Matthew Brown became chief financial officer in 2025. Management has beaten its guidance in every quarter of 2026 and raised the full-year outlook each time, and non-GAAP margin is up 380 basis points in a year. The offsets are the size of stock pay, a debt-funded repurchase, a leadership structure with two chief executives and a growth rate that has not accelerated. Assessment: 13/20, good on guidance and margin and weaker on the cost of stock pay. The next test is whether sales growth moves above 10% in the fourth quarter and the 2027 guide.

Today none of my red triggers is hit. Two readings are yellow: falling deferred revenue and the lower fourth-quarter margin guide. Margin, cash and net expansion are green. That is why the verdict rests on price and not on a broken business.

The three most important thesis breakers

  2. Stock pay that does not fall. At 2 points of sales higher in every year the Base is $17.87; with a lower margin it is $17.87.
  3. A fall of the multiple. At 40 times owner free cash flow in 2031 the shares return 8.0% a year; at 16 times, -7% a year.
