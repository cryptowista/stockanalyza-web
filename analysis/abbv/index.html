Humira is now 3.9% of AbbVie's revenue and Skyrizi plus Rinvoq are 48.7%, and the price already pays for it

ABBV, New York Stock Exchange. Published November 22, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close ($286.20). AbbVie is a US pharmaceutical company best known for Humira, and now for Skyrizi and Rinvoq, its two main immunology drugs. Adjusted EPS is earnings per share excluding items such as amortization of acquired products, the company's own measure. IPR&D is acquired in-process research and development, what AbbVie pays to buy drugs still in development. FCF is free cash flow, operating cash flow less capital spending. A basis for the Medicare price negotiation is a US law that lets Medicare set prices for selected drugs.

The price is 26.9% above my Base value of $225.59, outside the 15% band where I call a stock fairly valued, and only 2% below my Bull value of $292.00. It is 117% above my Bear value of $132.08. AbbVie is a strong company, but the shares already pay for the growth. My maximum buy price is $118.44.

What AbbVie is

AbbVie sells drugs for immune diseases, brain disorders, cancer and aesthetics. In the third quarter immunology was $9,165 million of revenue, neuroscience $3,389 million, oncology $1,696 million and aesthetics $1,176 million. Skyrizi and Rinvoq together are 48.7% of revenue, which is a concentration risk as well as a strength.

Balance sheet, cash flow and share count

The company generated about $14 billion of free cash flow in the first nine months and held $10.8 billion of cash at September 30, 2026. The release did not include a balance sheet. At June 30 debt was about $70.9 billion ($62.5 billion long-term and $8.3 billion due within a year), according to a third-party table of the filing, and I did not find the September 30 figure. In the third quarter AbbVie completed the acquisition of Apogee Therapeutics, which it valued at about $10.9 billion of equity, so debt probably rose. I assume debt of $75 billion and net debt of about $64 billion, my estimate, which is a data gap. A summary of the second quarter 10-Q also lists contingent consideration liabilities of $27.5 billion, which I could not check.

Weighted diluted shares were 1,772 million in the quarter and 1,767 million shares were outstanding on July 27, 2026, so the share count is stable. The quarterly dividend rises 5.2% from $1.73 to $1.82 with the payment on February 16, 2027 to holders of record on January 15, 2027.

Primary valuation: discounted owner free cash flow

I start with the 2026 adjusted EPS guidance midpoint of $13.97 and add back the $0.69 of IPR&D expense, which gives $14.66. I grow that 6% for 2027 and deduct a normalized $0.70 for IPR&D, because the company buys drugs every year, which gives FY27 adjusted EPS of $14.90. Free cash flow was about 78% of adjusted earnings over nine months ($14 billion against $17.9 billion, my arithmetic), so I use $11.62 a share.

I then discount 10 years of that cash flow per share, which is after interest, growing 5% a year for five years and 3% for five years with a terminal growth of 2.0%, at a cost of equity of 8.5%, my estimate for a large pharmaceutical company with leverage. Growth slows because Humira and Imbruvica keep falling, Vraylar's price is negotiated from 2027, and Skyrizi and Rinvoq will eventually face lower-priced competition, although I did not verify their patent dates. The terminal value is 56% of the total over 10 years, below the 75% level that needs close checking. The model gives $207.12.

Secondary valuation: multiple of normalized earnings

The second method applies 17 times to the same $14.90 of adjusted EPS. My judgment is that mature pharmaceutical earnings are worth about 12 to 16 times, and I add one turn above that for the growth of Skyrizi and Rinvoq and the dividend growth of 5% a year. The multiple is my judgment and not company guidance. The result is $253.30.

Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $207.12 and $253.30, a divergence of 20.1%, just above the 20% warning level. The gap comes from the multiple and not from the earnings: the discounted model values the shares at 13.9 times FY27 EPS, while the second method uses 17 times. The 17 times assumes growth lasts longer than the 5% and 3% in my model. The methods are not independent because both use the same FY27 earnings. I weight the discounted cash flow 60% and the multiple 40%, because the first states the growth and the cost of equity. The weighted value is $225.59. Because the divergence is above 20%, I raised the margin of safety by 5 points. The table converts each method into the other's terms.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $286.20 the equity is worth about $507 billion. That is 19.2 times my FY27 adjusted EPS and 20.5 times the 2026 guidance midpoint, with an FCF yield of 4.1% and a dividend yield of 2.5%. Holding everything else at Base, the price requires a cost of equity of 6.7% instead of 8.5%, or growth of 13.1% a year for five years instead of 5%. So the market expects growth to stay well above my Base for years, and I think that is optimistic given the Humira, Imbruvica and Vraylar headwinds.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is grade C because FY27 EPS, cash conversion and growth are my estimates, the September 30 debt and the contingent consideration are not verified, and the two methods differ by more than 20%. The margin of safety is 42.5% for grade C plus 5 points for the divergence, or 47.5%. Leverage and the patent schedule are already in the cost of equity and the growth path, so I add nothing more. The maximum buy price is $225.59 times 0.525, or $118.44, with an entry range of $108.74 to $132.98 by method. The price is 142% above it. A cost of equity of 8.0% would raise the maximum buy price to $124.04, still far below the price.

Dividend and payout

The dividend is $1.82 a quarter from February 2027, or $7.28 a year, a yield of 2.5%, after a 5.2% increase. The payout is about 63% of my FY27 free cash flow per share, or about $12.9 billion a year at the new rate (my arithmetic). The first nine months of free cash flow of $14 billion covered the dividend of about $9.2 billion at the old rate, and left about $4.8 billion for the Apogee purchase and other deals (my arithmetic). The dividend is safe in Base, and what limits capital return is the debt and the pace of acquisitions.

Reasons to own

  • Skyrizi and Rinvoq grew 22.7% and 23.9% in the quarter and are 48.7% of revenue, and Humira is only 3.9%.
  • The adjusted operating margin is 48.7%, free cash flow was about $14 billion in nine months, and 2026 guidance was raised.
  • The dividend is raised by 5.2% and is about 60% of my FY27 free cash flow.
  • Neuroscience grew 19.4%, and the company completed the Apogee deal for its pipeline in immunology.

What could go wrong?

  • Concentration. Skyrizi and Rinvoq are 48.7% of revenue, and slower growth lowers the Base to $211.27 at 2% growth in the first five years.
  • Medicare price negotiation. Imbruvica fell 26.1% and Vraylar, a product of $4.2 billion this year, faces its price from 2027.
  • Leverage. I estimate net debt near $64 billion, and contingent consideration of $27.5 billion would lower the Base by $15.52 a share if it is a claim on top of the debt.
  • Aesthetics. Juvederm fell 11.9% and the segment fell 1.4%.
  • Acquisitions. Apogee cost about $10.9 billion and the company says future deals will be bolt-ons, but IPR&D charges recur.

Management execution

Management beat its own guidance by $0.07 in the third quarter, raised the full-year range, completed Apogee in the same quarter, and raised the dividend. Humira is now 3.9% of revenue and total revenue still grew 10.3% in the quarter. The execution risk is that nearly half of revenue now sits in two drugs.
