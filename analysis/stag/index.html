STAG Industrial grew cash NOI 10.1% in the third quarter, but Core FFO per share grew 1.5%

STAG, New York Stock Exchange. Published November 22, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close ($35.98). STAG Industrial is a real estate investment trust, or REIT, that owns single-tenant industrial buildings such as warehouses and distribution centers. NOI is net operating income, rent less building operating costs. Core FFO is funds from operations adjusted for one-time items, the company's main earnings measure. CAD is cash available for distribution, which is close to the AFFO measure many REITs report; this company did not report AFFO in its release. NAV is net asset value, the estimated market value of the properties less debt.

At $35.98 the stock sits 0.5% above my Base value of $35.80, so I see it as fairly valued, with no margin of safety for new money.

My Base value is $35.80. The Bear value of $27.17 is 24% below the price and the Bull value of $40.68 is 13% above it. What STAG Industrial is

STAG owns single-tenant industrial buildings across the United States. At September 30, 2026 the company showed $7.11 billion of rental property after depreciation, including $853 million of land, and total assets of $7.50 billion. Occupancy was 95.9% for the whole portfolio and 96.2% for the operating portfolio, and the same store pool covers 89.8% of the square footage. The release did not give a building count, total square footage or annual base rent, so I cannot describe the tenant base in detail.

Leasing was strong in the quarter. The company commenced 30 leases covering 3.6 million square feet with a cash rent change of +23.8%, which is +31.1% on new leases and +21.4% on renewals, and it kept 90.3% of the 2.9 million square feet that expired. Over nine months retention was 75.8% and the cash rent change was +21.2%. Rent changes this large reflect leases signed years ago at lower rents, so they do not mean the whole portfolio rents at that level.

Primary valuation: net asset value

The company reported run rate Cash NOI of $177.7 million for the quarter, which is $710.8 million a year. I deduct corporate costs of $56 million, the top of the $53 million to $56 million G&A range the company gave for 2026, by capitalizing them at the same rate as the properties. That leaves $654.8 million, and at a 6.5% cap rate the real estate is worth $10.07 billion. I subtract the $3.41 billion of debt on the balance sheet, which is lower than face value, and divide by 196.5 million shares and units. The model gives $33.93. I include no cash, forward equity or development value, because I could not verify them.

The 6.5% cap rate is my judgment: it sits between 6.3% paid on nine months of purchases and the 6.9% paid in the third quarter, and below the 8% on the older properties sold. A 7.0% cap rate gives $30.27 and 6.0% gives $38.20, so this method moves by about $4 a share for half a point of cap rate.

Secondary valuation: multiple of cash available for distribution

The company's 2026 Core FFO guidance is $2.64 to $2.66 a share, raised from $2.60 to $2.64 in February. For FY27 I assume Core FFO of $2.72, about 2.6% growth, because rent increases and acquisitions are offset by higher interest costs and share issuance. Cash available for distribution was 83% of Core FFO over nine months ($320.1 million against $384.1 million, my arithmetic), so I use $2.27 of CAD a share. I apply 17 times, which is a CAD yield of 5.9%. With a payout of about two thirds and growth of around 3%, a long-term holder would earn about 9%, which I consider a fair cost of equity for an industrial REIT. The multiple is my judgment and not company guidance. The result is $38.59.

Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $33.93 and $38.59, a divergence of 12.8%, below the 20% warning level. They are not fully independent: both depend on the same rent growth and occupancy, but the first rests on the cap rate and the second on cash flow after capital spending. I weight the NAV 60% and the CAD multiple 40%, because property values are what the company itself transacts at. The weighted value is $35.80. The table converts each method into the other's terms.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $35.98 the equity is worth about $7.07 billion, or $7.07 billion with operating partnership units counted. Adding the debt gives $10.48 billion for the properties. My Base NOI after corporate costs of $654.8 million is a 6.25% yield on that, and before corporate costs it is 6.78%. That is close to the 6.3% to 6.9% at which STAG buys. The price also equals 15.9 times my FY27 CAD, a CAD yield of 6.3%, with the dividend yielding 4.3% and about 68% of FY27 CAD paid out. The market is pricing the portfolio at roughly what STAG pays for new buildings.

Reasons to own

  • Same Store Cash NOI grew 3.5% in the quarter and 3.9% over nine months, and the company raised its full-year guidance.
  • The cash rent change on new and renewed leases was +23.8%, and retention was 90.3% in the quarter.
  • Leverage is 5.1 times, liquidity is $546.0 million and the rating is BBB, stable.
  • The dividend is covered by about 70% of CAD, and the buyback authorization gives the board a second use for cash.

What could go wrong?

  • Cap rates. Each half point on the cap rate moves the NAV value by about $4 a share, and at 7.0% the Base falls to $33.60.
  • Dilution. Core FFO per share grew 1.5% against 4.1% for total Core FFO, and issuing shares below the $39 forward price would cost more.
  • Interest costs. Interest expense is up 23% and the 2027 step-up on the term loan adds about $0.02 a share.
  • Fading rent gains. A cash rent change of +23.8% depends on old leases that expire, and the company assumed market rent growth of only 0% to 2% at the start of the year.
  • Occupancy. The portfolio is 95.9% occupied against about 98% at the start of the year.

Management execution

Management raised its Core FFO guidance from $2.60 to $2.64 to $2.64 to $2.66 and its same store guidance, bought $475.8 million of buildings over nine months at a 6.3% cash cap rate, and sold $121.1 million. Guidance of $2.64 to $2.66 with nine-month Core FFO of $1.96 implies a fourth quarter of $0.68 to $0.70 (my arithmetic), above the $0.66 of the third quarter. The weaker point is the per-share result, since overhead, interest and share count grew faster than FFO per share.
