Through September, Novo Nordisk generated DKK 83.8 billion of free cash flow, about $12.6 billion. For the full year it guides to DKK 60 billion to 70 billion. By my subtraction, that implies free cash flow of minus DKK 14 billion to 24 billion in the fourth quarter. The release says the nine-month figure was helped by the timing of US rebate and tax payments, and by lower capital spending. A screener that annualizes the nine months shows a cash flow yield of about 9%; the guidance says 5.4%.

My Base value is $35.50 an ADR, the Bear value $20.13 and the Bull value $46.68. The price of $40.65 sits between the Base and the Bull. The quarter itself was good: adjusted operating profit rose 27% at constant exchange rates and the company raised its 2026 outlook for the third time. That outlook is still a range of -2% to +2% for both sales and adjusted operating profit, so the business is guided to stand still while the price list in its biggest market is cut. I could not find a company-specific reason for today's 1.19% decline.

Valuation route: large pharmaceutical company, valued with an existing-product cash flow model for 2027 to 2036 that includes US price cuts and the December 2032 US semaglutide patent expiry, cross-checked with a justified forward earnings multiple. The pipeline is not valued separately, because the growth path already assumes some of it succeeds. Danish kroner cash flows are discounted at 8.5% and converted at 6.66 kroner per dollar.

What Novo Nordisk is

Novo Nordisk makes semaglutide, the molecule in Ozempic, Wegovy and their pill versions, plus insulin and rare disease drugs. In the first nine months of 2026, Ozempic, Wegovy and the Rybelsus and Ozempic pills produced DKK 172.6 billion of the DKK 227.7 billion of adjusted sales, or 76%. The company is Danish, reports in kroner and trades in New York as an ADR that equals one B share.

Two things changed in 2026. First, the US price: Novo struck a pricing deal with the White House in November 2025, and from January 1, 2027 the list price of Wegovy and Ozempic falls to $675 a month, about 50% for Wegovy and 35% for Ozempic. Second, the product: the Wegovy pill launched in January and sold DKK 10.9 billion in nine months, DKK 5.4 billion in the third quarter. US injectable Wegovy sales fell 13% in the quarter at constant rates while combined US Wegovy sales rose 21%, so the pill is replacing injections as much as it is adding patients. At its investor day, the company said self-pay channels are now 50% of the Wegovy franchise, and about 90% of Wegovy pill demand is paid for in cash, which tells you how far pricing has moved from the insurance rebate model.

Free cash flow and what it leaves out

Novo's free cash flow is cash from operations (DKK 121.0 billion for nine months) less capital spending on property, plant and equipment (DKK 37.2 billion), which gives the DKK 83.8 billion. The company attributes the strength to phasing of operating expenses, the timing of US rebate and tax payments and lower capital spending. Those are payments postponed, not avoided, so I do not extrapolate the nine months. I use the full-year guide as the 2026 level and check it from the top down.

Primary valuation: free cash flow through the patent expiry

I project sales, operating margin and reinvestment for ten years. The Base has sales falling 1% in 2027 as the US list price cuts and net price pressure outweigh higher volume, then growing 1% to 4% a year as the pill, CagriSema (the next obesity injection, with a US launch targeted for early 2027 if approved) and other launches add volume. Sales are flat in 2032 and fall 6% in 2033, after the US semaglutide compound patent expires on December 5, 2032 per DelveInsight, and another 2% in 2034. The operating margin goes from 40.7% in 2026 to 38% in 2027 and 37.5% in 2028, back to 39% by 2030, down to 34% in 2034 after the patent expiry, and recovers to 36% by 2036. Free cash flow is taxed at 22%, reinvestment shrinks as the capacity build ends, and the value after 2036 grows at 1.5% with a 15% return on new investment. Cash flows are discounted at 8.5% from the valuation date of November 12, 2026.

Dividend and buyback

The ADR paid DKK 11.70 a share over twelve months, a 4.3% yield before withholding tax, and the company has restarted its buyback after suspending it in 2025. The company's dividend page shows DKK 11.70 for 2025 (DKK 3.75 interim and DKK 7.95 final), and the interim payment held at DKK 3.75 in 2026. Total cash returns are about the size of free cash flow, so the dividend is covered only while free cash flow holds near DKK 65 billion. In my Base the free cash flow is DKK 66 billion in 2027 and DKK 89 billion in 2030, so the current payout is affordable in the Base. In my Bear it is DKK 60 billion in 2027 and DKK 45 billion in 2033, which would not cover dividends and buybacks together.

Reasons to own Novo Nordisk

  • An adjusted gross margin above 80%, an adjusted operating margin of 43% over nine months and a return on invested capital I estimate at about 31% for 2026.
  • The Wegovy pill: DKK 5.4 billion in its third quarter on sale, eight markets launched, and the company says it has more than 50% of the branded US obesity market on new patient starts. Eli Lilly's competing pill sold $184 million in the quarter.
  • A record of beating its lowered bar: three guidance raises in 2026, sales and distribution costs down 13%, and a free cash flow guide raised from DKK 45-55 billion to DKK 60-70 billion.
  • Low debt for the size of the business, with net debt of about 8% of market value.
  • Semaglutide keeps US patent protection until December 2032 and the company has several next-generation assets, including CagriSema and oral zenagamtide, entering the final stage of testing.

What could go wrong?

  • US pricing: the list price cut on January 1, 2027, the White House pricing agreement, reduced Medicaid coverage and the shift to cash-pay all lower realized prices. The company says the 2027 cash flow will feel the cut, and I take 2027 sales down 1% and operating profit down about 8%.
  • Competition: Eli Lilly's tirzepatide has taken the lead in injectable obesity and diabetes sales, and its pill is now on the market. CagriSema delivered 20.2% weight loss against 23.6% for tirzepatide in a head-to-head obesity trial, so it is not clearly ahead of the competition.
  • The 2032 patent expiry: the US compound patent runs to December 2032, with possible pediatric extension into 2033. Outside the US, patent expiries in markets including Brazil, Canada and China are already in the 2026 numbers, with lower prices as the first effect.
  • Pipeline disappointments: in 2026 the company wrote off ziltivekimab (DKK 4.6 billion), monlunabant (DKK 4.0 billion) and other assets, DKK 15 billion in nine months.
  • Cash flow timing: if the fourth quarter outflow is larger than the guide implies, net debt is higher than I assume. DKK 10 billion is worth $0.34 an ADR.
  • Control and currency: Novo Holdings, owned by the Novo Nordisk Foundation, holds 28.1% of the shares and 77.3% of the votes. The dollar value of an ADR also moves with the krone: at 7.10 kroner per dollar the Base DCF would be $31.11.
  • Valuation of my own numbers: the Base is a top-down path with estimated 2026 profit, depreciation and 2027 onward. It is not a product-by-product forecast.

Management execution

The record is mixed. In 2025 the company cut its guidance several times, changed CEO and booked heavy restructuring costs. In 2026 it has raised the outlook three times, cut sales and distribution costs by 13%, launched the pill ahead of Lilly and restarted the buyback. The same year brought a failed heart drug trial, DKK 15 billion of impairments on pipeline assets and manufacturing, and a US price reset negotiated with the government. Capital spending of about DKK 50 billion is the largest bet on volume I can see. Governance is controlled by a foundation, and proxy adviser ISS has questioned the dual role of the chair, who also leads the Foundation.
