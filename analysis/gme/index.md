Valuation route: a retailer with a very large pile of financial assets, valued in two parts. Part one is the store business on owner free cash flow: EBITDA less stock-based pay, a full cash tax rate and capital spending, with interest income left out. Part two is cash, marketable securities, the bitcoin receivable and the eBay shares at market value less tax on the gain, minus the convertible notes at face value. Primary method: a ten-year path with a terminal value at 10%. Cross-check: a multiple of fiscal 2031 owner free cash flow. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What GameStop is today

GameStop is a video game and collectibles retailer with about 1,600 stores in the United States (a figure from the company's eBay proposal; I did not retrieve the current worldwide count), plus operations in Australia and Europe. It now reports three categories. Collectibles, mostly trading cards, were $356 million in the second quarter, up 57% and 45% of sales. Video games were $263 million, down 47%, because the prior year had the launch of Nintendo Switch 2. Pre-owned and refurbished goods were $171 million, down 32%. The United States was $608 million of sales, Australia $121 million and Europe $61 million, down 43% after the sale of the French business.

The profit story is the mix. Gross margin was 43.7% against 29.1% a year earlier, and selling, general and administrative costs fell to $187 million from $219 million. Operating income of $160 million is a 20% margin on sales that were lower, which is far above its own results before 2025. The company raised its fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA outlook to above $650 million from above $600 million, and gives no guidance for sales or earnings.

The other half of the story is the balance sheet. In 2025 GameStop raised $4.2 billion of zero-coupon convertible notes, put about $500 million into 4,710 bitcoin, and in 2026 built a position of 43.4 million eBay shares, close to 10% of the company. It bid $125 a share, about $55.5 billion in cash and stock, for all of eBay in May. eBay's board said the offer was neither credible nor attractive, and on November 2 Bloomberg reported that the chief executive, Ryan Cohen, was considering withdrawing the bid in favor of a partnership or joint venture with seats on eBay's board.

## Reasons to own GameStop

- Net financial assets of $14.69 a share put a floor under the price that is unusual for a retailer; even my Bear value of $14.91 is more than half the price.
- The stores earn real money: $160 million of operating income in the second quarter and a raised adjusted EBITDA outlook above $650 million.
- Collectibles grew 57% and are 45% of sales, with a gross margin that is much higher than the games business.
- There is no financial pressure: the only debt is $2.8 billion of zero-coupon notes, and the company can fund the buyback from cash.
- The chief executive owns a large stake and keeps buying; his compensation is tied to performance.

## What could go wrong?

- Capital allocation. Cohen has said he wants to be the chief executive of eBay, the company bid $55.5 billion, and the financing it described depends on debt and new shares. A deal on those terms would swap a net cash position for a company four times the size of GameStop.
- Concentration. The eBay shares are 38% of the share price; a 20% fall in eBay takes about $1.6 a share off my value.
- Margin. The 43.7% gross margin comes from collectibles and a smaller store base; video game sales fell 47% and pre-owned 32%. If the margin goes back toward the 12% of my Bear, store cash flow falls by half.
- Bitcoin. The coins are pledged to Coinbase Credit, which can reuse them, so GameStop holds a receivable from a counterparty, not the coins.
- Convertible notes. $2.8 billion of notes, and holders of the 2032 notes can demand cash in December 2028; the shares converted in 2026 added 55.5 million shares, or 12%.
- Governance. A proposal about the chief executive's pay was withdrawn and a vote on more authorized shares was challenged, according to a summary of the company's filings; I did not retrieve the details.
- Data gaps. The size and terms of the chief executive's performance award, the current store count, the third-quarter results due around December 8, current bitcoin holdings and the basis for the tax on the eBay gain were not available to me.

## Management execution

Ryan Cohen has cut selling and administrative costs by about 15% in a year, closed unprofitable stores, sold the French business, refinanced $1.4 billion of debt with shares and a smaller cash payment, and put cash to work in bitcoin and eBay. The offsets: the bitcoin purchase has lost money so far, the eBay stake gained but is a large position in a company that rejected the bid, and share issuance raised the count by 12%. **Assessment: 13/20, strong on cost control and balance sheet protection, unproven on how he deploys $5 billion of liquid assets and a $5 billion eBay position. The next test is the third-quarter report, around December 8.**

- Gross margin back below 30% or an operating loss in a quarter outside the holiday season.

Today the balance sheet and profitability signals are green; the capital allocation signal is yellow because the eBay plan could change in either direction; the revenue trend is yellow. That is why the verdict is hold and not reduce: the price is only 13% above my Base value, and about 55% of it is assets I can mark to market.

## The three things that would make this verdict wrong

1. eBay at $167 or more. My Base reaches the price only at that level, 39% above today's $120. At $140 the Base is $25.15.
2. Store margins that hold at 23% or more of sales for a decade. The Base assumes 17% to 18.5%; at 4 points lower the value is $21.64.
3. A deal that destroys the cash, or one that creates value. A purchase of eBay at the proposed price would turn a company with $5 billion of cash into a levered one, and my Base would no longer apply in either direction.

These do not push the verdict to reduce, because even a poor outcome for the stores leaves about $20 a share; they explain why a price above $26 pays for good news already.
