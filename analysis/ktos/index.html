Kratos is one of the most interesting defense growth stories in the market: hypersonic systems, cheap drones, cruise missile engines and space ground systems, with revenue up 30% and $1.4 billion of cash. On sales multiples, the stock looks fair. On earnings, it is expensive, because margins are thin and stock compensation is large. The truth sits in between, and at $47.04 the price already captures it.

At $47.04 on September 24, 2026, the shares trade 4.4% above my triangulated Base value of $45.04. The Bear value of $36.84 is 21.7% below the price, and the required margin of safety of 40% puts the maximum buy price at $27.03. For existing holders this is a hold. For new money it is a watchlist name.

What Kratos is

Kratos builds hardware for the Pentagon's push toward affordable, mass-produced systems. Its Government Solutions segment covers hypersonic and rocket systems, turbine engines, microwave electronics, space and satellite ground systems and C5ISR. Its Unmanned Systems segment includes the Valkyrie combat drone and target drones. About 69% of revenue comes from the U.S. federal government, 20% from foreign customers and 11% from commercial ones. Hypersonics alone is tracking about $400 million of revenue in 2026, with management expecting $700 million or more in 2027.

The business is scaling fast through production investment and acquisitions, including Orbit ($352.7 million in cash) and Nomad. That adds goodwill and integration work, but diversification across many programs limits single-program risk.

Why adjusted EBITDA overstates the cash

Adjusted EBITDA of about $175 million in 2026 is not shareholder cash. It excludes stock compensation, which ran at $16.3 million in Q2 alone, about $60 million a year at that pace, and free cash flow is negative because of heavy capacity investment. The adjusted EBITDA margin is about 9.8% in 2026, with management targeting about 100 basis points of improvement into 2027. That is a respectable defense margin, but not the kind that usually supports a premium sales multiple.

Cash, debt and shares

Cash was $1.438 billion and conventional long-term debt zero. Finance leases are treated as debt-like, and expected cash use is deducted, so adjusted net cash is about $1.20 billion in the Base case. Shares rose from 168.9 million at the end of 2025 to about 187.7 million by July 31, 2026, mainly because Kratos sold 16.4 million shares at $84 in March, raising about $1.35 billion. That dilution funded cash, acquisitions and capacity, so it is not automatically destructive, but it raises the bar for per-share returns. Forward shares are set at 190 million.

How the buy price is set

Confidence B starts at a 30% margin of safety. Five points are added for dependence on margin execution and five for the valuation divergence, for 40%. The strong balance sheet offsets further penalties. A 40% discount to the $45.04 Base gives a maximum buy price of $27.03.

Dividend

Kratos pays no meaningful dividend.

Reasons to own KTOS

  • Strategic exposure. Direct participation in hypersonics, missiles, drones, propulsion and space, the areas where defense budgets are shifting.
  • Visible demand. $2.084 billion of backlog, $1.572 billion of it funded, a 1.3x twelve-month book-to-bill and a $15 billion pipeline.
  • A fortress balance sheet. About $1.44 billion of cash and no conventional long-term debt.
  • Operating leverage. Heavy capacity investment now could produce much higher margins once revenue scales.
  • Execution. Q2 beat the company's own estimates and guidance was raised.

What could go wrong?

  • Valuation. The price assumes substantial growth and margin expansion. On earnings, it is already demanding.
  • Cash conversion. 2026 free cash flow is negative despite rising adjusted EBITDA. Failing to turn backlog into margins and cash is the main thesis breaker.
  • Stock compensation. At about $60 million a year, it absorbs a large share of adjusted EBITDA.
  • Execution and supply chain. Scaling needs labor, components, facilities and working capital.
  • Capital allocation. Acquisitions and equity issuance must earn returns above the cost of capital per share.

Management execution

Q2 beat the company's own revenue and EBITDA ranges, guidance was raised, book-to-bill reached 1.3x over twelve months, capital was raised at $84 per share, and management openly discloses the investment-driven pressure on cash flow. The main test is converting backlog and capacity into higher 2027 margins and positive free cash flow. Management credibility scores 15/20, good.

Adversarial review

The first draft's primary valuation and sensitivity table reconciled exactly. Three things were corrected. First, its weighted Base was stated as $50.21, but its own weights and values give $50.43. Second, its secondary value of $47.50 could not be reproduced from its stated inputs: about $2.2 billion of revenue at an 11% EBITDA margin and a low to mid-20s multiple gives about $34 to $37, and reaching $47.50 needs about 33x EBITDA. Third, the EBITDA it used adds back stock compensation, which ran at about $60 million a year in Q2. The rebuilt secondary value is $29.54. The Base moved from $50.21 to $45.04, the maximum buy price from $32.64 to $27.03, and the Bear and Bull, which had not been derived from the primary method, from $36.50 and $69.00 to $36.84 and $69.74. The draft's twelve-month price-touch probabilities were dropped as not part of the framework. The verdict is unchanged.

The strongest counter-argument is that hypersonics, affordable mass, unmanned combat aircraft and propulsion could sustain more than 20% growth for years, justifying a much higher multiple. The Bull case captures a 5x forward sales regime. The Base does not capitalize unproven long-duration growth before positive free cash flow appears.

Final verdict: hold at $47.04, watchlist for new money

Kratos has an excellent growth and backlog profile and exceptional liquidity, but the price already discounts substantial success in hypersonics, propulsion, drones and space, before that success shows up in margins and cash.

Verdict: HOLD at $47.04, WATCHLIST for new money. Triangulated Base value $45.04, downside 4.2%, Confidence B (18/25). Required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $27.03.

For an existing holder, the strategic position and balance sheet support holding. New money waits for $27.03 or below with the thesis intact, or for proof that margins and free cash flow are scaling with revenue.

The main thesis breaker is a failure to convert backlog and heavy capacity investment into sustained margin expansion and eventually positive free cash flow.
