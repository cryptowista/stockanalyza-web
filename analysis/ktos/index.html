In September I valued Kratos at $45.04 a share with the stock at $47.04, and called it a hold. Two months later the stock is $26.96, down 42.7%, and my Base value is $20.21, down 55.1%. The stock fell a lot and my number fell more. Most of that cut is not news from the quarter, which was strong. It is a change in how I turn Kratos's growth into a price.

In March Kratos sold 16.4 million shares at $84 and raised about $1.35 billion. That cash is still there: net cash is about $1.21 billion, or $6.36 a share. The shares that raised it now cost $26.96, and the company that issued them is still burning cash. The third quarter looked good on the numbers management reports. Revenue rose 35.7% to $471.8 million, adjusted EBITDA was $50.9 million, and backlog reached about $2.65 billion. On the numbers that pay shareholders it did not: the GAAP operating loss was $4.9 million, free cash flow was about -$35 million, and 2026 free cash use is now guided at $110 to $120 million.

My triangulated Base value is $20.21. The price is 33.4% above it, which makes the stock slightly overvalued. The Bear value of $11.39 is 57.8% below the price and the Bull value of $29.93 is 11.0% above it. At Confidence C the framework requires a 42.5% margin of safety. That gives an entry range of $10.30 to $12.95, and the lower end, $10.30, is the conservative buy limit. The stock would have to fall another 52.0% to 61.8% to reach it. This is a hold, and new money should not add.

What changed since September

The business news was mostly good. Management raised 2026 guidance, set 2027 targets of $2.1 to $2.2 billion of revenue and $230 to $240 million of adjusted EBITDA, and reported a book-to-bill of 2.2 in the quarter. The cash news was worse: 2026 free cash use rose from $85 to $105 million to $110 to $120 million.

The valuation changed for a different reason. In September a 4.0x multiple of 2027 sales carried 55% of the weight, even though I noted then that it implied about 37 times 2027 adjusted EBITDA and that the earnings-based method came out 56% lower. That weighting is the main reason the September number was too high. Kratos has negative free cash flow, so the current framework requires a transition model, which values the interim cash flows plus a discounted exit value and treats stock compensation as a cash cost. A sales multiple cannot do that without ignoring margins, so it is now a reference and carries no weight. The table below recomputes the step in the current model.

What Kratos is

Kratos builds hardware for the Pentagon's push toward affordable, mass-produced systems: hypersonic vehicles and rocket systems, jet engines for drones and missiles, microwave electronics, target drones, the Valkyrie combat drone, and ground systems for satellites. About 69% of revenue comes from the U.S. federal government. Management said earlier this year that hypersonics would be about $400 million of revenue in 2026 and $700 million or more in 2027.

Growth is concentrated in one segment. Government Solutions revenue was $397.2 million in the third quarter, up 52.5% and 35.8% organically. Unmanned Systems fell to $74.6 million from $87.2 million, because the year-earlier quarter included $16.1 million of Valkyrie shipments to an international customer. Unmanned Systems adjusted EBITDA was $4.6 million against $6.4 million. The drone business that gets the headlines is, for now, the small and lumpy half of the company.

Why adjusted EBITDA overstates the cash

Adjusted EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation, interest and taxes, with some costs added back) of $178 million in 2026 is not shareholder cash. It excludes stock compensation, which I estimate at about $67 million for the year, so EBITDA after stock compensation is about $111 million. Free cash flow is negative because Kratos is spending to build capacity, and working capital absorbs about 40 cents of every new dollar of revenue. That is why this analysis values Kratos on EBITDA after stock compensation and on cash flow, and not on the adjusted figure.

Cash, debt and shares

Net cash was $1,214.3 million at September 30 after counting finance leases as debt. The fourth quarter cash burn is deducted separately in the valuation, and cash is counted once, in the bridge from enterprise value to equity. I use 191.0 million shares: about 189 million outstanding plus about 2 million of unvested awards, both my estimates because the exact count at the valuation date is not yet reported. The company raised equity in March, so another raise is a live risk while free cash flow stays negative, although net cash is about five times the Base case burn of roughly $240 million from the fourth quarter of 2026 through 2029.

Secondary valuation: six-year cash flow model

It projects cash flow before financing for six years, discounts it at 11% and values the years after 2032 with a terminal value at a 9.5% discount rate and 3.5% growth. What is the market already pricing in?

At $26.96 the market value is about $5.10 billion and the enterprise value about $3.88 billion. That is 21.8x 2026 adjusted EBITDA and 35.0x after stock compensation, or 16.6x and 23.6x on 2027. Solving backwards with everything else in the Base case fixed, the price needs an end-2027 enterprise value of about $4.57 billion. That is 27.8x EBITDA after stock compensation on my Base 2027 figure, against my 22x. Or, at 22x, it needs 2027 EBITDA after stock compensation of about $208 million, 26% above my Base. That equals about $278 million of adjusted EBITDA, roughly 16% above the top of management's $230 to $240 million range. The price sits between my Base and Bull cases.

The return arithmetic points the same way. If Kratos delivers my Base case and the stock is worth 22x EBITDA after stock compensation at the end of 2027, the equity is worth about $24.55 a share, 8.9% below today's price (the model adds no interest on the cash). If the market keeps paying today's 35.0x, the equity is worth about $35.71, 32% above it. The business case is the same in both. Only the multiple differs, and the multiple is what no one controls.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C starts at a 42.5% margin of safety. I add nothing on top, because each risk is priced once: stock compensation and cash burn are already costs in the cash flows, the method disagreement is already in the Confidence score, and the balance sheet needs no further penalty. Applying 42.5% to each method's Base value gives $12.95 on the exit multiple method and $10.30 on the cash flow model. The weighted Base of $20.21 gives $11.62. The entry is therefore a range of $10.30 to $12.95, driven by how much weight the 2030 to 2032 cash flows deserve. Buying at or below $10.30 would satisfy both methods. Reasons to own KTOS

  • Demand is visible. Bookings of $1.04 billion against revenue of $471.8 million produced a record backlog of about $2.65 billion, and the bid pipeline is about $15.5 billion.
  • Government Solutions is accelerating. It grew 35.8% organically in the quarter, and hypersonics is expected to grow from about $400 million to $700 million or more next year.
  • The balance sheet is a cushion. Net cash of about $6.36 a share is about five times the Base case burn of roughly $240 million through 2029.
  • Operating leverage exists. Adjusted EBITDA margin is guided up from about 9.9% in 2026 to about 10.9% in 2027.
  • The stock has fallen about 79% from its January high of $130.72, so a lot of the optimism has already left the price.

What could go wrong?

  • Cash conversion. Free cash use rose to $110 to $120 million in 2026 and my Base case has another $106 million in 2027. If backlog does not turn into margin and cash, the thesis breaks.
  • The multiple. Most of the Base value is an end-2027 multiple. A re-rating to 14x, the Bear multiple, takes the primary value to $13.39 even if revenue is on plan.
  • Dilution. The March raise at $84 shows management will issue equity to fund growth. A raise at $27 would cost three times as many shares per dollar.
  • Budget and supply chain. Management itself named both when it called the first quarter of 2027 the lightest.
  • Program concentration. Hypersonics is the fastest growing line, and its growth rests on a small number of U.S. programs.

Management execution

Management beat analyst expectations again in the third quarter, with adjusted EBITDA of $50.9 million, raised guidance on revenue and EBITDA, and set 2027 targets. Against that, the 2026 free cash flow guidance has moved against shareholders by about $20 million at the midpoint since August, and the company has raised equity once this year. Management credibility scores 14/20, good, one point lower than in September, and it is the cash guidance that lowers it.

Final verdict: hold at $26.96, do not add

Kratos is a real growth business with a record backlog and net cash of about $6.36 a share. It is also a company that loses money after stock compensation and burns cash while it grows, and the price still assumes both problems are temporary. At $26.96 the stock is 33.4% above my Base value and 162% above the conservative buy limit. Existing holders have no thesis breaker to act on. New money should wait.

Verdict: HOLD at $26.96. Triangulated Base value $20.21, downside 25.0%, Confidence C (13.8/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range $10.30 to $12.95, maximum buy price $10.30.
