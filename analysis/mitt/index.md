MITT, NYSE. Published September 21, 2026. Price as of the September 18, 2026 close.

At first glance, the investment case looks unusually attractive. The shares closed at $6.31 on September 18, 2026, compared with reported book value of $10.00 per share. The quarterly dividend of $0.24 implies an annualized yield of approximately 15.2%.

Yet the discount is not free money.

TPG Mortgage Investment Trust, formerly AG Mortgage Investment Trust, is a leveraged residential mortgage REIT. Its earnings depend on credit spreads, financing costs, securitization execution, loan performance and management's ability to protect book value. The proposed acquisition of Cherry Hill Mortgage Investment Corporation adds potential scale and earnings diversification, but it also introduces dilution, integration risk and greater exposure to Agency RMBS and mortgage servicing rights.

The central question is therefore not whether MITT looks statistically cheap. It does. The question is whether the company can sustainably earn more than its cost of equity while preserving book value and covering the dividend.

MITT is undervalued relative to book value and offers a substantial income yield, but the current price does not provide the full confidence-adjusted margin of safety required for a leveraged, externally managed mortgage REIT undergoing a material acquisition.

Economic classification: Standard Company. Primary valuation method: normalized price to book value based on sustainable economic ROE. Secondary method: dividend discount and required-yield valuation.

## The investment thesis

MITT is attempting to transform itself from a small, discounted mortgage REIT with legacy assets into a larger residential mortgage credit platform.

Its core strategy is to acquire residential loans, securitize them, retain selected credit tranches and recycle capital into new investments. The company also owns 66% of AG Arc, whose operating subsidiary Arc Home gives MITT access to proprietary mortgage origination.

This structure may support attractive returns on equity when financing markets remain open and residential credit performs normally. At June 30, 2026, management calculated a 17.6% portfolio ROE before corporate expenses, preferred dividends and other common shareholder costs.

But that headline return does not flow directly to common shareholders.

In Q2 2026, the portfolio generated $20.5 million of net interest income and $1.2 million of Arc Home EAD attributable to MITT. After management fees, investment expenses, corporate expenses, preferred dividends and taxes, only $7.7 million remained as Earnings Available for Distribution, or $0.24 per diluted share. This exactly matched the common dividend.

The investment case therefore rests on three conditions:

1. Book value remains broadly stable.
2. EAD rises above the dividend rather than merely matching it.
3. The CHMI acquisition increases per-share earning power without destroying book value.

## Routing and economic metric

MITT is routed to the SF-04 REIT framework, with a mortgage REIT subtype.

Traditional property REIT metrics such as occupancy, same-store NOI and leasing-adjusted AFFO do not describe MITT's economics. For a mortgage REIT, the closest sector-equivalent metrics are book value per common share, Earnings Available for Distribution per share, economic return on equity, dividend coverage, economic leverage, credit performance, and liquidity and financing structure.

Traditional corporate free cash flow is not an appropriate valuation anchor. Large asset purchases, loan repayments, securitizations and financing flows make conventional cash-flow statements economically misleading for this business.

EAD is useful, but it is not a perfect cash metric. It excludes realized and unrealized investment gains and losses, transaction expenses and certain nonrecurring items. These exclusions can be reasonable for measuring recurring spread income, but realized credit or portfolio losses still affect book value and shareholder wealth.

> The hardest honest economic test is therefore EAD plus change in book value, not EAD in isolation.

## What has changed

MITT has made meaningful progress in repositioning its portfolio.

At June 30, 2026, it reported a $7.7 billion investment portfolio. Approximately $6.3 billion of financing was non-recourse securitized debt, while about $1.0 billion was recourse financing. This distinction matters because the consolidated 13.4x GAAP leverage ratio overstates the direct recourse risk to common shareholders. Management's economic leverage ratio was 1.8x.

The residential loan portfolio also showed relatively strong reported borrower characteristics:

| At June 30, 2026 | Non-agency loans | Home-equity loans |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unpaid principal balance | $6.4B | $1.0B |
| Weighted average current LTV / CLTV | 61% | 64% |
| Weighted average FICO | 752 | 750 |
| 90+ days delinquent | 1.2% | 0.6% |

These metrics indicate meaningful borrower equity and comparatively strong credit quality. However, reported current loan-to-value ratios partly incorporate estimated home-price movements, which means they should not be treated as contractual facts.

Arc Home is becoming a more relevant earnings contributor. MITT's share of Arc Home EAD reached approximately $1.2 million in Q2, equivalent to $0.04 per MITT share. Arc Home's origination volume increased, and MITT valued its $46.4 million investment at 1.05x book value.

## The CHMI acquisition

MITT has agreed to acquire Cherry Hill Mortgage Investment Corporation. Under the proposed terms, each CHMI common share would receive:

- 0.3063 MITT common shares
- $0.41 in cash from MITT
- $0.52 in cash from MITT's external manager

MITT expects to issue approximately 11.6 million new common shares. Compared with approximately 31.8 million diluted shares in Q2 2026, the share issuance represents an increase of about 36.5%.

The transaction adds Agency RMBS and mortgage servicing assets to MITT's primarily residential credit platform. On a June 30, 2026 pro forma basis, the combined company would have had approximately $9.3 billion of assets and $739.9 million of total stockholders' equity.

The strategic logic is credible:

- A larger equity base can reduce fixed costs per share.
- CHMI adds mortgage servicing assets, which can benefit when prepayment speeds slow.
- Additional scale may improve capital-market access.
- The manager is contributing approximately $20 million of the cash consideration.

The risk is equally clear. MITT is issuing a large amount of stock while acquiring a company with a different asset mix. The transaction can increase earnings power, but only if synergies, portfolio performance and financing remain favorable.

The merger also changes MITT's character. The company will no longer be as close to a pure residential credit securitization strategy. Agency RMBS and servicing-related assets will become materially more important.

## Legacy assets

MITT still owns legacy commercial assets inherited through the Western Asset Mortgage Capital acquisition.

At June 30, 2026, legacy commercial loans had a fair value of $49.3 million and negative reported ROE of 4.3%. Management expects asset resolutions to release approximately $30 million of equity that could be redeployed into investments targeting 15% to 20% returns.

This is a legitimate earnings catalyst. It is not guaranteed. The loans are in hotel, retail and office-related collateral, and several are on non-accrual or cost-recovery status. Sales or deed-in-lieu transactions can take longer than expected and recovery values may change.

## Bear, base and bull valuation

### Primary method: price to book value

For a mortgage REIT, book value deserves a justified multiple based on sustainable return on equity, cost of equity, asset risk and management structure.

| Scenario | Normalized book value | Sustainable ROE | Justified P/BV | Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear | $8.75 | ~8% | 0.55x | $4.81 |
| Base | $10.10 | 11% to 12% | 0.78x | $7.88 |
| Bull | $10.40 | ~13% | 0.98x | $10.19 |

**Bear** assumes credit-spread widening, weaker asset marks, limited dividend coverage and disappointing merger execution. **Base** assumes broadly stable book value, EAD improving toward the disclosed 2027 projection and only partial recognition of merger benefits. **Bull** requires successful CHMI integration, sustained dividend coverage, productive redeployment of legacy commercial capital and no material residential credit deterioration.

### Valuation triangulation

A 60% weight is assigned to the book-value method and 40% to the dividend method. This gives a Bear Value of approximately $4.70, a Base Value of approximately $7.50 and a Bull Value of approximately $9.90.

A probability weighting of 30% Bear, 50% Base and 20% Bull produces an expected value of approximately $7.10.

The Base Value is above the current market price, but the range is wide. That dispersion is the reason the stock does not qualify as a high-confidence bargain.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $6.31, MITT trades at approximately 0.63x reported book value.

Using a 14% cost of equity and 1% long-term growth assumption, the current price implies a sustainable common ROE of approximately 9.2%.

Management's standalone 2027 EAD projection of $1.18 against projected book value of $10.11 implies an EAD return on book value of approximately 11.7%.

The market is therefore pricing in one or more of the following:

- EAD forecasts will not fully convert into durable economic returns
- Book value will decline
- The dividend will remain vulnerable
- The CHMI acquisition will deliver less accretion than expected
- External management and preferred-stock costs justify a persistent discount
- Investors require a cost of equity materially above 14%

These concerns are not irrational. However, a 0.63x book multiple appears severe if MITT can preserve book value and produce EAD close to its published forecasts.

## Where the stock becomes attractive

MITT's Base Value is approximately $7.50, but fair value is not the same as a disciplined buy price.

Because the company has leveraged financial assets, a pending acquisition, thin current dividend coverage and meaningful exposure to market-value movements, the required margin of safety is 30%. Applying that discount to the Base Value gives a maximum buy price of approximately **$5.25**.

At $6.31, the shares are below estimated fair value but above the confidence-adjusted buy price.

## Reasons to own or follow MITT

- **Large discount to book value.** The stock trades at approximately 63% of reported book value.
- **High current income.** The annualized dividend yield is approximately 15.2%.
- **Predominantly non-recourse securitized financing.** This reduces the economic relevance of the headline 13.4x GAAP leverage ratio.
- **Strong reported borrower characteristics.** Current LTV ratios, FICO scores and delinquency rates remain supportive.
- **Potential earnings recovery.** Redeployment of capital from negative-return legacy commercial assets could improve EAD.
- **Arc Home provides origination access.** The platform can supply assets and contribute operating earnings.
- **The CHMI acquisition may improve scale.** A larger equity base could reduce per-share overhead and diversify earnings.

## What could go wrong?

- **Book value erosion.** A large dividend yield cannot compensate indefinitely for declining book value.
- **Credit deterioration.** Higher unemployment or falling residential property values could increase defaults and reduce recoveries.
- **Dividend pressure.** Current quarterly EAD only matches the dividend, while the 2026 projection is slightly below the annual distribution.
- **Merger dilution.** MITT plans to issue approximately 11.6 million shares. If the acquired assets underperform, the transaction could dilute economic value per share.
- **Interest-rate and spread volatility.** Asset values and financing economics can deteriorate even when borrower credit remains acceptable.
- **External management conflicts.** The manager receives base and incentive fees, while common shareholders bear dilution and book-value risk.
- **Preferred capital burden.** Preferred dividends consumed $5.2 million in Q2 2026 before earnings became available to common shareholders.
- **Legacy commercial recoveries.** Expected asset sales may be delayed or completed below carrying value.

## Management execution

Management deserves credit for maintaining book value in Q2, operating with a relatively moderate economic leverage ratio and shifting capital toward residential credit and home-equity loans. The WMC acquisition also created the opportunity to recover and redeploy capital from legacy assets.

However, management's record must be judged over a full credit and financing cycle. The CHMI transaction is large relative to MITT's existing common equity and will be the most important test of capital allocation.

Management credibility is therefore assessed as mixed to good rather than exceptional.

## Adversarial review

The arithmetic review independently recomputed price to book value, dividend yield, dividend coverage, scenario values, valuation weights, reverse valuation and the maximum buy price.

The economic counter-thesis review tested the strongest bullish interpretation: management's 2027 EAD forecast, stable book value, successful legacy-asset redeployment and merger benefits. Under that combination, value can approach the Bull range.

The strongest bearish interpretation was also tested: lower book value, sub-cost-of-equity returns and a reduced sustainable dividend. That produces value below the current market price.

No unresolved arithmetic inconsistency remains. The gate status is PASS\_WITH\_WARNING because the final economics of the pending CHMI transaction remain uncertain.

## Final verdict: hold at $6.31

MITT offers a compelling headline combination of a 37% discount to book value and a 15.2% dividend yield.

The discount appears wider than justified if book value remains stable and EAD reaches management's 2027 projection. On that basis, the shares have approximately 19% upside to a $7.50 Base Value, in addition to distributions.

But the dividend is only narrowly covered today, the company is externally managed, and the pending CHMI acquisition will materially change both the share count and portfolio structure. The Bear Value of approximately $4.70 also shows that current buyers do not have full downside protection.

**Verdict: HOLD at $6.31. Triangulated Base Value $7.50, upside 18.9%, required margin of safety 30%, maximum buy price $5.25.**

The stock becomes more attractive below $5.25, or at a higher price if MITT demonstrates sustainable dividend coverage above 1.10x and completes the CHMI acquisition without material book-value dilution.

The main thesis breaker is sustained book-value erosion combined with inadequate EAD coverage of the dividend.
