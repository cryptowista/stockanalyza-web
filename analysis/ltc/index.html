LTC Properties reported net income of $6.39 a share for the third quarter. Core FFO, the recurring cash earnings measure for real estate investment trusts, was $0.71. The gap is a $323.9 million gain on selling real estate, which is the cost of turning a skilled nursing landlord into an operator of senior housing. At $42.13 the shares trade at 15.2 times the FY26 Core FFO outlook with a 5.4% dividend yield, and my model puts them 8.6% below a Base value of $46.12.

The business has changed more than the per-share numbers. In the third quarter SHOP net operating income was $19.7 million against $4.8 million a year earlier, and SHOP is 45% of annualized net operating income. LTC sold $464 million of assets in the quarter: 17 skilled nursing centers for $304 million and 17 seniors housing communities for $160 million. Core FFO per share rose from $0.69 to $0.71 while the company kept issuing shares ($236.8 million of common stock proceeds so far this year), so the new money has bought only a small amount of per-share growth. Management described new acquisitions as roughly earnings neutral in the first year when funded 70% with equity and 30% with debt, according to coverage of the call.

My Base value is $46.12, 9.5% above the price. The Bear value of $33.62 is 20.2% below the price and the Bull value of $55.63 is 32.0% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is $45.37. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of $26.52, or $27.74 to $24.69 depending on the method. The verdict is Hold with a watchlist for new money.

Valuation route: a seniors housing and health care real estate investment trust in the middle of a change of model, so I value it on owner cash flow per share (Core FAD less stock-based compensation) in a ten year model as the primary method and on price to Core FFO as the second. Economic classification: Quality Growth (7.2 out of 12 on the REIT scorecard). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

What LTC is

LTC is a seniors housing and health care real estate investment trust in the United States. Seniors housing is 80% of gross real estate investments and skilled nursing the rest. Since launching its SHOP platform in May 2025 the company has grown it from zero to 50 communities run by 14 operators, 12 of them new relationships, with an average property age of 9 years and 60% in primary markets. SHOP is 53% of gross investments. The rest is triple-net leases, where tenants pay rent and carry the operating costs, plus mortgage loans of $396.8 million, financing receivables of $284.7 million and notes of $24.8 million.

The shift is large. Management expects 2026 gross investments of about $1.0 billion, raised from $900 million, and SHOP acquisitions reached nearly $950 million through October. Asset sales and loan payoffs of $730 million are expected for the year, including $179.8 million in the fourth quarter, and a $180 million loan payoff is scheduled for November 24. The asset sales were done at an implied cap rate of about 5.5%, according to coverage of the call.

Capital and share count

On the balance sheet at September 30 the revolver was $429.9 million, term loans $198.5 million and senior unsecured notes $356.7 million, net of costs. The press release says $410 million is drawn on the revolver and $11 million of cash on hand on a pro forma basis, after the October acquisitions, and I use that basis: about $965 million of debt and $954 million of net debt. The balance sheet showed $183.0 million of cash at quarter end, and I could not reconcile the two figures. Debt to annualized adjusted EBITDA for real estate is 4.4 times in coverage of the call, inside the 4 to 5 times range management has targeted. Pro forma liquidity is $903 million: $11 million of cash, $490 million on the revolver and $402 million under the at-the-market equity program, which was renewed with a new $500 million agreement.

That program is the main source of dilution. Shares outstanding were 54.8 million at September 30 and about 56.2 million in a later count, and I use 56.2 million. I could not find the diluted count or any forward equity position. Interest expense, secured debt and the debt maturity schedule were not in the text I read, so the refinancing profile is an open data gap. The revolver is the floating-rate piece, and the interest coverage ratios are not shown.

Primary valuation: owner cash flow per share

FAD adds back non-cash items, including stock compensation, which is a real cost to owners. I deduct $0.06 a share for it, which is my estimate. My FY27 Core FAD is $2.90, up 2.1% from the 2026 midpoint of $2.84, because new SHOP assets are earnings neutral in the first year and the sold triple-net assets carried rent. Owner cash flow in FY27 is therefore $2.84 per share. It grows 4.5% a year in years 2 to 5, which needs core SHOP growth near the guided 13% and the new communities to mature, then 3.5% for years 6 to 10 and 2.5% in perpetuity. I discount at 9.0%, my estimate of the return an owner of a leveraged landlord with operator risk should require. The growth is durable because seniors demand is demographic, but it needs reinvestment funded by new shares, and I count the shares at today's price.

The model gives $48.24. Terminal value is 56% of it, over a ten year horizon and below my 75% attention level. Years 1 to 10 contribute $21.44 and the terminal value $26.79. The FY27 FAD of $2.90 is my estimate: the company has not issued a 2027 outlook.

Secondary valuation: price to Core FFO

The second method applies 15.5 times to the FY26 Core FFO outlook midpoint of $2.77, which gives $42.94. I use 15.5 times because the company is growing earnings at about 2% to 3% a year, the dividend is a large part of the return and a seniors housing operator carries more operating risk than a triple-net landlord. The multiple is my judgment and is not company guidance.

Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $48.24 and $42.94, a divergence of 11.6%, below the 20% warning level. They are not independent: both rest on the same FFO and FAD outlook, so agreement is not independent evidence. I weight the owner cash flow model 60% and the multiple method 40%, because the first charges for stock compensation and models the ramp of the SHOP assets. The weighted value is $46.12. What is the market already pricing in?

Holding everything else at Base, the price of $42.13 requires a cost of equity of 9.9% instead of 9.0%. Holding the cost of equity at 9.0% instead, it requires owner cash flow growth of 2.2% a year for years 2 to 5 and 1.7% for years 6 to 10, against 4.5% and 3.5% in Base. The market is therefore paying for less than my central case, and for more than my Bear case, which assumes growth of 1.5% a year.

On a one year view, if Core FFO per share grows from $2.77 to my $2.84 and the multiple stays at 15.2 times, the shares return about 7.9% including the 5.4% dividend. If the multiple moves to the 16.6 times that my Base value implies, the return is about 17.6%. These are model returns for information, not forecasts.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is grade C, so the base margin of safety is 42.5%. I add no separate margin for leverage or dilution, because both are in the Bear inputs and the stress tests. The maximum buy price is $46.12 times 0.575, or $26.52, with an entry range of $27.74 to $24.69 by method. The price is 59% above my maximum buy price, so this is not a buy. A cost of equity of 8.0% would raise the maximum buy price to $29.61, which is still well below the price.

Reasons to own

  • SHOP net operating income grew from $4.8 million to $19.7 million in a year, and the core 27 properties are guided to about 13% growth.
  • The portfolio now has less skilled nursing, with seniors housing at 80% of gross investments, and loans are being repaid.
  • Leverage of 4.4 times is inside the 4 to 5 times target and liquidity is $903 million.
  • The price is 8.6% below my Base value and the yield is 5.4%.

What could go wrong?

  • Dilution. Core FFO per share is up 2.9%, from $0.69 to $0.71, while the share count keeps rising. If new acquisitions stay earnings neutral, growth of 3.0% a year gives a Base value of $43.83.
  • Operators. SHOP depends on 14 third-party operators, 12 of them new, and the occupancy assumption was already cut once in 2026.
  • Medicaid and labor costs. A retroactive Medicaid rate decrease hit two memory care communities.
  • Funding. The plan relies on the equity market for about 70% of each acquisition, so a lower share price raises the cost of growth.
  • Rates. A cost of equity of 10.0% gives a Base value of $42.17.

Management execution

The company raised its 2026 investment target to $1.0 billion and sold $464 million of assets in one quarter at about a 5.5% implied cap rate. Against that, SHOP occupancy assumptions have already been cut, total SHOP net operating income guidance was narrowed to $73.5 million to $76.4 million with a lower midpoint, and the benefit to earnings has been pushed out, with coverage of the call pointing to 2028. The record is mixed. I would like to see the first 2027 outlook, which is expected with the fourth-quarter report on February 23, 2027.

What I would watch from here

Green: the fourth-quarter report on February 23, 2027 shows Core FFO per share at or above $0.70, a 2027 outlook with Core FFO above $2.85, and SHOP occupancy above the 2025 average of 89.7%.

Yellow: core SHOP net operating income below $53.5 million, debt to EBITDA above 5.0 times, or more than 60 million shares outstanding without a rise in Core FFO per share.

Red: a Core FFO outlook below $2.70, a dividend that exceeds Core FAD, or a SHOP operator failure.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Further equity issuance at a lower price without growth in Core FFO per share, which takes growth to 3.0% and the Base value to $43.83.
  2. A cost of equity of 10.0% or more, which lowers my Base value to $42.17.
  3. SHOP occupancy and Medicaid problems that move the model toward the Bear case of $33.62.

Final verdict: hold at $42.13, watchlist for new money

LTC is repricing a skilled nursing landlord as a seniors housing operator, and the market has paid for part of that, not all. At $42.13 the shares are fairly valued on my numbers and not cheap enough to buy under my rules. My maximum buy price is $26.52.

Verdict: HOLD at $42.13, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $46.12, upside +9.5%, Confidence C (13.8/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by method $27.74 to $24.69, maximum buy price $26.52.

The date I would mark is February 23, 2027, when the fourth-quarter report shows whether $6.39 of net income was a one-time gain and the $0.71 is starting to grow.
