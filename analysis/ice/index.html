Twenty consecutive years of record revenue is the kind of fact that makes a price feel safe. At $172.22 it is also the fact that has already been paid for. Intercontinental Exchange, the owner of the New York Stock Exchange, is heading for adjusted earnings of about $8.20 a share this year, up from $6.95 in 2025, and the stock has risen about 40% from its June 29 close of $122.91.

At $172.22 the shares trade 20.9% above my weighted Base value of $142.44, so the Base is 17.3% below the price. The Bear value of $101.18 is 41.2% below the price and the Bull value of $182.55 is 6.0% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade C, which gives a required margin of safety of 42.5% and a maximum buy price of $81.90. The verdict is Hold, do not add: there is no valuation reason to leave a franchise with a 61% adjusted operating margin, and no cushion in the price for a new buyer.

Valuation route: an operating exchange, clearing and data group, not a bank, so it is valued like a stable operating business on owner free cash flow, meaning adjusted free cash flow less an estimate of stock compensation. Primary method: a five-year cash flow path with a terminal value, discounted at 10%. Cross-check: earnings in five years at an exit multiple, plus dividends. The pending MarketAxess acquisition is treated as value-neutral at the price ICE pays, and tested separately. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Intercontinental Exchange is

ICE runs exchanges and clearing houses and sells data and software. Its exchanges include the New York Stock Exchange and the ICE Futures markets for oil, gas and other energy products, and its clearing houses sit behind them. Its fixed income and data segment sells pricing, indices and analytics. Its mortgage technology segment is the former Black Knight, bought in 2023, which runs loan origination and servicing software for US lenders.

On July 30 ICE agreed to buy MarketAxess, an electronic bond-trading network, for $167 a share in cash. That is about $6.0 billion of equity value and $5.7 billion including debt, a 33% premium. ICE is financing all of it with new debt (bonds, a term loan and commercial paper), expects $100 million of annual cost savings within three years and says the deal will add to adjusted earnings in the first full year. It plans to raise baseline buybacks from $350 million to $400 million a quarter and to bring gross leverage back to 3.0 times or lower within 18 to 24 months of closing. US antitrust review ended early on October 21 and the company now expects to close in the first quarter of 2027, subject to other approvals.

The jump in restricted cash is about the size of the new debt ($3.5 billion against $3.7 billion), so I infer that the financing is largely raised in advance, though the footnote that confirms it was beyond the part of the filing I could read. My owner free cash flow is already after interest, so I do not subtract debt a second time and I give no credit for the restricted cash. MarketAxess enters only as a test. If ICE overpaid by 20% of the $6.0 billion equity price, that would be $1.2 billion, or $2.15 a share.

Dividend and buybacks

The board approved the fourth-quarter dividend of $0.52 a share on October 29 (ex-dividend date December 16, payable December 31). The 2026 total of $2.08 is 8% above 2025 and is about 25% of my adjusted earnings estimate, a 1.2% yield. Together with buybacks the company returned $2.5 billion in nine months, or about 3.5% of today's market value on an annualized basis (my arithmetic), which is about two thirds of the $3.7 billion of adjusted free cash flow earned over the same nine months. The rest goes to debt reduction and the MarketAxess deal.

The buyback record is better than the dividend headline. In nine months ICE repurchased 11 million shares for $1.6 billion, an average of about $147, which is 15% below today's price, and it will raise the baseline to $400 million a quarter after the MarketAxess close. At today's price each dollar of buyback retires fewer shares than it did at $147, and after the deal buybacks compete with debt reduction.

What the options market says

Options on ICE are liquid enough to read, though not heavily traded: Market Chameleon showed 1,116 contracts on November 13. Its 30-day implied volatility was 26.6% on November 17, up from 22.5% four days earlier, which implies a one-month move of about 7.7%, or $13.22 on $172.22. The jump came with a 3.4% fall in the stock. I could not confirm the cause of the November 17 move. Exchange operators have traded on worries about perpetual futures and tokenized trading since regulators approved perpetual futures in late May. This is context for timing and does not change my value, margin of safety or verdict.

Reasons to own ICE

  • Twenty consecutive years of record revenue, adjusted earnings up 14% in 2025 and about 18% expected for 2026, and adjusted free cash flow of $4.2 billion in 2025, up 16%.
  • Exchanges earn a 77% adjusted operating margin, and the group as a whole earned 61% in the third quarter. Little incremental revenue has to be spent on.
  • Half of net revenue is recurring, from data, listings and software, which gives some stability when trading volumes slow.
  • Owner free cash flow roughly equals adjusted earnings, so the earnings are real cash.
  • Management cut leverage from 3.3 times to 3.0 times in 2025, raised the dividend 8% and bought back a record $1.6 billion in nine months.
  • Optionality in new markets: an affiliate of the NYSE is launching 24-hour tokenized stock trading this month.

What could go wrong?

  • The multiple falls. At a 17 times exit and Base earnings, the return from $172.22 is 3.7% a year, and at 15 times it is 1.2%.
  • Trading volumes revert. Transaction revenue was 51% of third-quarter net revenue and grew 23%. Part of that is cyclical, and it is the part my normalization takes back.
  • Competition. Regulators approved perpetual futures for regulated crypto platforms in late May, and tokenized stock trading is starting. Exchange stocks have fallen on both, and ICE itself is on both sides of the second.
  • Leverage rises. Gross leverage goes from 3.0 to about 3.4 times when MarketAxess closes and is meant to return to 3.0 times only after 18 to 24 months. Interest expense was already up 16% in the third quarter.
  • Mortgage technology is the weak segment: 4% revenue growth, a 2% margin under accounting rules, exposure to housing activity, and a 2026 guide for only low to mid single digit growth that includes client losses from earlier industry mergers.
  • The MarketAxess price of $167 a share was a 33% premium. If the business is worth 20% less than that to ICE, it costs about $2.15 a share. Closing also depends on approvals beyond US antitrust.

Management execution

  • Leverage was brought down on schedule in 2025, to 3.0 times from 3.3 times, before the new deal. The plan to repeat that after MarketAxess is credible given $4 billion or more of annual free cash flow, but it is a plan.
  • The company beat the previous year's adjusted earnings in every recent quarter, with 5% growth in the second quarter and 18% in the third. The second quarter missed expectations narrowly ($1.90 against $1.92), which shows the numbers are not guaranteed.
  • ICE paid a 33% premium for MarketAxess after a first half in which its own shares fell, and then said it would buy back more of its stock, not less. Doing both is a choice about leverage, and the company has attached a deleveraging target to it.
  • The Black Knight purchase of 2023 has delivered three years of integration, and the segment's accounting margin is still 2%. The adjusted margin of 40% shows the cash economics, and the gap is acquisition amortization, not an operating loss.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • The multiple drifts back toward 17 to 19 times on earnings that keep growing, so the price moves toward value without a loss of cash flow.
  • Recurring revenue growth stays at or above the guided 8% to 9% while transaction revenue normalizes.
  • MarketAxess closes on schedule with the stated 3.4 times and the first quarters show the planned fall in leverage.

Yellow

  • Transaction revenue growth stays above 20% for another quarter, which makes my normalization look too small.
  • Closing slips beyond the first quarter of 2027, or leverage at closing is above 3.4 times.
  • Mortgage technology revenue growth falls below 4%.

Red

  • Monthly statistics show falling volumes in the main energy and equity-derivative contracts, with new competing products taking share.
  • Buybacks are cut to protect the balance sheet, or the 3.0 times target is pushed out.
  • Recurring revenue growth falls below 5%.

The next hard data are the monthly volume statistics, the fourth quarter results in early February and the closing of MarketAxess.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Trading volumes fall back and the exit multiple drops to 17 times. The Base return from $172.22 is then 3.7% a year.
  2. Cash flow grows 4% a year and not 7%. The value at a 10% rate is then $125.0, 27% below the price.
  3. The MarketAxess price turns out too high, or financing and integration weigh on cash flow just when buybacks are meant to rise.

The weakest point of the model is the normalization of trading volumes, which cannot be read from any filing, and the sensitivity above shows it moves the Base by about $9 either way. The strongest bull argument is that ICE has compounded through every kind of market for twenty years, that recurring revenue and a 4.7% cash yield protect the floor better than my Bear case allows, and that a lower required return of about 9% is entirely reasonable for a low-beta infrastructure franchise, which would make the shares fairly priced. The strongest bear argument is that the price already needs that lower return, a multiple of 21 times that is 40% above where it stood in June, and no help from volumes, in a year when competition for exchange business is arriving.

Final verdict: hold at $172.22, do not add

ICE is the kind of business that is easy to admire and hard to buy cheaply. The record is real, the cash is real and the balance sheet has a plan. The price is the only part of the story that has changed this year, and it has changed by 40%.

Verdict: HOLD at $172.22, do not add. Weighted Base $142.44, 17.3% below the price, cross-check $143.19, Confidence C (16.3/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $81.90 (range $70.76 to $95.83).

An existing holder has no valuation reason to sell a franchise like this at once, because the Base return is about 6% a year and 8% if the multiple holds, and I would consider trimming near $190. A new buyer earns about 6% a year in my Base and about 13% in the Bull, and under my rules new money waits for a price near $82. At $120 the Base return would be about 14% a year, but that is still above my maximum buy price. The next review is the fourth quarter report and the MarketAxess closing. The record is twenty years long. The price needs five more.
