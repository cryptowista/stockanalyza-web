## What Brandywine is

Brandywine owns, develops and manages 57 core office and mixed-use properties totaling 10.8 million square feet, concentrated in Philadelphia's central business district and suburbs (about 73% of core net operating income) and Austin, Texas (about 8%). It also holds development projects, including life-science space at 3151 Market Street and the mixed-use Uptown ATX campus in Austin, and interests in several unconsolidated joint ventures.

The two markets tell different stories. Philadelphia CBD and the Pennsylvania suburbs are performing: second-quarter comparable same-store net operating income rose 1.9% on a cash basis. Austin is not. The Austin portfolio was 67% occupied at quarter end and, per management's own comment on the earnings call, dragged overall company occupancy down by more than four percentage points. Brandywine sold its largest remaining wholly owned Austin office property, 405 Colorado, on July 9 for $151.0 million, which should shrink that drag going forward.

FFO (funds from operations) is the REIT-industry earnings measure: net income adjusted to add back real-estate depreciation. It is a starting point, not a cash-flow number, because it says nothing about the capital a landlord must keep spending on tenant improvements and leasing commissions to hold onto existing tenants. That's what CAD (cash available for distribution) tries to capture, and it is Brandywine's own disclosure, not an outside estimate: FFO minus straight-line rent adjustments minus revenue-maintaining capital expenditures. In the first half of 2026 that capital took $17.0 million out of $43.6 million of FFO, and Brandywine also spent a further $15.4 million on revenue-creating capital (backfilling vacant space) and $46.7 million on development, none of which CAD counts as recurring.

## Which numbers matter for Brandywine

The primary metric is normalized CAD per share, what I call hard AFFO to keep the after-recurring-capital idea explicit. My estimates are Bear **$0.22**, Base **$0.31** and Bull **$0.40**. These sit close to, and are now grounded by, the company's own reported number: first-half CAD of $29.1 million on 181.0 million weighted-average diluted shares and units is $0.16 for the half, or about **$0.32** annualized. My Base of $0.31 is 3% below that run rate, which is conservative given the business plan explicitly targets a declining CAD payout ratio (from about 90% early in the year toward 70% by year end) as leasing capital normalizes.

The gap between FFO and hard AFFO is the whole investment case. At the $0.55 FFO guidance midpoint, the shares look like a 5.2x cash machine. At $0.31 of hard AFFO, they are a 9.2x cash flow, and the dividend eats essentially all of it.

## Shares, debt and the maturity wall

Brandywine had 174.6 million common shares outstanding at June 30, and 182.1 million fully diluted (including options, restricted shares and operating-partnership units) at the quarter's spot price. Management's FFO guidance uses a 180.0 million weighted-average diluted share count for the full year.

Total debt at June 30 was $2,624.9 million face value ($2,617.0 million net of premiums and financing costs), against $38.7 million of cash and restricted cash, for reported net debt of **$2,586.2 million**. The debt is 94% unsecured, 78% fixed rate, at a weighted-average effective rate of 6.2%.

The 2027 wall of $849 million is the single largest near-term event: it combines the $450 million of 3.95% notes maturing in November 2027 with the $600 million credit facility (extended to December 2026, with room to push further) and the $250 million term loan, both due June 2027. Refinancing that at anything close to today's rates would push the weighted average cost of debt meaningfully higher.

Since quarter end, Brandywine used the proceeds from its two July dispositions, King of Prussia ($37.8 million net) and the Austin sale ($146.1 million net), to pay the $149 million credit-facility balance down to zero, and reported about $35 million of cash on hand. On a pro forma basis that puts net debt closer to **$2.44 billion**. It's real progress, and it's also less than the roughly $97 million of the $305 million disposition target still to close.

A separate warning sign: in the first quarter of 2026, S&P downgraded Brandywine's senior unsecured credit rating from BB+ to BB-. Under a coupon step-up clause in the 2028 notes, that downgrade raises their rate by 50 basis points to 8.80%, effective this September. On the bond indenture's own leverage covenant, Brandywine was at 57.0% against a 60% limit at June 30, only three points of headroom.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $2.86 the shares trade at 9.2x my Base hard AFFO of $0.31. Turned around: at the Base multiple of 11x, the price implies sustainable hard AFFO of only **$0.26 per share**, which sits between my Bear ($0.22) and Base ($0.31) estimates and below the company's own annualized first-half CAD run rate of $0.32. So the market is pricing meaningfully worse cash conversion than Brandywine actually delivered in the first half of 2026, or is pricing a higher discount for the refinancing risk. Either way, the price does not need a full office recovery, but it also does not yet pay for the recovery Brandywine has already shown.

## How the buy price is set

For a Confidence C score, the framework's base margin of safety is higher than for a well-covered business, and the leverage and structural office risk here push it to the top of that range: **40%**. Applied to the $3.45 Base, the **maximum buy price is $2.07**. The preferred accumulation zone runs from the $1.70 Bear value to $2.07. The shares would need to fall about 27.6% from $2.86 to reach it.

## Dividend

The quarterly dividend is $0.08, or $0.32 a year, a yield of 11.2% at $2.86. Against 2026 FFO guidance it looks safe, at a 58% payout. Against the company's own second-quarter CAD it was not: the reported CAD payout ratio was 102.9% for the quarter and 97.5% for the first half. Management's own business plan targets bringing the annualized CAD payout ratio down from around 90% toward 70% by year end, which depends on leasing capital normalizing as retention holds and disposition proceeds reduce the drag from Austin. Until that shows up in the numbers, the double-digit yield is a warning as much as an attraction.

## Reasons to own BDN

- **Material discount to Base value.** The shares trade well below both the CAD-based and NAV-based fair values.
- **Real leasing improvement.** Retention jumped to 85% in the second quarter and same-store cash NOI grew 1.9%.
- **Leased ahead of occupied.** 90.6% leased versus 89.1% occupied means commencement of already-signed leases should lift revenue without new leasing.
- **Asset-sale proof of liquidity.** $208 million of 2026 dispositions closed near or above book value, with the credit facility already repaid from the proceeds.
- **High operating leverage to a recovery.** A modest improvement in occupancy or leasing spreads moves hard AFFO, and therefore the valuation, by a large percentage.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: the 2027-2029 maturity wall.** $849 million comes due in 2027 alone. Refinancing it expensively, or being forced to sell more assets at a discount to do so, would hit both cash flow and NAV.
- **Credit downgrades are already biting.** The 2028 notes step up 50 basis points to 8.80% this September following the S&P downgrade to BB-, and the bond leverage covenant has only 3 points of headroom left.
- **Structural office demand.** Hybrid work continues to pressure occupancy and rents industry-wide; cash rent spreads were still negative 4.2% in the second quarter.
- **Leverage magnifies bad outcomes.** At 9.0x combined net debt to EBITDA, a property-value decline hits the equity layer disproportionately.
- **Buybacks versus deleveraging.** The plan allocates a smaller share of asset-sale proceeds to opportunistic share repurchases; every dollar there is a dollar not paying down the maturity wall.
- **Dividend coverage.** A payout ratio above 100% of CAD is not sustainable indefinitely without either growth in cash flow or a cut.

## Management execution

Management delivered on what it controls this quarter: retention up sharply, same-store NOI positive, the Avira construction loan refinanced at a fixed 5.81%, the 2026 disposition target raised and largely executed, and the credit facility paid down with the proceeds. It hasn't yet delivered a CAD payout ratio under 100%, and the credit-rating downgrade happened on its watch. **Assessment: 12/20, fair. The next real test is whether the 2027 wall gets refinanced without further credit deterioration.**

## Final verdict: hold at $2.86, watchlist for new money below $2.07

Brandywine's leasing metrics tell a genuine recovery story. Its balance sheet still needs one. A stock can't be judged cheap on a metric that isn't the one paying the dividend. On the metric that is, cash after tenant capital, the payout ratio is still over 100%.

**Verdict: WATCHLIST at $2.86. Triangulated Base value $3.45, upside 20.6%, Confidence C (16/25). Required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $2.07.**

The case improves if the CAD payout ratio drops toward the plan's 70% target while occupancy holds, or if the 2027 maturity wall gets refinanced without further credit deterioration, or if the price falls into the buy zone with the fundamentals intact.
