Unity makes the software that builds a large share of the world's mobile games, and it now earns most of its growth from selling ads inside them. The third quarter, reported on November 5, was the best it has had: revenue up 32%, the first profit under accounting rules since the 2020 listing, and a guide for next quarter that implies the core business growing about 66%. The shares have gone from a closing low of $17.13 in February to $48.24, and Reuters reported that Silver Lake and Saudi Arabia's PIF discussed buying the company at about $25 billion. My Base case has Unity growing about 13% a year for five years at a 48% margin, and it is still worth $44.41 a share standalone. The market is paying 8.6% more than that, for a story that has to keep working for years.

The Bear value is $16.78 and the Bull value is $71.28, so the price sits 187% above Bear and 32% below Bull. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 42.5%, so the maximum buy price is **$25.53**. The shares are 89% above that and would need to fall 47% to reach it.

Valuation route: a software and advertising platform valued on the free cash flow that belongs to shareholders after stock compensation, taxes and capital spending, not on adjusted EBITDA. The primary method is a ten-year discounted cash flow. The cross-check is five years of cash flow plus a discounted exit at a multiple of EBITDA after stock compensation. Economic classification: Standard. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What Unity is

Unity has two businesses. Create sells the game engine and tools that developers use to build games, mostly as subscriptions, and made $165 million of revenue in the third quarter. Grow sells advertising: game studios pay Unity to find players, and other studios earn money by showing those ads. Its newer ad system, Vector, uses data from games built on the engine to match ads to players. Strategic Grow revenue was $431.5 million, up 90% from a year earlier, and management said Vector grew 126% in the year and 40% from the previous quarter, double its own guide of about 20%.

The business was cleaned up in 2026. The ironSource ad network was shut down in the second quarter and the Supersonic publishing business was sold on August 4, so the old "non-strategic" revenue fell 73% to $25.5 million and will be about $5 million in the fourth quarter and zero in 2027. What remains is the engine, Vector and a smaller set of tools. The share price has followed the change: 52-week range $16.78 to $54.27, and a one-year high close of $52.81 on November 13, before a slide to $44.75 on November 20.

## The reported takeover talks

On November 5 Reuters reported that Silver Lake, which already owns about 8% of Unity, and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund approached the company in recent weeks about a take-private deal at roughly $25 billion. The two investors were part of the group that took Electronic Arts private in a $55 billion deal that closed in August. Unity is working with an investment bank, no agreement has been reached, the terms could change, and Unity declined to comment. The $25 billion figure was discussed before the latest results, when the market value was lower. I found no later update, and management did not mention the report on the earnings call.

On my share count of about 459 million, $25 billion is about $54.50 a share, which is 13% above today's price and 23% above my standalone Base (on 443.8 million basic shares it is $56.30, on 498 million diluted shares $50.20). I do not put any of this in the Base, the Bear or the Bull. A deal that is never signed is worth nothing, and the terms, the price and whether it is cash are unknown. It does change how to read the verdict: the shares are already priced partly for a sale, and a buyer paying $54.50 would be paying more than my standalone value.

## Why I deduct stock compensation and use a normal tax rate

Reported free cash flow of $219.7 million in the quarter treats stock compensation as free, includes interest earned on $2.6 billion of cash, and benefits from a low tax rate. I make four adjustments. Stock compensation of $75 million a quarter in 2027 ($288 million a year in my Base, 9.0% of revenue falling to 6.5%) is deducted as a real cost, because it dilutes shareholders: the share count rose from 427.9 million in October 2025 to 443.8 million at September 30, a 3.7% increase. Interest income is excluded, because the cash goes into the bridge instead. Tax starts at 8% in 2027 (management's 5% to 10%) and rises to 21% by 2030, because Unity's accumulated losses will shield profit for a while but not forever (I could not confirm the size of the loss carryforwards). Capital spending is 1.5% of revenue, above the near-zero level of the quarter.

On that basis my Base owner free cash flow is $977 million in 2027, or $2.13 a share, against about $880 million of reported free cash flow at the third-quarter rate. All values below use the same definition.

## Primary valuation: ten years of owner free cash flow

The primary method discounts ten years of owner free cash flow at 10%, the framework default, and I test 9% and 11%. The valuation date is the September 30 balance sheet, so the fourth quarter of 2026 counts as a stub ($194 million) and 2027 to 2036 follow.

Base revenue is $3.20 billion in 2027, 16% above the fourth-quarter run rate of $2.77 billion, then grows 16%, 13%, 11% and 9% to $5.08 billion in 2031, and 7% down to 3.5% by 2036. Adjusted EBITDA margin rises from 42% in the fourth quarter to 44% in 2027 and 48% from 2030. Owner free cash flow is $977 million in 2027, then $1,166, $1,297, $1,427 and $1,576 million, reaching $2,050 million in 2036. Terminal growth is 3%.

The ten years are worth $19.97 a share, the terminal value $24.74 and the net bridge $0.27. The primary value is **$44.98**.

Terminal value share: 55% of the primary value, at a ten-year horizon, below the 75% level where I would check that the terminal value is supported by sustainable flows.

## Secondary valuation: five years of cash flow and an exit at 12 times EBITDA after stock compensation

The cross-check takes the stub and the next five years of owner free cash flow and values the company at the end of 2031 at 12 times that year's EBITDA after stock compensation of $2,081 million, or $24.97 billion. Discounted at 10%, the five years add $10.58 a share, the exit adds $32.98 and the bridge adds $0.27. The secondary value is **$43.84**.

**Why 12 times.** By 2031 my Base has Unity growing 9% with a 48% margin, a business that turns about 76% of EBITDA after stock compensation into free cash flow. Twelve times is 15.8 times that year's free cash flow, which at a 10% return implies growth of about 3.5% a year after 2031, close to my terminal growth of 3%. A growing ad platform with a protected engine could deserve more, and a business that depends on one ad system could deserve less. Bear uses 8 times, a company whose ad growth has stalled and whose margin is 35%. Bull uses 15 times, a company still growing near 9% a year with a 52% margin.

Exit value share: 75% of the secondary value comes from the exit, at a horizon of five years, below the 85% level where I check that the exit is supported by sustainable flows.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Run backward on the Base case, $48.24 needs one of these: revenue growth 1.8 points higher every year after 2027, owner free cash flow 14% higher every year, an adjusted EBITDA margin 3.5 points higher, or a discount rate of 9.2% instead of 10%. On the cross-check method alone it needs an exit multiple of 13.6 times, and on the primary method alone terminal growth of 3.8%. The market is pricing a case slightly better than my Base and well short of the Bull: Vector keeps growing about 15% a year in the middle of the decade and the margin goes past 50%.

As a return, buying at $48.24 and holding the Base case to the end of 2031, with the cash that builds up added to an exit at 12 times 2031 EBITDA after stock compensation, gives about **9.2% a year**, below the 10% I require. The same math gives -15.9% a year in Bear and 23.9% in Bull. Unity pays no dividend and has no buyback program that I could find, so the return comes entirely from growth and the exit.

On earnings, GAAP profit is one quarter old, so a price to earnings ratio is not meaningful, and a growth-adjusted ratio (PEG) would reward a profit that started this quarter. Growth durability: structural, with a high concentration risk, because 90% of the Vector growth came from existing advertisers. The free cash flow growth check says the same: free cash flow was $151 million in the third quarter of 2025 and $220 million in 2026, so I use no multi-year rate from it.

## How the buy price is set

Confidence C starts at a 42.5% margin of safety. I added nothing, because each risk is charged once: the advertising cycle, platform rules and competition are in the Bear scenario, and the thin evidence for the forward path is in the Confidence score. The reported takeover talks are not in the margin of safety either. The two methods differ by 2.6%, so no divergence modifier applies. A 42.5% discount to the $44.41 Base gives a **maximum buy price of $25.53**. The framework's valuation gap (the price is within 15% of Base) maps to a Hold, and the price is above the maximum buy price. A single input moving by 15% of its own size, such as the 10% discount rate moving to 11.5%, changes the Base by 12.5%, so the Base is moderately sensitive to one assumption, and growth 3 points lower every year lowers it by 12.9%.

## Dividend and buybacks

Unity pays no dividend, and management gave no details on buybacks, a free cash flow outlook or future convertible notes. It cited flexibility for product investment, possible acquisitions and returning capital to shareholders. With $2.6 billion of cash and about $1 billion a year of owner free cash flow, it has room for a program, and a buyback at a price below my Base would add value, but at $48.24 it would not. I do not credit any in the value.

## Reasons to own U

- **Growth that is accelerating.** Strategic revenue grew 59% and the fourth-quarter guide implies about 66%, the seventh quarter in a row of faster growth.
- **Operating leverage.** Adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 23% to 41% in a year, and expenses were flat in dollars in the quarter.
- **A strong balance sheet.** $2.6 billion of cash covers every convertible note, and the 2027 notes are covered with a large surplus.
- **Proprietary data.** Vector uses runtime data from games built on the engine, and more than 90% of new projects opt in.
- **Strategic value.** A buyer already holds about 8% and reportedly discussed $25 billion, which is above my standalone value.

## What could go wrong?

- **One product.** Vector produces nearly all the growth and has a short track record. Its revenue also depends on a handful of large advertisers.
- **Competition and platforms.** Mobile ad networks compete on the same install budgets, and Apple and Google control the privacy rules that decide how well ads can be targeted.
- **Margin durability.** The 41% margin is new. Part of it comes from selling Supersonic and shutting ironSource, which removed revenue and costs together.
- **Dilution.** Stock compensation is $75 million a quarter in 2027, the share count rose 3.7% in a year, and two convertible notes sit near or below the price.
- **Deal risk.** If the takeover talks end without an offer, any premium in the price may disappear. If an offer comes, it may be above or below my Base.
- **Trust with developers.** The company has had public disputes with the developers who use its engine over pricing and policy changes. I did not review the current state of them for this analysis.

## Management execution

Management, led by President and CEO Matt Bromberg, has delivered the cleanup it promised: the ironSource ad network is shut, Supersonic is sold, expenses are flat in dollars, margin is up 18 points and the first GAAP profit has arrived. Vector grew at double its guide in the quarter, and revenue and earnings beat analysts' estimates. Against that, the record of reaching this point is short, the company described a "portfolio reset" in 2025 that cut revenue, the share count is rising and no 2027 guidance was given. Management credibility is **11/20**: adequate and improving.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Vector's growth stalls once the shift from the old networks is complete, which is the Bear path.
2. A platform privacy change or a competitor reduces the targeting advantage that the engine's data gives Unity.
3. Stock compensation and convertible notes keep raising the share count faster than the free cash flow per share grows.

**Stacking audit.** Combining every generous choice (9% discount rate, 4% terminal growth, a 15x exit, growth 3 points higher and margin 3 points higher) gives $71.24. Combining every conservative one (11%, 2% terminal growth, a 9x exit, cash flow 20% lower and stock compensation 1 point higher) gives $29.96. The price of $48.24 is inside that range, and the Base of $44.41 sits 35% of the way up it. The choices that lean conservative are the full tax rate by 2030, stock compensation as a real cost, capital spending of 1.5% of revenue and the notes treated as debt. The ones that lean generous are 48% margins held for a decade, a high 2027 starting point (16% above the fourth-quarter run rate) and no allowance for a cyclical fall in advertising.
