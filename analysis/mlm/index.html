Martin Marietta reported record revenue and record adjusted EBITDA for the third quarter of 2026, and earnings per share from continuing operations fell 34%, to $3.94 from $5.97. The gap is interest and deal costs: $138 million of interest in the quarter against $59 million a year earlier, on the debt behind the $12.9 billion Lhoist North America (LNA) purchase, plus $99 million of acquisition and integration charges. At $502.37 the market pays 15.7 times my 2027 EBITDA estimate for the combined company, and the shares are 29.3% below their 52-week high.

At $502.37 the shares trade 17.2% above my weighted Base value of $428.56. The Bear value of $270.44 is 46.2% below the price and the Bull value of $642.01 is 27.8% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade C, which sets a required margin of safety of 47.5% and a maximum buy price of $225.00. The verdict is Hold, do not add: the business is good, but at this price the Base case earns 7.4% a year, below my 10% hurdle, and the Bear case is a loss of nearly half.

Valuation route: a cyclical building-materials company (aggregates, lime and magnesia products) that has just changed its size and debt, so I use a transition model and not a single multiple. Primary method: EBITDA for 2027 to 2030, with debt paid down from cash flow, sold at an exit multiple at the end of 2030 and discounted at 10%. Cross-check: ten years of free cash flow before interest, discounted at 8.4% with a terminal value. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Martin Marietta is

Martin Marietta sells aggregates (crushed stone, sand and gravel) for roads, buildings and data centers, and since August 21 it also sells lime, limestone and magnesia-based products through LNA. Management says infrastructure, data centers, power generation and warehouses together are about half of aggregates shipments, while housing and light commercial building remain weak. Roughly half of heritage shipments are fixed-base work and half is quoted work.

The exit multiple needs a reason. Today the market pays 15.6 times my 2027 estimate. In 2030 about 72% of EBITDA would be aggregates, which I value near 17 times, and about 28% specialties, which I value near 12 to 13 times (my estimates of the mix and my judgments of the multiples), so the blend is about 15.7 times, and I take 15.0 times because leverage and a cyclical mix deserve a small discount. At an 8.4% cost of capital and the 2030 cash flows, 15.0 times implies perpetual growth of about 4.2%, above my 3.5% terminal rate in the cross-check. That is the premium the market pays for aggregate pricing power, and the next section shows what it costs if the premium fades.

Dividend and buybacks

The dividend is $3.36 a share a year, a yield of 0.67%, and the company paid $161 million of it in nine months. It bought back $200 million of stock in the same period, and about 10.7 million shares remain authorized. Management did not discuss buybacks on the call. It said about $500 million of debt will be repaid in the coming weeks and that bolt-on acquisitions will continue, so I assume no buybacks in my Base and all spare cash goes to debt.

Reasons to own Martin Marietta

  • Aggregates are a strong business: 2025 aggregates gross profit rose 16% to a record $1.68 billion, and aggregates cash gross profit was a third-quarter record of $696 million this year.
  • LNA is ahead of plan so far: $104 million of EBITDA in 41 days, with management guiding about $820 million for 2027 and an incremental cash flow target of $350 million that it reaffirmed.
  • Demand is split in a useful way. Data-center shipments more than doubled year on year, and infrastructure and heavy nonresidential work is about half of aggregates volume.
  • Pricing should help in 2027. Management expects mid-to-high single-digit mix-adjusted pricing and organic price-cost to turn positive early in the year.
  • The business is investment grade, with $1.4 billion of unused liquidity and a covenant that is comfortable in my Base.
  • Free cash flow reached a quarterly record of $426 million, and in my Base net debt falls to about 2.0 times EBITDA by 2030.

What could go wrong?

  • Leverage. Net debt is about 4.0 times my 2027 EBITDA, the covenant steps down from 4.75 to 4.25 to 3.75 times, and two of three rating agencies have negative outlooks. In my Bear case the ratio peaks at 4.1 times against 4.25 times.
  • Heritage weakness. Organic shipments fell 1.1% in the third quarter and management expects a high single-digit drop in the fourth, with price-cost still negative. Residential and light commercial construction are soft, and the first quarter of 2027 faces a strong weather comparison.
  • Costs. Diesel is about a $25 million quarterly headwind and management plans 2027 on about $4.70 a gallon, with cost inflation of 3% to 4% a ton.
  • Price paid. LNA cost about 15.7 times its 2027 EBITDA. On the capital in the balance sheet the combined company earns about 5% after tax by my estimate, below an 8.4% cost of capital, so the deal needs synergies and growth to create value.
  • Dilution. The share count rose 18.4% in August, so every dollar of EBITDA is now shared across 71 million shares and not 60 million.
  • Refinancing. About $1.2 billion matures in 2027, including two notes with coupons near 3.5%, and replacing them will cost more.
  • Unverified inputs. LNA has been in the numbers for only 41 days, purchase accounting is preliminary, and the filing does not give the actual covenant ratio.

Management execution

  • The record is good through 2025: revenue of $6.15 billion, up 9%, adjusted EBITDA from continuing operations of $2.07 billion, up 17%, and operating cash flow up 22% to $1.8 billion. Net debt was 2.3 times EBITDA at the end of 2025.
  • The company completed a share repurchase program that began in 2015, buying back 8.8 million shares, about 13.7% of the shares, for $2.26 billion, according to a third-party summary of its filings.
  • The Quikrete exchange in February gave a $1.4 billion after-tax gain and was followed within six months by the LNA deal. Two large transactions in one year change what the company is.
  • Guidance moved down at the third quarter, when the 2026 midpoints were lowered, and the company has not given 2027 guidance.
  • Capital allocation turned from buybacks to a large, debt-funded acquisition. The $5.9 billion of stock was issued at about $535 to $539 a share, above today's price, which is better for existing holders than issuing at today's price would have been.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Organic price-cost turns positive in the first half of 2027, as management expects.
  • Net debt to EBITDA falls below 3.5 times by the end of 2027 with the covenant cushion widening.
  • LNA delivers first-quarter 2027 EBITDA near management's implied $189 million, or the company puts a number on cross-selling synergies.

Yellow

  • Fourth-quarter organic shipments fall by more than a high single digit, or 2027 guidance starts below $3.0 billion of EBITDA.
  • A new acquisition is announced while net debt is still above 3.5 times EBITDA.
  • Diesel stays above $4.70 a gallon and the price-cost gap does not close.

Red

  • A rating downgrade to BBB- or lower, or a covenant amendment.
  • LNA first-quarter EBITDA below about $170 million, which would put the $800 million assumption at risk.
  • Heritage EBITDA falls in 2027 and the company still reports net debt above 4 times.

The next hard data are the fourth-quarter results with 2027 guidance, expected in February 2027 (I have not confirmed the date), and the first quarter that includes a full three months of LNA.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Heritage EBITDA does not grow in 2027 or 2028 because price-cost stays negative. The weighted Base falls to $383, 23.8% below the price.
  2. LNA earns $700 million and not $800 million. The weighted Base falls to $412, 18.1% below the price.
