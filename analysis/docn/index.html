DigitalOcean: priced for 50% growth and a 31% cash margin, so reduce at $99.97

DOCN, NYSE. Published October 30, 2026. Price as of the October 29, 2026 regular session close. Values in US dollars. Latest reported quarter: second quarter 2026; the third quarter will be reported on November 3, 2026.

Reduce at $99.97

DigitalOcean trades 83% above my Base value of $54.60, and the price is above even my Bull value of $95.89. At $99.97 the shares pay about 25 times my estimate of 2027 EBITDA after stock pay. To justify the price, free cash flow after stock pay would have to reach about 31% of revenue in ten years, against 13% in my Base. A 50% margin of safety puts the buy range at $20.78 to $33.82. Existing holders should consider trimming, and new money should wait.

DigitalOcean spent 2026 turning from a cloud provider for small developers into an AI cloud with large customers. Revenue grew 29% in the second quarter, annual recurring revenue reached $1,125 million, and the company raised $888 million of equity in the first half and issued another 12.5 million shares in July to retire convertible notes. The shares are still priced as if the growth and the margins of the new business are already proven, and the second quarter does not show that yet.

At $99.97 on October 29, 2026, the shares trade 83.1% above my Base value of $54.60, so the Base is 45.4% below the price. The Bear value of $25.98 is 74.0% below the price and the Bull value of $95.89 is 4.1% below it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The required margin of safety of 50% gives a buy range of $20.78 to $33.82 and a conservative limit of $20.78. The verdict is Reduce. The third quarter report on November 3 is the next test.

Valuation route: Cloud infrastructure and AI compute provider, valued on unlevered free cash flow after stock pay and capital spending. Economic classification: quality growth, with the return on the new AI capital not yet established. Primary method: ten years of free cash flow with a terminal value. Cross-check: 17 times 2027 estimated EBITDA after stock pay. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What DigitalOcean is

DigitalOcean rents computing power, storage and managed software to more than 680,000 customers, historically small companies and developers. Its newer business is AI computing: GPU servers and an inference platform, sold increasingly to large customers on multi-year contracts. Customers paying more than $1 million a year generated 23% of revenue in the second quarter, and their revenue grew 214%. Contract life on these deals is above three years.

The company has about 155 megawatts of committed data center capacity. Management says demand for its AI cloud exceeds supply, which is the reason it needs so much capital.

It expects growth of about 30% for the year, 35% or more by the fourth quarter and above 50% in 2027.

What the earnings are made of

  • Adjusted measures leave out real costs. Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock pay, which was 11.6% of revenue in the second quarter. I treat it as a cash cost in every value in this analysis.
  • Adjusted free cash flow leaves out financed equipment. It subtracts purchases of property, equipment and software, but not the $51.5 million of equipment acquired under financing arrangements in the first half, nor the principal repaid on those obligations.
  • Leases are growing fast. Finance leases and equipment financing rose from $130.5 million at the end of 2025 to $577.7 million at June 30, and operating leases stood at $479 million. These are the cost of capacity that the revenue growth depends on.
  • Margins are guided down. The third quarter adjusted EBITDA margin guide of 38% to 39% is below the second quarter's 40%, and the full-year adjusted free cash flow margin guide of 11% to 13% is half the second quarter's 22%.

Adjusted free cash flow for the first half was $62.8 million, and second-quarter stock pay alone was $32.7 million.

The July transaction changed the picture. I count the remaining $153 million of 2030 notes as 3.9 million shares at the $39.17 conversion price, and I count no value for the capped call, which is conservative. For the per-share values I use 124.0 million shares: about 117.0 million outstanding after the July issue and the planned repurchase, 3.9 million for the remaining 2030 notes and about 3.1 million for equity awards, which is my estimate. Management's own guidance for the third quarter of 126 million to 127 million includes the 2026 notes. Net debt for my purposes is $123 million: debt of about $312 million that is settled in cash, plus $578 million of finance leases and equipment financing, less $767 million of cash. The June cash figure is the latest I have, and the third quarter report may change it.

Secondary valuation: EBITDA multiple

I estimate 2027 revenue at $1.669 billion (42% growth) and EBITDA after stock pay at 30% of it, or $501 million. At 17 times this gives an enterprise value of $8.52 billion, and after $123 million of net debt, $67.63 a share. The market pays about 25 times the same figure.

I use 17 times because the business grows 30% to 40%, but earns less per dollar of revenue as it adds GPU capacity, and because stock pay and leased equipment are real costs. The first method implies about 10.5 times the same earnings in the Base, so the gap between the two methods is mostly a gap between a market-style multiple and a view of what the business will earn on its capital in the long run.

Historical cross-check and growth diagnostic

Revenue grew 29% in the second quarter, and the third quarter guide of $304 million to $307 million implies continued growth. The growth is structural for the AI contracts, which are longer and larger, and I have not identified acquisitions as a main driver. Earnings per share, though, are not following: non-GAAP earnings per share were $0.45 in the second quarter and the third quarter guide is $0.28 to $0.30. I did not collect a multi-year history of the share price multiple, so the historical component of the valuation score is neutral.

Valuation triangulation

  • Primary (free cash flow): $41.56, weight 50%.
  • Secondary (17 times 2027 EBITDA after stock pay): $67.63, weight 50%.
  • Divergence between the two: 47.7% of their midpoint, above the 30% limit, so I flag a valuation divergence.
  • Weighted Base: $54.60. The unweighted range of $41.56 to $67.63 is the honest uncertainty, and $54.60 is a point inside it.

The gap comes from one assumption. If the terminal free cash flow margin were 20% instead of 13%, the first method would give $64.48, close to the second, and at 25% it would give $80.85. The weights are equal because I cannot show that either margin is right. Both methods are below the price, so the choice between them does not change the verdict.

Dividend and buybacks

DigitalOcean pays no dividend. It plans to buy back about 500,000 shares to offset part of the July issue. The share count rose from 91.9 million to 105.0 million in six months, and by another 12.5 million in July.

Reasons to own DOCN

  • Revenue grew 29% in the second quarter, with AI customer recurring revenue up 212% and $93 million of incremental recurring revenue in the quarter.
  • Larger customers and longer contracts: contract life above three years.
  • A stronger balance sheet: $767 million of cash and the retirement of $472 million of convertible notes with shares, not cash.
  • Management guides to growth of 35% or more by the fourth quarter and above 50% in 2027, and says demand exceeds the capacity it can build.

What could go wrong?

  • Guidance shows the AI build-out lowering margins and earnings per share: a lower EBITDA margin in the third quarter, a full-year free cash flow margin of 11% to 13% and third quarter earnings of $0.28 to $0.30 a share.
  • Capital needs are large and partly financed through leases, which keeps adjusted free cash flow high and pushes the cost into future payments.
  • Further share issuance dilutes holders: the share count went from 91.9 million at the end of 2025 to about 117 million after the July issue, an increase of about 27%.
  • A small number of large customers may account for a growing share of AI revenue, and their contracts may be renegotiated.
  • The shares could fall if the third quarter report, due November 3, disappoints.

Management execution

  • Management gave 2026 guidance for revenue, margins and earnings per share and targets growth above 50% in 2027.
  • The 2030 notes were retired with shares at $117.54, a price above today's, which was a good use of the high share price for existing holders.
  • Stock pay of $32.7 million a quarter is 11.6% of revenue, which weakens the margin story.
  • I did not find a figure for the capital and financing needed to reach the 2027 growth target.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • The third quarter report on November 3 shows revenue above $307 million and an adjusted EBITDA margin above 39%.
  • Free cash flow including financed equipment and lease payments is disclosed and stays positive.
  • Non-GAAP earnings per share turn up from $0.28 to $0.30 in the fourth quarter.

Yellow

  • The 2026 convertible notes are repaid in cash and the cash balance falls below $500 million.
  • Stock-based compensation stays above 10% of revenue.
  • Guidance for 2027 growth is cut below 50%.

Red

  • Another large share issue at a price below $100.
  • Third quarter revenue falls below the guided $304 million to $307 million.
  • A large AI customer reduces its commitment.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The AI business earns a free cash flow margin well below 13% after leased equipment and stock pay, which would make the Base too high.
  2. Growth slows below 30% in 2027, which would remove the case for a high multiple.
  3. Further dilution of 10% or more a year, which would lower the value per share even if the business grows.

The weakest point of the model is the long-term margin of the AI business, which no one can yet show. The strongest bull argument is that AI demand for DigitalOcean's capacity is as strong as management says, that growth above 50% in 2027 becomes 40% or more for years, and that margins on long contracts are higher than I assume, which would justify the price. The strongest bear argument is that the second quarter already shows margins and earnings per share falling as AI grows, and that the price has not adjusted to it.

Final verdict: reduce at $99.97

DigitalOcean is a growing company with a stronger balance sheet and a real AI business. At $99.97 the market pays for growth and margins that the reported numbers do not yet show, and the price is above even my Bull value.

Verdict: REDUCE at $99.97. Weighted Base $54.60, 45.4% below the price, cross-check $67.63, Confidence D (10/25). Required margin of safety 50%, buy range $20.78 to $33.82, conservative buy limit $20.78.

For an existing holder, the original cost of the shares is irrelevant. I would trim, and keep a smaller position only if I accept management's 2027 growth target and a very high long-term margin. A review is warranted on November 3, when the third quarter report is published. New money waits for a price at or below $33.82.
