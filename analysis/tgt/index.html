Every screener shows Target at about 15 times this year's earnings. Management guides to $10.65 to $11.40 a share for fiscal 2026, and $2.14 of that is tariff refunds, a payment from the federal government that will not repeat. Take it out and the stock at $165.83 trades at 18.7 times earnings, and that is the multiple to judge.

The quarter itself was strong. Third-quarter sales rose 7.1% to $27.07 billion, comparable sales rose 5.6% with traffic up 5.0%, and the stock has roughly doubled from its 52-week low of $83.44, set a year ago. A real turnaround is under way. The open question is what it is worth when the one-time refunds are removed and the heavier spending that comes with it is included.

My triangulated Base value is $147.41. The Bear value of $104.18 is 37.2% below the price and the Bull value of $183.05 is 10.4% above it. At Confidence C my method requires a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives an entry range of $81.81 to $87.72. The lower end is the conservative buy limit, and the stock would have to fall another 47.1% to 50.7% to reach it. This is a hold, and new money should not add.

Valuation route: a large U.S. retailer with stable, needs-based categories and a growth program that is raising capital spending. Economic classification: Standard Company (3/12). Why 15 times earnings is really 18.7 times

In February the Supreme Court ruled that the tariffs imposed under the emergency powers law were unlawful, and importers began collecting refunds. Target received $994 million before tax in the second quarter and $301 million in the third, a total of $1.3 billion in nine months, or $2.14 a share. In August the finance chief called the second-quarter payment the significant majority of what Target expects, and guidance includes only the refunds already booked.

The headline says third-quarter adjusted earnings per share rose 49%. Without the refund they rose 21%, which is still a good number and the one I carry forward.

What Target is

Target is a general merchandise retailer with more than 2,000 U.S. stores. In fiscal 2024 food and beverage made up 23% of sales and beauty and household essentials 30%, so about half the business is bought because the house ran out. The rest, mostly apparel and home, is discretionary, and that is where the turnaround has not arrived.

Michael Fiddelke became chief executive in February. His third quarter brought traffic growth of 5.0% after a 2.2% decline a year earlier, store comparable sales up 4.6%, digital up 9.8%, and non-merchandise sales (advertising through Roundel, the Target+ marketplace and membership programs) up 19.3%. Women's fashion and shoes are still weak, and management is cutting apparel style counts by about 20%.

Cash, debt and shares

At October 31 Target held $4,464 million in cash against $15,330 million of debt and other borrowings, a net financial debt of $10,866 million. Cash swings with the holiday inventory cycle, so I use the average of October 31 and January 31, which is $10,917 million. Operating leases of $3,719 million are left out of net debt because rent is already deducted in EBITDA. Net debt is 1.3 times EBITDA without refunds, or 1.7 times with leases, and management says it is managing to keep its middle-A credit ratings.

Target had 452.7 million shares outstanding at October 31. I add 3.6 million for dilutive awards, which gives 456.3 million shares, a market value of $75.67 billion and an enterprise value of $86.59 billion. One public quote page lists 494.7 million shares, which does not match the filing, and I did not use it. Stock compensation of about $300 million a year is already an expense inside operating income, so I do not deduct it again.

The primary Base value is $152.55. I chose 8.5x with two checks in mind. According to TickerLeague, Target's year-end EV to EBITDA ran between 6.3x and 8.1x from 2014 to 2019, and between 7.5x and 14.3x since, with a five-year average of 10.0x. Those figures come from one aggregator and may treat leases differently from mine. The stock now trades at 9.1x my Base EBITDA, so 8.5x sits between the old range and today's market.

The second check is what the multiple implies. With an 8.0% discount rate and a 12% return on new capital, 8.5x on this EBITDA only works if cash flow grows 4.3% a year forever, against 2.8% in my model. At 7.5x the implied growth is 3.2%. I accept 8.5x because retailers with stable demand are often priced on lower discount rates than mine, and the cash flow model below shows how much that choice is worth: about $10 a share. The Bear multiple of 7.5x sits below the 8x floor of the standard retailer band in my method, for a case where sales stall and the margin slips back to 4.5%.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $165.83 the enterprise value is $86.59 billion, which is 9.1x my Base EBITDA for the next twelve months, 10.0x trailing EBITDA without refunds and 9.0x fiscal 2027. Solving backwards with the rest of the Base case fixed, the price needs one of these: an operating margin path 0.75 point higher in every year, ending at 6.5% (against my 5.8%), a discount rate of 7.4% (against 8.0%), terminal growth of 4.2% (against 2.8%), or sales growth 2.7 points higher every year for five years. In the multiple method it needs 9.1x against my 8.5x. Each of these is a Bull-type assumption, and the margin one equals my Bull case.

How good is the growth?

The price to earnings ratio of 18.7 divided by my 8.4% earnings growth gives a PEG ratio of 2.2. I treat it as a diagnostic only, and part of the growth is a rebound from a year hurt by tariffs and markdowns, so it flatters the next two years more than the next ten. The structural part is the traffic gain of 5.0%, and it is only one quarter old.

Free cash flow has not grown. The last twelve months without refunds, $3.16 billion, compare with $5.08 billion in the fiscal year ended January 2022, a decline of 9.5% a year, or 8.9% a year per share ($6.93 against $10.78) because the share count fell. Capex of $5 billion to $6 billion in 2027 is the bet that this reverses, and the company says returns on its recent investment are strong, but its reported return on invested capital of 16.5% includes 3.0 points from refunds. Without them it is 13.5%, close to the 13.4% a year earlier.

Dividend and buybacks

The quarterly dividend is $1.16, or $4.64 a year, a yield of 2.8%. The board raised it 1.8% in June, which makes 2026 the 55th consecutive year of increases. The dividend costs about $2.1 billion a year, a 52% payout of earnings without refunds and 1.5 times covered by free cash flow of the last twelve months without refunds.

The cushion thins next year. My Base case free cash flow after interest for fiscal 2027 is about $2.2 billion, which covers the dividend only 1.05 times while capex rises to $5.5 billion. Buybacks are small: $251 million in the third quarter, 1.6 million shares at an average of $155.49, with about $8.0 billion of authorization left and a similar pace expected in the fourth quarter.

Reasons to own TGT

  • Traffic is back. Comparable traffic rose 5.0% against a 2.2% decline a year earlier, and traffic grew in five of six key categories.
  • Profit flow-through is real. Operating income without refunds rose 23.6% on sales up 7.1%.
  • Guidance keeps moving up. Earnings guidance without refunds is $8.51 to $9.26, above the $7.50 to $8.50 range given at the start of the year.
  • Half the sales are needs-based. Food, beverage, beauty and household essentials were 53% of fiscal 2024 sales.
  • The balance sheet is sound. Net debt is 1.3 times EBITDA and net interest was $95 million in the quarter, against $1.7 billion of operating income.

What could go wrong?

  • Margin has to keep rising. The price needs 6.5%, which Target reached in one of the last five fiscal years. At 4.5%, as in my Bear case, the stock is worth about $104.
  • The refund does not repeat. Guidance includes only refunds already booked, and the finance chief described the large payment as most of what Target expects.
  • Capex is rising fast. The plan is $5 billion to $6 billion in 2027 against $3.7 billion in fiscal 2025, and my Base case free cash flow falls 21% as a result.
  • Discretionary categories are still weak. Apparel and home are soft, adjusted SG&A grew about 10% in the quarter, and inventory grew faster than sales.
  • Tariffs. Tariff costs that return under other legal authority would hit the margin. The earnings call did not discuss the tariff rate outlook.

Management execution

Management has raised its outlook for the year more than once and has been open about the refund, which is why the normalization above is possible. Against that, the company lowered full-year profit guidance in November 2025, per-share earnings are well below the level of the year ended January 2022, and the call gave no formal outlook for 2027. Management credibility scores 14/20, fair to good, on a lookback that includes the weak 2023 to 2025 stretch.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Comparable sales growth of 3% or more with positive traffic
  • Gross margin rate without refunds at or above 29.5%
  • Operating margin without refunds at or above 5.4%
  • Adjusted SG&A growing no faster than sales
  • Net debt below 1.5 times EBITDA

Yellow

  • Comparable sales growth between 0% and 3%
  • Gross margin rate without refunds between 28.5% and 29.5%
  • Operating margin without refunds between 4.8% and 5.4%
  • Adjusted SG&A growing 1 to 3 points faster than sales
  • Free cash flow without refunds between $2.5 billion and $3.5 billion

Red

  • Negative comparable traffic
  • Gross margin rate without refunds below 28.5%
  • Operating margin without refunds below 4.8%
  • Inventory growing more than 5 points faster than sales
  • Free cash flow without refunds below the $2.1 billion dividend

Today's readings are green on traffic, comparable sales and the balance sheet, and yellow on the operating margin (about 5.1% for the year), SG&A growth (about 10% against sales growth of 7.1%) and free cash flow ($3.16 billion). Fourth-quarter results are expected in early March 2027, and the date is not yet confirmed. They should show four things: comparable sales and traffic still positive, earnings inside the $2.18 to $2.93 a share implied by guidance, a gross margin rate above 29% without refunds, and an outlook for 2027 that puts numbers on sales, margin and the $5 billion to $6 billion capex plan.

The case improves if the price falls toward $130 with margins intact, or if an operating margin of 6% or more persists without refunds for two quarters and lifts my fiscal 2031 estimate.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Comparable traffic turns negative again after one good year, as consumers trade down or apparel and home weakness spreads.
  2. The operating margin stalls near 5% without refunds while capex reaches $6 billion, so free cash flow no longer covers the dividend.
  3. Tariff costs return without pricing power, and the margin falls back toward the 4.5% of my Bear case.

Final verdict: hold at $165.83, do not add

Target is a better business this year than last, with traffic growing, a clean balance sheet and a 55-year dividend record. At $165.83 it is 12.5% above my Base value and 102.7% above the conservative buy limit, and about $2.14 of the earnings behind the 15 times is already spent. There is no reason to sell on the numbers, and no margin of safety if the margin stalls near 5%.

Verdict: HOLD at $165.83. Triangulated Base value $147.41, downside 11.1%, Confidence C (17.5/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range $81.81 to $87.72, maximum buy price $81.81.

The turnaround is working. The price assumes it keeps working until the margin sits at 6.5%, a level Target has touched once in five years.
