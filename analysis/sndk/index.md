## What Sandisk is

Sandisk makes NAND flash, the memory that stores data in phones, laptops, memory cards and data center solid-state drives (SSDs). It was part of Western Digital until the spin-off in February 2025. It does not run its own wafer factories. Its flash comes from Flash Ventures, three joint ventures with Kioxia that operate eight fabs in Japan. Sandisk holds 49.9% of each, takes about half the output at cost plus a small markup, and pays half of the ventures' fixed costs whether or not it takes the wafers. The ventures run until the end of 2034, after the two older ones were extended in January 2026.

Fiscal fourth-quarter revenue was $8,965 million, of which Edge (phones, PCs and client SSDs) was $5,432 million, Datacenter $2,977 million and Consumer $556 million. Datacenter was only $213 million a year earlier. Management says about one-third of the quarter's growth came from higher volume and two-thirds from higher prices. No customer was more than 10% of revenue in fiscal 2026, but the top ten were 44%.

Cash flow quality passes with a warning. Free cash flow was 101% of net income, but it includes $2.75 billion that the company itself strips out of adjusted free cash flow (customer prepayments under the new contracts), a $1.37 billion increase in income taxes owed that will be paid later, and a $3.64 billion rise in receivables that offsets part of it. Receivables were $4.7 billion at year end, about 48 days of fourth-quarter revenue. Stock-based compensation (SBC, pay in shares) was $232 million, 2% of free cash flow, and sits inside the GAAP operating income I use.

## Do the new contracts change the cycle?

This is the strongest argument for the bulls, and it deserves a straight answer. Sandisk has signed what it calls New Business Model (NBM) agreements with eight customers, five of them since April. They run up to five years with a weighted average above four years. Management says they carry at least $93.9 billion of revenue at floor pricing, and that remaining performance obligations were $59.8 billion at quarter end and $91.1 billion including two agreements signed after it. They are expected to cover more than half of fiscal 2027 bits and about two-thirds of fiscal 2028 bits. Management also says it expects NBM margins around 80%, with "attractive margins even at floor pricing".

Spread over four years, $93.9 billion is about $23 billion of revenue a year before any upside, and it is the reason my Base never lets revenue fall below that level before fiscal 2031. Three things limit how much protection it gives. The 10-K says the agreements carry stated volume commitments from both sides and financial guarantees that offset part of lost revenue if a customer fails to perform. The guarantees total $16.5 billion, which is 17.6% of the floor revenue, mostly provided by third-party financial institutions. In the parts of the 10-K I read, I did not find take-or-pay terms or the price-floor mechanism. And the floors were set at today's prices, so they protect revenue only for as long as customers are willing and able to buy. The contracts also tie a large share of bits to a few buyers, who are exposed to the same AI spending cycle as Sandisk.

## Primary valuation: mid-cycle EV/EBITDA

Enterprise value (EV) is market value plus debt minus cash. For a cyclical business the framework asks for a multiple of mid-cycle EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization), not of the peak. I define mid-cycle as the average EBITDA of a five-year path, fiscal 2027 to 2031, that starts at today's peak, falls as new supply and normal price declines arrive, and settles at a level above history because of mix and the contracts. I apply **7x** to that average in all three cases, add the net bridge of -$1.8 billion, and divide by 154.4 million shares. Holding the multiple fixed means the cases differ only in their operating paths, so the same risk is not counted twice. The 7x is my assumption for a cyclical manufacturer at mid-cycle earnings, and the grid below tests it.

The Base path is anchored on four facts: the first-quarter guide ($10.55 billion at the midpoint, 78% operating margin); fiscal 2027 consensus revenue of $49.1 billion (my Base is 2% below it, built from the guide plus sequential growth of 9%, 9% and 8%); the $23 billion annual contract floor; and the fiscal 2023 precedent of a 39% price drop.

## Peak-multiple cross-check and growth diagnostics

No reliable history of through-cycle multiples exists for Sandisk (it has traded alone only since February 2025), and I did not build a peer table of Micron, SK hynix, Samsung and Kioxia. That is a stated limitation. In place of a historical multiple I use the convention that cyclical memory companies trade at a low multiple of peak-year EBITDA, because the market expects the profit to fall. I apply my own assumptions to fiscal 2027 EBITDA: 3.5x in the Bear, 5.0x in the Base and 6.5x in the Bull. That gives $726, $1,205 and $1,728. This is a convention check on the same operating assumptions, not independent evidence, and the multiples differ by case because the multiple is meant to carry the expected decline after the peak. Today the stock trades at 6.2x my Base fiscal 2027 EBITDA, between my Base and Bull multiples.

The growth valuation cross-check is a diagnostic only. The forward P/E is 6.9x on consensus fiscal 2027 non-GAAP EPS of $214.82, which is 203% above fiscal 2026 non-GAAP EPS of $70.88, so the PEG ratio is about 0.03. That supports nothing. Fiscal 2025 non-GAAP EPS was $2.99, the growth comes from a price spike, and the GAAP trailing P/E of 20.2 includes investment gains. Growth durability: cyclical rebound. Cyclical distortion: high. Flags: PEG\_NOT\_MEANINGFUL, CYCLICAL\_REBOUND, ACCOUNTING\_BASIS\_MISMATCH. A low PEG during a memory pricing recovery cannot support fair value by itself, and This check does not set fair value here.

The free cash flow growth quality check says a growth rate is not meaningful, because free cash flow was -$120 million in fiscal 2025 and $11,494 million in fiscal 2026 (absolute change +$11.6 billion). Free cash flow per share was about $74 on 155 million diluted shares, or $56 on the company's adjusted figure, with a free cash flow margin of 56.8% and cash conversion of 101%. The current free cash flow yield is 5.0% on reported and 3.8% on adjusted free cash flow. The forward yield looks high, 12.9% on my Base fiscal 2027 free cash flow and 10.9% on fiscal 2028, but both years are the peak of the path and fall to 3.1% by fiscal 2031. Basic shares are up 0.4% in 13 months. Consensus fiscal 2027 EPS ranges from $186.97 to $238.36 across 21 analysts, which I rate medium reliability, and the revision trend was not available to me. Bridge of the growth: higher prices (two-thirds of the fourth-quarter increase), higher volume, an 84.6% gross margin, $2.75 billion of prepayments and deferred taxes, offset by $3.6 billion of receivables. Stress test: cutting operating income in fiscal 2029 to 2031 by 20% gives a triangulated Base of $897 and by 40% gives $832. Distortion flags: low base, cyclical rebound, working capital, capital spending timing (equipment sits in the joint ventures). Classification: cyclical rebound, diagnostic only.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $1,487.33 and 154.4 million economic shares, market value is about $229.6 billion. After the -$1.8 billion bridge, enterprise value is about **$231.4 billion**, which is 18.4 times trailing EBITDA of $12.5 billion and 6.2 times my Base fiscal 2027 EBITDA. At 7x the price requires average EBITDA of about **$33.1 billion** a year over the cycle, 52% above my Base of $21.8 billion. The Sandisk of the last five years averaged net income of $1.6 billion.

The cash flow view is stricter. My Base cash flows are worth the current price only at a discount rate of about 5.3%. At 11%, the price needs my Base operating income path to be 1.9 times larger in every year, an average of $41.6 billion against my $21.7 billion. Put another way, at an 11% discount rate and 2% growth, the price is the present value of about $20.8 billion of free cash flow every year forever (operating income of $25.1 billion), which is 70% of my fiscal 2027 free cash flow. A buyer today needs five things to be true: contract prices keep rising through the next quarters, the NBM floors hold in practice and are renewed at similar economics after fiscal 2030, new supply from China and from the incumbents does not close the shortage, AI storage demand grows faster than supply for years, and gross margin settles far above any level Sandisk has seen in a normal year.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence D score carries a base margin of safety of 50% or more, and I start at 50%. I add 10 points for operating leverage and cyclicality (gross margin went from 84.6% to the 7% seen in fiscal 2023 in a single downturn) and 5 points for concentration (a single manufacturing partner, Kioxia, and top-ten customers at 44% of revenue). The valuation divergence modifier does not apply, since the primary and secondary methods differ by 23.5%. No positive modifier applies, because a net cash balance sheet is not enough when predictability is low. The required MOS is **65%**. Above 60%, the framework prefers a NO-BUY or WATCHLIST verdict to a pseudo-precise bargain price. **No maximum buy price and no buy zone are issued.** For orientation only, a 60% discount to the Base would give $384.64. That is not the real 65% requirement and must not be used as an automatic order or price alert.

## Dividend and capital return

Sandisk pays no dividend, and management says buybacks are its preferred way to return cash. It repurchased $5.2 billion of shares in fiscal 2026, $4.5 billion of it in the fourth quarter at about $1,595 a share, and the remaining authorization of $15.5 billion is 6.7% of the current market value. Buybacks add value only when they are done below intrinsic value. At my Base of $962, shares bought at $1,595 are a transfer from the cash the remaining holders own to those who sold.

## Reasons to own SNDK

- **Contracted demand.** Remaining performance obligations of $91.1 billion including recent signings, eight NBM customers, more than half of fiscal 2027 bits under multi-year agreements and a weighted average term above four years.
- **Margins and pricing.** The first-quarter guide is $10.3 billion to $10.8 billion of revenue at an 83% to 85% gross margin, and a third-party forecast quoted on October 12 expects NAND contract prices to rise another 15% to 20% in October to December.
- **A clean balance sheet.** No financial debt, $4.8 billion of cash at year end and $15.5 billion of buyback capacity.
- **Data center mix.** Datacenter revenue was $2,977 million in the fourth quarter, up 103% from the third, and management expects data center to be about half of the NAND market in 2026, up from about 30% in 2025.
- **Little capital of its own.** Cash capital spending was $177 million for the year, and the manufacturing venture runs to 2034.
- **The multiple on next year's numbers.** 6.9 times consensus fiscal 2027 EPS, and about 8.3 times the annualized first-quarter guide of $45.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: the price cycle turns.** NAND is a commodity. In the last downturn revenue fell 38%, gross margin fell to 7% and the company lost $2.1 billion. On October 12 the stock fell 6.0% on reports of progress at China's CXMT and softer flash pricing, and a quarter in which volume has to offset lower prices would change the story.
- **Contract enforceability.** Guarantees cover 17.6% of floor revenue, I did not find take-or-pay terms in the 10-K excerpts I read, and the floors were set at today's prices. If prices fall far below the floors, renegotiation is more likely than payment.
- **Partner and structure.** Sandisk pays half of the Flash Ventures fixed costs whatever it takes, guarantees 50% of their lease obligations, owes Kioxia $1.2 billion through 2029 and buys flash in yen.
- **Quality of cash.** $2.75 billion of fiscal 2026 free cash flow came from prepayments and other items the company excludes from its adjusted figure. Receivables rose to $4.7 billion and inventory rose 20.6% in a quarter in which revenue rose 51%, to about 178 days of cost of sales from 158.
- **Capital allocation at the top.** $4.5 billion was spent on buybacks in one quarter at about $1,595, and $14 billion more was authorized in August.
- **Customer concentration and demand.** The top ten customers were 44% of fiscal 2026 revenue, and demand from AI data centers is itself cyclical. Management's own forecast of a NAND market near $500 billion in calendar 2027, after about $300 billion in 2026, is a demand projection that depends on prices staying high.

## Management execution

Management has delivered what it guided since the spin-off: the fiscal fourth quarter beat expectations, fiscal 2026 debt of $1.9 billion was repaid, adjusted free cash flow is disclosed next to the reported figure, and the company signed multi-year contracts that change how the business sells. On the other side, the record is only about six quarters long, so the framework's three-year minimum is not met, and the fiscal 2026 buybacks were made at prices well above my value. Basic shares were flat. **Assessment: 11/20, good but unproven. The next test is whether the first and second fiscal 2027 quarters hold the 83% to 85% gross margin as price increases fade.**
