In the quarter that Bank of America received the largest fine its regulator has ever issued, it paid shareholders $8.3 billion, more than the $6.5 billion it earned for common shareholders. The shares opened 8% lower the next day. Underneath the headline the business keeps compounding: net interest income grew 8% to $16.4 billion, loans have grown for ten quarters in a row, and the bank earned $1.08 a share before the $1.2 billion penalty. The shares closed at $51.02 on November 13.

My Base value is $48.45 a share, the Bear value $33.47 and the Bull value $60.32. The Base blends two methods that agree fairly well: residual income, which values the excess return a bank earns over its cost of equity, gives $46.20, and a multiple of 2027 earnings gives $51.82. The price sits between the Base and the Bull. At 1.75 times tangible book the market is paying for a return near 15% to last, not for a recovery.

Valuation route: large diversified U.S. bank, valued with a residual income model on tangible common equity (a ten year path of excess returns over a 10% cost of equity, plus a terminal value) and cross-checked with a multiple of 2027E earnings per share. Free cash flow methods do not apply to banks, so I did not use them.

What Bank of America is

Bank of America is the second largest U.S. bank, with $3.4 trillion of assets, $2.04 trillion of deposits and $1.25 trillion of loans. It earns money in four businesses. Consumer Banking has 39 million checking customers. Global Wealth and Investment Management (Merrill and the Private Bank) grew revenue 13%, with asset management fees up 16%. Global Banking serves companies, and Global Markets is the trading arm.

Growth is coming from the core. Loans have grown for ten quarters in a row, and net interest income benefits from fixed-rate assets repricing to higher yields. Management expects net interest income to grow about 8% in 2026 and at least 8% in 2027, and it targets a net interest yield of 2.3% against 2.16% in the third quarter. Markets were weaker in the quarter: fixed income, currencies and commodities revenue fell 17%, to about $2.5 billion, as interest rates moved sharply, while equities set a record third quarter at $2.5 billion.

Returns, capital and payout: what drives a bank

A bank is valued on what it earns on the equity it holds, compared with what shareholders require. The measure I use is return on tangible common equity (ROTCE: earnings to common shareholders divided by equity without goodwill and intangibles). It was 12.6% in the third quarter, or 14.9% without the penalty, and about 14% in the fourth quarter of 2025. My estimate for 2026 is 15.2%, because the first half was very strong.

The bank is paying out roughly all of its earnings. In the third quarter it returned $8.3 billion, $2.2 billion of dividends ($0.32 a share, $1.28 a year) and $6.0 billion of buybacks. Shares outstanding fell from 7.21 billion at the end of 2025 to 6.92 billion, 4% in nine months. That is a 6.8% annual buyback yield on the market value of $353 billion, plus a 2.5% dividend yield. It cannot continue at this pace without the capital ratio falling again, so my Base cuts the payout to about 80% of earnings from 2027, which matches growth in tangible equity of 3.0% to 3.5% a year.

One caution on share counts. The company reports average diluted shares of 7.22 billion for the third quarter and a period-end count of 6.92 billion. The period-end figure matches book value, tangible book value and the market capitalization on quote pages, so I use it for per-share equity. I could not reconcile the 4% gap and I flag it in the confidence score.

The anti-money laundering penalty

On October 13, 2026 the U.S. Treasury announced penalties of more than $1.21 billion. FinCEN, the Treasury's financial crimes unit, assessed $1.2 billion, including a $750 million fine by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, the largest in that regulator's history, and the Treasury's sanctions office added $11.6 million for apparent sanctions violations. The bank admitted that it willfully violated the Bank Secrecy Act. According to the Treasury, it failed to report more than $5 billion of suspicious transactions on time. The consent order requires a third-party lookback to find and report transactions that went undetected.

The shares opened 8% lower the next day. Management said the order does not impose an asset cap, which would limit how large the bank could grow, and that it has moved about 1,000 employees to the remediation work. A shareholder class action covers statements made from August 1, 2025 to October 13, 2026. For the valuation, the charge is a one-off in third quarter earnings and I do not repeat it. The ongoing remediation costs are already in the $18.5 billion expense base. The real risk is not the penalty but the regulatory attention that comes with it, and I count that in the confidence score and in the margin of safety, not in the earnings path.

Primary valuation: residual income on tangible equity

Residual income starts with the tangible equity the bank has today ($201.8 billion) and adds the present value of every dollar it earns above a 10% cost of equity, year by year, plus a terminal value. I use a 10% cost of equity: in line with Treasury yields above 4.5% plus a premium for owning bank equity. That is a judgment, not a measurement. The Base path starts with the fourth quarter at a 14.8% return, takes 2027 and 2028 to 15.5% as net interest income grows 8% and expenses grow more slowly, and fades to 13.5% by 2033 as trading normalizes, competition erodes deposit margins and the credit cycle turns. Tangible equity grows 3.0% to 3.5% a year, and terminal growth is 3.0%.

Secondary valuation: forward earnings

The second method multiplies my 2027E earnings per share by a multiple: 8.5 times in the Bear, 11.0 times in the Base and 13.0 times in the Bull. The 11.0 times comes from the justified multiple for a bank that earns 11.5% on book equity, grows 3% and has a 10% cost of equity: (1 minus 3% / 11.5%) divided by (10% minus 3%), or 10.6 times. The market pays 11.8 times my 2026E earnings of $4.32 and 10.8 times 2027E. In the Bear the return falls to 12.5% and then 11.5% with credit losses and slower markets, which justifies a lower multiple, and in the Bull it stays at 17%, which justifies a higher one.

Dividend and buyback

The dividend is $0.32 a quarter, $1.28 a year, a 2.5% yield and about 30% of earnings, so it is safe. Buybacks are the bigger lever: $6.0 billion in the third quarter alone. At $51.02 the shares trade at 1.75 times tangible book, so every buyback dollar cuts tangible book value per share a little while raising earnings per share. That is fine as long as the return on equity stays well above the cost of equity.

Reasons to own Bank of America

  • Net interest income grew 8% in the third quarter to $16.4 billion, and management expects at least 8% growth in 2027 as fixed-rate assets reprice.
  • Loans have grown for ten consecutive quarters, wealth revenue was up 13%, and 39 million checking customers give the bank a stable, low-cost source of deposits.
  • Credit is benign: net charge-offs of 0.44% and a reserve release in the quarter.
  • Shareholders get a 2.5% dividend yield and a buyback yield near 7%, and the share count is down 4% in nine months.
  • The penalty carries no asset cap, and the charge is already behind the bank in third quarter earnings.

What could go wrong?

  • Regulation: the consent orders stay in place, the lookback may uncover more, and a bank that admitted willful violations has less room for another failure. A shareholder class action is pending.
  • Capital: the common equity tier 1 ratio is 10.9% against 11.4% at the end of 2025, and the bank returned more than it earned. If rates keep rising, unrealized losses on securities reduce capital further and buybacks slow.
  • Markets and fees: fixed income revenue fell 17% in the third quarter and management said early fourth quarter conditions had moderated. Trading and investment banking are the most volatile part of earnings.
  • Rates and credit: bank strategists at Bank of America itself expect the Federal Reserve to raise rates in January. Higher rates raise net interest income but can strain borrowers. Net charge-offs of 0.44% are low.
  • My own numbers: the fourth quarter, the return path from 2027, the payout and the 10% cost of equity are my estimates.

Management execution

The adjusted efficiency ratio was 62% in the third quarter, and earnings per share are heading from $3.81 in 2025 toward about $4.30 in 2026 by my estimate. It guides to operating leverage of 200 to 300 basis points in the medium term and a net interest yield of 2.3%. Against that, the penalty, with the admission of willful violations and more than $5 billion of late suspicious transaction reports, shows that compliance lagged the business. I score management 6 out of 10: strong at operating a franchise, weaker on control and, so far, on the cash discipline of paying out more than it earns.

The three most important thesis breakers

These are the events that would make the Hold call wrong, in either direction.

  1. The return on tangible equity stays near 16% to 17% for years as net interest income grows 8% a year, which moves the value toward the Bull of $60.32 and makes the shares cheap.
  2. Credit turns, trading and fees fall back and the return slides to 12%, which moves the value toward the Bear of $33.47.
  3. A regulatory step beyond the penalty, such as an asset cap, a new order or restrictions on buybacks, which would hit both growth and the payout that supports today's price.

Final verdict: hold at $51.02, watchlist for new money

Bank of America is a high-quality franchise at a fair price. It earns about 15% on tangible equity, grows net interest income 8%, returns almost all its earnings and trades at 1.75 times tangible book and 10.8 times my 2027 earnings. The Base earns 9.3% a year at this price, a little below the 10% I require, and the required 10% needs a purchase near $46.20.

The call is Hold because the price sits 5% above the Base and 15% below the Bull, and the two methods agree within 12%. It is not a buy at any price above $31.49. The shares need returns to hold near 15% while the capital ratio recovers and a regulator watches closely.

The penalty was a one-quarter charge. The lasting question is whether a bank that pays out more than it earns can hold its returns near 15%.

Verdict: HOLD at $51.02, watchlist for new money. Base $48.45, -5.0% against the price, Bear $33.47, Bull $60.32, Confidence B (18/25). Required margin of safety 35%, maximum buy price $31.49, range $30.03 to $33.68 under the two methods.
