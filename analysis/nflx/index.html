Every stock screener shows Netflix at about 22 times earnings. That number includes a $2.8 billion check from Warner Bros. Discovery for a takeover that never happened. Take the check out and the multiple is closer to 27 (my estimate). The shares are still down 44% from their 52-week high of $124.86, and the business is still growing revenue 13% with a 33% operating margin.

At the October 9, 2026 close of $70.30, the shares trade 20.2% above my triangulated Base value of $58.50. The Bear value is $34.80 and the Bull value is $81.81. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 45%, so the maximum buy price is $32.17, 54% below the price.

Valuation route: standard value (media with stable free cash flow). The sector-specific method does not apply: Netflix is a consumer subscription business with large positive free cash flow, not an ARR software story. Economic classification: Quality Growth, 8/12 on the generic scorecard, one point below the Compounder line, so the engine is GROWTH_ADJUSTED_MULTIPLE with an explicit multiple build. Primary method: owner free cash flow times a multiple. Secondary method: a ten-year DCF at 10% with a growth fade. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets. Owner free cash flow here is cash after interest, taxes, capex and stock pay, and it excludes the one-off Warner Bros. fee.

What Netflix is

Netflix sells subscription streaming of series, films, live events and games, plus an advertising-supported tier. It had revenue of $48.4 billion over the last twelve months.

  • Revenue grew 13.4% in Q2 (12% at constant currency), with double-digit growth in every region: Latin America +21% (16% at constant currency), Asia-Pacific +16% (18%), EMEA +14% (11%) and US and Canada +10%.
  • Advertising is on track for about $3 billion in 2026, roughly double 2025 and about 6% of revenue, so subscriptions are still about 94% of revenue (CALCULATED from guidance).
  • The letter gives view hours, up 2% in the first half to more than 97 billion, and no membership count. Revenue is growing more than six times faster than viewing, so price and advertising are doing the work, and the filings do not say how many people are paying.
  • Netflix had agreed to buy Warner Bros. Discovery. On February 27, 2026 WBD terminated that agreement to merge with Paramount Skydance, which paid Netflix a $2.8 billion termination fee on WBD's behalf. Co-founder Reed Hastings announced he would not stand for re-election to the board at the June meeting.

History and growth diagnostics

Valuation history. The enterprise value is 18.8 times 2026E operating income and the price is 28.5 times 2026E owner free cash flow (32.7 times 2025). The reported trailing P/E is 22.2. Removing the fee ($0.53 a share after tax, CALCULATED at the 19.3% Q1 effective rate) gives trailing EPS of about $2.64 and a P/E of about 26.6 (ESTIMATED). Five to ten year multiple history is UNAVAILABLE from the sources I could read.

PEG (diagnostic only). The price is about 23.2 times my 2026E EPS excluding the fee of $3.03 (Q1 $0.70, Q2 $0.80, Q3 guidance $0.82, my Q4 estimate $0.71). My Base operating income grows 11.3% a year from 2026E to 2031E, so the PEG is about 2.1, with GAAP earnings on both sides. I treat it as a diagnostic only. Growth type: STRUCTURAL for pricing and advertising, NORMALIZING as growth fades from 13% to 7%. CYCLICAL_DISTORTION: LOW, because there is no genuine cycle. The fee is an accounting distortion of the reported P/E (ACCOUNTING_BASIS_MISMATCH) and I remove it.

Free cash flow growth quality (diagnostic only). Free cash flow rose from $6,926 million in 2023 to $9,461 million in 2025, a compound growth of 16.9% a year and an absolute increase of $2.5 billion. Per share it grew from $1.54 to $2.18, or 19.0% a year, and the share count fell 1.3% a year from 2021 to 2025. A start in 2022 ($1,619 million) would give 80% a year, which is LOW_BASE_DISTORTION, so I reject it. FCF margin was 20.5% in 2023 and 20.9% in 2025, and free cash flow was 86% of net income in 2025. Forward, my Base owner cash flow compounds at 11.4% a year from $10.44 billion in 2026E to $17.87 billion in 2031E. The forward owner cash flow yield on market value is 3.5% for 2026E and 4.1% for 2027E. The growth bridge is revenue growth (about 13% to 11%), operating margin gains of about 2 points a year in 2026 and 2027, and a cash conversion that stays near 68% because cash content spend runs above amortization. Flags: ACCOUNTING_CASHFLOW_MISMATCH for the $2.8 billion fee (removed) and rising stock pay (up 78% in the first half). Estimate dispersion and revisions are UNAVAILABLE (consensus is behind a paywall), so forecast reliability is MEDIUM. Stress test: 2028E owner cash flow 20% lower gives a primary value of $49.56, and 40% lower gives $37.99.

What is the market already pricing in?

Reverse valuation: at $70.30 the market value of $297.8 billion implies a multiple of 23.2 times on my Base 2028E owner cash flow of $13.76 billion, against my 20 times. Holding my multiple, the price needs 2028E owner cash flow of $15.95 billion, 15.9% above Base. That would take an operating margin of about 39% in 2028 on Base revenue, against 34% in Base, or revenue near $72.5 billion at my 22.0% cash margin, against $62.5 billion. The market is not pricing in a Bear case. It is paying for the ad business to scale and for margins to keep rising, and that is why a 44% fall does not make Netflix cheap.

Dividend and capital return

Netflix pays no dividend. It returned $10.0 billion through buybacks over the last twelve months, and the count of shares outstanding fell 2.0% in the year to June 2026. I do not count future buybacks in value per unit, and at today's price they would be buying shares that are still above my Base value.

Reasons to own NFLX

  • Cash generation. Free cash flow was $9.5 billion in 2025 and the 2026 guidance excluding the fee is about $11 billion,, with cash content spend running above amortization.
  • Pricing power. Revenue grew 13.4% in Q2 while hours viewed grew 2% in the first half, so price and advertising are carrying growth.
  • Advertising. Ad revenue is on track to roughly double to about $3 billion in 2026, from a small base.
  • Margins and returns. The operating margin rose from 17.8% in 2022 to 29.5% in 2025 and is guided to 31.5%, with return on invested capital of 28% to 32%.
  • Balance sheet and buybacks. Net debt is about 0.3 times operating income and $27.1 billion of buyback authorization remains.

What could go wrong?

  • Viewing growth is 2%. Hours viewed grew 2% in the first half. If price increases slow, revenue growth has little else under it.
  • Competition is consolidating. Warner Bros. Discovery is going to Paramount Skydance, and Netflix has lost the largest acquisition it had agreed to.
  • Content and live costs. Content amortization is guided up about 10% in 2026 and the Q2 operating margin fell 0.7 points year over year.
  • Less disclosure. There is no membership count, and the viewing report moves to an annual format from Q1 2027, so churn is harder to see.
  • Buybacks and refinancing. Net debt rose $3.1 billion in one quarter and about $2.5 billion of notes are classified as current.
  • Leadership and M&A appetite. The co-founder is leaving the board, and the company was willing to pursue a very large acquisition. It also bought a business for about $587 million in March.

Management execution

Management has held the 31.5% operating margin target through two quarters, raised free cash flow guidance in April and narrowed revenue guidance in July without moving the midpoint ($51.2 billion before and after). It also collected $2.8 billion on a deal it lost. Against that, Netflix no longer reports quarterly memberships, is moving its viewing report to annual, bought back stock at about $96 in Q1, and pursued a transformational acquisition that would have changed what the company is. Management credibility scores 13/20.
