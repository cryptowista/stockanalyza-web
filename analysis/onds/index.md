Valuation route: a fast-growing defense technology group without profits or free cash flow, valued from a scenario path to 2029 operating profit after share-based pay, with the cash already paid for acquisitions after June 30, earn-outs, the warrant overhang and ongoing dilution. ## What Ondas is

Ondas started as a maker of private radio networks for railroads. That business, Ondas Networks, was deconsolidated in January 2026. What remains is a holding company with two operating groups. Ondas Autonomous Systems sells counter-drone systems, surveillance drones and ground robots, with much of its engineering in Israel. Ondas Sentinel, formed in July, combines World View and DZYNE: the ULTRA long-endurance surveillance aircraft, now flown by the U.S. Air Force, the IonStrike interceptor and the Dronebuster counter-drone system. A third unit, Ondas Capital, invests in partner companies.

The group was built quickly. In 2026 alone Ondas bought at least 13 companies: Bird Aerosystems, Rotron, Indo Earth Moving, World View, Mistral, Omnisys, DZYNE for $879 million, Cyberhawk, GATE Technologies and Bron Technologies, and Insignito, Ottopia Defense and Caribou Labs. On November 5 it agreed to buy Stemme, the German maker of the ULTRA airframe, for an undisclosed cash price, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2027.

## Which numbers to trust

- **Net income tells you nothing here.** The first half showed a $271.6 million profit, made of a $404.8 million gain on the warrants as the share price fell and a $48.8 million gain on deconsolidating Ondas Networks. The operating loss was $205.6 million.
- **Adjusted EBITDA leaves out the largest cost.** Share-based pay was $88.8 million in the first half, about 66% of revenue, including grants to the teams of acquired companies. I treat it as a real cost in the long run and as dilution until 2029.
- **Pro forma growth is not organic growth.** Management says pro forma organic growth was about 85% in the second quarter. The reported 13 times is mostly bought.
- **Backlog includes acquired order books.** Of the rise from $457 million to $757 million in the second quarter, part came with DZYNE and Cyberhawk.
- **Earn-outs are a future bill.** Ondas owed $132.5 million in contingent purchase payments at June 30 and has since added earn-outs of up to $217 million for GATE and the three September deals. GATE's can be paid in cash or shares.

## Cross-check: what Ondas paid for DZYNE

In July Ondas paid $879 million in cash and shares for DZYNE, which it expects to bring more than $300 million of revenue in 2027. That is about 2.9 times next year's revenue for a business management expects to be EBITDA positive from 2026. Applying the same multiple to the whole group's Base 2027 revenue of $900 million, and keeping the same cash, earn-outs, warrants and shares, gives about **$4.29** a share. It is a generous check, because it pays a profitable unit's price for a group that still loses money, and it is still 18.5% below the price.

## How the buy price is set

Confidence is D, which carries a default margin of safety of 55%. I add nothing to it, because the warrants, earn-outs and dilution are already in the value. That gives a maximum buy price of **$1.41**.

With the 2029 margin between 8% and 12% and a multiple between 16 and 20 times, the same margin gives buy prices from $1.12 to $1.73. The price of $5.27, and $4.57 on November 5, are above all of them. The 52-week range is $4.38 to $15.28.

Ondas pays no dividend.

## Reasons to own Ondas

- Demand is real: $757 million of pro forma backlog at June 30, more than $270 million of new orders in the three months after, and a large U.S. Army contract already producing orders.
- The balance sheet is strong for a company of this size, with about $1.4 billion of cash and investments at June 30 and almost no debt.
- Gross margin above 40% leaves room for profit once overhead stops growing.
- ULTRA is flying with the U.S. Air Force, and buying Stemme would put its airframe production under Ondas's control.
- The share price has fallen about two thirds from its 52-week high, so a clean fourth quarter would surprise the market.

## What could go wrong?

- The fourth quarter. Guidance needs about three times the second quarter's revenue in a single quarter.
- Integration. At least 13 businesses bought in one year, across Israel, Europe and the U.S., each with its own systems, customers and earn-out incentives.
- Overhead. Adjusted cash operating costs were about $93 million in the second quarter, including $29 million of growth spending at the holding company that management expects to stay at a similar level for six to twelve months.
- Dilution. Share-based pay, shares due to sellers and earn-outs payable in stock keep adding to the count, and the warrants cap the upside.
- Price paid. Goodwill and intangible assets were $1.24 billion at June 30, before DZYNE added $807 million more. If the bought units disappoint, write-downs follow.

## Management execution

- Ondas raised capital in October 2025 at $11.50, above the market price, and in January 2026 at $16.45, and still holds most of the cash.
- The January raise came with warrants worth more than the cash received, which cost $235 million on the day and leaves 195 million warrants outstanding.
- Revenue targets have been raised, not cut, so far in 2026.
- The pace of buying, at least 13 companies in ten months plus Stemme, leaves little time to prove that each one works.
- Share-based pay of $88.8 million in six months is very high for a company with $134 million of revenue.

On my ten-point management check (two points each), Ondas scores 9 of 20: credit for raising money well and delivering growth, little for dilution, pay and the speed of acquisitions.

## What I would watch from here

The third quarter report should show revenue inside the $140 to $155 million range, gross margin above 40%, an adjusted EBITDA loss well below the second quarter's $51 million, and 2026 guidance kept at $525 million or more. The fourth quarter then has to deliver the rest.

### Green

- Fourth-quarter revenue above $240 million with adjusted EBITDA near break-even.
- A 2027 outlook of at least $1 billion of revenue with falling share-based pay.
- A pause in acquisitions and no new share issuance.

### Yellow

- 2026 revenue below $500 million.
- Share-based pay above $60 million a quarter.
- Another large acquisition paid mainly in shares.

### Red

- Third-quarter revenue below $130 million.
- Gross margin below 35%.
- Cash and investments below $500 million without a funding plan.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. The fourth quarter delivers guidance and operating costs flatten, so profit arrives faster than in my Base and the value moves toward the Bull.
2. Revenue misses and the bought units underperform, which brings write-downs and leads toward the Bear.
3. Ondas keeps paying for growth with shares and earn-outs, so the value per share stays flat even if the business grows.

The weakest point is the 2029 margin, because two points move the value by about $0.45 and the group has never been profitable. The strongest bull argument is that orders and backlog are growing faster than revenue. The strongest bear argument is that most of the growth was bought, much of it with shares, and the price already assumes the profit that would justify those purchases.

## Final verdict: reduce at $5.27

Ondas used a strong share price to raise a billion dollars and buy a defense business that did not exist two years ago. The orders are real and the cash is real. What the price asks for is a 2029 margin above even my Bull case, delivered by a group still learning to run itself.

**Verdict: REDUCE at $5.27, watchlist for new money. Base $3.13, downside 40.7%, Bear $1.17, Bull $6.77, cross-check $4.29, Confidence D (11.25/25). Required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $1.41, range $1.12 to $1.73 under nearby assumptions.**

For holders this means trimming, not fleeing: the Bull case at $6.77 needs a $1 billion year in 2027 and a 15% margin, and the next two quarters will show whether that is coming. For new money it means waiting. Ondas has the cash, the orders and the shopping list; what it has not bought yet is the margin.
