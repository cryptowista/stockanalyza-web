## What Photronics is

Photronics makes photomasks, the quartz plates that carry a circuit pattern and are used like a stencil to print chips and display panels. It sells them to chipmakers and display makers as an independent, or merchant, supplier. In fiscal 2025 revenue was $849.3 million, of which integrated circuit (IC) masks were $615.1 million and flat panel display (FPD) masks $234.2 million. It competes with the in-house mask shops of the largest chipmakers and, in mainstream masks, with local mask houses in China.

The investment case is node migration. As customers move to smaller, more complex chips, the price per mask rises. High-end IC masks reached a record 44% of IC revenue in the third quarter. Photronics is spending heavily to meet that demand: a new facility in Allen, Texas expects first revenue late in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, and Korea is adding capability for 8-nanometer masks within about 18 months. It is not investing in extreme ultraviolet (EUV) masks at scale until a merchant market develops.

The stock reflects that debate. It reached $54.96 in May 2026, then fell about 37% in the week of the second-quarter report on May 28, when revenue missed and management guided the third quarter below expectations. It has drifted lower since, closing at $24.03 on November 20 according to a third-party recap, and the one-year low is near $20.

## Why headline earnings overstate what shareholders keep

Three things stand between Photronics' reported profit and cash in a shareholder's hands. First, about 29% of consolidated net income belongs to the partners in its joint ventures: $44.0 million of $147.3 million in nine months, and $53.8 million of $190.2 million in fiscal 2025. Second, capital spending is running far ahead of depreciation. Third, three quarters of the company's cash is inside those same joint ventures.

In fiscal 2025 operating cash flow was $247.8 million and capital spending $188.1 million, leaving free cash flow of $59.7 million for all consolidated entities, or about $42 million after the joint venture partners' share. In fiscal 2026 I expect roughly $10 million: nine-month free cash flow was $90.2 million, and my estimate for the fourth quarter is about -$80 million (about $70 million of operating cash flow less capital spending near the middle of the guidance range). The company's earnings per share of about $2 is real profit, but very little of it has been free cash flow for two years.

Stock compensation was $10.4 million in nine months. GAAP operating profit is already after it, so I do not add it back.

## Primary valuation: three years of cash flow and a discounted exit

The primary method counts the free cash flow attributable to Photronics shareholders for fiscal 2027 to 2029 and then values the business at a multiple of its normalized free cash flow at the end of fiscal 2029, all discounted at 10% (the framework default; I test 8% and 12%). I use a transition model because the company is mid-investment: fiscal 2026 capital spending is about 32% of revenue, and I assume a more normal 14.5% only from fiscal 2030. The Base path follows.

Normalized free cash flow to shareholders at the end of fiscal 2029 is **$98 million**: after-tax operating profit of $174 million, plus depreciation of 11.5% of revenue, less capital spending of 14.5% of revenue and working capital, times 71%.

**Why 12x.** A 12 times multiple of that cash flow is a yield of 8.3% and implies growth of about 1.5% a year forever at a 10% required return. I think that is fair for a business with durable demand tied to every new chip design but with flat revenue over three years, price competition from Chinese mask makers in mainstream nodes, heavy reinvestment and 29% of profit leaking to partners. Bear gets 9x, which implies a 1% decline forever, and Bull gets 15x, which implies about 3% growth. The exit value is $1,174 million in Base.

Exit value share: 65% of the primary value comes from the exit, at a horizon of three years, below the 85% level where I check that the exit is supported by sustainable flows. Interim cash flows add $1.83 a share and the net cash $6.05. The primary value is **$22.69**.

## Secondary valuation: ten-year discounted cash flow

The cross-check discounts ten years of free cash flow to shareholders at 10% and ends with a perpetual growth of 2.5%. Growth fades from 6% to 3% by fiscal 2033 and the operating margin holds at 21%. It gives **$26.09** per share, and 43% of its value comes from the terminal value, below the 75% I treat as a warning level for a ten-year model.

## Reasons to own PLAB

- **Node migration raises prices.** High-end IC masks were a record 44% of IC revenue, and the more advanced the chip, the more each mask is worth.
- **Net cash.** About $417 million attributable to shareholders, $7.01 a share, with $3.9 million of debt.
- **Profitability.** Operating margin of 21% to 25% across the last three years, and operating cash flow of 35% of revenue in the third quarter.
- **Capacity is on schedule.** Allen is due to deliver first revenue this quarter and Korea's 8-nanometer expansion is on schedule, with the capital spending cut attributed to timing.
- **The price is fair, not extended.** The stock is 56% below its May high and trades at 10.7 times estimated GAAP earnings.

## What could go wrong?

- **Design-release delays.** Management says visibility into chip design releases is limited and has widened the guidance range. Another miss produced a 37% weekly fall in May.
- **Capital intensity.** Capital spending of $255 to $305 million in fiscal 2026 is about a third of revenue, and depreciation will follow. Returns on that spending are not yet visible in revenue.
- **China competition.** Management reports intense competition from local mask houses in mainstream masks, which is where the new Allen capacity partly aims.
- **Joint venture leakage.** 29% of consolidated net income and about 75% of cash sit with the 50.01% joint ventures, and distribution terms are not disclosed in what I read.
- **No EUV position.** Management will not invest at scale in EUV masks until a merchant market exists. If that market never forms, the most advanced masks stay with captive suppliers.
- **Volatility.** The options market implies a typical monthly move near 18%.

## Management execution

Management guided the third quarter conservatively and beat it: revenue of $216.0 million against a $207 to $215 million range. That followed a second quarter miss and a weak outlook that cost shareholders more than a third of their value in a week. Capital spending guidance was then cut by $25 million to $75 million, a timing change management says is not a cancellation. Buybacks stopped as the investment program began, which is a reasonable choice but also a sign of how much cash it takes. Management credibility is **12/20**: reliable on guidance when it is conservative, with a record of capital spending that arrives later and costs more than it first said.
