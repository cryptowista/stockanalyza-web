# Main Street paid $1.095 a share in the third quarter on $0.96 of distributable income, and trades at 1.61 times NAV

MAIN, New York Stock Exchange. Published November 22, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close ($55.05). Main Street Capital is a business development company, or BDC: a listed lender and investor that must pay out nearly all of its taxable income. NII is net investment income, the interest, dividends and fees it earns after its own costs. DNII is distributable NII, the company's own measure. NAV is net asset value per share, the reported value of its investments less debt. LMM means lower middle market, small private companies in which Main Street holds loans and equity.

## Primary valuation: NAV and sustainable income

A BDC pays out almost all of its income, so its value is the income it pays plus the NAV that remains. I model 10 years. FY27 NII is $3.55 a share, my estimate, a yield of 10.4% on NAV and just below the third-quarter run rate of $3.56, because dividend income is falling and refinancing costs more. It is paid out each year. In Base, NAV stays at $34.22, which assumes that normal credit losses of about $0.35 a share a year are offset by gains on LMM equity. That is a judgment, and the $720 million unrealized gain on LMM could also reverse.

I discount at 10.0%, my estimate of the cost of equity for a lender with leverage, and I assume the shares trade at 1.35 times NAV in year 10. That is a premium, since Main Street earns a return on equity above 14%, but it is below the current 1.61 times. The exit value is 45% of the total, below the 75% level that needs close checking. The model gives **$39.62**.

## Secondary valuation: multiple of earnings

The second method takes FY27 NII of $3.55 less $0.35 of normal credit losses, which is $3.20 of normalized earnings per share, and applies 15 times. A lender that pays out all its earnings and does not grow is worth about 10 times at a 10% cost of equity. I add five turns, because Main Street grows NAV, is internally managed with low costs, and has paid a supplemental dividend for 21 consecutive quarters. The multiple is my judgment and not company guidance. The result is **$48.00**.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $39.62 and $48.00, a divergence of 19.1%, just below the 20% warning level. They are not independent: both start from the same NII forecast and the same credit loss assumption. I weight the NAV and income model 60% and the earnings method 40%, because the first captures NAV drift and the exit price directly. The weighted value is $42.97. ## What could go wrong?

- Premium. At 1.61 times NAV, a fall to 1.10 times lowers the primary value to $36.33.
- NAV marks. About $720 million of LMM unrealized gains rest on private company valuations, and Q3 realized losses were $27.6 million.
- Falling income. Dividend income fell 18%, NII per share fell 8%, and total dividends paid exceeded DNII.
- Refinancing. $400 million of 6.50% notes mature in June 2027 and SBIC debentures start maturing in 2027.
- Rates. A lower base rate cuts income on floating-rate loans, and NII of $3.30 gives a Base value of $40.55.
