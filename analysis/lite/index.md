## What Lumentum is

Lumentum makes the lasers and optical parts that move data inside and between AI data centers: semiconductor laser chips, cloud transceivers, optical circuit switches (OCS, which steer light without converting it to electricity), external laser sources for co-packaged optics (CPO, which puts the optics next to the chip) and high-power lasers for industry. In the fourth quarter Components revenue was $649 million, up 103% year over year, and Systems revenue was $357 million, up 123%.

Two events changed the company in fiscal 2026. In March 2026 NVIDIA bought 2,876,415 shares of Series A convertible preferred stock at $695.31 each, $2.0 billion in total, alongside a non-exclusive multi-billion purchase commitment and future capacity access rights. The preferred converts into common stock one for one. Lumentum also bought a fab in Greensboro, North Carolina to add U.S. indium phosphide (InP, the material used for these lasers) capacity. On the May 2026 call management said revenue from that site was about six quarters away, so it is not in this year's numbers.

Concentration is the other side of the same story. One customer was 26.6% of fiscal 2026 revenue, up from 15.4% a year earlier, and a second was 15.0%. Together they were 41.6% of revenue against 31.4% the year before, and one customer holds 30.4% of receivables. About 79.2% of revenue comes from customers outside the United States.

## Why GAAP earnings and headline cash flow cannot be used

Fiscal 2026 GAAP earnings are useless as an anchor, because the $7.8 billion debt extinguishment charge swamps the operating result. Headline cash flow is not much better. Operating cash flow of $751.4 million minus capital spending of $451.3 million gives free cash flow of **$300.1 million**. After deducting $170.2 million of stock-based compensation (SBC), a real cost paid in shares, the hard figure is **$129.9 million, or 43.3% of the headline**.

My normalized fiscal 2027 scenarios are EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) of **$2.10 billion** in the Bear case, **$2.56 billion** in the Base case and **$2.84 billion** in the Bull case. Both consensus EBITDA and free cash flow add SBC back.

## Historical cross-check and growth diagnostics

Lumentum's forward P/E has ranged from 11.8x in fiscal 2022 to 54.6x in fiscal 2024, with 25.9x in fiscal 2025 and 51.6x in fiscal 2026; the average of those five years is about 33x. These figures come from a public data provider cited in the framework analysis and I could not confirm them independently, so the check carries only a 20% weight. A normalized 30x on fiscal 2027 consensus EPS of $21.78 gives **$653**. For the scenarios, the Bear applies 20x to $16.00 (about the first-quarter guidance midpoint of $4.20 times four, less 5%), which gives $320, and the Bull applies 38x to $25.27, the top of the estimate range, which gives $960. Non-GAAP EPS leaves SBC out, so multiples quoted on it already reflect that convention.

The growth valuation cross-check (SM-19) is a diagnostic only. The forward P/E of 49.8x divided by expected EPS growth of about 151% gives a PEG of 0.33, which looks absurdly cheap and supports nothing. Fiscal 2027 growth is a mix of structural AI optics demand, higher utilization and a low prior-year base. Growth durability: structural and normalizing. Cyclical distortion: high. Lumentum trades at roughly a 39% premium to Coherent and 96% to Fabrinet on forward P/E, using screener figures that I could not reproduce and do not weigh. SM-19 does not set fair value.

The free cash flow growth quality check (SM-20) says the same thing from the cash side. Free cash flow of $300.1 million in fiscal 2026 would have to reach the consensus $4.34 billion in fiscal 2029, an increase of $4.04 billion and a compound growth rate of about 144% a year over three years. On 103.1 million shares that is $2.91 a share rising to $42.12. The implied free cash flow margin goes from 10.0% to roughly 31%, which depends on third-party revenue estimates that I could not verify. Measured on today's economic equity value of $111.9 billion, the free cash flow yield is 0.27% on fiscal 2026 (0.12% after SBC), 1.0% on fiscal 2027, 2.2% on fiscal 2028 and 3.9% on fiscal 2029. ## What is the market already pricing in?

At $1,085.42 and 103.1 million economic shares, market value is about $111.9 billion and enterprise value about **$110.8 billion**. That is 43.3x my Base EBITDA, 39.0x consensus EBITDA and 108x fiscal 2026 adjusted EBITDA. At the Base multiple of 27x the price requires EBITDA of about **$4.10 billion**, 60% above my Base and 44% above the consensus. Even at the Bull multiple of 34x it needs $3.26 billion, more than my Bull EBITDA of $2.84 billion.

In cash flow terms, the price equals about 1.83 times my entire Base free cash flow path at a 10.5% discount rate, which would mean roughly $5.9 billion of free cash flow in fiscal 2029 instead of $3.2 billion. Alternatively, it equals the full consensus path with a discount rate of about 9.3% and 3% terminal growth. A buyer today needs six things to be true: AI optics growth is structural, Lumentum wins a large share of CPO, OCS and 1.6T demand, fiscal 2029 free cash flow reaches or beats consensus, margins stay far above the pre-2026 cycle, new capacity does not cause oversupply and customer concentration does not erode pricing. The market is not pricing a good outcome. It is pricing something near my best case.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence C score carries a base MOS range of 35% to 50%, and I start at 40%. I add 5 points for operating leverage and cyclicality, 5 points for customer concentration and 5 points for convertible and valuation complexity, and subtract 5 points for the net cash balance sheet. The required MOS is **50%**. Applied to the $651 Base, the **maximum buy price is $325**, and a stronger 60% margin marks a strong-buy reference near $260. Without the extra margin the maximum buy price would be $390. The shares would need to fall about 70% from $1,085.42 to reach $325.

## Reasons to own LITE

- **A genuine secular driver.** Electrical links are running into limits inside AI systems, and optical content per system keeps rising.
- **Differentiated technology.** InP lasers, ultra-high-power lasers, coherent components, VCSELs and OCS make this more than a single-product story.
- **External validation.** NVIDIA's $2.0 billion investment, purchase commitment and capacity rights add demand visibility.
- **Operating leverage is visible.** Non-GAAP operating margin rose from 18.7% to 36.6% in four quarters, and the guidance points to about 40%.
- **Several growth layers.** 1.6T modules, CPO and external laser sources, scale-up connectivity and OCS, which had a backlog above $400 million in February.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: consensus cash flow.** The price needs extraordinary free cash flow growth. A material miss on fiscal 2027 to 2029 could compress the multiple severely even if revenue keeps growing.
- **Customer concentration.** Two customers are 41.6% of revenue. A design loss, a deployment delay, an inventory correction or a price negotiation at one of them changes the growth path.
- **Capacity and cycle.** Net property and equipment rose to $1.16 billion from $726 million and inventory rose 47% to $692 million. If demand normalizes after the capacity is installed, utilization and margins can reverse quickly.
- **Dilution and capital structure.** Shares rose 28% in a year, and the remaining notes, the NVIDIA preferred stock and employee awards add about 13 million shares more.
- **Technology and competition.** CPO, NPO, VCSEL, silicon photonics and other suppliers can change customer choices and market share.
- **Export controls and geopolitics.** About 79% of revenue is shipped outside the United States.

## Management execution

Michael Hurlston became chief executive in February 2025, after senior roles at Synaptics, Finisar and Broadcom. Since then, Lumentum has met or beaten its revenue guidance quarter after quarter, reached its target revenue model earlier than planned, lifted gross margin sharply and secured the NVIDIA agreement. The offsets are a short record under this chief executive, a capital structure that produced extraordinary dilution and accounting complexity in fiscal 2026, and expectations that are now so high that good execution may not be enough. **Assessment: 17/20, good to very good. The next test is whether margins hold as the new capacity comes online.**

## Final verdict: reduce at $1,085.42

Lumentum is a high-quality AI optics business whose fundamentals have changed dramatically. The shares, though, now trade above my Bull value, and the price needs the full consensus cash flow path plus a low discount rate. The 2032 convertible notes carry a conversion price of $187.77, so the holders who bought them paid for the right to own the company at that level. A buyer of the shares today pays 5.8 times as much.

**Verdict: REDUCE at $1,085.42. Triangulated Base value $651, downside 40.1%, Confidence C (13/25). Required margin of safety 50%, maximum buy price $325.**

The main risk to this verdict is that the AI optics opportunity proves much larger and more durable than my Base, so that fiscal 2029 free cash flow reaches $4.3 billion or more with margins and multiples intact. New money belongs on the watchlist until the price approaches $325.
