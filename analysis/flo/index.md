In its latest quarterly filing it also told investors it is seeking an amendment or waiver of its leverage and interest coverage covenants, and that all three rating agencies have moved it one notch below investment grade.

The third quarter explains why. Adjusted EBITDA fell 33.2% to $78.9 million, unit volume fell 7.0%, and guidance for the year was cut for the second time in three months, to $415 million to $430 million of adjusted EBITDA. That range leaves $65.7 million to $80.7 million for the fourth quarter.

## What Flowers Foods is and why volume is the problem

Flowers Foods bakes and sells Nature's Own, Dave's Killer Bread, Canyon Bakehouse, Simple Mills, Wonder and Tastykake. Much of the bread reaches stores through independent distributors who buy their territories, which is why the cash flow statement carries route-system lines that most food companies do not have. Branded retail was 66% of third quarter sales ($772.1 million of $1,169.1 million) and the rest is the company's other segment.

Price and mix added 2.3% to sales in the third quarter and did not come close to covering a 7.0% fall in units. In branded retail, price and mix added 3.5% and volume fell 8.6%. The release blames lower volume, more marketing, higher logistics costs and more outside purchases of product, partly offset by lower incentive pay, cheaper ingredients and lower distributor fees. A summary of the call says Dave's Killer Bread units, which were still growing in the first quarter, are now falling, and management links that to weight-loss drug use among higher-income households and to price sensitivity.

## Secondary valuation: capitalized owner cash

The second method ignores multiples. It takes year 2 owner cash of 140.06 million, assumes all of it is distributable with zero growth and the debt stock refinanced as it is, and capitalizes it at 10%: $1,400.7 million, less $38.1 million of other claims, or $1,362.6 million of equity. That is $6.38 a share at exit. Add the two dividends and discount: **$5.71 today**.

The zero growth is deliberate, since renewal capital spending is already fully deducted. A 10% rate is not generous for an equity with a covenant amendment pending, and at 12% the second method gives $4.80.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Solving the primary model backward at $5.15, with Base dividends and a 6.5 times multiple, the price needs about $429 million of management EBITDA, or $414 million economic. That is the top of the $415 million to $430 million guided for this year, and this year includes a first quarter of $159.0 million. Holding my Base cash, debt and EBITDA instead, the price implies an exit multiple of about 7.1 times against my 6.5. In the cash method the price is justified by about $126 million of owner cash a year against $140 million in Base. These are three separate answers, not one set of market expectations.

The price is not asking for bankruptcy. It is asking for volumes to stop falling at roughly this year's guided EBITDA, with the amendment signed on benign terms. Buying at $5.15 and receiving the Base outcome would earn about 5.3% a year over two years before tax and fees, a modeled figure for information only and not a forecast.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence score of 12 is grade D, which carries a base margin of safety of 55%. I add nothing on top, because the leverage, the amendment and the operating risks are already in the Bear case, the debt bridge and the Confidence grade. Applied to the $4.73 Base, the **maximum buy price is $2.13**, which is 12.1% above the Bear value. At the 42.5% margin of a grade C score the reference price would be $2.72. The current price is 142% above the maximum buy price, so a buy case needs a price far below today's, or an amendment signed on good terms plus volumes that have stopped falling.

Four quantities are easy to confuse, so I keep them apart. Fair value today is $4.73. The modeled return at $5.15 is 5.3% a year. An entry price for an illustrative 18% return, using the same Base dividends and exit, would be about $4.12. That 18% is an example, not a universal hurdle. And the margin of safety against model error gives $2.13. For a conservative decision the margin of safety threshold controls.

## Reasons to own Flowers Foods

- **Cash flow is real so far.** Free cash flow was $334 million over 53 weeks, and year-to-date cash from operations is level with last year.
- **No maturity before 2029.** The October notes are repaid, the $500 million revolver is undrawn and the receivables facility is unused.
- **Some brands are working.** Per the earnings release coverage, Canyon Bakehouse gained share and Wonder cake passed $100 million of sales within two years of launch, and a summary of the call says Nature's Own share improved after its relaunch.
- **Cost savings.** A summary of the call says management expects about $20 million of savings to help in 2027.
- **A low multiple.** 6.5 times guided EBITDA and a yield of 9.7% at the current dividend.

These justify watching the stock. They do not show that the price already gives the margin of safety I require.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: volumes keep falling.** Unit volume fell 7.0% in the third quarter and branded retail volume 8.6%. Price and mix of +2.3% does not cover it, and promotional intensity is high.
- **The covenant amendment.** The company needs lenders to relax the leverage and interest coverage tests from April 2027. The terms (margin, fees, guarantees, limits on dividends or buybacks) are unknown. A breach would be an event of default.
- **Another dividend action.** A halved dividend frees about $53 million a year. Suspending it would free about $107 million but would tell the market how tight the covenant is.
- **Cost inflation.** A summary of the call says commodity costs build in the fourth quarter as hedges roll off and relief is not expected before the second half of 2027.
- **Book value.** Equity of $1,314.2 million sits against $2,004.8 million of goodwill and other intangibles, so an impairment would hit book value and could matter for the covenants. I did not find the covenant definition.
- **Product quality and asset reviews.** The Nature's Own Hawaiian Bread recall in October has no verified cost. The August 10-Q carried $102.2 million of spare parts under a recoverability review, and I did not re-check its status.

## Management execution

The evidence got worse. Management cut guidance in August and again in November, said after the second quarter that earlier pricing and the Nature's Own relaunch should help stabilize performance, and then reported a larger volume decline. The company also lost its investment grade rating in September. On the other side, it refinanced the October maturity early, has an undrawn revolver, cut the dividend in May, and is negotiating with lenders before a breach. **Assessment: 6/20, recent underdelivery and a balance sheet that now constrains choices. The next test is the amendment terms and fourth-quarter EBITDA against the $65.7 million to $80.7 million implied.**
