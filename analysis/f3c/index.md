## What SFC Energy is

SFC Energy, based in Brunnthal near Munich, makes off-grid power systems. The Clean Energy segment sells methanol and hydrogen fuel cells under the EFOY name for surveillance cameras, traffic and environmental monitoring, industrial sites and soldiers in the field, where a quiet, low-heat power source that runs for weeks matters. The Clean Power Management segment, built from acquisitions in the Netherlands, Canada and Romania, makes power electronics and drives for semiconductor equipment makers, oil and gas producers in Canada, life science and defense customers. The company employs about 500 people and is in the SDAX.

On May 12, 2026 it won the largest order in its history: about €42.7 million of fuel cell systems for Ukraine under a German government support program, for military and civilian use, including charging drones in the field. About half was shipped by June and nearly all of the rest by the end of September. SFC is building local training and service in Ukraine and preparing to return to the US defense market, where management expects programs to recover to earlier levels within 24 to 36 months.

## Which numbers to trust

- **Adjusted figures leave things out.** SFC adjusts for items such as impairments of capitalized development costs and other one-offs. I deduct €1.5 million a year from adjusted operating profit as a recurring cost of doing business.
- **2025 shows the downside.** Sales of €143.3 million produced adjusted operating profit of €8.9 million, a net loss of €0.6 million after taxes, and negative operating cash flow of €5.4 million.
- **Working capital eats growth.** Working capital reached 42% of trailing sales in June, up from 37% at the end of 2025. Every €10 million of extra sales ties up roughly €4 million, so profit converts slowly into cash.
- **The tax rate is high.** In 2025 the tax bill of €4.5 million was larger than pre-tax profit of €3.6 million. I use 30%.

## Primary valuation: normalized cash flow

The question is what SFC earns in a normal year after 2026. In the Base, sales drop from about €170 million in 2026 to €165 million in 2027, as civilian business recovers and defense orders continue at a smaller scale, then grow 6% to 8% a year to €217 million in 2031. The adjusted operating margin rises from 11.5% to 13.5%, above the 10.7% of 2024, the best year before Ukraine. I take 30% tax, add working capital at 38% of new sales, spend €1 million a year more on investment than depreciation, and discount at 9.5% with 2.5% growth after 2031. The Base value is **€14.54**.

- **Why sales fall in 2027:** the Ukraine order was a single €42.7 million delivery. Without it, 2026 sales would be about €128 million. I assume the core grows 10% in 2027 and defense adds about €24 million from Ukraine follow-ons, the US and other armies, a little more than half of this year's order.
- **Why 13.5% and not this year's margin:** the 2026 margin of about 15.5% comes from one high-margin defense contract. Civilian fuel cells and power electronics have earned 6% to 11% in recent years. More defense business should lift the mix, but not to Ukraine levels across the whole company.
- **Why 9.5%:** a beta near 1.3, small size and lumpy government orders. The company has no debt, so this is the cost of equity.

## Cross-check: normalized operating profit

The second view skips the forecast years. I take the Base's 2027 adjusted operating profit of €19.0 million, deduct the €1.5 million of recurring items, and apply 10.2 times, the multiple that fits 4% long-run growth, a 20% return on new capital, 30% tax and a 9.5% cost of capital. Adding net cash gives **€12.61** a share, 13.3% below the Base. The gap is the faster growth to 2031 that the Base includes and this view does not.

## Valuation triangulation

- Primary (normalized cash flow, Base): €14.54. This is the central value.
- Cross-check (normalized operating profit): €12.61, 13.3% below.

Every version I can build puts fair value below the price unless the 2026 margin becomes permanent. That is the bet the market is making.

## How the buy price is set

Confidence is C, which carries a default margin of safety of 42.5%. I add nothing to it, because order lumpiness already sits in the scenarios. That gives a maximum buy price of **€8.36**.

With the long-run margin 1.5 points higher or lower and a discount rate between 9% and 10%, the same margin gives buy prices from €6.95 to €9.99. The price of €18.43 is far above all of them, so the decision does not depend on which is right. Over the past year the shares traded between €11.36 and €24.80.

SFC pays no dividend. The return comes only from growth in value, which here means proving that 2026 was the start of a defense business and not a single good year.

## Reasons to own SFC Energy

- The product works where it matters: SFC calls its systems combat-proven, and fuel cells give soldiers quiet, low-heat power that runs for weeks with little fuel.
- European defense spending is rising and modern warfare needs far more electricity in the field, especially for drones.
- Profit rises fast with volume: adjusted EBITDA more than tripled in nine months on sales up 29%.
- A clean balance sheet with about €41 million of net cash and no meaningful debt.
- Several options at once: Ukraine follow-ons with local service, a return to the US defense market, India, the Middle East and semiconductor equipment.

## What could go wrong?

- The Ukraine order does not repeat. It was financed by a German government program; a ceasefire, a budget change or a competitor could end it. Without it, 2026 sales would have fallen.
- Customer and contract concentration. One contract was about a quarter of 2026 sales.
- Working capital. At around 40% of sales, growth absorbs most of the cash it creates.
- Supply chain. Specific components limited deliveries in 2026, and platinum prices affect costs.
- Execution of the ERP project and the build-up abroad, which already push costs into the fourth quarter.

## Management execution

- The team won and delivered the largest order in the company's history on time, in under five months.
- Guidance was raised twice in 2026 and earnings already beat the old full-year range after nine months.
- 2025 was a step back: lower profit, a net loss and negative operating cash flow.
- Communication on the fourth quarter is candid: weaker mix and higher costs were flagged in advance.
- Diversification is still mostly ambition: the US, India and the Middle East have not yet shown up in the numbers.

On my ten-point management check (two points each), SFC scores 12 of 20: credit for execution on Ukraine and disclosure, less for cash conversion and the 2025 setback.

## What I would watch from here

The full nine-month release on November 11 should confirm the preliminary figures and show cash flow and the order backlog at September 30. After that, the order book matters more than the income statement: any new defense contract, in Ukraine or elsewhere, and the guidance for 2027 in the spring.

### Green

- A follow-on Ukraine or other defense order of €20 million or more.
- Civilian fuel cell sales back above 2025 levels as customers rebuild stock.
- Operating cash flow above €15 million for 2026.

### Yellow

- Fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA at the bottom of the implied €2.3 to €4.3 million.
- Working capital above 40% of sales at year end.
- 2027 guidance below this year's sales.

### Red

- No new large defense order through mid-2027.
- Adjusted operating margin back below 10% for a full year.
- A capital raise to fund growth.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Ukraine-size defense orders become routine and lift the long-run margin above 16%, which would make the price fair or cheap.
2. The civilian business keeps shrinking, so 2027 looks like 2025 rather than 2026.
3. Growth keeps absorbing cash through working capital, so profit never turns into free cash flow.

The weakest point is the long-run margin, because 1.5 points move the value by about €1.75 and there is no history of mid-teens margins outside this year. The strongest bull argument is that European armies are only starting to buy field power at scale, and SFC is already qualified and deployed. The strongest bear argument is that the business that existed before May 2026 shrank this year.

## Final verdict: hold at €18.43

SFC Energy had the best year in its history and earned it by delivering a hard contract fast. The share price treats that year as the new floor. Until a second order of that size arrives, it is still the ceiling.

**Verdict: HOLD at €18.43, watchlist for new money. Base €14.54, 21.1% below the price, Bear €7.94, Bull €22.22, cross-check €12.61, Confidence C (15/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price €8.36, range €6.95 to €9.99 under nearby assumptions.**

New money buys only at or below €8.36. For an existing holder the original cost is irrelevant: with the price above the Base, the holding is a bet on repeat defense orders, and I would size it as one. The next tests are the nine-month release on November 11 and the 2027 guidance.
