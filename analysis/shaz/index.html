At the end of September, Sharon AI owed its suppliers $1.24 billion and had just earned $9.9 million of revenue in a quarter. Both numbers belong to the same plan: the payables buy the chips and the contracts are meant to pay for them. The market now pays $22.32 for the shares, and my model of the contracts alone gives $17.46 before the cross-check, so the question is how much to pay for a plan before it bills.

At $22.32 the shares trade 19.1% above my weighted Base value of $18.74, so the Base is 16.0% below the price. The Bull value of $44.31 is 98.5% above the price and the Bear value is zero. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade D, which gives a required margin of safety of 55% and a maximum buy price of $8.43. The verdict is Hold, do not add: the contracts may well be worth the price, but there is no margin for delay, and a new buyer is paid for none of the risk.

Valuation route: a pre-profit operator of rented data center capacity and GPU clusters, so it is valued on its contracted cash flows: a path from today's spending to the cash the contracts return, less everything owed ahead of shareholders. Primary method: those cash flows discounted at 12%. Cross-check: book value per diluted share. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Sharon AI is

Sharon AI is an Australian "neocloud": it rents out clusters of Nvidia GPUs, housed in data centers in Australia and New Zealand, to AI labs and technology companies that want capacity without building it. It listed on Nasdaq in February 2026 with a $125 million IPO at $30 a share, and has since raised over $2.6 billion of debt and equity. The business model has moved from usage-based billing to take-or-pay contracts, in which the customer pays for the capacity whether or not it uses it.

TCV is aggregate contracted spend under binding contracts. It is not revenue and it can change with contract changes. ARR is one month of expected billing times twelve, and it is not a forecast. Most contracted capacity does not bill until the clusters are commissioned, tested, verified by Nvidia and accepted by the customer. So far one cluster of about 1,000 Nvidia B300 chips has been delivered and accepted, and about 8,000 B300 and 4,000 GB300 chips have been powered up since the quarter ended and are being verified.

The share count is the weakest number in this analysis. A pre-funded warrant is a share that has already been paid for, so I count it as a share. At roughly the $30 IPO price, $438.1 million would be about 14 million shares, and I use that as an estimate, which gives 52.5 million shares. The convertible notes are carried at fair value, which already includes what holders would gain by converting at today's price, so I deduct them as debt and do not also add shares.

Management's statement that prepayments run to up to 30% of TCV, or 60% of capex, implies capex of about half of TCV, or $4.8 billion for the $9.6 billion contract book. I use that. About $1.9 billion of it is already on the balance sheet (property and equipment plus right-of-use assets), so about $2.9 billion is still to be incurred, plus the $1.24 billion of payables to be paid: about $4.1 billion of uses against $1.66 billion of cash and deposits, a $356 million facility and further customer prepayments. The rest has to come from more GPU-backed debt, which is why my discount rate is 12% and not 10%.

Valuation triangulation

  • Primary (contracted cash flows, 12% rate): $17.46, weight 60%.
  • Secondary (book value per diluted share): $20.66, weight 40%.
  • Divergence between the two: 16.7% of their midpoint, inside the 20% limit.
  • Weighted Base: $18.74.

The two methods are close enough, but they are not independent. Both use my estimate of 52.5 million shares, and both depend on the same equipment spending. I weight the primary method higher because its assumptions can be read and changed. Book value is a floor-like cross-check, and its meaning depends on the GPUs being worth what was paid for them. A growth-adjusted valuation ratio is not meaningful with negative earnings.

Dividend and buybacks

Sharon AI has never paid a dividend and says it does not expect to. It issues shares, convertible notes and pre-funded warrants to fund the build, and the weighted average share count in the third quarter, 38.5 million, was 135% above the second quarter.

Reasons to own Sharon AI

  • A $9.6 billion contract book over 5.5 years, with $2.5 billion added since the second quarter. Management says the majority is with investment-grade customers, and the new contracts are take-or-pay.
  • Pricing is moving up. Long-term contracts rose from about $15 million to over $19 million of annual revenue per megawatt in 2026, and shorter Nvidia program contracts are expected above $30 million.
  • Customers fund a large part of the build. Prepayments of up to 30% of TCV, or 60% of capex, and about $0.8 billion of deposits cut what shareholders and lenders must fund.
  • The first cluster of about 1,000 B300 chips has been delivered and accepted, and about 12,000 more chips are powered up and in verification, so the first billing is real, not only signed.
  • Revenue is guided to rise from a $50 million annual run-rate in September to $400 million to $450 million in January 2027 if the verification is completed.
  • At 0.79 times reported book value the shares are priced below the capital paid in, if the share count is the reported one.

What could go wrong?

  • Funding. Uses of about $4.1 billion against $1.66 billion of cash and deposits and one $356 million facility. More GPU-backed debt is "well advanced" but not signed, and payables are $1.24 billion. If lenders wait, the company can issue more shares.
  • Billing starts late. The January target of $400 million to $450 million depends on Nvidia verification of two deployments in December and January. One customer's revenue has already slipped from the third quarter into December and January. A one-year delay takes my primary value to $8.72.
  • The Nvidia program. Its floor price is $4.9 billion, more than half of the contract book, for chips that are expected online in 2027 and still need end customers. The guarantee, clawback and revenue-share terms are not disclosed, and 155 MW of capacity is available for sale.
  • Dilution. The pre-funded warrant count and the conversion terms of the $1.01 billion of notes are not disclosed. My 52.5 million share count is an estimate, and 60 million shares would cut the Base to $16.40.
  • Hardware and power. GPUs lose value as new generations arrive, and the contracts cover five or six years. The company's own list of risks includes power reliability and obsolescence.
  • Customer concentration is not disclosed. The large customers are unnamed, apart from one named Indian cloud provider.
  • Governance. Yahoo showed an ISS governance quality score of 8 on November 1, where 10 is the highest risk, and the finance and legal leadership are new in 2026.

Management execution

  • Contracting has outrun billing. The company added $2.5 billion of TCV in one quarter and signed a further $1.17 billion after it, but third-quarter revenue of $9.9 million was far below the consensus of $26.94 million.
  • The team is new. A chief financial officer (ex-Macquarie) and a chief legal officer were appointed in the third quarter, a chief operating officer (ex-Telstra) in August and a new chairman during the second quarter. It is a credible team that has not yet run this build to the end.
  • The company has raised over $2.6 billion of institutional debt and equity since December 2025. It has not given a total capex figure or an adjusted EBITDA guide, and the mid-2027 target of 64,000 GPUs was not repeated in the quarter, as one third-party review noted.
  • Disclosure is thin in the places that matter most for shareholders: note terms, warrant count, capex commitments and customer concentration are all absent from the release.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • The Nvidia verification of the December and January deployments is completed and the January run-rate lands inside $400 million to $450 million.
  • Further GPU-backed facilities close, with disclosed rates and terms, and payables fall.
  • The next filing gives the pre-funded warrant count and the note terms, and the diluted count is close to 52.5 million or lower.

Yellow

  • The January run-rate arrives below $400 million, or acceptance dates slip another quarter.
  • New debt is priced well above the 12% I assume for the whole company.
  • Adjusted EBITDA margin at the first scaled quarter is below 58%.

Red

  • The company issues more shares or notes because lenders have not closed, with payables still above $1 billion.
  • The Nvidia program floor is cut, clawed back or not sold on to end customers.
  • A large customer delays acceptance or contests billing.

The next hard data are the verification results in December and January, the January run-rate and the full quarterly filing.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The EBITDA margin comes in at 58% and not 65%. The weighted Base falls to $14.91, 33% below the price.
  2. Billing starts a year late. The primary value falls to $8.72, 61% below the price.
  3. The warrant count and the notes add more shares than I assume, or new equity is needed to pay the suppliers. At 60 million shares the weighted Base is $16.40.

The weakest point of the model is the combination of the share count and the margin, neither of which can be read from a filing yet. Final verdict: hold at $22.32, do not add

Sharon AI is a contracted build, not yet a business with earnings, and the price is close to what the contracts are worth if everything goes on time. That is not a bargain and not a disaster. It is a stock where the downside is a total loss and the upside is a double, and the margin of safety does not exist at this price.

So far one cluster of about 1,000 chips has passed customer acceptance, and the January target depends on the next ones passing in December and January.

Verdict: HOLD at $22.32, do not add. Weighted Base $18.74, 16.0% below the price, cross-check $20.66, Confidence D (10.0/25). Required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $8.43 (range $6.54 to $12.95).

A holder has no strong reason to sell before the January billing is known, because the Base is 16% below the price and the Bull is almost twice it. A new buyer under my rules waits for $8.43 or lower, or for the January numbers, the note terms and the warrant count, any of which would change the model.
