DISCO earned a 47.4% operating margin in the September quarter, a record, and its shares hit a 2026 low the next day. Both are true because management guided the December quarter's operating income to ¥58.1 billion from ¥67.6 billion, a fall of 14%. At ¥49,540 the shares pay 32 times a run-rate that is already below the quarter just reported.

At ¥49,540 on October 30, 2026, DISCO trades 45.6% above my weighted Base of ¥34,025, which is 31.3% below the price. The Bear value of ¥21,078 is 57.5% below the price and the Bull value of ¥49,112 is 0.9% below it, so even the Bull case does not reach the price. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The verdict is Reduce.

Valuation route: a semiconductor equipment maker with a leading position in precision cutting and grinding (a 71.7% gross margin is the evidence), cyclical in demand and unusually profitable, valued on five-year net income with an exit multiple and cross-checked on through-cycle earnings. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What DISCO is

DISCO makes the machines that cut silicon wafers into chips (dicing saws), grind them thin (grinders) and polish them, and the blades, wheels and other consumables that wear out in those machines. Its customers are chip makers and the outsourced assemblers that package chips. Management says the annual mix is roughly half high bandwidth memory and half logic, and that memory was just under 40% of shipments in the September quarter.

The current fiscal period is guided as April to December 2026. All growth rates below compare with the same period a year earlier unless stated.

Shipments are higher in the guidance but sales and profit are lower, because sales trail shipments and the gross margin is expected to fall. The company guided the nine months to ¥389.5 billion of sales, ¥174.7 billion of operating income and ¥121.9 billion of net income (¥1,123 a share), and the December quarter ends the guided period; I found no outlook beyond it. It assumes ¥151 to the dollar.

Management said high bandwidth memory shipments will pause in the December quarter after a high September quarter, and called that quarter-to-quarter variability and not a change in demand. It also said it could not clearly explain its forecast for the quarter after that. Both statements are fair, and neither is something to value as a trend.

What the cash flow is made of

  • Cash conversion is lower than the margin suggests. First-half operating cash flow was ¥111.7 billion on net income of ¥80.4 billion, but investing outflow was ¥54.8 billion, so free cash flow was ¥56.9 billion, 71% of net income.
  • Spending is rising. Capital spending for the period is about ¥33 billion against depreciation of about ¥16 billion, including a new plant in Hiroshima (about ¥33 billion over three years) and a research center in Haneda (about ¥14 billion).
  • Inventories are large. ¥160.8 billion of inventories is about 1.1 times the September quarter's net sales, a number to watch if orders slow.
  • Hiring is accelerating. About 100 more support staff will be added by March 2027, for a total of about 200 in that group.
  • Dividends are formulaic. The policy is 25% of half-year net income, so the ¥186 interim dividend rises and falls with profit.

Secondary valuation: through-cycle earnings

The equipment industry has downturns. I take net income of ¥135 billion as through-cycle in the Base, 82% of the December quarter's run-rate, and apply 24 times, then add net cash. That gives ¥33,005. The Bear uses ¥105 billion at 18 times and the Bull ¥165 billion at 28 times.

Growth diagnostic

Sales rose 32% in the first half and net income 44%, so profit grew faster than sales as the gross margin reached a record. This is structural at the level of the franchise and cyclical at the level of the year: the company expects consumables, the part that does not depend on new machine orders, to be roughly flat excluding currency in the December quarter. I classify the growth as strong but cyclical, with the margin close to a peak.

Valuation triangulation

  • Primary (five-year net income value): ¥35,046, weight 50%.
  • Secondary (through-cycle earnings): ¥33,005, weight 50%.
  • Divergence: 6% of the midpoint, below the 20% warning level.
  • Weighted Base: ¥34,025.

The methods agree because both assume 24 times earnings and net income in the low ¥130 billion to ¥210 billion range. Their agreement is not independent evidence, since both rest on my multiple. The gap to the price is the main finding, and it does not depend on which method is used.

Dividend and buybacks

The interim dividend is ¥186 a share, up from ¥129, and the year-end dividend has not been decided. The policy of 25% of half-year net income gives a yield of about 0.8% on an annualized basis at the current price. I found no buyback in what I read.

Reasons to own DISCO

  • A 71.7% gross margin and 47.4% operating margin are exceptional for equipment makers, and they reflect a position that competitors have not displaced.
  • Net cash of about ¥340 billion and an equity ratio of 76% mean no financing risk.
  • Shipments were a record ¥148.6 billion, ahead of plan, and the December quarter guidance for shipments is higher still.
  • Demand is broad: logic, power chips, China and new fields such as co-packaged optics are all expected to rise in the December quarter.
  • Consumables, which are tied to chips already being made, are expected to hold roughly flat excluding currency in the December quarter, a floor under the cycle.

What could go wrong?

  • The multiple. At about 32 times run-rate earnings, a re-rating to 24 times with unchanged profit is a 25% fall.
  • The cycle. Management says it cannot clearly explain its forecast for the quarter after December, and high bandwidth memory is about half of the mix.
  • Gross margin. It is expected to fall just under 2 points in the December quarter, partly from currency.
  • China. China demand is described as strong and centered on outsourced assemblers; export rules could change that.
  • Currency. The yen assumption of ¥151 to the dollar matters for about 1 point of gross margin.
  • Capital spending. Spending of about ¥33 billion against ¥16 billion of depreciation raises the cost of a downturn.

Management execution

  • Guidance was beaten: September shipments were ¥148.6 billion against a plan of ¥141 billion and the gross margin came in 1.5 points above assumption.
  • The company now publishes a nine-month forecast, which is new, and it is cautious: December operating income is guided below the September quarter.
  • Management disclosed that it cannot clearly explain its forecast for the following quarter, which is an honest statement and a sign of limited visibility.
  • The dividend policy ties payments to profit, and the company keeps most of the profit as cash.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • December quarter operating income comes in above ¥58.1 billion.
  • The company gives a full 2027 outlook with shipments above ¥600 billion a year.
  • High bandwidth memory shipments resume after the December pause.

Yellow

  • Gross margin below 70%.
  • Inventories rising faster than sales.
  • Chinese assembler shipments falling for a second quarter.

Red

  • Operating margin below 40%.
  • A cut to the shipment plan from a major memory customer.
  • Free cash flow staying below 60% of net income.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The equipment cycle turns down in 2027, as in my Bear case.
  2. The multiple falls to 18 to 20 times earnings on any growth miss.
  3. Competitors close the technical gap in thinning and dicing for advanced packaging.

The strongest bear argument is that equipment demand reverses and the multiple falls to 18 times, which is the ¥21,078 Bear.

Final verdict: reduce at ¥49,540

The question is not whether DISCO is a great company. It is whether a business that guides profit down 14% from one quarter to the next deserves to be valued as if profit only goes up.

Verdict: REDUCE at ¥49,540. Weighted Base ¥34,025, 31.3% below the price, cross-check ¥33,005, Confidence C (16/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, buy range ¥18,978 to ¥20,151, conservative buy limit ¥18,978.

For an existing holder, the original cost is irrelevant. The price is above even my Bull value, so I would trim a large position and keep a core holding for the franchise, which is the part of the business I trust. New money waits for a price at or below ¥20,151. The next test is the December quarter result and the guidance for the quarter after December.
