# Nebius revenue rose 571% and the shares fell 16%, because 2027 capex is $40 to $45 billion

Nebius reported third quarter revenue of $980.8 million, up 571%, a small operating profit and customer prepayments that cover more than 100% of the capital for the quarter's new deals. The shares fell about 16% anyway, because the same report put 2027 capital spending at $40 billion to $45 billion.

That number is the whole valuation. Nebius spent $15.4 billion in nine months and guides to about $27 billion for 2026, and the question for a shareholder is whether each dollar of GPU capacity earns more than it costs. Management says it does: payback on revenue in about 1 year 7 months and annual contract value of more than $25 million per megawatt. My model says it earns roughly its cost of capital unless prices and useful lives hold up better than I assume, and the equity is what is left after that.

My triangulated Base value is **$52.17**, which is 51.0% below the $106.51 reference price. The Bear value is $5.00 and the Bull value is $334.49. Confidence is grade D (7/25), which requires a 55% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at **$23.48**. The verdict is a reduce. The price is not absurd: it needs about 10% more revenue per dollar of capital than my Base, which is inside what the company is reporting, but I cannot verify it.

Valuation route: capital-intensive AI cloud with heavy customer prepayments. Primary method: a vintage cash flow model that values the installed GPU capacity and each year of new capacity through 2031 on its own contracted economics, less all claims, discounted at 11%. Secondary method: installed capacity at a multiple of run-rate EBITDA after stock pay, plus the same value from new capacity. Classification: quality growth company, 5 of 12 on the generic scorecard. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets. The reference price is an intraday quote, see the stress test section.

## What Nebius is now

Nebius Group is an AI cloud: it builds or leases data center capacity, fills it with GPUs and rents it to AI developers and large customers. Nebius AI Cloud was $973.8 million of the $980.8 million of revenue in the quarter. Avride (robotaxis and delivery robots), TripleTen (education) and stakes in ClickHouse and Toloka sit alongside. The company says it has more than 4 GW of contracted power, aims for 5 GW by year-end, names Microsoft and Meta among large customers, and has a remaining performance obligation (RPO, contracted revenue not yet recognized) of $38.7 billion.

Annualized run-rate revenue (ARR) was $6.4 billion at the end of September, up from $3.0 billion at the end of June. The company has dropped its old December ARR target of $7 billion to $9 billion in favor of above $7 billion in February, and from 2027 it will guide to revenue, pre-tax earnings and capacity instead.

## Which cash to value, and why prepayments matter

There is no free cash flow to value. The business spent $15.4 billion on capital in nine months, and the positive operating cash flow came from customers paying in advance. Deferred revenue, the accounting record of cash received for service not yet delivered, was $9.4 billion at September 30 ($1.4 billion current and $8.0 billion non-current), up from $1.6 billion at the end of 2025. That money is real and has been spent on GPUs, but the revenue it buys will be recognized without new cash. I therefore treat 90% of it as a claim in the Base and 100% in the Bear.

I value the business as a set of capital purchases, called vintages, each with its own return. For every dollar of capital I assume annual revenue of $0.62, a 50% adjusted EBITDA margin and stock pay of 5% of revenue, a useful life of 5.5 years and 20% tax on profit after depreciation. The $0.62 is below the 0.63 implied by the company's 1 year 7 month payback, because capacity arrives on average about a year after the money is spent. The margin sits between the AI cloud's 53.2% and the group's 47%. All of these are my estimates.

## Primary valuation: vintage cash flow model

Each vintage earns revenue starting the year after the capital is spent (60% of a full year in the first, a full year after that, and a partial last year), pays out EBITDA less stock pay less tax, and is discounted at 11%, a cost of capital for a levered, high-beta company in a young market. The installed capital is sunk, so only its future cash flows count. New capital for 2027 to 2031 is $42.5 billion, $45 billion, $45 billion, $40 billion and $35 billion, with the first year at the guided level. Customer prepayments are financing, so I count their float benefit at 10% of the prepaid amount (45% of new capital) and nothing else. After 2031 I assume new capital earns exactly its cost, so the value is only what the existing and planned capacity produces.

The installed capital is worth roughly what it cost, and the planned $207 billion of new capital adds only $5.7 billion, about 2.7% of its cost. That is the thin-spread result at the center of this analysis: at my assumptions each new GPU dollar earns about 11% to 13% a year, close to what investors require. Value to shareholders comes from the spread, and the spread is a function of three things the company controls only partly: price per GPU, how long the GPUs stay rentable, and how much of the build is delayed.

## Secondary valuation: installed capacity at a multiple

The second method values the installed capital by what a buyer of GPU capacity would pay. Run-rate EBITDA after stock pay on the installed capital is $8.6 billion a year, and a multiple of 4.5 times (about the present value of a five and a half year stream at 11%, with a small residual) gives $38.8 billion. The value of new capital is the same as above, so the method is not independent of the first. After net claims the secondary Base is **$66.60**.

## Growth check and history

A growth-adjusted check is not meaningful: reported earnings are positive for nine months only because of an $780.6 million revaluation of equity stakes, and operations lost $253.5 million over the same period. The growth is plain in revenue. Nine-month revenue was $1,962.1 million against $302.1 million, and the company guides to $12 billion to $13 billion in 2027. What I cannot verify is how much of the 2027 revenue is already in contracts at known prices. I did not verify a history of valuation multiples from a consistent source, so it carries no weight here.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $106.51 the market values the equity at $32.7 billion. Holding everything else at Base, that requires revenue of 0.68 per dollar of capital instead of 0.62 (11% more), or GPUs that keep earning for 6.4 years instead of 5.5, or a 8.3% cost of capital. None of these is extreme. The company says contract value per MW is above $25 million, up from above $20 million a quarter earlier, and the average GPU-hour price rose more than 20%. If the price increases persist, the market is right, and if they reflect scarce capacity during a build-out, they will not be there when the GPUs are older. I cannot tell which from the filings, and I believe that is why the shares moved 16% on the report.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence score of 7 is grade D, which carries a base margin of safety of 55%. I add nothing on top, because pricing risk, delay risk and the funding need are already in the Bear case, the claims and the Confidence grade. Applied to the $52.17 Base, the **maximum buy price is $23.48**. The reference price is 354% above it. A wide spread between outcomes means that the margin of safety rule decides, not the precision of the Base: the shares would have to fall below $23.48 for the rule to be met.

## Dividend and capital return

Nebius pays no dividend and funds growth with convertible notes, asset-backed debt, customer prepayments and share sales. It sold $3.9 billion of treasury shares in nine months. Stock-based compensation was $196.5 million over nine months, or 10% of revenue, and I deduct it as a cost of the business.

## Reasons to own Nebius

- **Large contracted demand.** RPO of $38.7 billion, more than 4 GW of contracted power, and $12 billion of new contract value signed since the start of the third quarter.
- **Prepayments fund the build.** More than 90% of Q3 contract value carried a prepayment, which covered over 100% of the capital for those deals.
- **Improving unit economics.** The company reports payback of 1 year 7 months, down from 1 year 10 months, and contract value per MW above $25 million, up from above $20 million.
- **Operating profit.** Income from operations turned positive in the quarter at $50.4 million.
- **Hidden assets.** The ClickHouse stake, raised at about a $15 billion valuation in January, sits in $1.6 billion of non-marketable equity at carrying value, and I count it at carrying value only.

These support the Base. They do not show a margin of safety at $106.51.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: GPU pricing and useful life.** The value is a thin spread on a very large amount of capital. A fall in revenue per dollar of capital from {B["r"]:.2f} to 0.55 takes the Base value to zero.
- **Funding.** $40 billion to $45 billion of 2027 capital needs 75% to 85% to come from prepayments and asset-backed financing, and the rest from debt or shares. Higher interest rates or weaker equity markets raise the cost at once.
- **Delays.** The remaining New Jersey tranches slipped into 2027. Power and construction timing decide when revenue starts and how much of 2026 and 2027 revenue lands where.
- **Customer concentration.** Microsoft and Meta are named as large customers. Changes in their plans would change both revenue and the asset-backed financing that depends on it.
- **Dilution.** The share count rose from about 252 million diluted a year ago to 292.9 million outstanding, and the company funds growth partly by selling treasury shares and issuing convertible notes.
- **Earnings quality.** About $325 million of quarterly revenue was non-recurring, adjusted EBITDA excludes stock pay and nine-month net income includes an $780.6 million revaluation gain.

## Management execution

Management has delivered what it promised on contracted power, signed customers and prepayments, and moved the unit economics in the right direction. It also lowered its 2026 revenue to the low end of the range, delayed some capacity, and set a capital plan roughly 50% above the range it had before. **Assessment: 11/20, strong commercial execution and a high bar on delivery and financing. The next tests are the New Jersey deliveries by year-end, the February ARR above $7 billion, and the first reported quarter of 2027 capital spending.**

## Final verdict: reduce at $106.51

Nebius is growing faster than almost any company I have valued, with prepayments that fund its build. It is also spending $27 billion this year and $40 billion to $45 billion next, so the equity value is a thin spread on a very large amount of capital. At $106.51 the price is 104% above my Base and 354% above the maximum buy price, and the market is already paying for pricing and useful lives better than I can verify.

**Verdict: REDUCE at $106.51. Triangulated Base value $52.17, downside 51.0%, Confidence D (7/25), required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $23.48.**

The price needs about 10% more revenue per dollar of capital than I model, and the company says contract value per megawatt is already above $25 million. The question for shareholders is whether it is still there when the GPUs are five years old.
