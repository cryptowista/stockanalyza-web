# Salesforce earned $3.5 billion in the quarter, and $2.6 billion of it was a paper gain

CRM, NYSE. Published November 17, 2026. Price as of the November 17, 2026 intraday quote (about 12:12 p.m. New York time). Latest reported quarter: May to July 2026, released on August 26, 2026.

Salesforce reported $3.5 billion of net income for the quarter ended July 31, 2026, and $2.6 billion of it, 74%, was a paper gain on private company shares. The 10-Q puts the quarter's gain on the Anthropic stake alone at about $2.7 billion. Take that out and the business still produced $1.1 billion of free cash flow in the quarter, and guides to about $15 billion for the year.

That cash flow is the reason the stock is not a clear sell after a 9.9% one-day fall. It is also the reason I do not call it a buy. About $4.2 billion of the $15 billion goes to staff as stock, the company has borrowed $39.5 billion, and organic revenue growth is about 7% once the Informatica deal is removed.

My triangulated Base value is **$224.48**, which is 15.9% above the $193.63 reference price. The Bear value of $105.56 is 45.5% below the price and the Bull value of $334.60 is 72.8% above it. Confidence is grade C (13/25), which requires a 42.5% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at **$129.08**. The price sits 50% above that. The verdict is a hold, a watchlist name for new money.

Valuation route: large recurring-revenue software company with strong free cash flow and a debt-funded share count reduction. Primary method: a growth-adjusted multiple of fiscal 2028 owner cash flow, where owner cash flow is free cash flow before interest, less stock pay. Secondary method: a two-stage cash flow model discounted at 10%. Classification: quality growth company, 7 of 12 on the software scorecard. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets. The reference price is an intraday quote, see the stress test section.

## What Salesforce does and what changed in the numbers

Salesforce sells subscription software for sales, service, marketing, commerce and data, plus Slack, Tableau, MuleSoft and, since November 2025, Informatica. Subscription and support was 95% of second quarter revenue ($10,820 million of $11,345 million). The newer products matter for the story: the company says Agentforce and Data 360 annual recurring revenue reached nearly $3.9 billion, up 210%, and Agentforce alone passed $1.5 billion. That is about 9% of annual revenue on a run-rate basis, which is a long way from replacing the core.

The core is growing slowly. Revenue rose 11% in the quarter, but the company says Informatica contributed $456 million, so my arithmetic gives organic growth of about 6.5%. In the Agentforce Apps segment ($7,193 million of subscription and support), growth was 8% in constant currency. The Data 360, Headless Platform and Other segment ($3,618 million) grew 20%, which very likely includes most of the Informatica contribution.

## Primary valuation: growth-adjusted multiple of owner cash flow

Salesforce scores 7 of 12 on my software scorecard, which routes it to a growth-adjusted multiple rather than a compounder model. The multiple is applied to fiscal 2028 owner cash flow, which covers February 2027 to January 2028, with owner cash flow defined before interest so that enterprise value is the right match. The result is a value today on the same convention I used for ServiceNow, not a 12 month target.

Fiscal 2027 revenue is $46.05 billion, the guided midpoint less $200 million from deals that have not closed. The 7.0% growth in fiscal 2028 sits between the 6% to 7% organic rate and guided growth that includes acquisitions, and then fades by half a point a year. The free cash flow margin before interest stays flat at about 35.5%, in line with guidance of flat operating margins, and stock pay declines slowly from 9.0% to 8.4%, which is my assumption and not company guidance.

Why 14 times? The market pays 13.3 times fiscal 2028 Base owner cash flow today. The 14 times I use implies that owner cash flow keeps growing about 6% to 7% for years at a 10% required return, then fades, which matches the Base path. The Bear uses 9 times for a business whose revenue stops growing, and the Bull 18 times for growth near 10% with falling stock pay. These multiples are my judgment inside a plausible range and not a measured peer median, because I did not build a synchronized peer table. The Base owner cash flow of $13.2 billion times 14 gives an enterprise value of $184 billion, less $20.0 billion of net claims, or **$205.28 a share**.

## Secondary valuation: ten year cash flow model

The second method discounts owner cash flow explicitly: the second half of fiscal 2027 ($5.7 billion, from guidance less the first half), five explicit years, five years of growth fading to 3.5%, and a terminal growth rate of 3.0%. The discount rate is 10%, a cost of equity for a large software company with a beta near 1.1 and new leverage, tested at 9% to 12%. Enterprise value is $223 billion, of which the terminal value is 53% over a horizon of about ten years. After net claims and 800 million shares: **$253.29**. The first half cash flows are excluded because the July 31 balance sheet already holds them.

## Growth check and history

The growth-adjusted check is weak for a different reason than usual. Reported earnings are distorted by investment gains, and growth in free cash flow of 4% to 5% for fiscal 2027 is below revenue growth, so a PEG ratio would flatter or punish the shares depending on which earnings figure I choose. I use it as a diagnostic only. Free cash flow per share is a better test: guided free cash flow of about $15.0 billion on 800 million shares is $18.75 a share, against about $15 a share in fiscal 2026 on about 960 million shares. The growth in that figure comes mostly from the lower share count, which was bought with borrowed money, and not from the operating business. I rate its growth quality as moderate, with low reliability of the forward estimates because I found no dispersion data.

I did not verify historical multiples from a consistent source, so they carry no weight in the valuation. The one history I can state is price: the shares were down about 18.8% for the year at $213.70 on November 9, and the stock fell 9.9% on November 17 from a $214.83 close, according to news reports.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $193.63 the enterprise value is $175 billion, or 13.3 times my Base fiscal 2028 owner cash flow. Solving the multiple method backward, the price needs 13.3 times, against my 14. Solving the cash flow model backward, it needs a discount rate of 11.9% against my 10%. Put plainly, the market is asking for a return near 12% a year on a business growing owner cash flow about 7%, which is what investors demand when they think seat-based software will lose pricing power or when they doubt that borrowed buybacks create value.

An owner free cash flow yield of 7.0% is high for a company with 95% subscription revenue. It does not make a buy, because the same table shows that the Bear value of $105.56 is 45% below the price and my margin of safety wants a price of $129.08 or lower.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence score of 13 is grade C, which carries a base margin of safety of 42.5%. I add nothing on top, because the debt-funded buyback, the strategic investment marks and the AI disruption risk are already in the Bear case, the haircut on investments and the Confidence grade. Applied to the $224.48 Base, the **maximum buy price is $129.08**, which is 22% above the Bear value. The reference price is 50% above it.

Four quantities are easy to confuse, so I keep them apart. Fair value today is $224.48. The probability-weighted value is $210.83. The margin of safety against model error gives $129.08. The yield the market is demanding, about 12%, is a fourth number and describes the price, not the value. For a conservative decision the margin of safety threshold controls.

## Reasons to own Salesforce

- **Strong cash generation.** Operating cash flow was $1,269 million in the second quarter, up 71%, and guided free cash flow is about $15 billion, or 9.7% of the market value.
- **A growing contracted base.** Current remaining performance obligation of $33.5 billion grew 14% in constant currency, and unearned revenue was $18.8 billion.
- **New products with real revenue.** Agentforce annual recurring revenue passed $1.5 billion, up 240%, and Agentforce with Data 360 reached nearly $3.9 billion.
- **Manageable debt.** No principal falls due before fiscal 2029, the $5.0 billion revolver is undrawn and interest is covered about five times by operating income.
- **Optionality in private holdings.** The Anthropic stake is carried at about $5.1 billion, or $6.38 a share, and I count only part of it.

These justify the Base above the price. They do not show a margin of safety at $193.63.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: AI shrinks seats.** Organic growth is already about 7%, and the Agentforce Apps segment grew 8% in constant currency. If AI agents reduce the number of paid users, the Bear case ($105) becomes the base case.
- **Acquisition overload.** Informatica, Qualified, Contentful, Fin and Braze together add up to a large sum, and free cash flow guidance grows 4% to 5% against 11% to 12% revenue growth. The pending deals need regulatory approval.
- **Debt-funded buybacks.** $39.5 billion of debt was added to shrink the share count, and a falling share price makes the repurchases look worse. The first 103 million shares from the $25.0 billion repurchase averaged $198.34, above the price today.
- **Earnings quality.** Net income includes $2.6 billion of unrealized gains that can reverse, and 45% of the $11.3 billion book is one company.
- **Stock pay.** About 9% of revenue is paid in shares, which is only partly offset by buybacks. A higher level would cut the Base materially.
- **Data security.** A news report mentioned a data breach, and I did not find the details or the cost.

## Management execution

The record is mixed. In August management raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook and reported subscription growth of about 7% organic. It repurchased about $27 billion of stock in six months, which will look well timed or poorly timed depending on where the shares go, and it funded that largely with debt. **Assessment: 11/20, strong cash discipline on operations, a looser approach to leverage, acquisitions and the stock repurchase price. The next test is the Contentful, Fin and Braze closings and whether free cash flow growth catches up with revenue growth.**
