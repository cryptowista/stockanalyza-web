In the second quarter IonQ reported a net loss of $1,868.6 million on revenue of $80.05 million. Of that loss, $1,649.1 million was a non-cash charge on the fair value of warrants, and the loss from operations was $337.2 million. Both figures are correct, and the gap between them is the first thing to understand about this stock. The accounting loss is mostly a bookkeeping entry, the operating loss is cash actually leaving the company, and the share price is paying for neither.

At $39.89 on October 9, 2026 the shares trade at 2.3 times my triangulated Base value of $17.23 and 1.5% above my Bull value of $39.29. The Bear value of $7.25 is 82% below the price. The spread between Bear and Bull is 5.4 to 1, and a gap that wide is simply what a speculative model produces. My method warns that this route cannot give a reliable intrinsic value, so treat every figure here as orientation. On Monday, October 12, the shares fell about 9% to roughly $36 after IonQ announced an all-stock purchase of the cybersecurity company Elisity, a quote taken during the session, which had not closed when I wrote this. At $36 the shares are still 8% below the Bull value and 109% above Base, and the verdict does not change.

How I valued it: IonQ is a deep-technology company (quantum computing and networking) that has just bought a chip foundry, and it has no free cash flow yet, so I value it on revenue with a path-to-cash-flow model as a cross-check. Primary method: enterprise value (EV) to 2027 revenue, plus SkyWater at its own multiple. Secondary method: a model of revenue, cash burn, dilution and an exit multiple to 2031, discounted at 15%. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets. I give no TAM (total addressable market) figure, because I found no independent estimate I trust and vendor figures are not evidence. The reverse valuation below does that job instead.

What IonQ is

IonQ builds quantum computers from trapped ions (charged atoms held in place by electric fields) and sells access to them, systems, and related networking and sensing products. It has grown mostly by acquisition: Oxford Ionics for about $1 billion in September 2025, paid mostly in stock, and SkyWater Technology, which closed on July 31, 2026. IonQ reported 99.99% two-qubit gate accuracy in 2025, a leading figure for the field, and says it has 84 optical terminals in orbit. The 256-qubit system is a stated goal that I did not find demonstrated, and I found no demonstration of a quantum computer doing commercially useful work that a classical computer cannot. So the risk to the thesis is the market, not the lab: IonQ is selling real products and leads on accuracy, but the large demand that today's valuation needs has not shown up.

SkyWater is a different business. It is a US contract chip foundry with Minnesota, Florida and Texas sites and 2025 revenue of about $442 million, up about 29%. IonQ paid $15.00 in cash plus 0.4883 IonQ shares per SkyWater share, about $741 million in cash and 24.1 million shares for a total of about $1.8 billion, and used about $1.1 billion of cash in total including repayment of about $315 million of SkyWater debt and costs. SkyWater is not in the second-quarter results and not in the raised 2026 guidance.

Revenue is growing fast and cash per unit of revenue is moving the wrong way. Gross margin of 24.9% against 59.8% means that each additional dollar of revenue brought less gross profit than last year, which is the opposite of what scaling should do. The company gives no explanation in the sources I read. Lower-margin system and hardware sales and acquired businesses are the likely causes, but that is my inference, not something IonQ has confirmed. A falling gross margin is the first thing I check before trusting any growth story, and here it fails that check. The cost of revenue also excludes depreciation, so the margin on a full-cost basis would be lower.

Two events came after the quarter. SkyWater closed on July 31. On October 12 IonQ announced it will buy Elisity, a cybersecurity company, for up to $1.3 billion in IonQ stock, which includes up to $150 million tied to performance (I could not confirm whether that earnout is inside the $1.3 billion cap in the shares count). The number of shares is set by IonQ's 20-day average price within a band of $33.07 to $49.61, which means about 26 million shares at the top of the band and about 39 million at the bottom if the whole $1.3 billion is paid in shares (my arithmetic). That is 6% to 10% more shares. Elisity's revenue is not disclosed. IonQ says it has more than 100 deployments, doubles revenue every year and has had no customer churn, none of which I can check. The deal is expected to close in the fourth quarter, pending regulatory approval. The next earnings report is due around November 4, 2026.

Cash burn after stock pay

The headline number is adjusted EBITDA of -$120.3 million for the quarter. Cash burn tells a harsher story. Free cash flow was -$159.4 million in the first quarter and -$114.0 million in the second (third-party data, adding to the -$273.4 million I calculate from the filing). Stock-based pay was $270.4 million in the half, 187% of revenue. It is not cash, but it is paid in shares, and the shares have a price. Treating it as a cost, free cash flow after stock pay is about -$543.8 million for the half, or an annual pace of about -$1.09 billion. Counting stock pay roughly doubles the burn, and on a quarterly basis it is about 2.3 times the adjusted EBITDA loss of the second quarter.

At the cash pace alone (about $547 million a year) the $1.85 billion of net cash covers roughly 3.4 years, before any SkyWater cash flow and before cash for Elisity, which is all stock. The runway is not a red flag today. The stock-pay figure is the lever to watch: dilution is how IonQ pays for the part of the cost that is not cash.

Cash, debt, warrants and shares

At June 30, 2026 IonQ held $1,235.7 million of cash, $883.2 million of short-term investments and $840.4 million of long-term investments, about $2,959 million in all, against debt of about $54.5 million (third-party data). The SkyWater deal then used about $1.1 billion, so I use about $1.85 billion of net cash. The company says about $2.0 billion pro forma, and I test both. Goodwill is $2,186.0 million and other intangibles $778.9 million, together 44% of the $6,778.6 million of assets. IonQ's equity is $3,327.2 million after an accumulated deficit of $2,256.5 million.

The warrants. The balance sheet carries a warrant liability of $3,052.4 million (December 31, 2025: $2,471.6 million), more than the cash. A warrant is a right to buy shares at a fixed price. Two issues sold to Heights Capital Management, an affiliate of Susquehanna, matter most. In July 2025 IonQ raised $1.0 billion by selling 14.2 million shares at $55.49, 3.9 million pre-funded warrants and seven-year warrants for 36.0 million shares at $99.88. In October 2025 it raised $2.0 billion by selling 16.5 million shares at $93, 5.0 million pre-funded warrants at $93 and seven-year warrants for 43.0 million shares at $155. The public warrants with a strike of $11.50 expired on September 30, 2026, with about 1.07 million outstanding at June 30.

I treat the liability as a non-cash claim on the shares, not as debt. The strikes of $99.88 and $155 are far above every value I calculate, including the Bull value, so by the treasury method (new shares are counted only for the part of the warrant that is in the money) they add no shares in any of my scenarios. They would matter above $99.88, and exercising all 79 million warrants would bring in about $10.3 billion and add 79 million shares, which shows how much of a Bull outcome is already owed to someone else. The pre-funded warrants (8.9 million shares) behave like shares, and I do not know how many have been exercised, so I test them in the share count. The total count and terms of every warrant series are UNAVAILABLE to me, because I could read only part of the quarterly filing. That is a gap, and it is one reason Confidence is D. I could also not reconcile the $3.05 billion liability to the two Heights series alone, so other instruments may be in it.

The share count. The cover of the 10-Q shows 381.0 million shares on July 29, 2026. Add the 24.1 million issued to SkyWater holders on July 31 and the base is 405.1 million, which is my count (a quote source shows 397.3 million, probably stale). Weighted diluted shares rose from 214.3 million in the third quarter of 2024 to 367.7 million in the second quarter of 2026, up 72%, mostly through the Oxford Ionics deal and the two Heights offerings. Elisity would add 26 to 39 million more. I model stock pay as continued dilution of 3% a year in the Base case, 5% in the Bear and 2.5% in the Bull, and I test the Elisity shares at 32 million with no value attached.

Primary valuation: enterprise value to revenue

My tiers for companies like this are 1x to 3x revenue for weak growth with an uncertain path to profit, 3x to 8x for growth above 40% with a visible path to profit, and 8x to 20x for a dominant position in a large market with growth above 80%. IonQ's guidance implies about 100% organic growth in 2026, but the margin trend and the burn make the "visible path" claim weaker, so I use the growth tier in the Bear (5x), the bottom of the hypergrowth tier plus a premium in the Base (12x) and the top of the hypergrowth tier in the Bull (20x). I apply the multiple to forward 2027 revenue of the core IonQ business, which I project from the 2026 guidance midpoint of $285 million, and I value SkyWater separately because a foundry deserves a foundry multiple. Quality of free cash flow growth (diagnostic only). Free cash flow is negative, so a conventional growth rate is not meaningful. The relevant question is the path to breakeven: in the Base case cumulative burn of $1.25 billion is 68% of the net cash, the pace of burn must fall from about $547 million a year now to near zero by 2029 while revenue rises 2.1 times from 2027 to 2029 (my model), and the margin trend is currently going the other way. I see no cyclical rebound and no low-base distortion. The acquisition effect is large: SkyWater and Oxford Ionics inflate reported growth, which is why I use the organic guidance. Forecast reliability is LOW, since revenue guidance is one year and no adjusted EBITDA guidance is given.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $39.89 market value is $16.2 billion and enterprise value $14.3 billion. In my path model, holding Base growth of 40% a year, the price needs a 2031 exit multiple of 22.4x revenue, against the 6x I use. Said the other way, at 6x revenue the 2031 core revenue would have to be about $5.8 billion, 3.7 times my Base, a growth rate of about 83% a year for five years from $285 million. That is the hypergrowth path with no slowdown, in a field where I found no demonstrated commercial advantage over classical computers. The market is pricing in my Bull case almost exactly.

For the Base case to be the right one an investor needs organic growth to stay above 40% for five years, gross margin to turn back up, burn to fall below $150 million a year by 2029, and dilution to stay near 3% a year. Evidence so far: the first is on track (guided organic growth near 100%, backlog up 297%), the second is moving the wrong way, and the last two cannot yet be tested.

How the buy price is set

Confidence D starts at a 50% margin of safety. I add 5 points for the warrant structure and dilution (79 million warrants outstanding, a share count up 72% in two years, and a pending issue of 26 to 39 million shares), 5 points for the falling gross margin, and 5 points for numbers I cannot see: SkyWater is outside the reported results and Elisity has no disclosed financials. That is 65%, which is above my 60% ceiling, so no buy price is issued. For reference only, a 60% discount to the $17.23 Base is $6.89, which is 83% below the price.

Reasons to own IONQ

  • Fast, backed growth. Revenue rose 287% in the quarter, guidance was raised to $280 million to $290 million (about 100% organic), and remaining performance obligations of $485 million are up 297%.
  • Net cash of about $1.85 billion. With debt near $55 million and a cash burn of about $547 million a year, the runway is about 3.4 years, so there is no near-term financing pressure.
  • A technical lead. The company reported 99.99% two-qubit gate accuracy in 2025, and it is buying up the surrounding stack: networking, sensing, space links and now a chip foundry.
  • A large option. If quantum advantage does arrive, IonQ is one of very few listed companies positioned to profit, and my Bull value of $39.29 shows what that is worth.
  • Warrants are far out of the money. At $99.88 and $155 strikes they add no shares in any of my scenarios.

What could go wrong?

  • Falling gross margin. A move from 59.8% to 24.9% in a year, while revenue grew 287%, says growth is being bought with lower-quality revenue, or that costs are rising faster than sales.
  • Dilution. Stock pay of $270.4 million in six months, two Heights offerings, the SkyWater shares and now Elisity. The model's Base assumes 3% a year; at 6% the Base falls to $16.50 from $17.23.
  • Technology that may not pay. I found no demonstrated commercial advantage over classical computing. If the field takes ten years instead of five, the Bear case is the path.
  • Acquisition execution. Two large deals in three months, a chip foundry and a cybersecurity company, neither of them a quantum computer. SkyWater's results are not yet visible in the IonQ numbers.
  • Accounting noise. A $1.65 billion warrant charge in one quarter moves with the share price, which makes reported earnings useless as a guide. The company's own adjusted EBITDA excludes stock pay of $141.8 million a quarter.

Management execution

Management has beaten its revenue guidance (the second quarter was about 20% above the midpoint), closed the SkyWater deal, and raised guidance. Against that, gross margin fell sharply, the share count rose 72% in two years, the acquisitions are mostly paid in stock, and the Elisity deal was announced on the day the shares fell 9% on dilution concerns. The company gives no adjusted EBITDA guidance and no combined revenue guidance with SkyWater. Management credibility scores 9/20: strong on top line delivery, weak on margin and on how it pays for growth. I did not check insider transactions, which belong on any red-flag list.

Stress test and independent check

I rebuilt the key figures from IonQ's second-quarter 2026 results release (revenue, costs, operating loss, net loss, adjusted EBITDA, stock pay, cash flow) and the quarterly filing (balance sheet, warrant liability, share count on the cover). The gross margin is my calculation: revenue less the cost of revenue shown excluding depreciation. Net cash after SkyWater is my estimate, from the June 30 balance and the deal's disclosed cash cost. The price is the October 9 close from the history page of the quote source. The Elisity terms come from press reports of the October 12 announcement, not from a filing.

Weak points, in order. First, the full warrant count and terms are unavailable to me, and the $3.05 billion liability is larger than I can explain from the two Heights series, so the warrant picture is incomplete. Second, SkyWater revenue ($450 million) and its multiple are my estimates, since IonQ has reported none of it. Third, every growth path, margin path and multiple is my judgment, with no peer table on the same basis. Fourth, the 15% discount rate and the 3% annual dilution are assumptions, and a different rate moves the secondary value. Fifth, the 405.1 million share count is my sum of the cover count and the SkyWater issue, and I did not reconcile it to a later filing or to other issuance since July 29. Sixth, the share count from Elisity depends on a price band and on whether the earnout is included.

The strongest counter-argument is that IonQ is early. Revenue is growing at about 100% a year organically, a $485 million backlog is up 297%, the company holds net cash for more than three years, and the markets that quantum computing could serve are huge if it works. In that case the Bull value of $39.29 is a floor on the way to something much larger, and my 6x exit multiple on 2031 revenue is too low. The reply is that the price already pays for that outcome (the exit multiple needed is 22x revenue on Base growth), that gross margin is moving in the wrong direction while revenue grows, and that the company's own accounting shows how much of the upside has already been sold to others as warrants and shares.

Final verdict: reduce at $39.89, no buy zone

IonQ is a company with a technical lead, fast growth and a funded runway, priced as if the rest of the story will go perfectly. At $39.89 the shares trade at my Bull value, so a buyer gets the best case already in the price and carries a Bear case 82% below it. For a speculative company like this I ask one question: would I take the position at this price? My answer is no, with Confidence D (5/25).

Verdict: REDUCE at $39.89. SPECULATIVE POSITION: NO. Triangulated Base value $17.23, downside 56.8%, Confidence D (5/25). No buy zone: the required margin of safety is above my 60% ceiling.

For an existing holder this means sizing the position as if it can fall 80%. For new money it means waiting for evidence, not for a price: gross margin back up, burn down, the warrants and the Elisity terms disclosed. The main thesis breaker is a third quarter in which SkyWater lifts revenue and gross margin stays near 25%. The technology may be real. At $39.89 the price already assumes it is.
