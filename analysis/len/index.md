Lennar says it is land-light: it owns fewer than 2.5% of the roughly 488,000 homesites it owns or controls, and rents the rest through options. Yet in the nine months to August 31 its operating activities used $822.2 million of cash, because inventories grew by $1,738.8 million and deposits on land by $794.9 million. In the same nine months the company paid shareholders $1,358.6 million, $992.7 million of it in buybacks. At $82.31 the shares trade at 0.91 times book value and 15.8 times my fiscal 2027 earnings, which assume margins recover from today's 15.8%.

At $82.31 the shares trade 9.6% above my weighted Base value of $75.12. The Bear value of $48.54 is 41.0% below the price and the Bull value of $110.97 is 34.8% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. My two methods give $70.03 from earnings and an exit multiple and $82.75 from book value and returns on equity, so the price sits between them. Confidence is grade C, which sets a required margin of safety of 42.5% and a maximum buy price of $43.19, about half of book value. The verdict is Hold, watchlist for new money: the price is not far from my Base, but the Base case earns only 5.5% a year on the primary method, and the Bear case loses 41.0%.

Valuation route: a US homebuilder with a land-light model, a mortgage arm and strongly cyclical earnings. Primary method: earnings a share for fiscal 2027 to 2030, sold at an exit price-to-earnings multiple at the end of fiscal 2030 and discounted at 10%. Cross-check: residual income on book value, which values the equity as book value plus the present value of earnings above a 10% cost of equity. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What Lennar is

Lennar is one of the largest US homebuilders by deliveries. It builds mostly entry-level and move-up homes at an average price near $372,000, finances a large share of its buyers through its own mortgage business (83% of buyers' loans in the third quarter, according to a third-party summary), and buys land mainly through options and land banks, so most land sits off its balance sheet. Class A and Class B shares trade as LEN and LEN.B, and insiders hold about 10% of the company according to MarketBeat.

A homebuilder that grows inventory consumes cash by design, and nine months are not the year: the fourth quarter is guided to deliver 22,000 to 23,000 homes, more than the third. Even so, the money for buybacks and dividends came from the balance sheet. Homebuilding cash was $3.44 billion at the end of November 2025 and $1.15 billion at August 31, and the revolver carried $650 million of net borrowings over the nine months. Deposits and pre-acquisition costs on land rose to $7.33 billion from $6.38 billion, and $1.41 billion of inventory sits on the balance sheet only because of land-bank deals. "Land-light" lowers the land on the balance sheet. It does not remove the cash that land deals tie up.

There is no free cash flow to value this year, which is why I value earnings and book value and not cash flow. The growth-adjusted valuation check is not meaningful: earnings fell by about half in the quarter, so a price-to-growth ratio would be unstable.

The exit multiple needs a reason. Ten times fiscal 2030 earnings is 0.82 times the book value I project for that date ($112.86 a share), and that is what a return on equity of 8.8% earns when the cost of equity is 10% and growth is 3%. The test of the choice is what the market would have to believe instead: at 11.9 times, the price is fair. Lennar now carries $21.6 billion of equity against about $30 billion of sales, which holds its return on equity down, so I do not use the higher multiples that a high-return homebuilder would earn.

## Secondary valuation: book value and returns on equity

This method starts from book value of $91.60 a share, which is August's $90.62 plus my estimate of the fourth quarter's earnings less dividends. Each year it charges 10% of opening book value as the required return and keeps the difference as residual income. Book value grows by earnings less the dividend. The same earnings path feeds both methods, which is why they are not independent. After fiscal 2031 I assume returns equal the 10% cost of equity, so there is no value beyond book value at that date. Returns are below 10% in each of the five years (from 5.7% to 9.0%), so the present value of residual income is -$8.85 a share, and the value is $82.75.

The gross margin shift applies to every year from fiscal 2027. This method is less sensitive to the margin than the primary one because book value sets most of its result, but its terminal assumption is generous: if returns stayed at 9.0% and never reached 10%, the value would be lower.

- Secondary (residual income on book value, 10% cost of equity): $82.75, weight 40%.

- Weighted Base: $75.12, inside a range of $70.03 to $82.75.

Both methods value the same equity, so no enterprise-value bridge is needed. They agree within the limit, but they are not independent: both use the same earnings forecast, so the agreement does not raise my confidence. The gap comes from the end: the primary method sells the business at 0.82 times book value in 2030, while the secondary assumes returns reach 10% after fiscal 2031 and so values it at book value then. That is the same disagreement the market has: whether a homebuilder earning 9% on equity deserves less than book value or exactly book value. The choice does not change my decision, because the price sits between the two values.

## Reasons to own Lennar

- Costs are falling. Construction cost a square foot is down 1% from the second quarter, 6% from a year ago and 14% from the fourth quarter of 2023.
- The land-light model limits owned land to under 2.5% of about 488,000 homesites, and owned inventory is $11.54 billion.
- The balance sheet can take stress: net homebuilding debt is 12.7% of capital and total liquidity is $3.6 billion.
- The shares trade at 0.91 times book value, and book value is largely inventory and land deposits carried at cost.
- Gross margin improved from 15.6% to 15.8% in the quarter as incentives eased to about 12%, and the fourth-quarter guide of 15.5% to 16.0% holds that level.
- Financial services earned $129 million in the quarter, a cushion when homebuilding is weak.

## What could go wrong?

- Demand and price. Orders fell 9% in the third quarter, the average price of new orders fell to $359,000, and mortgage rates are about 6.8% and rising. Resale sellers cutting prices compete directly for Lennar's buyers.
- Margin. Every point of gross margin is worth about $6 a share in my weighted Base, and management chooses volume over margin.
- Cash. Operations used $822.2 million in nine months while the company returned $1.36 billion, homebuilding cash fell by about $2.3 billion and the revolver was drawn by $650 million.
- Land commitments. Deposits and pre-acquisition costs of $7.33 billion, plus $1.41 billion of inventory held only because of land banks, are exposed if the company walks away from deals in a downturn.
- Guidance. The fiscal 2026 delivery target was cut, and third-quarter orders fell below the range the company gave.
- Labor and costs. Management said labor supply is short and the cost structure is starting to feel it, with immigration policy and data-center construction named as reasons.
- Governance and noise. Two share classes trade and insiders hold about 10%. Technology investment gains and losses swing quarterly earnings by tens of millions of dollars.

## Management execution

- Volume is hit and margin is missed: deliveries of 20,840 were inside the 20,500 to 21,500 range, orders of 20,879 were below the 21,000 to 22,000 range, and the gross margin came in just below the roughly 16% guided.
- The strategy has not changed. The chief executive says the company will keep production even and offer the incentives buyers need, and that it is leaning further into the land-light model.
- Capital allocation favors buybacks over cash: $992.7 million in nine months, at prices above my Base.
- The company redeemed $400 million of notes from cash in the quarter and has said the next maturity is in June 2027.
- The 2025 Millrose spin-off moved much of the land to a separate company, and part of the share count drop came from the exchange offer and not from buybacks.
