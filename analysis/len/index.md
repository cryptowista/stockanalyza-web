## What Lennar is

Lennar, based in Miami, is one of the largest U.S. homebuilders. It delivered 82,583 homes in fiscal 2025 at an average price of $391,000 and guides to 80,000 to 81,000 for fiscal 2026. The third quarter shows how wide the range is: 5,969 homes in South Central at $230,000 each, 4,529 in the West at $571,000. Behind the homes sit a mortgage and title business (financial services, $323 million of operating earnings in nine months), a small multifamily arm, and Lennar Other, which holds technology investments and swings earnings with their market value.

The company chooses volume over price. Management says incentives ran about 12% of the sales price on third quarter deliveries (automated call transcript, so approximate). The company also sells on speed: cycle time was 116 days against 126 a year earlier. The margin is where the bill arrives.

Gross margin on home sales has fallen from 27.5% in fiscal 2022 to a guided 15.5% to 16.0% in the fourth quarter. SG&A has gone the other way, from 6.2% to about 9.2% of home sales. The two together took the operating margin on homes from about 21% to about 6.6%. The fiscal 2023 gross margin and the SG&A ratios for 2023 to 2025 are my arithmetic from the release tables.

In February 2025 Lennar spun off Millrose Properties, a land owner that buys land and sells finished homesites back to Lennar. The 2025 cash flow statement shows $416 million of cash distributed in the spin-off. Lennar now controls about 488,000 homesites and owns fewer than 2.5% of them. The other 97.5% are held through options and similar arrangements, and the $7.3 billion of deposits and pre-acquisition costs that secure them is on Lennar's balance sheet. Jonathan Jaffe, the co-CEO, retired on December 31, 2025 and the company has said it has no plans to replace him. Stuart Miller stays as executive chairman and chief executive.

## Primary valuation: returns on book equity

Book value is $90.62 a share at August 31 ($76.15 excluding $3.44 billion of goodwill). The return on opening equity is 5.3% for fiscal 2026, against 22.8% in fiscal 2021. Value today is book value plus the present value of net earnings above a 10% charge on opening equity, with the terminal spread halved to reflect competition. Terminal growth is 3.0% in Base.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $77.69 and 237.9 million shares the market value is **$18,482 million**. A reverse calculation holding everything else at Base says the price is the right value if gross margin on home sales climbs to 17.5% by fiscal 2031. That is 1.7 points above today's 15.8% and below fiscal 2025's 17.7%. In book terms the price assumes a lasting return on equity of about 9.0%. In earnings terms it is 16.1 times my fiscal 2026 estimate of $4.84.

So the market has already priced in roughly two thirds of the Base margin recovery: from 15.8% to 17.5%, of the 2.7 points to 18.5%. My Base asks for the rest. Modeled that way, buying at $77.69 and receiving the Base outcome would earn about 11.2% a year, a figure for information only, not a second valuation.

## Dividend and buybacks

Lennar pays $0.50 a quarter, $2.00 a year, a 2.6% yield at $77.69 (aggregator; the ex-dividend date was October 7). Nine months of dividends came to $366 million, 45% of net earnings. Dividends are safe on any reading of the balance sheet: the annual cost of about $470 million is small beside $1.15 billion of cash and $3.6 billion of liquidity. The pressure is on buybacks, which have run at $993 million in nine months and, with the dividend, have exceeded operating cash flow in each of the last two fiscal years and in the latest nine months. ## Reasons to own Lennar

- **The book is large and mostly tangible.** Equity is $90.62 a share, $76.15 without goodwill, and the price is 1.02 times tangible book. A buyer of the shares is paying about what the assets cost.
- **Costs are coming down.** Cycle time is 116 days, construction cost per square foot is 14% below the fourth quarter of 2023, and unsold finished homes are down to 1.8 per community.
- **The gross margin has stopped falling.** It went from 15.6% to 15.8% in the quarter, and the fourth quarter guide is 15.5% to 16.0%.
- **The share count is falling.** Shares outstanding are down 3.7% in nine months, and a share buyback below book value adds to book value per share.
- **Financial services helps.** It earned $323 million in nine months, a cushion when homebuilding margins are thin.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: the margin does not recover.** Land cost is up about 6% a year, incentives are around 12% of price, and the mortgage rate is about 6.8%. If gross margin stays at 15.7%, the triangulated value is $64.87.
- **Cash returns ahead of cash flow.** Homebuilding cash fell $2.3 billion in nine months, the revolver was drawn $650 million, and net homebuilding debt rose from 2.8% to 12.7% of capital.
- **Land deposits.** $7.3 billion of deposits and pre-acquisition costs secures land Lennar does not own. In a weaker market Lennar can walk away from options but forfeits deposits and writes off pre-acquisition costs, and the balance has grown $944 million since November.
- **Reliance on a land supplier.** The 10-K says Millrose "does not have the capacity to provide all the land acquisition funding we require." I did not read the Millrose contract terms, so I cannot size the spread Lennar pays.
- **Control and large holders.** A shareholder letter filed with the SEC in March 2026 says Class B shares carry ten votes, that Stuart Miller controls about 39% of the votes and holds about 9% of the economic ownership. The shares outstanding include 30.0 million Class B (12.6%). - **Earnings noise.** Technology investment marks swung Lennar Other from a $62.5 million profit to an $83.6 million loss in a year, and the multifamily and other segments are guided to losses of about $45 million in the fourth quarter.
- **Data quirk.** One aggregator shows Lennar's net debt as $4.92 billion by including financial services warehouse debt. I use homebuilding net debt of $3.15 billion and treat the mortgage borrowings as self-funding.

## Management execution

Execution on cost and speed is good: a 116-day cycle time, build costs per square foot down 14% in under three years, and a land model that has reduced the capital in the business. Against that, SG&A came in above its own guided range, the delivery guide fell 5% from December to September, and the company bought back stock at an average of $85.49 in the quarter and $993 million in nine months while cash fell by $2.3 billion. The co-CEO retired without a successor and the company says it will not name one. **Assessment: 12/20, strong operators, capital returns running ahead of cash.**
