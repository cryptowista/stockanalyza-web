In March 2026 Limbach told investors to expect $90 to $94 million of adjusted EBITDA this year, with no acquisitions in the number. Since then it has bought two companies, CYMCOR in August and 1901 in September, and raised its revenue guidance. The adjusted EBITDA guidance is now $78 to $84 million, including the two acquisitions. The company got bigger and earns less, and the stock, which peaked above $150 in 2025, closed at $54.50 on November 5.

At $54.50, Limbach trades 5.1% below my Base value of $57.46. The Bear value is $35.45, 35.0% below the price, and the Bull value is $79.67, 46.2% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The verdict is Hold, with new money waiting for $40.22 or less.

Valuation route: a building systems contractor whose profits move with project timing, mix and acquisitions. The primary method is a five-year free cash flow model with a terminal value; the cross-check is a forward multiple of enterprise value to 2027 EBITDA after stock-based pay. Both use the same September 30 debt and the same share count. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Limbach is

Limbach designs, installs and maintains heating, cooling, electrical, plumbing and building control systems for hospitals, universities, factories, entertainment venues and, more and more, data centers. It has about 2,100 employees in 23 offices across the Eastern and Midwestern United States, and its roots go back to 1901.

Since 2020 the strategy has been to work directly for building owners (Owner Direct Relationships, ODR: service, maintenance and owner projects) and to take less work as a subcontractor for general contractors (General Contractor Relationships, GCR). It worked. Revenue slipped from $568 million in 2020 to $519 million in 2024, but adjusted EBITDA went from $25.1 million to $63.7 million, and in 2025 it reached $81.8 million on revenue of $646.8 million. That is the business the market paid more than $150 a share for.

The third quarter was a relief after a weak second quarter, when the stock fell 35.5% in one day on August 5. Organic revenue grew 14.4%, bookings ran ahead of revenue for the fourth quarter in a row and management now expects 2026 to finish at or near the top of its $760 to $790 million revenue and $78 to $84 million adjusted EBITDA ranges. The shares rose 16.4% on November 4.

Look at where the growth came from, though. Owner direct work, the high-margin core, grew 2.2% organically in the quarter and fell 1.6% over nine months. The fast growth was general contractor work, the kind Limbach spent five years shrinking, and acquisitions. ODR fell from 76.6% to 65.4% of revenue, and for the first nine months the gross margin was 22.5%, down from 26.4% a year earlier.

Which numbers to trust

  • Company free cash flow is generous. Limbach defines free cash flow as operating cash flow before working capital changes, minus capital spending excluding rental equipment, and stock-based pay is added back. On that definition it made $43.6 million in nine months, 87.1% of adjusted EBITDA. Reported operating cash flow for the same period was $31.2 million.
  • The vehicles are leased. Most of the fleet sits in finance leases ($24.5 million), so the repayments run through financing, not capital spending. I treat new leased vehicles as capital spending.
  • Stock-based pay is a real cost. It was $8.1 million over the last twelve months. Adjusted EBITDA adds it back; I deduct it.
  • The tax rate is temporarily low. The nine-month effective rate was 11.9% because of tax benefits on vested shares. I use a full 26%, less the tax deduction on acquired goodwill.
  • Earnouts are debt. $8.1 million of contingent payments to sellers of acquired companies is included with net debt.

After those adjustments, 2027 EBITDA in my Base is $83.3 million instead of $93.6 million adjusted, and free cash flow to the whole company is $52.1 million.

Debt, shares and the acquisitions

Limbach paid $66.0 million for 1901, a Wisconsin contractor with about 450 employees, and $29.9 million for CYMCOR, a data center consulting firm. For 2027 the company expects about $11 million of adjusted EBITDA from 1901 and about $4 million from CYMCOR, so the prices work out to roughly 6 and 7.5 times. That is cheaper than the 9.4 times the market puts on Limbach itself, which is the arithmetic of a roll-up. The catch is that 1901 runs at lower margins, and management talks about lifting them over two to three years.

To pay for it, Limbach replaced its bank line in September with a $300 million PNC facility. At September 30 it owed $75 million on the revolver and $50 million on a term loan, both due in 2031, at about 5.5%. Net debt was $125.8 million, 1.63 times trailing adjusted EBITDA. The covenant limit is 3.0 times, and $39.9 million of unused capacity was already unavailable because of it.

The share count has hardly moved for four years: 12.0 million diluted in the third quarter, the same as in 2024. In the quarter Limbach bought back 194,788 shares at $51.31, and a director bought 4,008 shares at $50.71 in September. I value 11.98 million shares, the 11.73 million outstanding plus 0.25 million unvested awards.

Secondary valuation: a forward multiple

The cross-check applies 9.5 times to 2027 EBITDA after stock pay of $83.3 million. That gives an enterprise value of $790.9 million and $55.43 a share.

Why 9.5 times, when EMCOR, IES Holdings and Comfort Systems traded at 14.6, 15.1 and 18.3 times trailing EBITDA in late October? They have EBITDA margins of 10.7% to 18.1%, net cash and much faster expected growth. Limbach has a margin of about 9% after stock pay, debt of 1.6 times EBITDA and low single-digit organic growth. At 9.5 times, its 2027 free cash flow is a 6.6% yield, which at a 10.5% cost of capital is consistent with growth of about 3.9% a year forever. That is a fair price for this business, not a cheap one.

The market is not paying for a return to 2025. It assumes margins settle about where they are this year, slightly above 10.6%, with ordinary growth. If you think Limbach can get back toward 12%, the stock is cheap. If you think the acquired businesses and the new general contractor work keep diluting margins, it is about right or a little expensive.

Where the return would come from: at an unchanged 9.4 times forward EBITDA, the Base gives about 15.6% a year to the end of 2029. Roughly half of that is EBITDA growth and half is debt repayment, which assumes Limbach stops buying companies. It will not, so the deleveraging half is less certain than the table makes it look. In the Bear, the same unchanged multiple gives 3.4% a year.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is B, which carries a margin of safety of 30%. I add nothing to it, because the margin risk is already in the scenarios. That gives a maximum buy price of $40.22. The 52-week low is $40.74, set in late August.

One point of adjusted EBITDA margin changes the Base by $7.68 a share, one point of discount rate by $4.74 to $6.20, 5% of 2027 revenue by $3.10 and one turn of multiple by $2.78. Each $10 million of extra net debt costs $0.83 a share. None of these moves alone pushes the buy price anywhere near today's price.

For a reference point on the other side: at about $70 the expected return under my Base falls to about 9.4% a year, below the 10.5% cost of capital. That is where I would review the position, not a target.

Dividend

Limbach pays no dividend. It has a $50 million buyback authorization running to December 2027, of which $10 million has been used, and management says acquisitions come first.

Reasons to own Limbach

  • Bookings have exceeded revenue for four quarters in a row; third quarter bookings were $311.2 million, and contracted work not yet performed was $656.5 million at September 30.
  • Cash generation is real: operating cash flow over the last twelve months was $59.3 million, 77% of adjusted EBITDA.
  • The two acquisitions were bought at roughly 6 to 7.5 times expected EBITDA, below Limbach's own valuation.
  • The balance sheet has room: about $135 million of available credit and no maturities before 2031.
  • The company bought back shares at $51.31, and a director bought at $50.71.

What could go wrong?

  • Margins. The gross margin guidance for 2026 fell from 26% to 27% in March to 23% to 24% now. If the acquired businesses and general contractor work keep it there, the Base is too generous.
  • Owner direct growth. Organic ODR revenue fell 1.6% in nine months. The healthcare and institutional customers that delayed budgets in the first half have to keep releasing work.
  • Leverage. Two more acquisitions of this size on debt would take net leverage toward the 3.0 times covenant.
  • Credibility. The 2026 guidance was cut in August, and several law firms announced investigations after the August 5 drop. I found no filed lawsuit.
  • Union pension plans. Limbach contributes to multiemployer pension plans and could owe withdrawal payments that it says it cannot estimate.

Management execution

  • From 2020 to 2025, the shift to owner direct work more than tripled adjusted EBITDA while revenue grew 14%.
  • 2025 adjusted EBITDA of $81.8 million landed inside the $80 to $86 million guidance.
  • The 2026 plan of $90 to $94 million turned into $78 to $84 million, even after adding two acquisitions.
  • Adjusted earnings per share rose from $1.07 in 2020 to $4.51 in 2025 with only modest dilution.
  • The company's free cash flow measure excludes working capital, which flatters conversion in a growing contractor.

On my ten-point management check (two points each), Limbach scores 14 of 18 scored points; the dividend point does not apply. The long record is good, this year's forecasting was not.

What I would watch from here

The fourth quarter decides whether 2026 ends at the top of the range, and the 2027 guidance in March will show what management expects from the acquisitions. The four things the next report has to show: 2026 adjusted EBITDA of at least $82 million, a fourth quarter gross margin of 23% or more, year-end net debt below about $110 million, and 2027 guidance that implies adjusted EBITDA above about $92 million.

Green

  • Gross margin of 24% or more in a quarter.
  • Organic ODR growth above 5%.
  • Net debt below 1.5 times adjusted EBITDA.

Yellow

  • Gross margin between 22% and 24%.
  • Organic ODR growth between 0% and 5%.
  • Another acquisition funded with debt before the current two are integrated.

Red

  • Gross margin below 22%.
  • Organic ODR revenue falling for two quarters with bookings below revenue.
  • Net debt above 2.5 times adjusted EBITDA.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Gross margin stays below 22% through 2027, which means the mix shift is permanent and leads toward the Bear.
  2. Owner direct revenue keeps shrinking while general contractor work grows, which undoes the strategy the 2025 valuation was built on.
  3. Margins at 1901 rise as they did at Jake Marshall and ODR growth returns above 5%, which leads toward the Bull.

The weakest point is the margin path: the Base needs 11.3% by 2030 and the company has only shown that it can fall. The strongest bull argument is that the price assumes no recovery at all while bookings run at 1.4 times revenue. The strongest bear argument is that the recovery depends on businesses that were just bought and on a customer mix that is moving the wrong way.

Final verdict: hold at $54.50

Limbach is a decent contractor that briefly got valued like something rarer. At $54.50 that premium is gone, and so is most of the gap to my Base. What is left is a bet on margins coming back, and the price does not offer much reward for taking it yet.

Verdict: HOLD at $54.50, watchlist for new money. Base $57.46, 5.4% above the price, Bear $35.45, Bull $79.67, Confidence B (18.75/25). Required margin of safety 30%, maximum buy price $40.22.

New money buys only at or below $40.22. For an existing holder the original cost is irrelevant: the price sits close to fair value, and the March guidance will say more than the last three months did. The revenue line can keep growing by acquisition; the margin line is the one that has to earn the next re-rating.
