Digi Power X spent the first half of 2026 changing from a small bitcoin and colocation business into a builder of AI data centers. In May it signed a ten-year contract with Cerebras Systems for 40 megawatts in Columbiana, Alabama, with an initial value of about $1.1 billion. The shares have since fallen from $3.97 in mid-August to $2.38, and 61% over twelve months. Today the contract is worth only what the company can build, finance and operate it for, and the filings do not say what the building costs.

At $2.38 on October 29, 2026, the shares trade 1.7% below my Base value of $2.42. The Bear value of $1.09 is 54.2% below the price and the Bull value of $5.37 is 125.6% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The required margin of safety of 55% gives a buy range of $1.01 to $1.14 and a conservative limit of $1.01. For existing holders this is a hold, with a position size that allows for a total loss. For new money it is a watchlist name.

Valuation route: Pre-profit infrastructure developer with one contracted project, valued on a project cash flow model plus net cash and other assets. Economic classification: not yet assessable, because the main business has no operating history. Primary method: discounted after-tax cash flow of the Cerebras contract, less the cost still to be spent, plus cash and other assets, less capitalized corporate costs. Cross-check: 0.9 times estimated book value. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Digi Power X is

The company has three activities. The first is the legacy business of colocation, bitcoin mining and energy sales, which earned about $5.5 million of revenue in the second quarter and is being wound down. The second is NeoCloudz, a rental business for NVIDIA graphics processors, which produced $1.1 million of revenue in about five weeks of operation. The third is the AI data center campus in Columbiana, Alabama, which is the reason the shares exist at this price.

Under the Cerebras agreement the company must build, equip and commission two phases: 15 megawatts targeted for December 15, 2026 and another 25 megawatts by the end of the first quarter of 2027. Cerebras pays a monthly fee based on the power delivered, part of it prepaid, and makes one-time payments tied to construction. The initial term is ten years from the later phase start. Cerebras can extend by one, three, five or seven years, and the company puts the total value with one seven-year extension at about $2.5 billion. The agreement for the second phase is conditioned on the company securing adequate financing.

The company also has plans for a 75 megawatt campus in North Carolina, sites in New York, a non-binding letter of intent for a 1.3 gigawatt power asset in West Virginia and a Silicon Valley laboratory. None has a contract, so I give them no value in the Base.

What the earnings are made of

  • Reported earnings are noise. The net loss includes warrant fair value changes and the revaluation of bitcoin holdings, which move with the share price and the crypto market.
  • The legacy business is shrinking. Revenue fell 18% in the quarter and the company says the wind-down is planned.
  • The contract is the value. At $1.1 billion over ten years, the monthly fee works out at about $110 million a year, or $2.75 million per megawatt a year, before any one-time construction payments. How much of it is profit is not disclosed.
  • Power and operating costs are not disclosed. The filings do not say whether Cerebras pays for the electricity, so the margin on the contract is my estimate.

I therefore model the contract rather than the reported results. This is the largest source of uncertainty in the analysis and it is the reason Confidence is D.

Secondary valuation: adjusted book value

Shareholders' equity was $265.0 million at June 30. I subtract about $10 million for the third-quarter loss and spending, my estimate,, which gives $255 million, or $2.50 a share on 102.1 million shares. I apply 0.9 times, which gives $2.25. The discount reflects the cash that the company is still spending, the risk that assets built for one tenant are worth less than their cost, and the dilution that has funded the balance sheet.

This method does not use the contract economics, but it is not fully independent of the first: both rely on the money spent being worth about what it cost. If the project is worth more than its cost, book value understates the equity, and if less, it overstates it. I give no extra Confidence for the agreement.

Historical cross-check

The shares fell 61% over twelve months, from about $6.03, according to a broker chart. The chart shows a spike toward $9 earlier in the year, followed by a decline to $2.38. The fall in mid-August followed the second-quarter report, which showed a wider loss, and the financing announcement on October 13 did not reverse it. I did not collect a multi-year history of the price to book value, so the historical component of the valuation score is neutral.

Valuation triangulation

  • Primary (contract cash flow model): $2.54, weight 60%.
  • Secondary (0.9 times estimated book value): $2.25, weight 40%.
  • Divergence between the two in the Base: 12.1% of their midpoint, inside the 20% limit.
  • Weighted Base: $2.42. The Bear and Bull values differ by much more than 30% between the methods ($0.82 against $1.50 in the Bear, $6.78 against $3.25 in the Bull), which flags the wide uncertainty of the cases.

I weight the contract model higher because it shows each assumption, and the asset-based check lower because it values cost, not future cash flow.

Dividend and buybacks

The company pays no dividend and does not buy back shares. It has funded itself by selling shares, and while no shares were sold under the at-the-market program in the third quarter, the shelf registration remains available. Management says the project loan is meant to limit dilution.

Reasons to own DGXX

  • A signed ten-year contract worth about $1.1 billion for 40 megawatts, with a possible $2.5 billion including one extension.
  • About $135 million of cash and no debt on the balance sheet.
  • A $200 million loan commitment from a major lender, arranged by Goldman Sachs, which would cover most of the remaining spending.
  • Further sites in North Carolina, New York and West Virginia, which I do not value but which could add to the Bull.

What could go wrong?

  • The loan does not close, or closes on harsh terms, and the second phase, which depends on financing, is delayed or lost.
  • The campus costs more than my $360 million estimate, or is late, which triggers penalties and service credits under the contract.
  • All of the value rests on one customer, Cerebras, and the termination provisions are not public.
  • More shares are sold at lower prices to fund the gap, as in the first half of the year.
  • The legacy business continues to lose money while it winds down.

Management execution

  • Management signed a large contract and raised cash during a strong market, and says that long-lead equipment for both phases is on site or on its way, some ahead of schedule.
  • Phase 1 is targeted for December 15, 2026. It is the first real test, because the shares will be judged on delivery.
  • The company has changed focus several times, from bitcoin to colocation, GPUs and data centers, and has funded itself by issuing shares. I give neutral credit until a campus runs.
  • Disclosure is thin on the numbers that matter: project cost, margin, the construction payments and the loan terms.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • The $200 million loan is signed, with a rate that leaves the project's cash flow intact, before about January 11, 2027.
  • Phase 1 of 15 megawatts is delivered close to December 15, 2026, and the company reports the first monthly fees.
  • The company discloses the total project cost and the construction payments from Cerebras.

Yellow

  • The third-quarter report on November 12 shows cash below the $135 million reported in October.
  • The proxy statement or shelf registration asks for a large increase in the number of authorized shares.
  • Phase 1 slips by more than a month.

Red

  • The loan commitment lapses or is cut, or the second phase is delayed for lack of financing.
  • A new equity offering at a price well below $2.38.
  • Cerebras raises a dispute, claims a penalty or uses a termination right.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The project loan fails to close, which would leave the company to pay for the second phase with shares.
  2. The campus costs about $420 million or more, which cuts the Base value to about $2.00 at a 65% margin and to $1.39 at 55%.
  3. Cerebras's contract turns out to carry weak payment protection, so that the $1.1 billion is not a reliable cash flow.

The weakest point of the model is the combination of project cost and margin, which management has not disclosed and which together decide whether the contract creates or destroys value. The strongest bull argument is that AI computing demand makes a ten-year contract with the tenant a scarce asset, that the construction payments lower the capital needed, and that the other sites are worth far more than I credit. The strongest bear argument is that a company that has sold shares at every stage has not yet shown that it can finance and build on time, and that the market has been marking the shares down for that reason.

Final verdict: hold at $2.38, watchlist for new money

Digi Power X is an option on one contract. At $2.38 the market pays about what I think the contract is worth after the capital still to be spent, so there is no margin of safety, and the range of outcomes is very wide.

Verdict: HOLD at $2.38, watchlist for new money. Weighted Base $2.42, 1.7% above the price, cross-check $2.25, Confidence D (7/25). Required margin of safety 55%, buy range $1.01 to $1.14, conservative buy limit $1.01.

For an existing holder, the original cost of the shares is irrelevant. I would size the position so that a total loss is acceptable, and review it on November 12, when the third quarter is reported, and again when the loan is signed or lapses. New money waits for a price at or below $1.14, or for the company to disclose the project cost, the margin and the loan terms.
