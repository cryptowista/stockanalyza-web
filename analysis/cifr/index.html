Cipher Digital reported net income of $31.6 million for the third quarter, on revenue of $26.7 million and an operating loss of $71.9 million. The gap is a $127.2 million non-cash gain on the warrants it issued to Google, and without that gain the quarter was a loss of about $96 million.

The company many investors still know as a bitcoin miner is now a developer of leased data centers. It has $16.6 billion of contracted revenue over a weighted average term of 17.3 years, three campuses under construction for hyperscale tenants, and $6.7 billion of debt to build them. The shares have fallen from a 52-week high of $30.14 to $6.29, and nearly all the contracted cash flow is still ahead of the company.

My triangulated Base value is $3.40, which is 45.9% below the $6.29 reference price. The Bear value is $0.50 and the Bull value is $9.34. Confidence is grade D (7/25), which requires a 55% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at $1.53. The verdict is a reduce: the contracts are real, but after debt there is little left for the shares, and the market is paying about $1.4 billion more than my Base for projects that have no signed lease.

Valuation route: development-stage data center lessor with project-level debt. Primary method: a finite-life cash flow model of contracted net operating income (NOI, rent less property costs) through the lease terms plus a residual, less all claims. Secondary method: stabilized NOI capitalized at a market cap rate. Classification: standard company, 2 of 12 on the generic scorecard. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets. The reference price is an intraday quote, see the stress test section.

What Cipher Digital is now

Cipher Digital, formerly Cipher Mining, develops, owns and operates data centers in Texas and leases them to AI and cloud tenants. Three campuses are under construction, 700 gross megawatts (MW) in total: Barber Lake (300 MW, leased to Fluidstack, with a Google backstop, and now extended by a second 10-year term directly with an AI lab), Black Pearl (about 300 MW, a 15-year lease with an investment-grade hyperscaler) and Stingray (100 MW, commencing in 2027). The company says its pipeline is about 5.9 GW, including a 1 GW conditional base load classification at Colchis and plans for gas pipelines to support up to 2.5 GW of on-site generation.

Bitcoin mining still produces most of the revenue ($20.8 million of $26.7 million in the quarter) and is shrinking: 9-month mining revenue was $80.5 million, against $164.2 million a year earlier. The first HPC (high-performance computing) lease revenue, $5.9 million, started in August when rent began at Black Pearl. Management is considering turning the Odessa mining site into an HPC site, but its power contract has about 0.8 years left.

Which cash to value

There is no free cash flow to value yet: operating cash flow was -$283.2 million for nine months. The value sits in contracts, so I value the contracted net operating income instead. The company projects an average annualized NOI of about $864 million across the three campuses over a weighted term of 17.3 years, and says Barber Lake has a yield on cost of about 15%. These are management figures; I did not find the lease schedules, rent escalators or the definition of NOI in the parts of the filings I read, so I treat $864 million as an unverified projection and model a haircut in the Bear.

The project notes are nonrecourse to the parent, and no principal amortizes until each facility is complete. Annual interest on them is about $295 million, or 34% of the projected NOI. The convertibles carry no cash coupon, but they are parent-level debt that must be repaid or converted, and at $6.29 the shares are far below both conversion prices. Restricted cash of $2.2 billion is mostly note proceeds earmarked for construction, and supplier commitments were about $1.6 billion at September 30, so I treat restricted cash as spent on completion. The Google arrangement matters more than its small share count suggests. Google holds warrants over 24.2 million shares at a $0.01 exercise price, exercisable from the Barber Lake lease start, and if the shares are worth less than $435 million the company must issue more shares or pay the shortfall. At $6.29 the 24.2 million shares are worth only $152 million, so I count a $435 million claim instead of the shares. Diluted shares used are 473.5 million: 456.4 million outstanding on October 30, which includes about 40.7 million shares issued when most of the 2030 convertible notes were exchanged in October, plus 17.1 million unvested restricted stock units. Shares outstanding rose from 405.0 million at year-end.

At $6.29 the equity is worth $2.98 billion and the enterprise value is $9.4 billion, about 10.9 times the projected stabilized NOI. Liquidity is thinner than the headline debt suggests: unrestricted cash of $724 million compares with $283 million burned in operations in nine months.

Primary valuation: finite-life cash flow of contracted NOI

The model values the contracts as they are written. NOI of $864 million a year starts in 2028 after a partial 2027 (55% of run rate, because Barber Lake finishes in the first quarter and Stingray starts in 2027), runs for 18 years, and is reduced by corporate cash costs of $120 million a year growing 3%. At the end of the term the company keeps the buildings and the tenants hold renewal options, so I add a residual of 4 times final-year NOI. Everything is discounted at 8%, a rate between the 6% to 7% the market charges on this project debt and the cost of the equity. Cash flow from the 2026 stub is ignored. The pipeline adds $300 million (my estimate, about 200 MW of future leasing at $1.5 million of value per MW) and execution costs beyond the budget subtract $100 million.

Enterprise value from the contracts is $7.4 billion. After $6.4 billion of claims, $100 million of extra capital spending and $300 million for the pipeline, the primary Base is $2.48 a share. I use a cash flow model rather than a multiple because the leases end: a perpetuity would credit the company with rent that no tenant has promised yet.

Secondary valuation: stabilized NOI at a cap rate

The second method values the portfolio as a buyer of income property would. Stabilized NOI less corporate costs is $740 million; at an 8.0% cap rate that is $9.3 billion, discounted 1.1 years at 8% to $8.5 billion. Less claims plus the same pipeline and cost adjustments, this gives $4.80 a share. This method assumes the income continues beyond the lease terms, which is why it is higher. A buyer of long-lease data centers could pay less than 8% for the highest-quality tenants and more for assets still under construction, so the cap rate is a judgment, not a measured comparable.

Growth check and history

A growth-adjusted check does not apply: earnings are negative and distorted by warrant gains, and free cash flow is negative while the company builds. The relevant growth number is contracted NOI, which goes from almost nothing in the third quarter to about $864 million a year at full delivery, if the schedule and the figures hold. That growth is real only to the extent the buildings are finished on time and on budget, and Barber Lake already cost about 25% more per IT MW than first financed. I did not verify any historical valuation multiples from a consistent source, so they carry no weight here.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $6.29 the enterprise value is $9.4 billion. Holding my Base operating assumptions, the price needs one of three things: a discount rate of 5.4% instead of 8%, a cap rate of 7.4% instead of 8%, or about $1.4 billion of extra value from the pipeline beyond the $300 million I include. The last is the most natural reading. The market is paying for Colchis, the gas-fed capacity and the unleased sites, and it is paying for them in a state where the election result of November 3 raised questions about new approvals.

How the buy price is set

A Confidence score of 7 is grade D, which carries a base margin of safety of 55%. I add nothing on top, because the leverage, the cost overruns and the Texas risk are already in the Bear case, the claims and the Confidence grade. Applied to the $3.40 Base, the maximum buy price is $1.53. The reference price is 311% above it. The two methods give maximum buy prices of $1.11 and $2.16, so the price would have to fall below $1.53 before my rule is met.

Dividend and capital return

Cipher pays no dividend and does not buy back shares. The share count has risen by 12.7% since year-end, and 3.0 million shares were bought back only to cover tax on vested awards. Stock compensation was $26.6 million in the quarter. Value to shareholders would come from rising cash flow, and from the project notes, which amortize only after each facility is complete.

Reasons to own Cipher Digital

  • Long contracts with strong tenants. $16.6 billion of contracted revenue over 17.3 years, with Google backing the Barber Lake lease and an investment-grade hyperscaler at Black Pearl.
  • Rent has started. Black Pearl delivered first capacity two months early, and $5.9 million of HPC lease revenue was recognized in the quarter.
  • Cheaper funding over time. Project bond coupons improved from 7.125% at Barber Lake to 6.125% at Black Pearl and 6.000% at Stingray.
  • Funded through completion. Management says contracted projects are funded, with $724 million of unrestricted cash pro forma.
  • Option value in the pipeline. About 5.9 GW of sites, a 1 GW conditional classification at Colchis, and 685 additional acres at Barber Lake.

These justify a watch, and they are in my Base. They do not show a margin of safety at $6.29.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: costs and delays. Barber Lake cost per IT MW rose to about $13 million, Cipher absorbs the first $359.3 million of overruns, and management expects labor and supply inflation into 2027. Third-party summaries put the final Barber Lake data hall in the first quarter of 2027.
  • Texas politics. Cipher fell 20% on November 4 after the election of a governor who campaigned on pausing data center approvals until new protections pass, according to a Simply Wall St summary. Management called the environment evolving and did not see Texas as materially riskier.
  • Thin equity cushion. $6.7 billion of debt against projected NOI of $864 million. Small moves in rate, rent or cost change the equity value by multiples.
  • Unleased pipeline. Two named sites, Reveille and Ulysses, have no signed lease, and management said new leases will wait until designs are further along.
  • Cash burn and dilution. Operating cash flow was -$283.2 million for nine months, share count is up 12.7%, and the Google warrant shortfall can be settled in shares.
  • Tenant concentration. Three leases, and part of the Barber Lake income depends on one AI lab through Fluidstack with a Google backstop.

Management execution

The record is mixed. Management signed long leases, delivered first capacity at Black Pearl early, raised the Barber Lake contracted revenue by $5.2 billion and got project bond pricing down to 6.0%. It also let the Barber Lake cost per MW rise by about 25%, issued shares and convertibles as the stock fell, and reported revenue about a third below what analysts had modeled. Assessment: 9/20, good commercial and financing execution and weaker cost control. The next tests are the Barber Lake data hall deliveries, the Colchis final classification expected in December and the first signed lease at a new site.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Barber Lake and Black Pearl halls delivered on the amended schedule with no new cost overruns
  • First signed lease at Reveille, Ulysses, Colchis or Horizon
  • Colchis final base load classification in December
  • Quarterly HPC lease revenue above $50 million
  • Operating cash flow turning positive

Yellow

  • Final Barber Lake hall slips into the second quarter of 2027
  • New leases signed at lower yield on cost than the 13% to 15% disclosed
  • Further convertible or equity issuance to fund pipeline work
  • Texas rules proposed but not yet applied to sites under construction

Red

  • Cost overruns above the agreed thresholds at Black Pearl or Stingray
  • A tenant delay or rent abatement extended beyond schedule
  • Texas law or regulator action that stops approvals for sites under construction
  • Project notes testing coverage tests, or the parent drawing on its $200 million revolver
  • Unrestricted cash below $400 million

Today's readings: the Barber Lake slip into early 2027 is yellow, no new leases are signed, which is yellow, and Texas policy is unresolved, which is yellow. The next points are the fourth-quarter release, the December Colchis decision and any Texas legislative action. I would re-underwrite after any lease signing, a financing, a cost change or a rule change.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Cost overruns beyond the agreed thresholds at Black Pearl or Stingray, which would turn the projected yield on cost into a smaller number.
  2. Texas rules or approvals that block the pipeline or sites under construction.
  3. Rent, coverage or delivery problems that interrupt cash flow from the project companies to the parent.

Main data limits: no verified NOI by campus, no rent schedules or escalators, no coverage test terms, no peer cap rate comparables, no synchronized history of multiples, and no view of the partial settlement of the Google warrants on October 16. The NOI haircut, the discount rates, the residual multiples, the pipeline values, the corporate cost level, the 2027 ramp and the treatment of the Google claim are my estimates.

Final verdict: reduce at $6.29

Cipher Digital has contracts that most developers would envy and a balance sheet that makes the equity a small residual. At $6.29 the price is 311% above the maximum buy price and 46% above my Base value, and the market is paying for a pipeline that has no signed lease and sits in a state that is reconsidering approvals.

Verdict: REDUCE at $6.29. Triangulated Base value $3.40, downside 45.9%, Confidence D (7/25), required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $1.53.

About $1.4 billion of the equity value is not in the leases at all. It is a bet that the next Cipher lease is signed in a Texas that still approves the sites.
