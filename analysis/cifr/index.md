## What Cipher Digital is now

Cipher Digital, formerly Cipher Mining, develops, owns and operates data centers in Texas and leases them to AI and cloud tenants. Three campuses are under construction, 700 gross megawatts (MW) in total: Barber Lake (300 MW, leased to Fluidstack, with a Google backstop, and now extended by a second 10-year term directly with an AI lab), Black Pearl (about 300 MW, a 15-year lease with an investment-grade hyperscaler) and Stingray (100 MW, commencing in 2027). The company says its pipeline is about 5.9 GW, including a 1 GW conditional base load classification at Colchis and plans for gas pipelines to support up to 2.5 GW of on-site generation.

Bitcoin mining still produces most of the revenue ($20.8 million of $26.7 million in the quarter) and is shrinking: 9-month mining revenue was $80.5 million, against $164.2 million a year earlier. The first HPC (high-performance computing) lease revenue, $5.9 million, started in August when rent began at Black Pearl. Management is considering turning the Odessa mining site into an HPC site, but its power contract has about 0.8 years left.

## Primary valuation: finite-life cash flow of contracted NOI

The model values the contracts as they are written. NOI of $864 million a year starts in 2028 after a partial 2027 (55% of run rate, because Barber Lake finishes in the first quarter and Stingray starts in 2027), runs for 18 years, and is reduced by corporate cash costs of $120 million a year growing 3%. At the end of the term the company keeps the buildings and the tenants hold renewal options, so I add a residual of 4 times final-year NOI. Everything is discounted at 8%, a rate between the 6% to 7% the market charges on this project debt and the cost of the equity. Cash flow from the 2026 stub is ignored. The pipeline adds $300 million (my estimate, about 200 MW of future leasing at $1.5 million of value per MW) and execution costs beyond the budget subtract $100 million.

Enterprise value from the contracts is $7.4 billion. After $6.4 billion of claims, $100 million of extra capital spending and $300 million for the pipeline, the primary Base is **$2.48** a share. I use a cash flow model rather than a multiple because the leases end: a perpetuity would credit the company with rent that no tenant has promised yet.

## Secondary valuation: stabilized NOI at a cap rate

The second method values the portfolio as a buyer of income property would. Stabilized NOI less corporate costs is $740 million; at an 8.0% cap rate that is $9.3 billion, discounted 1.1 years at 8% to $8.5 billion. Less claims plus the same pipeline and cost adjustments, this gives **$4.80** a share. This method assumes the income continues beyond the lease terms, which is why it is higher. A buyer of long-lease data centers could pay less than 8% for the highest-quality tenants and more for assets still under construction, so the cap rate is a judgment, not a measured comparable.

## Reasons to own Cipher Digital

- **Long contracts with strong tenants.** $16.6 billion of contracted revenue over 17.3 years, with Google backing the Barber Lake lease and an investment-grade hyperscaler at Black Pearl.
- **Rent has started.** Black Pearl delivered first capacity two months early, and $5.9 million of HPC lease revenue was recognized in the quarter.
- **Cheaper funding over time.** Project bond coupons improved from 7.125% at Barber Lake to 6.125% at Black Pearl and 6.000% at Stingray.
- **Funded through completion.** Management says contracted projects are funded, with $724 million of unrestricted cash pro forma.
- **Option value in the pipeline.** About 5.9 GW of sites, a 1 GW conditional classification at Colchis, and 685 additional acres at Barber Lake.

These justify a watch, and they are in my Base. They do not show a margin of safety at $6.29.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: costs and delays.** Barber Lake cost per IT MW rose to about $13 million, Cipher absorbs the first $359.3 million of overruns, and management expects labor and supply inflation into 2027. Third-party summaries put the final Barber Lake data hall in the first quarter of 2027.
- **Texas politics.** Cipher fell 20% on November 4 after the election of a governor who campaigned on pausing data center approvals until new protections pass, according to a Simply Wall St summary. Management called the environment evolving and did not see Texas as materially riskier.
- **Thin equity cushion.** $6.7 billion of debt against projected NOI of $864 million. Small moves in rate, rent or cost change the equity value by multiples.
- **Unleased pipeline.** Two named sites, Reveille and Ulysses, have no signed lease, and management said new leases will wait until designs are further along.
- **Cash burn and dilution.** Operating cash flow was -$283.2 million for nine months, share count is up 12.7%, and the Google warrant shortfall can be settled in shares.
- **Tenant concentration.** Three leases, and part of the Barber Lake income depends on one AI lab through Fluidstack with a Google backstop.

## Management execution

The record is mixed. Management signed long leases, delivered first capacity at Black Pearl early, raised the Barber Lake contracted revenue by $5.2 billion and got project bond pricing down to 6.0%. It also let the Barber Lake cost per MW rise by about 25%, issued shares and convertibles as the stock fell, and reported revenue about a third below what analysts had modeled. **Assessment: 9/20, good commercial and financing execution and weaker cost control. The next tests are the Barber Lake data hall deliveries, the Colchis final classification expected in December and the first signed lease at a new site.**

## Final verdict: reduce at $6.29

Cipher Digital has contracts that most developers would envy and a balance sheet that makes the equity a small residual. At $6.29 the price is 311% above the maximum buy price and 46% above my Base value, and the market is paying for a pipeline that has no signed lease and sits in a state that is reconsidering approvals.

**Verdict: REDUCE at $6.29. Triangulated Base value $3.40, downside 45.9%, Confidence D (7/25), required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $1.53.**

About $1.4 billion of the equity value is not in the leases at all. It is a bet that the next Cipher lease is signed in a Texas that still approves the sites.
