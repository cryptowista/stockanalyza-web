At $41.65, the October 20, 2026 regular session close, the shares trade 13.4% above my triangulated Base value of $36.73, a downside of 11.8%. The Bear value is $16.20, 61.1% below the price, and the Bull value of $58.49 is 40.4% above it. Confidence is grade C (13/25), the required margin of safety (MOS) is 45%, and the maximum buy price is $20.20. The price is above my Base and about twice the buy ceiling, so the verdict is Hold.

Valuation route: an IT services and digital engineering group that sells engineers' time and outcome-based projects, valued on the cash that reaches parent shareholders after all investment, stock compensation, leases, interest and minority holders. Primary method (65% weight): three years of that owner cash, discounted at 10%, plus an exit value of 11 times the third year. Secondary method (35%): 10 times sustainable operating profit after stock compensation and acquired intangible costs, converted to equity with the same debt and claims. Classification: standard company, 4 of 12 on the scorecard.

What Globant is

Globant S.A. is a Luxembourg holding company that reports in US dollars under IFRS and files a Form 20-F. It sells software engineering, data, design and integration work, and in the second quarter of 2026 North America was 52.8% of revenue. Revenue in 2025 was $2,454.9 million, up 1.6% from $2,415.7 million. IFRS operating profit was $171.7 million, a 7.0% margin, against an adjusted margin of 15.2%. The gap is made of items Globant adds back, mostly stock compensation, restructuring charges and amortization of acquired intangibles.

Growth has stopped. Second quarter revenue was $614.4 million, flat against $614.2 million a year earlier, and first half revenue was $1,221.5 million against $1,225.3 million. Customers above $1 million of trailing twelve month revenue fell from 339 to 331, and the ten largest customers rose from 29.3% to 30.6% of revenue. These are signs of a narrower customer base, not of distress.

The new story is AI. Globant's Glob.AI platform had $52.8 million of annualized recurring revenue in the second quarter, up 61% on the quarter, and the company expects at least $110 million by the end of 2026. Against guided revenue of about $2,445 million, $52.8 million is 2.2%. An annualized run rate is not recognized revenue, and I do not add a software multiple to a services company because one of its products is growing fast. The open question is whether AI lets Globant deliver more work with fewer engineers and keep the saving, or whether clients take the saving in lower prices.

Which cash to value

Globant's cash flow statement puts interest on borrowings and lease payments in financing, so operating cash flow minus capital expenditure leaves both unpaid. Capitalized development is a real investment even though it is booked as an intangible asset. And the statement adds stock compensation back, which is only fair if the shares issued cost shareholders nothing. I value the cash that reaches parent shareholders after all of these, and I count stock compensation as a cash cost at its recent level.

The primary Base is $36.14. The exit value is 78.7% of the value of the operating cash flows, or 81.3% of equity value after excess cash and claims. The explicit horizon is three years and Globant pays no dividend, so none of the owner cash is distributed in the period; the model counts it as value economically available to owners. For a horizon of up to five years I look harder when the exit share passes about 85%. The Base is below that line, the Bull case (83.3% and 84.9%) just under it, and the Bear (69.4% and 74.9%) well under.

A table default is not a justification for 11 times, so here is what it assumes. At a 10% required return, 11 times year-three cash is a 9.1% yield and implies growth of about 0.8% a year after 2029. Before that, Base cash grows 7.4% a year, from $125 million to $155 million, on revenue growth of 3.0% a year and a cash margin that rises from 5.1% to 5.8%. After 2029 the model assumes a franchise that lasts but no longer grows in real terms. The $85 million of annual investment is already deducted, so that small growth pays for itself. Debt stays at $422.0 million, refinanced, with $32 million of interest inside the cash. The Bear 8 times implies a decline of 2.2% a year, and the Bull 14 times growth of 2.7% a year. These multiples are my assumptions and not peer medians.

The primary Bear is $14.72 (cash falling to $80 million, 8 times) and the Bull is $59.65 (cash rising to $210 million, 14 times).

The secondary valuation: 10 times sustainable operating profit

The second method starts from the current business and not from the 2029 endpoint. Sustainable operating profit is guided revenue of $2,445 million times a 14% adjusted margin, or $342.3 million, less $74.0 million of stock compensation and $50.0 million for recurring amortization of acquired intangibles and similar items: $218.3 million. That is 16% above the $187.5 million of IFRS operating profit I calculate for the twelve months to June 2026 (a 7.7% margin). The $50 million is my estimate, with a range of $35 million to $80 million.

Ten times is an enterprise value of $2,183 million. After the $533.5 million of net claims above, equity is $1,649.5 million, or $37.83 a share. I use 8 times on $170 million for the Bear ($18.96) and 13 times on $230 million for the Bull ($56.34). A 10 times multiple for a services business with flat revenue and an unfinished AI transition is my assumption. I could not assemble a same-date peer set with enterprise values and after-compensation cash multiples, so there is no peer discount or premium here.

Lease treatment differs between the two methods on purpose. The cash method pays lease principal and interest in cash. The earnings method keeps right-of-use depreciation inside operating profit and leaves lease debt out of the bridge.

A price check against history

The monthly chart shows the shares falling from peaks above $200 in 2024 and 2025 to $41.65. A fall that large does not make the old price a fair one: it reflected earnings and growth that no longer exist. I could not build a reconciled history of price to owner cash after compensation, leases and interest, so history carries no weight and no premium from the past is added.

Peers help only on operations. In the quarter to June 2026 EPAM grew revenue 4.5% (3.4% organic in constant currency) with a non-GAAP operating margin of 16.4% and a GAAP margin of 10.8%. Cognizant grew 4.5% (4.1% in constant currency) and guides to an adjusted operating margin of 16.0% to 16.2% for 2026. Globant was flat with a 13.2% adjusted margin and a 3.2% IFRS margin. Accounting differs between IFRS and US GAAP, and so does the treatment of leases, so I do not turn this into a multiple.

The growth-adjusted check. On the guided adjusted EPS of $5.75 to $6.15 the shares trade at 6.8 to 7.2 times, 7.0 at the midpoint. On the trailing IFRS EPS of $2.58 shown by a broker, the multiple is about 16.1. I do not divide either by a growth rate. FY2026 is not a clean next twelve months estimate, earnings and utilization are depressed and distorted by restructuring, and I have no synchronized forecast. A PEG of 2.16 on the broker page has an unknown method and gets no weight. The cash growth ratio (price to normalized owner cash, 14.5 times, divided by 7.4% a year of growth) is 1.95, but it measures a rebound from my own start point and proves nothing.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C calls for a margin of safety of 35% to 50%. I start at 40% and add 5 points for the part of value that sits beyond the modeled horizon: the exit value is 78.7% of the operating value, and that reliance on 2029 and after is charged here once, and nowhere else. The Bear scenario covers a different risk, a franchise that shrinks. The confidence score covers shared drivers and the wide spread of outcomes. Near-term demand, AI price pressure and refinancing are in the cash scenarios and the interest line, so they carry no extra charge. The maximum buy price is $36.73 x (1 - 45%) = $20.20. It is a decision reference, not an order to place.

Three numbers answer different questions: what the shares are worth ($36.73), the annual return from buying at $41.65 and following the Base cash path (4.7%), and the price that would give a 12% return on that path ($34.31, cash method only). The 12% is an illustration and not a requirement I know you have, and it is not stacked on the margin of safety. At $41.65 the actual margin versus Base is -13.4%, so the shares do not qualify.

The dividend

Globant pays no dividend and its annual report describes a policy of retaining funds for operations and expansion, so the modeled yield is 0%. This is a capital appreciation and turnaround position, not an income holding. The buyback authorization is not a recurring payment: it improves per-share economics only after its cash cost, the replacement of compensation shares, liquidity needs and acquisition payments are covered, and a debt-funded repurchase around the 2028 maturity deserves a hard look.

Reasons to own

  • Recovery matters at this size. A small improvement in revenue and retained cash margin changes the per-share result a lot, and the Bull case of $58.49 shows it.
  • Enterprises still need implementation. Integration, data, security and process redesign around AI remain work to buy even if raw coding gets cheaper. That is not a promise about pricing power.
  • Existing relationships shorten adoption. Selling outcome-based projects to current clients is easier than finding new ones, provided the new contracts earn a decent cash return.
  • Cash flow has improved. First half free cash flow was $48.7 million against -$8.6 million, and my bridge still gives credit for the improvement after leases, interest and compensation.
  • Capital return can help. Purchases well below my value of $36.73 would be constructive, with the $125 million authorization as the option and not the plan.

Main risks

  • AI price compression. Fewer billable hours and client price savings can outweigh Globant's productivity gain.
  • A weak demand cycle. Postponed projects, a smaller base of large accounts and lower utilization. Accounts above $1 million fell to 331 and the ten largest customers are 30.6% of revenue.
  • Adjusted profit does the talking. Optimization charges recurred in 2025 and 2026, and stock compensation and amortization are large next to operating cash.
  • Working capital swings. Collection timing and payroll can flatter or hurt one quarter or one trailing year.
  • Debt and the 2028 refinancing. Higher rates, tighter covenants or restricted access to credit. A rate 300bp higher is worth about $1.39 a share in my weighted Base.
  • Acquisition and minority claims. Earn-outs and purchase options can consume common equity value, and I cannot split them with certainty.
  • Goodwill. Book equity overstates what the assets could earn or fetch.
  • The chart as a false bargain. An old multiple reflected economics that may no longer exist.

Management execution

Execution and forecasting should be judged separately, and the second is weaker. Globant opened 2025 with revenue guidance of $2,635 million to $2,705 million and delivered $2,454.9 million, 8.1% below the midpoint of $2,670 million. It opened 2026 with adjusted EPS guidance centered on $6.30 and cut it to $5.95 in August. It has booked business optimization charges in both years, and I count those as a recurring cost until the charges stop, not as a one-off.

On the other side, the diluted share count fell 2.4% in a year and first half cash flow improved. The decision to keep cash and pay no dividend suits a business in transition, but its acquisition record does not show that purchased goodwill earns more than its cost. I score management 5/10 inside business quality, with mixed forecasting and mixed allocation, and apply no separate premium to the multiple.

What I would watch from here

Supports the thesis

  • Organic revenue growth above 3% for two quarters across several clients, and accounts above $1 million back above 340.
  • Adjusted operating margin of at least 14.5% with clean conversion to cash, and rolling owner cash above $150 million without a pause in investment.
  • The 2028 debt refinanced on manageable terms, and buybacks funded from cash, below my value, with the share count falling.

Watch closely

  • Revenue between -2% and +3%. Today's flat quarter is here.
  • Adjusted margin of 13.0% to 14.5% (second quarter: 13.2%), accounts above $1 million between 325 and 340 (today 331) and top ten concentration of 30% to 33% (today 30.6%).
  • Owner cash of $100 million to $150 million (my estimate is $125 million to $128 million), and no refinancing decision by late 2027. Guidance credibility already sits at the yellow to red border after the August cut.

Breaks the thesis

  • Organic revenue below -2% for two quarters, or adjusted margin below 13% for good.
  • Rolling owner cash below $100 million, or accounts above $1 million below 325 and falling.
  • Covenant pressure, punitive refinancing or an equity raise, or buybacks funded with debt while cash and claims worsen.

The most important thesis breakers

  • Owner cash below $100 million with client erosion. A shrinking customer base and a thinner cash margin together would put the Bear at the center.
  • Financing. A refinancing of the 2028 debt on punishing terms, or an equity raise, would rewrite interest, share count and the exit value.
  • AI savings going to clients. Falling cash per share while adjusted EPS stays steady means the productivity gain is not being kept.
