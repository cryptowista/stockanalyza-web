# BMO: reported profit fell 25% in the quarter, adjusted profit rose 19%, and the price needs the 15% target

BMO, Toronto and New York. Published November 21, 2026. Price is the November 20, 2026 Toronto close of C$224.49, equal to about US$156.10 on the NYSE. Values in Canadian dollars. The fiscal year ends October 31, so "FY27" is the year to October 2027. ROE is return on common equity. PCL is the provision for credit losses.

In the quarter to July 31, 2026 BMO reported net income of C$1,750 million, down 25%, and adjusted net income of C$2,859 million, up 19%. The C$1,109 million gap is mostly a C$1,106 million charge, mainly goodwill, for the planned sale of its Transportation and Vendor Finance businesses. Adjusted return on equity was 14.0%, and management's medium-term target is at least 15%. At C$224.49 the shares trade at 1.99 times book value, and my model says that price needs a return on equity of about 15.2% for ten years, which is the target held forever.

The operating picture is good. Every segment reported record pre-provision, pre-tax earnings, Capital Markets net income rose 45%, adjusted EPS grew 22% to C$3.96, and the provision for credit losses fell to C$722 million from C$797 million a year earlier. The shares then fell 2.8% on November 20, and they are still about 32% above where they were a year ago.

My Base value is **C$206.77**, which is 8.6% below the price. The Bear value of C$138.93 is 38.1% below the price and the Bull value of C$242.00 is 7.8% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is C$198.62. At Confidence B my rules require a 30% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of **C$144.74**, or C$135.24 to C$151.07 depending on the method. The verdict is Hold for existing holders, and new money should wait.

Valuation route: a large Canadian bank with a big U.S. commercial franchise, so I value it on residual income (earnings above the cost of equity, built on book value) as the primary method and on forward earnings as the second. Economic classification: Quality Growth (5/12). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

## What BMO is

BMO has C$1,538.7 billion of assets, C$697.9 billion of net loans and C$1,017.8 billion of deposits. In the third quarter adjusted net income was C$983 million in Canadian Personal and Commercial Banking, C$925 million in U.S. Banking, C$649 million in Capital Markets and C$480 million in Wealth Management, with Corporate Services losing C$178 million. About a third of its profit comes from U.S. banking, and that makes it more sensitive to U.S. commercial credit than its Canadian peers.

The bank is reshaping its portfolio. It agreed in May to sell its Transportation and Vendor Finance businesses to Stonepeak, about US$9.2 billion of transportation assets and C$1.7 billion of vendor finance, for cash plus an earn-out, and it will keep a stake of about 19.9% in the new company. It also signed a definitive agreement with Royal Bank of Canada on August 10. Together the divestitures are expected to add about 50 basis points to its capital ratio on closing, and it plans a new buyback of up to 25 million shares.

## Primary valuation: residual income

Residual income values a bank as book value plus the present value of earnings above the cost of equity. I use a 9.0% cost of equity, which is my estimate: a Canadian long bond yield near 3.5% plus a 5.5% premium for equity risk at a beta close to 1. Base return on equity starts at 14.0%, the level of the last quarter, rises slowly to 14.5% by year five and stays there for ten years. That is below management's 15% target, because the target needs a lasting capital markets gain, further cost control and no U.S. credit surprises. Terminal return is 12.5% and terminal growth 3.0%.

## Secondary valuation: forward earnings

Adjusted earnings per share over the last four quarters were C$14.39. For FY27 my Base estimate is C$16.10, about 7% above the FY26 run rate of roughly C$15.00, which is the low end of management's medium-term target of 7% to 10% annual growth. I apply 12.0 times, in the range where large Canadian banks have usually traded on forward earnings. The result is **C$193.20**. The EPS estimates and multiples are mine and are not company guidance.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give C$215.81 and C$193.20, a divergence of 11%, which is below the 20% warning level. Both rest on the same earnings path, so agreement is not independent evidence. I weight residual income 60% and forward earnings 40%, because the first charges for the cost of equity explicitly and the second reflects what the market has paid for Canadian banks. The weighted value is C$206.77, a point inside the range. The price is 4.0% above the first method and 16.2% above the second, so the choice of method does not change the decision.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Holding the cost of equity at 9.0%, the price of C$224.49 requires a return on equity of about 15.2%, held flat for ten years. That is management's medium-term target of at least 15%, achieved and kept. The bank earned 14.0% in the quarter on an adjusted basis, 13.3% over nine months and 12.0% a year earlier. At the price the stock trades at 1.99 times book value and 15.6 times trailing adjusted earnings. The market is paying for the plan, and my Base value assumes the plan falls a little short.

## How the buy price is set

The base margin of safety is 30% at Confidence B. I did not add or subtract points: the U.S. credit exposure and the capital markets cycle are carried in the Bear scenario, and the capital ratio of 13.0% is lower than at peers but supported by the divestitures. The maximum buy price is C$206.77 times (1 - 30%), which is C$144.74, or C$135.24 to C$151.07 by method. The price is 55% above it, and the stock would need to fall 36% to reach it.

## Dividend

The dividend is C$6.84 a year, a 3.0% yield, with a payout of 43.1% of adjusted earnings. In my Bear case EPS of C$12.80 covers it nearly twice. The reported payout of 71.7% in the quarter is high only because of the goodwill charge.

## Reasons to own

- Record pre-provision, pre-tax earnings in every segment, with adjusted EPS up 22% in the quarter and 25% over nine months.
- Credit is improving: impaired provisions at 0.41% of loans and gross impaired loans at 0.97%.
- Medium-term targets of 7% to 10% EPS growth and at least 15% return on equity, with Capital Markets already above its own target.
- About 50 basis points of capital to come from the divestitures, and a buyback of up to 25 million shares.

## What could go wrong?

- Credit: U.S. commercial lending and Canadian mortgages, in a year with new Canada-U.S. tariffs.
- Earnings quality: Capital Markets and Wealth gains depend on markets, and expenses grew 9% in the quarter.
- Capital: a 13.0% ratio is down from 13.5% a year ago, so buybacks depend on the divestitures closing.
- Valuation: the price needs the 15% target, and the bank has not earned 15% on an adjusted basis in this cycle.

## Management execution

Management has delivered on the commitments from its March Investor Day: adjusted ROE rose from 12.0% to 14.0% in a year, and every segment reached record pre-provision earnings. The bank has also done the unglamorous work of selling businesses that earn less than its target. The weak point is the gap between reported and adjusted results. Over nine months reported net income was C$6,869 million and adjusted C$8,143 million, a gap of C$1.3 billion, and a bank that reports C$1.3 billion of adjustments in nine months asks the reader to trust its definition of normal.

## What I would watch from here

**Green:** fourth-quarter results in early December show adjusted ROE at or above 14%, a provision ratio at or below 0.41%, the divestitures closing, and the buyback starting.

**Yellow:** expense growth stays near 9%, or gross impaired loans rise above 1.0% of loans, or the capital ratio drops below 13.0%.

**Red:** adjusted ROE falls below 12%, the provision ratio rises above 0.55%, or a divestiture fails to close.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. A rise in U.S. commercial or Canadian mortgage losses that lifts the provision ratio above 0.55%.
2. A fall in capital markets earnings that removes the gain that lifted the ROE to 14.0%.
3. A cost of equity of 10%, which on my Base path lowers the value to C$192.83 at a 15% starting return.

BMO is earning more, provisioning less and selling lower-return businesses. At C$224.49 the market has already paid for management's 15% return on equity target, and my Base case assumes a little short of it. My maximum buy price is C$144.74.
