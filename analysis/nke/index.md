## What Nike is

Nike sells athletic footwear, apparel and equipment under Nike, Jordan and Converse. Fiscal 2026 revenue was $46.4 billion, flat on a reported basis and down 2% in constant currency. Wholesale was $27.5 billion and Nike Direct, its own stores and digital, was $17.7 billion. Footwear is nearly two thirds of Nike Brand revenue ($6.95 billion of $10.95 billion in the first quarter).

The company is in the middle of a reset it calls the Sport Offense, which pushes running, football, basketball and training and cuts back what it sold into the market too freely in the last cycle, especially Sportswear (just under half of revenue and down by low double digits) and Jordan (13% of revenue, down by the mid teens). Nike Direct, its own website and stores, fell 8% in the first quarter while wholesale fell 1%. Two of the best known areas of the business are shrinking at the same time as the performance business is growing, which is why falling revenue now sits next to a rising gross margin.

## What management now guides

For fiscal 2027 Nike guides revenue down by a high single digit percentage, which I take as 8% or about $42.7 billion, an adjusted operating margin of about 6%, and adjusted EPS of $1.15 to $1.35. Adjusted EPS excludes about $0.15 of Pace restructuring expense, which is about $0.3 billion of severance. Fiscal 2026 EBIT was $3.85 billion, an 8.3% margin, so guided EBIT of about $2.5 billion is roughly a one third fall on an 8% fall in revenue. Q1 EBIT was $907 million, so my arithmetic puts the rest of the year at a margin of about 5%.

At the November 17 investor day Nike added two things. The first is a 2031 framework with about $5 billion of extra revenue on the fiscal 2027 base, an operating margin of 12% to 14% and adjusted EPS of $3.00 to $3.50. The second is that fiscal 2028 will be a challenging year, with revenue still falling at a slower rate and adjusted EPS declining modestly. Growth returns to market level in fiscal 2029 and above market in fiscal 2030 and 2031. Pace targets about $2.5 billion of annual savings by fiscal 2031 before reinvestment, with most of them in fiscal 2029 and 2030 and about $1.3 billion of total pre-tax charges. The stock fell 6.3% that day, and the October guidance had already come in below the $1.61 to $1.67 analysts expected.

## Primary valuation: cash flow to the owners

The primary method discounts the cash Nike generates after tax, restructuring, capital spending and working capital. Because the earnings are depressed and the normal level is more than a year away, I model each year to fiscal 2031 and then add a terminal value, instead of applying one multiple to today's numbers. For fiscal 2027 I take the guidance (revenue down 8%, EBIT margin of 5.9%). In fiscal 2028 revenue falls 2% and the margin slips to 5.2%, in line with EPS declining modestly. Revenue then grows 3.0%, 4.5% and 4.5%, and the margin recovers to 6.8%, 8.0% and 9.0% as Pace savings arrive. That gives fiscal 2031 revenue of $47.1 billion, EBIT of $4.2 billion and EPS of about $2.28. The company's plan gets to $3.00 to $3.50, so my Base captures about half of the margin improvement it promises.

## Secondary valuation: exit multiple on EBIT

The second method keeps the same cash flows to fiscal 2031 and replaces the perpetuity with a multiple of 12 times fiscal 2031 EBIT, which is about 10.1 times EBITDA. The result is **$26.80**. The multiple is my judgment. Working it backward, 12 times EBIT only holds if Nike grows its cash flow by about 4.1% a year forever at a 9.5% cost of capital, so this method is the generous one. A reader who prefers 10 times gets $23.02 and 14 times gives $30.58. I did not verify a current peer or history table of multiples, so I derive the multiple from growth and the return on new investment and do not borrow one.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $35.76 the equity is worth $53.1 billion. Keeping my fiscal 2027 and 2028 numbers, the price needs an EBIT margin of 13.1% in fiscal 2031, which is EBIT of about $6.2 billion and EPS of about $3.28. That is the middle of management's 12% to 14% target and close to my Bull case's $3.34. Put another way, with my Base cash flows the price implies a cost of capital of 6.6%, against the 9.5% I use. The reported margin was 8.3% in fiscal 2026, which includes a $986 million tariff refund gain, and it is guided to about 6% in fiscal 2027, so the price pays for a large step up from there.

On returns, the cash flows in my Base case earn about 7.2% a year at $35.76 against my 9.5% cost of capital. The Bear gives 5.1% a year and the Bull 9.6%. The growth cross-check points the same way: at 28.6 times guided fiscal 2027 EPS the stock looks cheap against management's 2031 EPS (a compound growth rate of about 27%, about 1.0 on a growth-adjusted basis), but that growth starts from a depressed base, so I treat it as a low-base effect and not as evidence. On my Base EPS of $2.28 the same ratio is about 1.8.

## Reasons to own

- Gross margin rose 60 basis points to 42.8% and overhead fell 6%, so profit is holding while revenue falls.
- North America grew 2%, with wholesale up 9%, and Nike says running and global football are growing.
- Balance sheet: $8.4 billion of cash and investments against $7.9 billion of debt, and inventory is 3% below a year ago.
- Pace targets about $2.5 billion of annual savings by fiscal 2031, and the 2031 plan would double EPS if it works.
- The dividend yields 4.6% and management calls it a priority.

## What could go wrong?

- Greater China. Revenue fell 26% in constant currency and EBIT fell 34%, and the guidance assumes more weakness. The 2031 plan assumes the region is flat.
- Sportswear and Jordan. Together with China they are more than half of sales, and all three are shrinking. Converse fell 28% to $263 million.
- Fiscal 2028. Management says adjusted EPS declines modestly again, so the earnings low may be a year later than analysts assumed.
- Dividend cover. The payout exceeds guided earnings and free cash flow, and $2.0 billion of debt matures within a year.
- Tariffs. After the IEEPA tariffs were struck down, Nike said in June that it modeled a temporary 10% tariff and then a possible 15% levy. Each change moves gross margin, and the company gave no full-year gross margin guide.
- Direct channel. Nike Brand Digital fell 13%, and the channel Nike controls is the one shrinking.

## Management execution

Nike has a new finance chief, Dave Denton, since August 16, and the earlier recovery plan has slipped: in June the company pointed to gross margin expansion from the second quarter of fiscal 2027 and roughly flat earnings, and the October guide was well below analyst estimates of $1.61 to $1.67 a share. Management has beaten the first quarter, held fiscal 2027 guidance at the investor day and put numbers on 2031, which is a better record than saying nothing. Against that, buybacks have been tiny ($123 million in fiscal 2026 against a $18 billion program). I score management evidence 9 to 11 out of 20.

## What I would watch from here

**Green:** Greater China revenue declining by less than 15% in constant currency, gross margin above 43% in the second quarter, and fiscal 2028 guidance of flat or higher adjusted EPS. The price would also need to be near the entry range of $12.45 to $16.75 before I would act.

**Yellow:** Greater China down 15% to 25%, Nike Brand Digital still falling by 10% or more, or inventory up year over year.

**Red:** fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guided below $1.15, Greater China down more than 25% again, or any change to the dividend.

The next test is second quarter results, which data providers list for December 17, 2026 (the company had not confirmed the date in the sources I read). ## The three things that would change my mind

2. A cost of capital of 8.5% instead of 9.5%, which lifts it to $27.54.
3. An exit multiple of 14 times EBIT instead of 12, which lifts it to $26.79.

Even together on the favorable side these are not enough to reach $35.76 without most of the 2031 plan on top, which is why I rate it Reduce and not Hold.

## Final verdict: reduce at $35.76

Nike has a sound balance sheet, a strong brand and a believable plan, but the plan is already in the price. I value the shares at $24.90, 30% below the price, and new money would need a price near $12.45 to $16.75, which is less than half of today's price. The dividend is attractive on its yield and not on its cover, so I would reduce.
