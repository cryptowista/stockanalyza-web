# Agree Realty sold properties at 6.5% and bought at 7.3% last quarter, but its stock prices the portfolio near 6.0%

ADC, New York Stock Exchange. Published November 22, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close ($70.91). Agree Realty is a real estate investment trust (REIT) that owns retail properties let to one tenant each on long net leases, where tenants pay most property costs. FFO is funds from operations and AFFO is adjusted funds from operations, the cash earnings measures REITs report. ABR is annualized base rent. A cap rate is net operating income divided by property value.

## Primary valuation: net asset value

I start from annualized base rent of $823.4 million and subtract about $11 million of unreimbursed property costs, the middle of the 2026 guidance of 1.0% to 1.5% of adjusted revenue, which gives net operating income of about $812 million. I apply a cap rate of 6.75%. That is below the 7.3% Agree paid for acquisitions in the quarter and the 7.1% for nine months, because those purchases have longer leases, 14.5 years against 7.8 for the portfolio. It is close to the 6.5% for third-quarter sales and 6.8% for nine months. Ground leases are 10% of rent, and I treat them as a reason to lean toward the lower end of that range. I give no credit for projects under construction, which the release does not value.

That gives a real estate value of $12.0 billion. I subtract net debt of $3.66 billion and $175 million of preferred shares, add the $657.1 million of forward equity proceeds and subtract corporate costs of about $480 million, which is 10 times the roughly $48 million of general and administrative cost (my estimate of how the market capitalizes overhead). Divided by 139.2 million shares, the value is **$60.13**. The cap rate and the overhead multiple are my estimates.

## Secondary valuation: multiple of AFFO

The second method applies 15 times a normalized AFFO of $4.55 a share. That is $0.035 below the guidance midpoint of $4.585. New purchases at 7.3% earn only about 0.8 percentage points over the 6.5% AFFO yield on the stock, and 8.76 million forward shares are still to be issued, so I do not assume the full guided rate carries forward. The 15 times multiple implies an AFFO yield of 6.7% and, with a payout of about 70%, a dividend yield of 4.8%. With AFFO growth of 4% to 4.5% a year, that is a total return of about 9%, my cost of equity for this REIT. I use a premium multiple because the portfolio is 99.8% leased and 65% investment grade, debt is moderate, and AFFO per share has grown.

The model gives **$68.25**.

## Valuation triangulation

I weight net asset value 60% and the AFFO multiple 40%, because the first ties to observable sale prices. The weighted value is $63.38. The table puts both methods on one basis.

## Reasons to own

- The portfolio is 99.8% leased, 65.1% of rent is investment grade, and only 3.0% of rent expires in 2027.
- AFFO per share is up 6.7% in nine months and guidance was raised at the low end.
- Net debt to recurring EBITDA is 4.8 times, 3.9 times after forward equity, with $2.0 billion of liquidity.

## What could go wrong?

- Cost of capital. The AFFO yield of 6.5% is within 0.8 points of the 7.3% acquisition yield, and investment guidance was cut to about $1.6 billion.
- Dilution. Total AFFO grew 17.2% but AFFO per share only 4.9%, and 8.76 million forward shares are still to come.
- Premium valuation. At 14 times normalized AFFO the secondary value is $63.70.
- Cap rates. A cap rate of 7.25% gives a net asset value of $54.17.
- Other claims. The release did not show development commitments, other assets or other liabilities, and $150 million of other claims would lower net asset value to $59.05.
