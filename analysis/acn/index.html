Accenture closed at $186.11 on September 21, 2026, for one of the world's largest IT consulting and managed-services firms. The business remains durable: the company reports working with 195 of its top 200 clients for at least ten years. But the price already sits close to my triangulated Base Value of $195.33, leaving only about 5% potential return and little cushion if artificial intelligence reduces the labor hours behind existing service contracts faster than it creates new work.

The question is not whether Accenture is a durable business. It is. The question is whether the current price already reflects most of what that durability is worth.

At today's price, Accenture trades at about 15.4 times my Base starting owner cash flow per share. That multiple embeds a market expectation of roughly 3.4% annual owner cash flow growth over the next several years, a bit below my own 4.5% Base assumption. The market is not pricing in a collapse, but it is also not offering much of a discount.

Economic classification: Quality Growth. Primary valuation method: growth-adjusted multiple on owner cash flow after stock-based compensation and an acquisition spending allowance, per diluted share. Secondary method: ten-year two-stage discounted cash flow.

The investment thesis

Accenture is a diversified IT consulting, technology implementation and managed-services business, not a subscription software issuer. Roughly half of FY2025 revenue came from managed services, which behaves more like repeat, ongoing work than one-off consulting projects, though it is not equivalent to contractual software revenue.

The company has worked with 195 of its top 200 clients for at least ten years, a strong relationship moat. FY2025 revenue reached $69.673 billion, up 7% year over year. FY2026 guidance calls for 3% to 4% local-currency revenue growth, a slower pace than the roughly 9.5% five-year revenue compound growth rate from FY2020 to FY2025.

The investment case depends on three things:

  1. Managed-services bookings stabilizing after a weak quarter.
  2. Acquisition spending continuing to add more value than it costs.
  3. AI implementation work adding profitable new revenue faster than it erodes pricing on existing labor-intensive services.

Routing and economic metric

Accenture is routed to the SF-01 consulting and technology-services framework, with SF-10 added for its long dividend growth history and a yield above 3%. The hardest honest metric is cash available to owners after capital expenditure, stock-based compensation and a continuing allowance for acquisition spending, because both stock-based compensation and acquisitions are material and recurring.

Stock-based compensation was $2.094 billion in FY2025, more than 10% of operating cash flow after capital expenditure, which triggers the framework's harder denominator. FY2025 operating cash flow of $11.474 billion, less $0.600 billion of property additions and $2.094 billion of stock-based compensation, leaves $8.780 billion before any acquisition allowance.

Acquisition spending was $2.531 billion in FY2023, $6.583 billion in FY2024 and $1.471 billion in FY2025, material and volatile. The portion required to maintain the business rather than grow it is not separately disclosed. This analysis applies an estimated $1.50 billion ongoing acquisition allowance, tested from $0.50 billion to $2.50 billion in the sensitivity section below.

The acquisition spending question

Accenture spends heavily and unevenly on acquisitions: $2.531 billion in FY2023, $6.583 billion in FY2024 and $1.471 billion in FY2025. Some of that spending replenishes skills and market position that the business would otherwise lose, and some builds genuinely incremental value. Public filings do not separate the two.

This analysis treats $1.50 billion a year as an ongoing cost of staying competitive, deducted from cash flow before any of it counts as owner earnings. That judgment is the single largest driver of the valuation range: at $0.50 billion the Base discounted cash flow value rises to about $225 per share, and at $2.50 billion it falls to about $171.

Valuation triangulation

A 60% weight goes to the primary multiple method and 40% to the discounted cash flow, reflecting the multiple method's shorter explicit forecast and the discounted cash flow's greater sensitivity to the terminal assumption. This produces a Bear Value of about $137, a Base Value of about $195.33 and a Bull Value of about $285. The two methods differ by only 2.3% in the Base case, but both depend on the same $1.50 billion estimated acquisition allowance, so their agreement is weaker evidence of accuracy than two genuinely independent estimates would be.

A historical cross-check on reported GAAP earnings, not the strict owner cash flow metric used above, shows Accenture trading at about 13.8x FY2026 guided earnings, below its FY2021 to FY2025 year-end price to earnings range of roughly 21x to 37x. The comparison uses a different metric over different periods, so it argues against excessive pessimism rather than proving the current multiple is too low.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $186.11, price divided by Base starting owner cash flow per share equals 15.39x. Holding the Base discounted cash flow's other assumptions fixed, that price implies about 3.39% annual owner cash flow growth in years one through five, versus my own 4.5% Base assumption, a gap of about 1.1 percentage points. The market is not requiring a collapse in the business, but it is also not offering a meaningful discount to a reasonable growth path. If actual ongoing acquisition costs turn out higher than $1.50 billion, the growth the current price requires rises further.

Where the stock becomes attractive

The Confidence Score of 18/25, grade B, sets a starting margin of safety of 30% within the framework's 25% to 35% band. This analysis adds 10 percentage points for possible AI-driven structural disruption to labor-intensive service pricing, and subtracts 5 points for the net cash balance and long client relationships, for a required margin of safety of 35%.

Applied to the $195.33 Base Value, the maximum buy price is $127. An optional deeper-value review point sits at $100; a fall below it would call for re-underwriting rather than an automatic purchase. At $186.11, the shares trade above this $100 to $127 buy zone.

Reasons to own

  • Client relationships. Long client relationships and an approximately half-managed-services revenue mix support repeat work.
  • Balance sheet. Approximately $5.0 billion of calculated net cash at May 31, 2026.
  • Capital returns. Diluted shares declined year over year, and the latest $6.52 annualized dividend is covered by Base owner cash flow.
  • AI upside. If AI implementation adds profitable work without eroding existing contracts, the current valuation could understate earnings durability. This is an estimated investment hypothesis, not reported guidance.

What could go wrong?

  • AI substitution. Lower billable hours or pricing may offset new AI transformation demand.
  • Bookings weakness. Q3 managed-services bookings fell 16% in local currency; repeat weakness would threaten future revenue.
  • Acquisition economics. The split between growth investment and spending required to maintain capability is not disclosed, and FY2023 to FY2025 acquisition spending was material and volatile.
  • Cash-flow definition. Ignoring stock-based compensation would materially inflate owner earnings.
  • Model sensitivity. Raising the required return from 9.5% to 10.5% cuts the Base discounted cash flow value from about $198 to $173, before any operating deterioration.

Management execution

Management credibility is assessed as 13/20, good but qualified. Evidence for the assessment: FY2025 cash generation and capital returns were substantial; diluted shares fell year over year in the May 2026 quarter; and the quarterly dividend rose from $1.48 to $1.63. Against that, FY2026 local-currency revenue guidance narrowed from 3% to 5% in March to 3% to 4% in June, and the maintenance portion of acquisition spending cannot be measured from public filings.

Capital allocation is assessed as good, with an acquisition-return caveat. Buybacks are evidenced by falling diluted shares, but their economic merit also depends on the prices paid, which cannot be independently verified here.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Local-currency revenue growth above 4%, sustained
  • Total new bookings to revenue ratio above 1.05, sustained
  • Managed-services bookings returning to positive growth
  • Cash after capital expenditure and stock-based compensation per diluted share rising year over year
  • Diluted share count stable or falling
  • Dividend payout below 70% of Base owner cash flow

Yellow

  • Local-currency revenue growth between 2% and 4%
  • Bookings to revenue ratio between 0.95 and 1.05
  • One weak quarter of managed-services bookings
  • Cash after capital expenditure and stock-based compensation per diluted share flat
  • Diluted shares rising less than 2% a year
  • Dividend payout between 70% and 90% of Base owner cash flow

Red

  • Local-currency revenue growth below 2% for two quarters
  • Bookings to revenue ratio below 0.95 for two quarters
  • Repeated double-digit declines in managed-services bookings
  • Cash after capital expenditure and stock-based compensation per diluted share declining for two fiscal years
  • Acquisition spending rising while per-share owner cash flow falls
  • Diluted shares rising more than 2% a year without matching value creation
  • Dividend payout above 90% of Base owner cash flow, or a dividend cut

The three most important thesis breakers

These are the developments that would prove this cautious view wrong:

  1. Repeated weakness in managed-services bookings over more than one quarter.
  2. A persistent fall in cash flow after capital expenditure and stock-based compensation per diluted share.
  3. Acquisition spending rising while per-share owner cash flow does not.

Adversarial review

The arithmetic review independently recomputed the scenario values, the valuation weights, the market-implied growth rate and the maximum buy price.

The review found and corrected one material issue: a preliminary Bear headline value of $128 did not reconcile with its own displayed $6.30 billion starting cash flow, 13x multiple and discounted cash flow inputs. The recomputed Bear multiple value is $133 and discounted cash flow value is $143, and the 60/40 weighting produces about $137, adopted throughout this analysis. The corrected Bear Value is higher than the earlier draft, not lower, and a subsequent clean pass found no unresolved arithmetic error.

The strongest bullish counter-thesis is that the $1.50 billion acquisition deduction overstates required maintenance spending; using $0.50 billion instead raises the Base discounted cash flow value to about $225. Accenture's historical price to earnings premium, its repeat clients and its net cash position support this view, though the premium could shrink if AI changes the economics of the services Accenture sells. The strongest bearish counter-thesis is that repeated booking declines precede revenue declines and compress both owner cash flow and the multiple together, which a single lumpy quarter does not yet establish.

The gate status is PASS WITH WARNING because the maintenance portion of acquisition spending is not separately reported and remains the largest single judgment in the model.

Final verdict: hold at $186.11

Accenture's franchise is strong: long client relationships, a net cash balance sheet and a covered, growing dividend. But at $186.11, the price already sits close to my triangulated Base Value of $195.33, leaving only about 5% potential return and limited protection if managed-services bookings weaken further or acquisition spending proves less productive than assumed.

Verdict: HOLD at $186.11. Triangulated Base Value $195.33, potential return to Base +5.0%, required margin of safety 35%, maximum buy price $127.

For current holders, this means holding rather than adding at the current price. For new money, it means waiting for a better entry point inside the $100 to $127 buy zone, or for a change in the underlying acquisition and bookings evidence that would justify a smaller required discount.

The main thesis breaker for this view is a persistent deterioration in cash flow per diluted share as AI changes how clients pay for Accenture's services.
