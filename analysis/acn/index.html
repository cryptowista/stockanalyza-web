Accenture's shares have roughly doubled from their 52-week low of $118.15, while the company's guide for fiscal 2027 is revenue growth of 3% to 6% in local currency. The shares are at $240.76 today.

The rebound followed a fourth quarter in which revenue grew 7% in local currency, new bookings reached $22.2 billion and the company guided to free cash flow of $11.0 billion to $11.8 billion for fiscal 2027. My September analysis was a hold at $186.11 with a Base of $195.33. The new numbers replace my earlier estimates, and my triangulated Base is now $241.84, 0.4% above the $240.76 reference price.

The Bear value is $143.70 and the Bull value is $340.54. Confidence is grade C (17/25), which requires a 42.5% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at $139.06. The verdict is a hold: the price is almost exactly what I think the business is worth, and far above a price where the margin of safety rule would say buy.

Valuation route: global IT consulting and managed services firm with high returns on capital. Primary method: a five-year model of owner free cash flow (reported free cash flow minus stock-based compensation and a normal level of acquisitions), with a terminal value, discounted at 8.5%. Secondary method: a price-to-earnings multiple on fiscal 2027 earnings per share. Classification: quality growth company, 8 of 12 on the generic scorecard. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets. The reference price is an intraday quote, see the stress test section.

What Accenture is now

Accenture serves about 9,000 clients, employs about 814,000 people and generated $74.18 billion of revenue in the year ended August 31, 2026. Managed Services (long outsourcing contracts) brought in $37.30 billion, growing 8%, and Consulting (project work) brought in $36.88 billion, growing 5%. Managed Services bookings of $43.67 billion exceeded Consulting bookings of $40.86 billion for the year.

The market has spent 2026 asking whether AI shrinks the billable work that consulting firms sell. The shares fell sharply in June after the company cut its full-year growth guide to 3% to 4%. Then the fourth quarter showed the fastest local-currency growth of the year and 141 client bookings of at least $100 million each, according to the company.

Which cash to value

Reported free cash flow of $11.62 billion flatters the picture in two ways. Stock-based compensation was $2.10 billion, a real cost paid in shares that is added back in operating cash flow. Acquisitions took $4.94 billion in fiscal 2026 and $1.47 billion in fiscal 2025, and Accenture buys part of its growth.

I value owner free cash flow: reported free cash flow, minus stock-based compensation, minus $1.5 billion a year for acquisitions. For fiscal 2026 that is $11.62 billion less $2.10 billion less $1.5 billion, or about $8.0 billion. For fiscal 2027 I start from the middle of the guide ($11.4 billion), subtract about $2.15 billion of stock compensation and $1.5 billion for acquisitions, which gives about $7.8 billion. Cash taxes are not disclosed in the release, so I rely on the guided tax rate of 24.5% to 26.5% and the 25.1% rate actually booked. These are my estimates.

Secondary valuation: earnings multiple

I assume EPS of $14.60 in fiscal 2027, the middle of the company's range, and apply 16 times. That multiple fits a firm whose EPS is guided to grow 3% to 6%, whose returns on capital are high but whose revenue base is exposed to AI-driven pricing pressure, and which plans to return at least $9.5 billion, more than 6% of its market value, in buybacks and dividends in fiscal 2027. It gives $233.60. The two methods share the same view of earnings power, so their agreement is partial confirmation: the second tests the multiple, not the level of earnings. The multiple is applied to GAAP EPS, which includes stock compensation as a cost.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $240.76 the price equals my Base. On the cash flow method alone the price needs a discount rate of about 8.7% with Base cash flows, which means owner free cash flow growing from $7.8 billion in 2027 to $9.4 billion in 2031 and 2.5% a year after that. In plain terms, the market is paying for growth of about 5% a year in cash flow, a little above the guided EPS growth of 3% to 6%, and the Bear case is the allowance for an AI-driven price squeeze.

Reasons to own Accenture

  • Scale and breadth. About 9,000 clients, revenue growth in every geography and industry group in the quarter, and a Managed Services business growing 8%.
  • Cash generation. Reported free cash flow of $11.62 billion against a market value of about ${mcap:.0f} billion.
  • Shrinking share count. Diluted shares fell 4% in the quarter from a year earlier, after $7.5 billion of buybacks in the year.
  • Net cash and returns. Net cash of $2.7 billion and an operating margin near 16%.
  • Insider purchase. Press reports say the chief executive bought about $3 million of shares near $246 recently. I did not verify the filing.

These support the Base. They do not create a margin of safety.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: AI deflation. If clients use AI to cut consulting hours faster than Accenture sells new work, revenue and pricing fall together. The shares have shown how fast this fear can move the price.
  • Guide range. Fiscal 2027 revenue growth of 3% to 6% is wide, and first quarter revenue growth is guided to 2% to 6%.
  • Acquisitions. $4.94 billion spent in fiscal 2026, with long-term debt doubling, adds integration risk and uses cash I count as owner cash.
  • Cash conversion. Fourth quarter free cash flow fell 25% and receivables days rose to 50 from 47.
  • Reputation. The press reported accusations that Accenture published an AI paper with false citations. I did not verify the claim.
  • Client cost cutting and offshoring. Press reports of Qantas cutting 850 jobs in an offshoring deal with Accenture show the model, but also the political and client sensitivity.

Management execution

Management beat its own fourth quarter revenue guide ($17.75 billion to $18.40 billion against $18.68 billion reported), spent $4.94 billion on acquisitions and returned $11.5 billion to shareholders. It cut the full-year growth guide to 3% to 4% in June and then delivered 5%, so guidance has been cautious. Assessment: 14/20, a record of steady execution with cautious guidance. The next test is the first quarter report on December 17.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. AI tools cut billable hours and pricing faster than new AI work replaces them, and local-currency growth turns negative.
  2. Owner free cash flow falls below $6.5 billion because margins compress or acquisitions are overpaid.
  3. A large acquisition or client loss makes the guided $9.5 billion capital return unaffordable without more debt.
