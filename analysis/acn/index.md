## What Accenture is now

Accenture serves about 9,000 clients, employs about 814,000 people and generated $74.18 billion of revenue in the year ended August 31, 2026. Managed Services (long outsourcing contracts) brought in $37.30 billion, growing 8%, and Consulting (project work) brought in $36.88 billion, growing 5%. Managed Services bookings of $43.67 billion exceeded Consulting bookings of $40.86 billion for the year.

The market has spent 2026 asking whether AI shrinks the billable work that consulting firms sell. The shares fell sharply in June after the company cut its full-year growth guide to 3% to 4%. Then the fourth quarter showed the fastest local-currency growth of the year and 141 client bookings of at least $100 million each, according to the company.

## Which cash to value

Reported free cash flow of $11.62 billion flatters the picture in two ways. Stock-based compensation was $2.10 billion, a real cost paid in shares that is added back in operating cash flow. Acquisitions took $4.94 billion in fiscal 2026 and $1.47 billion in fiscal 2025, and Accenture buys part of its growth.

I value owner free cash flow: reported free cash flow, minus stock-based compensation, minus $1.5 billion a year for acquisitions. For fiscal 2026 that is $11.62 billion less $2.10 billion less $1.5 billion, or about $8.0 billion. For fiscal 2027 I start from the middle of the guide ($11.4 billion), subtract about $2.15 billion of stock compensation and $1.5 billion for acquisitions, which gives about $7.8 billion. Cash taxes are not disclosed in the release, so I rely on the guided tax rate of 24.5% to 26.5% and the 25.1% rate actually booked. These are my estimates.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: AI deflation.** If clients use AI to cut consulting hours faster than Accenture sells new work, revenue and pricing fall together. The shares have shown how fast this fear can move the price.
- **Guide range.** Fiscal 2027 revenue growth of 3% to 6% is wide, and first quarter revenue growth is guided to 2% to 6%.
- **Acquisitions.** $4.94 billion spent in fiscal 2026, with long-term debt doubling, adds integration risk and uses cash I count as owner cash.
- **Cash conversion.** Fourth quarter free cash flow fell 25% and receivables days rose to 50 from 47.
- **Reputation.** The press reported accusations that Accenture published an AI paper with false citations. I did not verify the claim.
- **Client cost cutting and offshoring.** Press reports of Qantas cutting 850 jobs in an offshoring deal with Accenture show the model, but also the political and client sensitivity.
