## What Aehr is

Aehr, based in Fremont, California, sells systems that test and burn in semiconductors: chips are run under heat and electrical stress so the ones that would fail early are found before they ship. Its FOX systems do this on whole wafers and its Sonoma systems on packaged chips. The demand driver is AI processors, where a failed chip inside a data center costs far more than the test. A lead hyperscale customer is taking more than a dozen Sonoma systems a month, according to a third-party report of the company's call, and that relationship drives most of the near-term numbers.

Revenue like this comes from a small number of large orders, and a year ago the company was losing money.

## Reasons to own Aehr

- Revenue is tripling and visible. The effective backlog of $109.3 million at November 1 is about 93% of the $117.0 million still to ship this year, and backlog has gone from $15.5 million to $100.9 million in twelve months.
- The guide looks conservative against the quarter. Revenue of $33.0 million a quarter is a $132 million annual rate, and the outlook of $150 million needs only about $39.0 million a quarter in the last three.
- Profit is real. Net income was $7.2 million, non-GAAP net income was 163% higher than the quarter before, and management committed to at least 20% of revenue for the year.
- Balance sheet: no debt and $105 million of cash, or $3.13 a share.
- The market is large and growing. AI processors need burn-in at volume, and Aehr says its customers' production plans keep expanding. If the Bull case of $400 million in fiscal 2030 is right, my Bull value on a 40 times multiple is $87.

## What could go wrong?

- One customer. A lead hyperscale customer takes more than a dozen Sonoma systems a month, which makes the next two years depend on that customer's build plans. I did not verify its share of revenue.
- Lumpy orders. Quarterly revenue went from $11.0 million a year ago to $33.0 million now. A single delayed order moves a quarter, and at 10 times sales the stock has little room for a miss.
- Gross margin. At 47.5% on an accounting basis, it depends on the mix of large systems sold to a few customers, who have the leverage to negotiate prices.
- Cash. Net income of $7.2 million was accompanied by an $11.4 million fall in cash. If growth keeps tying up working capital, the balance sheet shrinks even while the company reports profit.
- Dilution. Stock compensation is about $8 million a year and the high valuation makes a share sale easy if growth needs funding.
- Legal and tax items. A summary of the quarterly filing describes a patent dispute in China that is not resolved, and the $25.1 million deferred tax asset depends on future taxable profit. I read the filing through a summary, not in full.

## Management execution

- Guidance has risen. The fiscal 2027 outlook went from a range of $130 million to $150 million to $150 million, and the quarter beat the consensus of about $27.5 million of revenue and $0.11 of earnings.
- Fiscal 2026 was a year of transition. Revenue was about $50 million, the fourth quarter swung to a $1.4 million profit from a $2.9 million loss a year earlier, and bookings of $60.7 million were reported as a record. The company has only recently shown it can be profitable.
- Management has made a profit commitment: non-GAAP net income of at least 20% of revenue for the year. It is stated before stock compensation, so it is not the profit I value.
- Capital allocation is untested at this scale. Cash fell $11.4 million in the quarter, nothing is returned to shareholders and there is no long record of how the company deploys a larger cash balance.
