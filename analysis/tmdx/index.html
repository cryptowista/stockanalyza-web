What TransMedics is

TransMedics makes the Organ Care System (OCS), which keeps a donor heart, lung or liver functioning outside the body between donor and recipient. The company does not only sell a device. It sells a procedure: a per-case disposable set, clinical staff and, increasingly, its own aircraft and logistics to move the organ. FDA regulatory support exists for the commercial heart, lung and liver systems. Kidney, the next-generation system and wider Europe are opportunities, and I do not add a separate pipeline value for them on top of the growth already in my scenarios.

Revenue went from $241.6 million in 2023 to $441.5 million in 2024 and $605.5 million in 2025, a 58.3% compound rate over two years. In the first half of 2026 it grew 20.9%. That growth is concentrated: US liver-related revenue was $287.0 million in the first half, 78.9% of group revenue, against $59.2 million for US heart and $4.5 million for US lung. Everything else, including international liver, was about 3.6% (my arithmetic). A multi-organ product list is not yet a multi-organ earnings mix.

The business is changing shape. A high-margin product (Q2 product gross margin 77.0%) is being wrapped in a service operation (Q2 service gross margin 35.0%) and a growing asset base: a property finance lease of $347.7 million, aircraft, research spending and, since July 1, 2026, a strategic investment in PAD whose price was not disclosed in the filings I read. The competition is credible and different in method: OrganOx (owned by Terumo), XVIVO and Paragonix (owned by Getinge).

The primary valuation: a resale multiple on 2028 EBITDA

Base revenue for 2026 is $747 million, the midpoint of guidance, then growth of 18% in 2027 and 16% in 2028. That gives 2028 revenue of $1,022.5 million. At a 17% EBIT margin, EBIT is $173.8 million, and adding depreciation at 4.5% of sales ($46.0 million) gives EBITDA of $219.8 million, against $118.0 million over the last twelve months. A buyer at the end of 2028 paying 18 times that EBITDA would pay $3,957 million.

That multiple is my assumption for a still-growing franchise, not a peer median. I discount the exit value and the interim owner cash back to October 20, 2026 at 10% (a 2.197 year transition, with year-end timing), subtract the $395.0 million of net claims and divide by 36.0 million shares. The result is a primary Base of $81.74.

A price check against history

The 2025 annual report shows a June 2025 price of $134.01. The $72.22 price is 46.1% lower, but a share price drawdown is not a cheap multiple. I could not build a clean multi-year EV to EBITDA history that treats the new lease consistently, so history gets no bullish weight. At today's price the trailing multiples are 3.75 times sales and about 25 times EBITDA, and the free cash flow yield is 3.2% before SBC and 1.7% after it.

The growth-adjusted check is not much help. Consensus adjusted EPS is $1.51 for 2026 and $2.25 for 2027, which would be 47.8 times and 32.1 times at $72.22, and a PEG of 0.65 on 49% growth. The 2026 numbers I confirmed. I could not verify the 2027 estimate against its source, so the 32.1 times and the 0.65 are unconfirmed, and in any case the PEG compares recovering earnings against a low base. It sets nothing in my valuation.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C calls for a margin of safety of 35% to 50%. I use 45%: October cash, the PAD terms and the split between replacement and expansion spending are not verified, and I do not add a second penalty for risks already in the numbers (liver concentration, financing, method disagreement). The maximum buy price is $68.82 x (1 - 45%) = $37.85.

Four numbers answer different questions: what the shares are worth ($68.82), the return from buying at $72.22 and holding to the end of 2028 (7.8% a year), the price that gives a 12% annual return ($66.35) and the price that leaves a 45% margin of safety ($37.85). The return path assumes no distributions, notes refinanced at par in 2028, the building bought at the end of 2027 with a matching loan, and an end-2028 sale value of $85.12 a share. It is a future price illustration, not today's value, and it leaves out fees and personal taxes.

The dividend

There is none, and I assume none. Cash goes to the facility, the 2028 notes and growth. Owner cash is not a sign of a coming buyback or payout, so TransMedics does not serve an income investor.

Reasons to own

  • A product with a clinical moat. Familiarity with the OCS, FDA support for three organs and a delivery network that competitors do not match are the reasons transplant centers use it.
  • Repeat revenue from each procedure. Consumables and clinical services recur with transplant volume, even without subscription contracts.
  • High product margins. At 77.0% product gross margin, operating leverage is real if scale arrives without matching spending.
  • Optionality beyond liver. Heart, lung, Europe and kidney could widen the base, although I give none of them full credit.
  • A lower price could change the answer. If owner cash stabilizes and the larger asset base earns its cost, an entry well below today's price would have real asymmetry.

Main risks

  • Liver dependence. About 79% of first half revenue is US liver. A slowdown in one franchise would reach the whole company.
  • Investment-heavy growth. Revenue can rise while owner cash does not, which is what happened in the first half of 2026.
  • Facility financing and the 2028 notes. The $460 million convertible matures on June 1, 2028 and the property purchase option runs to the end of 2027. Cash is not all surplus, and refinancing rates are not fixed.
  • Controls. The company reported a material weakness over inventory movements, not yet declared remediated in the latest filing.
  • Competition and regulation. OrganOx, XVIVO and Paragonix can pressure price and share, and new indications need approval before they count.

What I would watch from here

Supports the thesis

  • 2026 revenue inside or above the $737 million to $757 million range, with owner cash positive and improving over rolling twelve months.
  • GAAP operating margin moving toward 14% in 2027.
  • A funded plan for the property purchase and the 2028 notes, and formal confirmation that the inventory control weakness is fixed.

Watch closely

  • 2027 growth between 10% and 18%, or operating margin between 10% and 14%.
  • Owner cash near zero, or capital spending above plan with no margin gain.
  • PAD or facility details still incomplete, or new share issuance above my allowance.

Breaks the thesis

  • 2026 revenue below $737 million or guidance withdrawn; growth below 10% for two quarters.
  • Owner cash at or below zero on two rolling twelve-month readings while revenue keeps growing.
  • No funded plan for the 2028 notes six months before maturity, a repeated control deficiency, or lasting price and share loss in liver.

The most important thesis breakers

  • Cash conversion. Growth continues but owner cash does not improve after capital spending, SBC and financing.
  • Liver erosion. Price or share falls in the franchise that supplies four fifths of revenue, with nothing to replace it.
  • Funding. The building, PAD or the 2028 notes need financing on destructive terms.

Stress test and independent check

I rebuilt the model from its stated inputs with separate code and reproduced: the twelve-month revenue, EBIT, cash flow and owner cash bridges; the net claims of $395.0 million; the annual rows and present values for Bear, Base and Bull; the primary and cash flow values ($81.74 and $60.20 in Base); the 30.3% divergence, the 40/60 blend and $68.82; the buy price of $37.85; the 7.8% return and $66.35 entry; the reverse solves; and the multiple, discount rate, margin and capital spending tables. I found no arithmetic or construction error. Treat that as a check on arithmetic, not on the assumptions.

From the company's documents I confirmed the first half 2026 income statement, cash flow and balance sheet figures, the lease and debt balances, the second quarter margins, the guidance range and the CFO change, the annual revenue for 2023 to 2025 and the $134.01 price in June 2025. I could not re-read the US organ split behind the 78.9% liver share, the details of the control weakness, the lease note, or the 2025 tax data in the annual report, so those come from the supplied analysis. Depreciation differs by up to $1.9 million between the cash flow statement and other sources, which would lift the trailing EV to EBITDA from 24.6 to about 25 times.

Four further points. Discounting at year-end rather than mid-year is conservative: mid-year timing would give a Base of about $70.97, 3.1% higher and still below the price. The joint Bull and 8% case, at $145.06, sits above the $141.88 capped call ceiling, which leaks about $0.43 a share and moves the entry to $79.55, with the same conclusion. The 2027 consensus figures are behind a paywall. And the price is an intraday trade; the October 19 close was $71.60, 0.9% lower, and the verdict is the same at either.

Main data limits: October cash and the PAD terms are not public in the documents I used, the split between replacement and expansion spending is an estimate, and terminal profitability is a model assumption. These are why Confidence is C.

Final verdict: hold at $72.22

TransMedics has a real clinical product, growth that has beaten guidance three years in a row and a delivery network that is hard to copy. But it is spending more of each dollar to keep growing, the owner cash yield is 1.7%, and the cheap-looking earnings come from a tax release. At $72.22 the price is close to my Base, 91% above the buy ceiling of $37.85, and inside a Bear to Bull range of $25.87 to $108.70.

Verdict: HOLD at $72.22, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $68.82, downside 4.7%, Confidence C (13/25), required margin of safety 45%, maximum buy price $37.85.

For holders this means reviewing position size and risk tolerance, not selling by default, because the gap to Base is small. For new money it means waiting for a much lower price or for owner cash to show up. The product growth is not the open question. The open question is who pays for the planes, the building and the 2028 notes before any of it reaches a shareholder.
