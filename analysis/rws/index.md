## What RWS is

RWS translates, localizes and protects content for large companies. It also sells software for managing that work, and since 2026 it reports three units. In the half year to March 2026:

Transform is still 58% of sales and fell 4.6% on an organic constant currency basis. Generate grew 52.0%, but the company says the figure includes an exceptional product-launch program for one strategic client, so it is not yet a repeating run rate. AI-related revenue was 32% of the group against 26% a year earlier, and net repeat revenue from the top 100 services clients was 109%. That last number covers a selected cohort of services clients. It is not software subscription retention, and I do not treat it as such. No single customer exceeded 10% of sales in fiscal 2025.

In 2026 the company bought two things. Obviously, a brand and IP protection platform, was acquired on May 5 for £16.5 million in cash plus up to £23.5 million of deferred payments tied to profit targets. Acogroup, the owner of the Acolad localization business, was agreed on August 3 for £40.2 million, of which about £17.8 million is cash on its balance sheet, an enterprise value of £22.4 million. The company expects about £155 million of revenue and £11 million of adjusted EBITDA from it in the year to September 2027, and expects to complete in the first half of that year, subject to French regulatory clearance. I assume completion on January 1, 2027.

## Which cash to value

RWS publishes two different cash measures, and they should not be mixed up. Operational free cash flow (£80.1 million in 2025) starts from adjusted EBITDA and is before interest, tax and the company's own exceptional costs. Its adjusted free cash flow (£56.1 million) adds back £25.7 million of exceptional cash costs. I value what I call owner cash: operating cash flow after tax, less all capital spending including capitalized software, less net interest, less all lease payments, less the stock pay that the cash flow statement adds back.

Less £66.7 million of gross debt leaves £561.3 million of equity, or 150.89p at September 2028. Adding the 2027 and 2028 cash flows and subtracting the Obviously tail gives **134.92p today**. Bear is 66.59p and Bull 219.87p.

At an 11% return and 2% long-run growth, an 8 times multiple on after-tax profit needs about 96% of that profit to turn into unlevered cash. Transform at 5 times with no growth needs about 73%. So the higher segment value assumes a more efficient mature business than the primary method's near-term cash basket. It does not prove that efficiency will appear.

- **Shared by both methods:** the recovery in revenue and profit, the acquisitions, the share count, the timing and the Obviously payments. More than half of each value rests on the same recovery.
- **Different in each:** the first capitalizes group owner cash at one multiple, the second values units separately and subtracts debt at the end.
- **What agreement would prove:** little. The two methods use the same forecasts, so close values cannot confirm that the forecasts are right, and I did not raise Confidence because they agree.
- **Cross-check on the Acogroup price:** an enterprise value of £22.4 million is 2.0 times its annualized EBITDA of £11 million. That is a declining, low-margin business, so it says little about what RWS itself is worth.

## How the buy price is set

I use 45%, near the upper end, because about 90% of value is in the exit, the key cash inputs are my estimates and the full-year cash is not yet reported. I add nothing on top. The maximum buy price is 122.76p times 0.55, or **67.52p**, which is 55.5% below the price. Because the cash conversion alone moves the ceiling a lot, I show it as a review zone: at 55% conversion the ceiling is about 55p and at 75% about 80p. The upper end is not a standing order. ## Dividend

The dividend is not a reason to buy. The total dividend was cut 43% to 7.05p for fiscal 2025, and the 2026 interim was 1.75p against 2.45p. The latest paid pair, 4.60p in February and 1.75p in July, is 6.35p, a 4.2% yield, but the 2026 final has not been declared. In fiscal 2025 the cash paid in dividends was £45.9 million, 172.6% of owner cash. At 6.35p the annual cost is about £23.6 million, which owner cash of £26.6 million covered 1.13 times, an 88.8% payout with little room for acquisitions. In Base 2028 owner cash covers it 2.2 times. Valued as a dividend that grows at 2% forever and discounted at 11%, 6.35p is worth 72.0p. That is a diagnostic with no weight in the value, but it shows that the current payout alone does not support the price.

## Reasons to own RWS

- **A specialist moat in costly-error work.** Regulated content and IP administration are less exposed to generic machine translation than general localization.
- **Evidence of an AI opportunity.** Generate grew 52% in the half year and AI-related revenue is 32% of the group.
- **A management reset.** Cost cuts, a rebased dividend and a low Acogroup price could improve capital allocation if integration costs stay under control.
- **Scale and client access.** The existing client base gives a way to cross-sell content platforms and brand tools without building a new sales force.
- **The Bull case is real.** It reaches 199.49p, 31.4% above the price, if conversion and the multiple both improve.

These justify watching the stock. They do not remove the gap between adjusted profit and cash that reaches owners.

## What could go wrong?

- **AI replaces work faster than new products pay.** Transform is 58% of sales and fell 4.6%. Lower prices or client insourcing could outweigh the growth in Generate, especially if that growth is a one-time launch program.
- **Adjusted profit overstates cash.** Capitalized software, recurring exceptional costs, working capital and stock pay kept owner cash at 26% of operational free cash flow in the latest half year.
- **Acquisitions.** Acogroup needs integration cash and French regulatory clearance, Obviously is small and was loss-making when bought, and larger profit can trigger larger earn-out payments. An £88.0 million goodwill impairment in 2025 shows the record on earlier deals.
- **Funding and the forecast.** Full-year cash is not yet reported, the facility disclosure conflicts, and the facility matures in October 2029, shortly after my exit date.

A further risk is concentration inside business lines: no client exceeded 10% of group sales, but a single program can dominate a unit. None of this is an immediate solvency concern.

## Final verdict: reduce at 151.77p

RWS has real operating progress and a plausible AI route, and my Base already counts it. But the price needs about 78% of EBITDA to become operating cash, while the company aims for 65% and the owner cash it actually delivered was a third of its headline figure. Until full-year cash shows that conversion, 151.77p pays for a result I cannot find in the numbers.
