## What Charter is

Charter sells broadband internet, video, mobile phone service and business connectivity under the Spectrum brand. In 2026 it closed two deals that changed its structure: the all-stock acquisition of Liberty Broadband and the purchase of Cox Communications. The result is a larger operator with a more complicated capital structure.

- The listed company owns Class A shares of Charter Holdings. Advance/Newhouse holds exchangeable units, and Cox's former owners hold common units and $6.0 billion of convertible preferred units paying 6.875%.
- The preferred converts at $477.41 a share, far above today's price, so I treat it as a $6.0 billion claim ahead of the common, not as shares.
- Per-unit values below divide by all common-equivalent units, 164.64 million, so the price is comparable with what each economic owner holds.

## History and growth diagnostics

**Valuation history.** Public data sets put Charter's long-run EV/EBITDA around 6.6x. The Cox deal changed the capital structure enough that history is only a loose cross-check here. Today's 4.9x is below the Base multiple and below that history, but Base does not assume a return to 6.6x.

**PEG (SM-19, diagnostic only).** Reported earnings per share of $38.43 give a P/E of about 2.7 on a heavily leveraged equity with a changed share count, so a growth multiple built on it is not meaningful. Flag: PEG\_NOT\_MEANINGFUL. Growth type: ACQUISITION\_AFFECTED.

**Free cash flow growth quality (SM-20, diagnostic only).** My Base owner free cash flow rises from $5.55 billion in 2026E to $7.58 billion in 2028E, a compound growth of 16.9% a year, or from $33.71 to $46.04 a unit with a constant unit count. That growth does not come from rising profits. EBITDA falls in the same period. It comes from capex dropping by $3.5 billion and from lower integration cost, so the flags are CAPEX\_TIMING\_DISTORTION and ACQUISITION\_AFFECTED. Forward owner cash flow yield on the $17.0 billion market value is 32.6% for 2026E, 38.1% for 2027E and 44.5% for 2028E, which tells you how leveraged the equity is and does not describe a normal yield. Forecast reliability: LOW. Stress test: 2028E owner free cash flow 20% lower gives a primary value of $234, 40% lower gives $177. Neither stress takes the primary value below today's price.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Reverse valuation: at $103.38 the market value of $17.0 billion plus claims gives an enterprise value of $134.7 billion, or 4.9x 2026E adjusted EBITDA and 5.1x 2027E EBITDA after stock pay. Using the same 7x multiple as my Base, the price is consistent with 2028E owner free cash flow of only $2.57 billion. That is 34% of my Base figure of $7.58 billion. Put the other way, the price equals about 2.4x my Base 2028E owner cash flow. My Base case implies 6.3x 2027E EBITDA, against 4.9x today.

The market therefore expects either a sharp EBITDA decline, capex that stays near today's level, or trouble refinancing. On the primary method alone even my Bear case, with standalone EBITDA falling about 5% a year and capex above $11 billion, is worth $111, above the price. The Bear value of $66 is lower only because the EV/EBITDA method puts Bear equity below zero.

## Reasons to own CHTR

- **Price against cash.** The shares sell at 2.4x my Base 2028E owner cash flow and 4.9x EBITDA.
- **Capex is guided down.** Management has said capex falls to $7.5 to $8.0 billion by 2028 to 2029, which is the engine of the Base case.
- **Mobile growth.** 12.5 million lines and 406,000 net additions in the latest quarter support convergence and retention.
- **Scale and synergies.** Cox adds customers and a cost synergy opportunity that I put at $0.8 billion by 2029 in Base.
- **Equity leverage works both ways.** If enterprise value holds and debt is repaid, the common gains faster than the business improves.

## What could go wrong?

- **Structural broadband decline.** Internet customers fell by 172,000 and internet revenue fell 3.2% in the latest quarter. Fiber and fixed wireless keep taking share.
- **Capex does not fall.** Each $1 billion of extra 2028E capex lowers the primary Base value by about $37 a unit at 7x.
- **Balance sheet.** A $1.5 billion change in net debt moves the triangulated value by roughly $9 to $10 a unit, and refinancing a large stack in a weaker credit market raises interest cost.
- **Integration.** Cox customer retention and the Spectrum rebrand are unproven, and a full combined quarter has not been reported.
- **Finance leadership.** Charter announced a chief financial officer transition on August 31, 2026, and Reuters reported that the current CFO would step down on October 15 with an interim CFO named.

## Management execution

Management has a record of aggressive buybacks and steady network investment, and it bought Cox at a multiple close to Charter's own. It has not given a leverage target or a post-close free cash flow figure, and the CFO is changing in the middle of the integration. Management credibility scores **9/20**.
