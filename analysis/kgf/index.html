Kingfisher's recovery is credible. On September 22, 2026, the owner of B&Q, Screwfix, Castorama and Brico Dépôt raised its FY2026/27 profit guidance, and the shares closed at 343.70p, up from 305.70p the day before. The question is whether that price is backed by cash that owners can actually take out of the business after rent, reinvestment and normal taxes.

On an EBITDA multiple, the shares look close to fair value. On conservatively normalized owner cash, they do not. My triangulated Base value is about 296p, 13.9% below the price, and the framework's maximum buy price is 177p. That entry price is a required safety threshold, not a forecast that the shares will fall there.

All share values are in pence (GBp), 100p = £1. Financial figures are in £ million. Route: SF-11 Retail and Consumer, mature European home improvement and trade retailer. Economic classification: Standard Company (3/12). Primary method: EV to normalized EBITDA after cash rent. Secondary method: DCF of post-rent, post-reinvestment operating owner cash. All values are present fair value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

Why cash after rent is the test

Under IFRS 16, store rent disappears from EBITDA and reappears as lease debt. Comparing lease-excluded enterprise value with pre-rent EBITDA, or using raw operating cash flow minus capex, would overstate what shareholders earn. Both valuation methods here therefore work after cash rent, and lease debt is not deducted a second time.

Marketplace gross merchandise sales are not IFRS revenue, and the property estate is not added on top of the going-concern value, because those stores already produce the cash being valued. The dividend history does not meet the framework's long uninterrupted growth test, so Kingfisher is not treated as a dividend growth stock.

Primary valuation: EBITDA after cash rent

The Base multiple of 6.5x sits in the framework's 5x to 8x low band for this retail subtype. Low group returns, mature growth and cyclical demand justify a lower multiple than a high-return stable retailer. It is applied to a rent-adjusted metric and must not be compared mechanically with published IFRS 16 EBITDA multiples. The same 6.5x is used in every scenario to isolate operating performance.

£841 million times 6.5 gives an operating value of £5,466.5 million. Adding £302 million of net financial cash gives equity of £5,768.5 million, or 343.57p per diluted share. At the market price, the implied multiple is 6.503x, so the multiple method alone says the shares are about fairly priced. The caution comes from cash conversion, not from an extreme multiple.

Historical and peer cross-check

A synchronized, lease-consistent historical multiple series is not available, and neither is a current peer panel on the same basis. No historical median, percentile or peer discount is claimed. The roughly 292p average price of completed 2026 buybacks is historical execution evidence, not an intrinsic value benchmark. The historical component of the valuation score is therefore zero.

Dividend and capital returns

The annual dividend is 12.4p, and a new interim of 3.8p goes ex-dividend on October 8 and is paid on November 13, 2026. The run-rate yield at 343.70p is 3.61%. The dividend was flat at 12.4p from FY2021/22 to FY2025/26 and the FY2019/20 final was cancelled, so this is an income-paying retailer, not a dividend aristocrat.

On about 1,649 million shares, the dividend costs about £204.5 million, a 62% payout of Base equity owner cash with 1.62x cover. In the Bear case the payout rises to about 90%. A £300 million buyback plus the dividend needs about £504 million, more than Base owner cash. Near-term reported FCF can fund that package, but repeating it indefinitely would need better recurring cash, smaller buybacks or balance sheet resources. That is a capital allocation caution, not a prediction of a dividend cut.

Reasons to own or follow Kingfisher

  • Trade distribution and fulfilment. Repeat repair and professional purchasing can support a more resilient franchise than purely discretionary project spending.
  • Incremental profit potential. Better sourcing, digital monetization and improved French execution could lift profit without proportional investment in new stores.
  • Capital returns and resilience. Buybacks have actually reduced the share count, the dividend is covered in Base, and the balance sheet holds net financial cash with investment-grade ratings.

These are reasons to hold or monitor the business, not a substitute for an attractive entry price.

What could go wrong?

  • Recurring cash disappoints. Normalized owner cash below about £230 million, or evidence that the recovery depends on temporary releases, would undermine the Base distribution capacity.
  • Sector and operations. A prolonged home improvement downturn, aggressive competition or worse economics in France and Poland could reverse operating leverage, and leases do not shrink with sales.
  • Balance sheet and allocation. Dividends plus repeated buybacks above sustainable cash can erode flexibility, and net financial cash does not remove £2.24 billion of lease commitments.
  • Management transition. Succession could interrupt the operating programme or change buyback discipline.

Business quality and returns

The latest audited group ROCE was 8.2% against an 8.4% WACC, up from 7.4% against 8.8% a year earlier. By region, UK and Ireland earned 15.5%, France 4.4% and Poland 6.1%. Even on the company's basis, group value creation is not yet comfortably above its capital cost. Reported revenue grew only about 1.0% a year from FY2020/21 to FY2025/26, a reported perimeter comparison affected by disposals.

Liquidity and credit are not the main Bear thesis. Lease-inclusive net debt to adjusted EBITDA is 1.4x, and the company reports investment-grade ratings and committed liquidity. Store rent and consumer cyclicality matter more than a refinancing cliff.

Management execution

Adjusted PBT of £528 million in FY2024/25 landed inside the final £510 to £540 million guidance, and £560 million in FY2025/26 inside £540 to £570 million. Cash buybacks were about £160 million, £225 million and £256 million over the last three years. Execution is visible, but value creation depends on the purchase price, and the current programme's average of about 292p is close to Base value rather than a deep discount.

CEO Thierry Garnier announced his resignation in May 2026 and remains in the role during a notice period while a successor is sought. No exact departure date is assumed.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Persistently inadequate normalized owner cash.
  2. A failed regional recovery that pushes group returns materially below the cost of capital.
  3. Capital returns that erode financial resilience instead of growing per-share value.

Adversarial review

The formulas were recomputed through a separate high-precision code path, and a second review challenged the economic assumptions, including the strongest case for owning the shares. All 88 arithmetic checks passed. The review rejected a stale quote on the issuer's own share price page, kept pence and pounds apart, applied rent, lease debt and interest consistently, avoided deducting the £64 million refund twice, and kept the 26% gap between the methods rather than forcing agreement. No historical or peer medians were invented, unspent buybacks were not credited as free value, and property value was only tested together with the rent a sale would add.

The strongest counter-thesis is that working capital discipline and favourable cash taxes are durable, so the Base normalization is too harsh. Starting from £500 million of guided FCF and deducting only recurring adjustments, SBC, the one-off refund and financial income gives about £427 million of operating cash and a value of 342.88p, almost exactly the price. It is not the Base case because the persistence of those tax and working capital benefits is precisely what remains unverified.

The most generous tested case, with the top of profit and FCF guidance, an 8x multiple and a 9% return, reaches 414.97p. Even with a lenient 25% margin of safety that gives about 311p, still below 343.70p.

Final verdict: hold at 343.70p, watchlist for new money

For an existing position, an unchanged operating thesis can support holding rather than an automatic sale, because the EBITDA valuation and a favourable cash conversion reading both support roughly the current price. For new money, the price does not offer the framework's required margin of safety.

Verdict: HOLD at 343.70p, WATCHLIST for new money. Triangulated Base value 295.77p, downside 13.9%, required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price 177p.

Verified recurring operating owner cash of around £430 million would bring the cash method up to the market price, and sustained better group returns would lift both value and confidence. Owner cash falling structurally below the Bear range, or buybacks funded by weakening financial capacity, would lower the valuation.

The main thesis breaker is failure to turn the operating recovery into sustainable cash after rent and reinvestment. A low forward adjusted P/E or an unchanged dividend does not override that test.
