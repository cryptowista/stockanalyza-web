What SentinelOne is

SentinelOne sells a cybersecurity platform, called Singularity, that protects computers, servers and cloud workloads, and increasingly stores and analyzes security data for customers. Tomer Weingarten, a co-founder, is chief executive and Sonalee Parekh is chief financial officer. The company says more than half of its annual recurring revenue now comes from products beyond endpoint protection: data, AI and cloud (Zacks). Annualized recurring revenue (ARR) was $1,218 million at July 31, up 22%, and the number of customers paying at least $100,000 a year rose 13% to 1,715.

The contracted pipeline is growing faster than revenue. Remaining performance obligations (RPO, contracted revenue not yet recognized) rose 45% to a record $1.7 billion, and the company expects to recognize 79% of it within 24 months. In May 2026 it announced a restructuring that cut about 8% of the workforce, which is where the $24.4 million quarterly charge comes from.

The model is capital light. Purchases of equipment were $0.5 million in six months and capitalized software $14.0 million, so the gap between profit and cash is not about the network, as at some peers; it is about stock pay. Free cash flow is thin because the company pays a large part of its payroll in shares and still spends the cash it collects.

Reasons to own SentinelOne

  • Growth is steady near 21%, with ARR up 22%, RPO up 45% and fifth quarter in a row of faster growth in the data products.
  • Profitability is moving fast: non-GAAP operating margin 10% against 2% a year earlier, and the guide implies about 13% in the second half.
  • The balance sheet is strong for its size: about $1.9 billion of cash and investments after the notes, and the notes carry no coupon until 2031.
  • The company is cutting costs, with an 8% staff reduction in May, and has raised its revenue and operating income guide.
  • Its product mix is shifting to data, AI and cloud, which are now more than half of ARR.

What could go wrong?

  • Valuation. The price is 2.7 times my Base and 8% above my Bull; it leaves no room for a slower path or a lower multiple.
  • Stock pay. At about 28% of sales it is almost three times non-GAAP operating profit. A reduction to 16.5% by fiscal 2032 is in my Base, and 2 points of sales higher in every year takes the Base to $7.00.
  • Gross margin. GAAP gross margin fell to 72% from 75% and non-GAAP to 77% from 79%.
  • Large customers. Customers over $100,000 grew 13%, slower than ARR at 22%, and the stock fell after the August report on that point.
  • The notes and the tax claim. $1,437.5 million falls due in October 2031 unless converted, and $162.3 million is owed to Israel with 7% interest and acceleration on a change of control.
  • Competition. Larger security vendors and platform companies sell overlapping products, and the 10-Q lists intense competition as a risk.
  • Data gaps. Third-quarter cash flow, a signed revolver, cash taxes paid and a peer table were not available to me.

The three things that would make this verdict wrong

  1. Stock pay that falls to 10% of sales within five years. At 4 points of sales lower in every year the Base is $12.62; the Bull case has 9% by fiscal 2037 and a value of $22.21.
