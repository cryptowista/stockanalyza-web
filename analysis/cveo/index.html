What Civeo is

Civeo owns and operates lodges and villages for workers in remote natural resource regions, and it sells catering, housekeeping and facility management at sites it does not own: 24 customer-owned locations with about 19,500 rooms. It reports two segments. Australia, mainly mining villages in Queensland's Bowen Basin metallurgical coal region, produced $460.3 million of 2025 revenue (72%), a record. Canada, oil sands lodges in Alberta plus integrated services, produced $178.6 million (28%), down 27% from 2024 as customers cut spending after oil prices fell more than 20% in 2025.

The two halves behave differently. Australia is the cash engine: contractual rate increases, a six-year A$1.4 billion integrated services contract announced in January 2025, and a stated goal of A$500 million of integrated services revenue by 2027, a business that needs little capital. Its weak spot is owned villages, where occupancy in the Bowen Basin is softer than coal prices would normally support. Canada is the swing factor: cost cuts in late 2024 and early 2025 lifted its fourth-quarter 2025 EBITDA margin from -13% to 8%, and in 2026 a stronger oil sands turnaround season and a new integrated services contract in Ontario lifted revenue 38% in the third quarter. Management also cites contract wins covering about 700 mobile fleet rooms in Canada.

The share price followed the quarter. It was about $22.87 at the end of 2025 and $27.73 on October 23, closed at $35.97 on October 30 after the third-quarter report and the raised guidance, and was $35.46 on November 9. At $32.24 it is up about 41% for the year but about 10% below the post-earnings close. The company is small: a market value of $335 million, and a third-party average volume of about 147,000 shares a day.

Secondary valuation: five years of cash flow and an exit at 6 times EBITDA

The cross-check takes the same first five years of cash flow and values the business at the end of 2031 at a multiple of EBITDA, less net debt. At the Base exit of 6.0 times, the enterprise is worth $611 million then, equity is $412 million after $198.8 million of net debt, and the secondary value is $34.92.

Why 6 times. Today's enterprise value is $533 million, or 5.9 times the $90.5 million guidance midpoint, up from about 4.9 times at the end of 2025 (my arithmetic: $22.87, 11.43 million shares, net debt of $168.4 million and EBITDA of $88.2 million). At a 9.5% return on the whole enterprise, 6.0 times 2031 EBITDA implies unlevered cash flow growing about 1.1% a year forever, which matches the 1% terminal growth in the primary method and fits a business with modest escalators, cyclical demand and a large reinvestment need. Exit value share: 65% of the secondary value comes from the exit, at a horizon of five years, below the 85% level where I check that the exit is supported by sustainable flows. All four are modest steps less favorable than my Base. The market is pricing a recovery that mostly holds, and that is why the verdict is Hold: the price is fair, not cheap.

Dividend and buybacks

Civeo pays no dividend now. The $0.25 quarterly payment was paid in early 2025 and the first quarter of 2026 shows none. Capital goes to buybacks instead, with a stated floor of 75% of annual free cash flow. At today's price, my normalized cash flow of $31.5 million would retire about 7.1% of the shares a year if 75% were used, which is why the per-share cash flow rises in the Base case even with flat EBITDA. I do not add that to the value, because buying at fair value does not change value per share, and I divide by today's diluted count. Buybacks at prices above value, however, destroy it, and 2026 buybacks of about $36.6 million over nine months exceeded the $15.1 million of operating cash flow less capex, so debt paid for the difference.

Reasons to own CVEO

  • The cash flow has recovered. Operating cash flow was $26.4 million in the third quarter against $13.8 million a year earlier, and $28.3 million over nine months against $3.1 million.
  • Canada is turning. Third-quarter EBITDA rose 47% to $11.1 million, after cost cuts took the fourth-quarter 2025 margin from -13% to 8%, with contract wins covering about 700 fleet rooms.
  • Australia has a capital-light growth leg. Integrated services has a six-year A$1.4 billion contract and a goal of A$500 million of revenue by 2027.
  • The share count is shrinking fast. Weighted diluted shares fell 24% in 18 months, and management plans to spend at least 75% of free cash flow on buybacks.
  • The price is fair. At 5.9 times EBITDA and a 9.4% yield on my normalized cash flow, the market is not paying for a boom, and an activist-backed board is in place.

What could go wrong?

  • Australian softness. Q3 EBITDA fell 7%, owned-village occupancy in the Bowen Basin is lower, and Australia produced 68% of segment EBITDA in the quarter ($23.2 million of $34.3 million).
  • A weaker fourth quarter. Management expects a sequential decline, part of the Canadian gain was pulled forward, and a third-party summary notes about $1 million of Ontario mobilization costs in the fourth quarter and turnaround cycles stretching to 18 to 24 months.
  • Leverage and refinancing. Net leverage is 2.1x, interest uses about a third of cash flow before financing, the bank debt had to be refinanced within three years of year-end 2025 (an April 2026 filing may have changed that), and the notes mature in 2031.
  • Cash flow volatility. Operating cash flow less capex was $2.1 million in 2025, and the 2026 figures include lower cash taxes and working capital timing.
  • Debt-funded buybacks. Nine months of buybacks of $36.6 million exceeded cash flow, and net debt rose $27.6 million.
  • Small and thinly traded. A $335 million market value and about 147,000 shares a day (third-party) mean large orders move the price, and it fell about 10% after the post-earnings high.
  • Capital spending may have to rise. Depreciation is about $69 million a year against capex of $25 million; capex of $32 million instead lowers the Base by about 13%.

Management execution

Management raised 2026 revenue guidance twice, from $650 million to $700 million in March to $675 million to $700 million and then $710 million to $720 million in October, and EBITDA guidance once, from $85 million to $90 million to $89 million to $92 million. Its 2025 outcome was mixed: adjusted EBITDA of $88.2 million landed inside the $86 million to $96 million range the company maintained in June 2025, while revenue of $638.8 million came in just under the $640 million low end of that range. It bought back 37% of the shares since 2021, moved $115 million of floating-rate debt into fixed-rate notes at 4.5% while repurchasing shares in the same transaction, and has given a clear rule for returns. Against that, 2026 buybacks outran free cash flow, the adjusted EBITDA definition changed to exclude currency effects, and the third-quarter release did not present free cash flow. A $0.87 million insider sale was reported on November 4 (a MarketScreener headline from an SEC filing), which carries no signal on its own. Management credibility is 12/20: reliable on guidance and capital allocation, weaker on cash flow disclosure and on forecasting Canada.
