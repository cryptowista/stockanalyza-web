# VICI Properties: below fair value, but not far enough below

VICI, NYSE. Published September 23, 2026. Price as of the regular session trade at 10:18 am EDT on September 23, 2026 (intraday, not a close).

VICI Properties owns some of the most important casino real estate in the United States, and its covered 7.7% dividend is real. The stock is below my estimate of fair value. But it is not far enough below it to compensate for how concentrated the tenant base is and how much development credit VICI is now taking on.

At $23.965 on September 23, 2026, the shares trade 24.4% below my triangulated Base value of $31.69, an upside of 32.2%. The required margin of safety is 30%, so the maximum buy price is $22.18, about 7.4% below the price. The distinction that drives the verdict is between being below fair value and being sufficiently below it. For existing holders this is a hold. For new money it is a watchlist name.

## What VICI is

VICI is a landlord, not a casino operator. It owns the real estate under casinos and other experiential venues and collects contractual rent under long triple-net leases, where tenants pay taxes, insurance and property costs. The portfolio is 100% leased. The reported 39.6-year weighted lease term includes extension options, so it should not be read as 39.6 years of guaranteed rent.

Rent is concentrated. Caesars-related rent is about $1,246 million a year and MGM-related rent about $1,065 million, together 69.8% of the $3,312 million annual rent schedule. A growing loan book adds a second risk: funded loan and securities principal rose 17.5% in six months, from $2,580 million at the end of 2025 to $3,030 million in June 2026, and about 57% of it is mezzanine or preferred. The filing discloses an $80.7 million non-accrual loan and a separate $90 million modified loan.

The One Beverly Hills commitment stands out: a $1.5 billion mezzanine position behind a $2.8 billion senior financing, replacing an earlier $450 million loan. The character of the business is shifting from a pure landlord toward some development credit. It is still overwhelmingly a rental business, but that shift is why the multiple is lower, the credit buffer exists and the verdict cannot be a strong buy.

## Why reported AFFO is not used as is

VICI is an exception to the usual REIT shortcut. Because most of its leases are accounted for as sales-type or financing leases, Q2 real estate depreciation was zero, and net income and FFO were both $526.5 million. Common AFFO of $679.6 million, 129% of FFO, is driven by noncash lease adjustments and a $271 million credit-loss allowance change, not by a depreciation add-back. A ratio above 100% does not mean more cash was collected than earned.

## Debt, refinancing and shares

At June 30, net principal debt was $16,930 million and liquidity was $2,520 million, of which only $288 million was cash. Company leverage was 4.9x and interest coverage about 4.1x. Ratings are investment grade (Baa3, BBB minus, BBB minus, all stable).

In August VICI issued $900 million at 5.40% and $850 million at 5.75% to repay $500 million at 4.50% and $1,250 million at 4.25%. The pro forma bridge counts both sides: annual coupon cost rises by about $21.9 million, and modeled net debt is $16,950 million after issue discount and fees. About $1.5 billion matures in 2027 and $2.0 billion in 2028.

Share count grew about 15.8% a year from 2020 to 2025, mostly around the 2022 acquisitions. Even so, AFFO per share grew 7.7% a year, from $1.64 to $2.38, so the dilution bought real rent.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $23.965, the price implies a property cap rate of **7.97%**, about 97 basis points above the 7.00% Base. In dividend terms, with a 9.5% required return, the price implies only 1.69% perpetual dividend growth, against a 3.5% Base assumption. Holding growth at 3.5%, the price instead implies an 11.45% required return.

At 14x, today's price would correspond to hard AFFO of only $1.71 per share, 27.6% below the normalized figure. So the disagreement with the market is mostly about the required return and multiple, not about a forecast of falling rent. Analyst targets compiled from 25 contributors average $32.25, close to the Base, though a 12-month target and a present fair value are not the same thing.

The framework's price rule alone would call a 32% upside a strong buy. The character change toward development credit requires a downgrade, and the 30% margin requirement then blocks a new-money buy at today's price.

## Dividend

On September 3 VICI raised the quarterly dividend about 2.2% to $0.46, or $1.84 a year, a 7.68% yield at $23.965. The payout is 74.8% of guided AFFO and 77.8% of hard AFFO, a coverage of 1.29x. About $579 million a year stays in the company for debt reduction and reinvestment. In the Bear case the payout rises to 91.2%, where growth could stall or the dividend could be cut. The annualized dividend grew 5.0% a year from $1.44 in 2021. The record date for the latest payment, September 17, has passed.

## Reasons to own VICI

- **Scarce real estate.** The leases sit on assets operators cannot easily replace, a real moat for a landlord, though not a guarantee that a tenant can always pay.
- **Contractual cash with light landlord capex.** The dividend is covered after stricter adjustments, with about $579 million of annual retention.
- **Per-share delivery.** AFFO per share beat the top of initial guidance in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and grew 7.7% a year despite heavy issuance.
- **Undemanding price.** The price implies low growth or a high required return. If either normalizes, the upside is meaningful.

## What could go wrong?

- **Tenant solvency.** Two operator groups pay about 70% of rent. A major restructuring with rent concessions is the main thesis breaker, and full occupancy does not replace property-level rent coverage data, which is not disclosed.
- **Gaming demand and regulation.** Weaker casino economics can strain tenants before rent visibly changes, and escalators become harder to absorb.
- **Leverage and duration.** Investment grade helps, but cap rates can rise and funding commitments could force expensive borrowing or equity issuance.
- **Subordinated development credit.** Non-accruals, a modified loan and larger mezzanine commitments show the diversification is not low-risk rent.

The next review point is Q3 2026 results, expected around late October. The approaching Caesars lease reset periods deserve explicit review rather than an assumption that every future increase is fixed.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. A lasting rent impairment at a major tenant.
2. Spreading credit losses or development funding stress that threaten the dividend.
3. Deteriorating refinancing access combined with rising leverage.

## Adversarial review

The arithmetic was recomputed independently from raw inputs and agrees to less than a billionth of a dollar per share. The review corrected errors in both directions. Upward: full-year rent from already acquired assets, counting both the new bonds and the notes they repay, and avoiding double deductions of minority interests and loan losses. Downward: current diluted ownership instead of a lower quarterly average, recurring noncash income removed, the credit character change recognized, and a real margin of safety required.

The strongest case against waiting is that the market treats contractual rent too much like cyclical casino earnings. At a 6.25% cap rate and 16x AFFO, the Bull value of $37.72 would allow buying up to $26.40, above today's price. That is a defensible optimistic case, and it reverses the verdict. It needs a lower required return, contained credit losses and continued rent growth all at once, which is why it carries 20% probability rather than being the Base.

The publication gate status is PASS\_WITH\_WARNING: the cap rate, project-level credit data and exact current liquidity remain estimates.

## Final verdict: hold at $23.965, watchlist for new money

The rental franchise and the covered dividend support value. Concentration, leveraged long-duration assets and growing development credit demand more protection than the current price offers. This is not an argument that the price must fall before it can rise. It is a statement of the price at which the risk is paid for.

**Verdict: HOLD at $23.965, WATCHLIST for new money. Triangulated Base value $31.69, upside 32.2%, Confidence B (18/25). Required margin of safety 30%, maximum buy price $22.18, conditional buy zone $19.01 to $22.18.**

For an existing holder whose position fits their risk limits, the dividend and the discount to Base support holding. New money waits for $22.18 or below with an intact thesis, or for verified improvement in tenant and loan performance.

The main thesis breaker is sustained rent or credit impairment that erodes dividend coverage while restricting access to affordable capital.
