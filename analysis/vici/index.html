VICI's net income fell 56.4%, its cash earnings rose 4.3%, and Caesars explains the gap

VICI, NYSE. Published November 17, 2026. Price as of the November 16, 2026 regular session close.

Hold at $24.85

The catch is that Caesars, about 38% of rent, was the main reason for a $458 million credit loss provision, and VICI is reassessing how it structures that lease.

VICI's net income to common shareholders fell 56.4% in the third quarter, to $342.5 million. Its AFFO per share, the cash earnings measure REITs report, rose 4.3% to $0.62. The gap is a $458 million credit loss provision, and according to the earnings call $313 million of it is for Caesars.

VICI Properties owns casino and entertainment real estate on long triple-net leases, where the tenant pays the property costs. The provision is a reserve against future losses, not a cut in reported rent: third quarter revenue grew 5.8% to $1.065 billion, and management raised the 2026 AFFO guidance to $2.47 to $2.48 per share. At $24.85, the November 16, 2026 close, the stock trades 3.7% above the intraday price I used in September, and my Base value is lower than it was then.

My triangulated Base value is $29.72, which is 19.6% above the price. The Bear value of $21.64 is 12.9% below it and the Bull value of $35.16 is 41.5% above it. I require a 35% margin of safety here, which puts the maximum buy price at $19.32, 28.6% below the current price. The verdict is a hold for existing holders and a watchlist for new money: the stock is cheaper than its Base value, but a tenant that pays about 38% of the rent is now an open question.

Valuation route: Real estate investment trust (REIT) owning property under long net leases, with a growing loan book that I monitor. Primary method: capitalized hard AFFO, which is AFFO after the recurring costs the headline number leaves out. Secondary method: adjusted net asset value (NAV). Economic classification: Quality Growth (6.6/12, rescaled). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

What changed since September

The September analysis put the Base value at $31.69 and the maximum buy price at $22.18, and it named a lasting rent impairment at a major tenant as the main thesis breaker. The Base is now $29.72, 6.2% lower. Two inputs moved: the AFFO multiple fell from 14.0x to 13.0x, and the cap rate in the NAV method rose from 7.00% to 7.25%. I also raised the credit and renewal buffer in hard AFFO from $15 million to $25 million.

The maximum buy price fell to $19.32 because the required margin of safety rose from 30% to 35%: the 30% base for Confidence B, plus 5 points for the unresolved structure of the Caesars regional lease. At 30% the limit would be $20.80, and at 40% it would be $17.83. The stock rose from $23.965 to $24.85, so the distance to the buy limit widened from 8.0% to 28.6%.

What VICI does and why Caesars matters

VICI collects rent on casino resorts and entertainment properties under triple-net leases with a weighted term of 39.6 years (this includes extension options, so it is not 39.6 years of guaranteed rent). Occupancy is 100%. Caesars pays about 38% of the rent and MGM about 32%, together about 70% (these shares come from my September analysis).

Three things changed in the third quarter. First, Caesars: the credit allowance charge, a downgrade of Caesars' secured debt, VICI saying it is reassessing how to structure income from the regional lease, and Fertitta Entertainment seeking to buy Caesars. Rent coverage at the property level is not disclosed. Second, the loan book, about $3.0 billion and roughly 6% of assets, grew 17.5% in six months, and income from it rose to $79.1 million from $61.0 million. Part of it is subordinated development credit, such as a $1.5 billion mezzanine loan on One Beverly Hills behind $2.8 billion of senior debt, with $80.7 million of loans on non-accrual. Third, VICI started its first buyback and added a new tenant, Highfield Investment Group in Alberta.

Which numbers matter for VICI

Shares and capital structure

Common shares were 1,101.1 million at September 30. VICI also has redeemable partnership interests, which I estimate at about 13 million units (from my earlier model, since the release does not give the count), for about 1,114 million economic units. Net debt is $16,947.5 million: $17,217.8 million of debt (unsecured $14,217.8 million and commercial mortgage-backed securities $3,000.0 million) less $270.3 million of cash. The credit ratings are investment grade at Baa3, BBB- and BBB-.

The $2.0 billion buyback is 7.3% of the market value at the current price. I do not model it in the Base case, because no executed amount is documented and the value effect depends on the price paid against value. Forward equity sales were not verified.

Primary valuation: capitalized hard AFFO

I multiply next-period hard AFFO per share by a justified multiple. Starting from 12.5x, I add 1.5x for AFFO growth of 4% to 5%, add 1.5x for the long lease term, subtract 1.0x for Caesars concentration and the lease reassessment, subtract 1.0x for credit, development and funding risk, and add 0.5x for incremental returns. That gives a Base multiple of 13.0x, against 14.0x in September. At a 9.5% required return, 13.0x implies perpetual growth of about 1.8%, in line with escalators of about 2%.

Historical multiples as context

Price to AFFO at year-end was 16.5x in 2021, 16.8x, 14.8x, 12.9x and 11.8x in 2025, a median of 14.8x. The stock now trades at 10.5x hard AFFO and 10.0x guided AFFO. Those earlier multiples came with lower rates and less tenant concentration risk, so I do not use them as a target. AFFO per share grew 7.7% a year from $1.64 in 2020 to $2.38 in 2025, but only 4.3% in the third quarter, and that earlier growth came with a 15.8% annual increase in the share count.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $24.85 the stock trades at 10.5x hard AFFO. Working backward, the price implies a property cap rate of about 7.8% against my 7.25%, and at a 9.5% required return it implies hard AFFO that does not grow at all, against the 1.8% growth my Base multiple assumes. Put the other way, at my 13.0x multiple the price implies hard AFFO of $1.91 a share, 19% below my $2.37. The market is pricing a permanent loss of rent on part of Caesars' regional leases, or loan losses, or both.

How the buy price is set

The margin of safety is 35%: 30% for a Confidence B score and 5 points for the unresolved Caesars lease structure, a measurement uncertainty that the Bear case does not capture. Applied to the $29.72 Base, the maximum buy price is $19.32. At a 12x multiple the Base would be $28.18 and the limit $18.32, and at 14x the Base would be $31.26 and the limit $20.32. The current price is above the limit in every case. The limit is 10.7% below the Bear value. Several layers of caution stack here (a lower multiple, a higher cap rate and a larger margin), so the Base is not a worst case.

Dividend

The dividend is $1.84 a year, a 7.4% yield at $24.85. It is 74% of guided AFFO and 77.6% of hard AFFO, covered 1.29 times, and VICI retains about $580 million a year, which funds buybacks and loans. The dividend has grown about 5% a year since 2021 and was raised 2.2% in September. I do not model a cut in the Base case. In the Bear case, with hard AFFO of $2.02, the payout would be 91%.

Reasons to own VICI

  • Long leases. A weighted term of 39.6 years with escalators, and 100% occupancy.
  • Growth in cash earnings. AFFO per share grew 4.3% and guidance was raised in the same quarter as the charge.
  • A 7.4% yield. Covered 1.29 times by hard AFFO.
  • A new $2.0 billion buyback. At about 10x AFFO, buying back stock is worth more than at the 14x of last year.
  • Investment grade balance sheet. About $2.5 billion of liquidity and a revolver that was amended on better terms on November 12.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: Caesars rent. Caesars pays about 38% of rent. A secured debt downgrade, a lease reassessment and a possible acquisition by Fertitta Entertainment make a rent reduction a live possibility.
  • MGM. About 32% of rent, so two tenants account for about 70%.
  • The loan book. Subordinated development credit with $80.7 million on non-accrual and another $90 million loan already modified.
  • Refinancing. $1.5 billion comes due in 2027 and $2.0 billion in 2028, at coupons that are higher than the debt they replace.
  • Gaming demand and regulation. Weaker casino economics can strain tenants before rent visibly changes.
  • Rates. The cap rate is the largest sensitivity in the NAV method, and higher rates push it up.

Management execution

VICI has beaten its own AFFO guidance every year from 2023 to 2025 and raised it three times in 2026. Against that, management called the share price performance disappointing, and the Caesars charge arrived five weeks after my September analysis named tenant solvency as the main thesis breaker. Assessment: 13/20, acceptable, down from 14. The next test is a clean resolution of the Caesars regional lease.

Today's readings: hard AFFO of $2.37 and a 77.6% payout are green, leverage of about 4.8x is green, and the top two tenants at about 70% of rent sit right at the yellow line. Non-accruals of $80.7 million are about 2.7% of the loan book, which is green. The case would improve with a clean Caesars resolution, buybacks executed below $24, or a stabilizing loan book. Next review: fourth quarter results, expected around February 2027.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. A lasting rent reduction at Caesars or MGM.
  2. Credit losses on the loan book or development funding stress that threatens dividend coverage.
  3. Loss of investment grade or refinancing at rates that push hard AFFO per share down.

The strongest counter-thesis for the bulls: AFFO grew, guidance went up, and a $2.0 billion buyback at 10x AFFO is accretive if Caesars pays. The strongest counter-thesis for the bears: the charge came in one quarter, the tenant is under a possible change of control, and the stock still trades 29% above a margin of safety that already assumes the Bear case is a real risk. Final verdict: hold at $24.85, watchlist for new money below $19.32

VICI's cash earnings, dividend and balance sheet are intact, and the stock is 16.4% below my Base value. But Caesars is now an active issue and the price is 28.6% above the maximum buy price. Existing holders have no thesis breaker to act on. New money should wait for clarity on the Caesars lease, or for a price near $20 to $21.

Verdict: HOLD at $24.85. Triangulated Base value $29.72, upside 19.6%, Confidence B (18/25). Required margin of safety 35%, maximum buy price $19.32.

In September I called tenant solvency the main thing that could break the thesis. The third quarter did not break it, but it put a number on it: $313 million.
