HubSpot guides to about $720 million of free cash flow for 2026 and to $502.6 million of stock-based compensation (SBC), the shares and share units it pays employees. Subtract one from the other and $217.4 million is left, 5.9% of revenue, which is what a buyer of the stock is really paying for. On November 12 the board replaced the chief executive with the co-founder, and the stock sits 46.2% below where it started the year.

At $216.07 the shares trade 7.6% below my weighted Base value of $233.86, so the Base is 8.2% above the price. The Bear value of $100.19 is 53.6% below the price and the Bull value of $323.36 is 49.7% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade C, which sets a required margin of safety of 47.5% and a maximum buy price of $122.78. The verdict is Hold, watchlist for new money: the price is close to what the business is worth if growth settles near 7% to 11% and stock pay keeps falling, and it leaves no margin of safety.

Valuation route: a profitable subscription software company with positive free cash flow and no debt, so it is valued on owner free cash flow, which is free cash flow after stock-based pay and after a normal tax charge. Primary method: five years of owner free cash flow discounted at 10% with a terminal value. Cross-check: an exit multiple on the fourth year. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What HubSpot is

HubSpot sells a customer relationship management (CRM) platform to small and mid-sized businesses: marketing, sales, service and now software "agents" in one subscription. It reported 311,704 customers at the end of September, up 12%, each paying an average of $12,038 a year, up 4%. Revenue is almost all subscription, and international customers are 49% of it.

| Subscription revenue | $930.2 million | up 18% |

| Calculated billings | $924.9 million | up 15% |
| Deferred revenue | $1,040.1 million | $1,034.6 million current, $5.5 million non-current |

Growth has slowed in steps. Reported revenue growth was 23% for subscription in the first quarter, 20% in the second and 17% in the third, and the guide for the fourth quarter is 12%. Net revenue retention of 101% means existing customers now barely spend more year to year, so almost all growth comes from adding customers and from price.

## Valuation triangulation

- Primary (owner free cash flow, 10% rate): $223.00, weight 60%.
- Secondary (exit multiple of 20 times in 2030): $250.16, weight 40%.
- Divergence between the two: 11.5% of their midpoint, inside the 20% limit.
- Weighted Base: $233.86.

The two methods agree, but they are not independent: both use the same forecast of revenue, margin and stock pay, so their agreement does not raise my confidence. They differ in how they treat the end of the forecast, one as a perpetuity growing 3.5% and one as a sale at 20 times. I weight the primary method higher because its terminal assumption is more conservative. A growth-adjusted valuation ratio is less useful here because earnings are held down by stock pay and restructuring.

## Dividend and buybacks

HubSpot pays no dividend. It spent $973.7 million on buybacks in nine months, about 9% of the market value, and approved a new program of up to $1.0 billion in August. Buybacks at a falling price do shrink the share count, but they mainly offset the shares paid to staff: $371.3 million of stock pay in nine months.

## Reasons to own HubSpot

- Free cash flow is real and growing: $188.0 million in the third quarter, up 28%, with a margin of 19.8%. About $720 million is guided for 2026.
- The balance sheet has no debt and $1.31 billion of cash and investments, 12% of the market value.
- Margins are rising while growth slows: GAAP operating margin of 7.6% against 1.4% a year ago, and stock pay down more than 3 points of revenue.
- The customer base is large and still growing at 12%, with 311,704 customers and deferred revenue of $1.04 billion.
- The business is cheap on its own free cash flow: 14.7 times 2026 free cash flow, with a yield of 6.8%.
- A founder who built the company to a market value of over $30 billion in 2021 is back with a mandate to reimagine product, sales and culture.

## What could go wrong?

- AI agents could reduce the number of seats customers need. Net revenue retention is 101%, net customer additions fell to 5,258 by a third-party count, and management cites customer budget caution.
- Growth is guided to 12% in the fourth quarter, below the 17% just reported. The guide was about 1.9% below the consensus revenue estimate.
- Stock pay is the biggest hole in the headline numbers: $502.6 million guided for 2026, 70% of free cash flow. If it does not keep falling, my Base falls to $151.
- Leadership is changing. The chief executive was replaced on November 12 in a move the filing describes as a termination without cause, and the chief financial officer retires on February 11, 2027, with a successor from Adobe starting on November 30. Guidance was reaffirmed, but the strategy is now a "refounding" that is not yet defined.
- Cost cutting has a price. The October reorganization removes about 660 roles and costs $65 million to $75 million, and a reorganization mid-transition can slow sales.
- Cash taxes are a gap. I assume they rise toward a normal rate, but I did not read the figure.
- Insider selling. Halligan reported ten planned share sales in 2026 totaling about $20.1 million by a third-party tally, which does not tell us what he will do now that he is back.

## Management execution

- The company has met its numbers on cash and margin: free cash flow grew 28% in the quarter, and full-year non-GAAP earnings guidance was raised by about 2.5%.
- Growth guidance has been the weak point. Two earnings reports in a row (May and August) were followed by a drop of about 20%, and the fourth quarter guide missed consensus.
- Capital allocation is aggressive on buybacks: $973.7 million in nine months at prices that fell from about $260 in March to under $200 in May, while the Base I compute for the business is only a little above the price.
- Stock pay is falling, by more than 3 points of revenue in a year, but a $502.6 million annual bill for a company with $3.7 billion of revenue is still the main reason the shares look cheaper on free cash flow than they are.
- The October reorganization and the November leadership change came within five weeks of each other, which suggests the board decided that the plan under the previous CEO was not working.

## What I would watch from here

### Green

- Stock pay falls below 11% of revenue in 2027 while growth stays near 11%.
- Net revenue retention stops falling and returns to 103% or more.
- The new CEO publishes a plan that keeps free cash flow margin above 21%.

### Yellow

- Fourth-quarter growth comes in at or below 12% and 2027 guidance starts below 11%.
- The refounding adds a new restructuring charge or a pause in selling.
- Cash taxes turn out higher than the 0.8% to 3.0% of revenue I assume.

### Red

- Net revenue retention falls below 100%.
- Stock pay stays above 13% of revenue as growth slows.
- The new CEO spends cash on a large acquisition or cuts the buyback while the share count stops falling.

## Final verdict: hold at $216.07

HubSpot is a good cash business with a slowing growth story and a new chief executive, at a price that is close to what I think it is worth. That is not a bargain and not a warning. At 48.6 times owner free cash flow it is fully priced, and at 14.7 times company free cash flow it looks cheap, and the stock pay line decides which is true.

The refounding will be judged by whether stock pay falls faster than growth does.

**Verdict: HOLD at $216.07, watchlist for new money. Weighted Base $233.86, 8.2% above the price, cross-check $250.16, Confidence C (13.75/25). Required margin of safety 47.5%, maximum buy price $122.78 (range $107 to $150).**

A holder has no strong reason to sell, because the Base is above the price and the Bear is a 53.6% loss, not a total loss. A new buyer under my rules waits for $122.78 or lower, or for evidence that stock pay is falling and retention is recovering.
