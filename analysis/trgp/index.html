In 2025 Targa Resources booked $529.7 million of income tax expense and paid $13.1 million of it in cash. The release does not split the difference, but it most likely reflects accelerated tax depreciation on a company that is spending billions on new plants and pipelines, and it flatters free cash flow for as long as the building continues. In 2026 Targa expects a tax charge of about $720 million, net growth capex of about $5.0 billion and EBITDA of at least $6.0 billion, and in the first nine months adjusted free cash flow of $466.8 million covered only part of the roughly $750 million of dividends (my estimate) and $187 million of buybacks.

The company is doing what it says. Permian gas inlet volumes were 7.5 billion cubic feet a day in the third quarter, up 14% from a year earlier, and management has raised its 2026 EBITDA outlook since February, when it guided $5.4 billion to $5.6 billion, to at least $6.0 billion now. The third quarter was still weaker than the headline: adjusted EBITDA of $1,504.3 million fell 6.2% from the second quarter, because marketing margins that added about $250 million in the first half faded. At $278.30 the stock is 12.1% below its October 30 high of $316.60 and still 12.4 times forward EBITDA.

My Base value is $232.94, which puts the price 19.5% above it. The Bear value of $138.20 is 50.3% below the price and the Bull value of $289.23 is -3.9% below it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is $223.33. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of $133.94, or $117.51 to $144.90 depending on the method. The verdict is Hold, and I would not add at this price.

What Targa is

Targa gathers and processes natural gas, mostly in the Permian Basin of West Texas and New Mexico, and moves, fractionates and exports the NGLs that come out of it. Gathering and Processing (G&P) earned an adjusted operating margin of $1,069.9 million in the third quarter, up 22% from a year earlier, and Logistics and Transportation (L&T) earned $888.1 million, up 10%. More than 90% of estimated 2026 adjusted operating margin is fee-based, and management has said a 30% move in commodity prices would change 2026 EBITDA by less than 2%.

Its edge is integration. Targa takes gas at the wellhead, processes it and moves the liquids through its own pipelines and fractionators to Mont Belvieu and its export terminal, which is why year-to-date Permian volumes are up 13% against roughly 7% for the basin. In August it signed two 20-year integrated fee-based agreements with ExxonMobil, which management says came out of a competitive process and a hard negotiation on price. Three new Delaware Basin plants are being built to serve them from the second quarter of 2028.

Cash, debt and shares

At September 30, 2026, Targa had consolidated debt of $19.8 billion (carrying value): $17.9 billion of senior unsecured notes, $860 million of commercial paper, $451 million drawn on the securitization facility and $796 million of finance lease obligations. Cash was $143 million, so net debt is $19,657 million. Management puts consolidated leverage at 3.4 times, inside its 3 to 4 times target, and liquidity at about $2.9 billion. Net debt was about $17.3 billion at the end of 2025, so it rose by about $2.4 billion in nine months while Targa paid dividends and bought back stock.

Reported debt already includes finance leases. Operating leases are left out because their rent is deducted in EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA is stated after the portion belonging to minority partners, so I make no further deduction for them. The release does not mention preferred stock and I assume none. Shares outstanding were about 214.5 million. Third-quarter earnings of $658.2 million at $3.04 a share imply about 216.5 million diluted shares, which I use, giving a market value of $60.25 billion and an enterprise value of $79.91 billion. Targa bought back $52 million of stock in the third quarter at $287.64 and has $1,187 million of authorization left.

The primary Base value is $252.00. The multiple needs a reason. Setting enterprise value equal to after-tax operating profit times one minus growth divided by the return on new capital, all over the 7.5% discount rate minus growth, 11.5x implies growth of 4.9% a year forever, with an 11% return on new capital. The Bear multiple of 9.0x implies 6.2% (at an 8% return) and the Bull multiple of 13.0x implies 4.9% (at 13%). These are not perpetual growth rates I would defend. They are acceptable only because Targa is in a decade of Permian volume growth, which management describes as years of double-digit percentage growth that usually tails off in the out years, and because most of its profit is fee-based. On a steady-state test the multiple is generous, and that is why the second method is lower.

My Base EBITDA path is $6,502 million in 2027, $7,152 million in 2028, $7,652 million in 2029, $8,035 million in 2030 and $8,356 million in 2031, a 8.3% annual growth rate from 2026 to 2029. Growth capex is sized at about seven times each year's EBITDA increase, spent a year ahead (my estimate of the build multiple), which gives $4.6 billion in 2027, falling to $1.8 billion in 2031. Management says 2027 capex will stay elevated and that free cash flow should grow meaningfully in 2028 as the larger projects start up.

Dividend

The third-quarter dividend was $1.25 a share, up 25% from a year earlier, and management expects to recommend $1.50 a quarter, or $6.00 a year, from the first quarter of 2027, payable in May. At $5.00 a year the yield is 1.8%, and at $6.00 it is 2.2%. The 2026 dividend costs about $1.07 billion a year, which free cash flow does not cover today: nine-month adjusted free cash flow was $466.8 million. The dividend is paid from operating cash flow with the growth spending financed by debt, and it is covered after growth capex only from 2028 on my Base path. Management has a stated payout target of 40% to 50% of free cash flow through dividends and buybacks over several years. The yield is small and I give it no weight in the valuation. What matters is that the dividend is growing 20% to 25% a year while net debt is also rising.

Reasons to own TRGP

  • Volumes are growing faster than the basin. Permian inlet volumes are up 14% from a year ago, and year-to-date growth of 13% compares with about 7% for the basin.
  • The earnings are mostly fee-based. More than 90% of 2026 adjusted operating margin is fee-based, and a 30% commodity price move changes EBITDA by less than 2%.
  • The pipeline of projects is visible. The Speedway NGL pipeline, Yeti and Yeti II plants, a 19 million barrels a month LPG export capacity target and Train 14 all have dates, and management has pulled Yeti earlier.
  • Long-term contracts add to the base. The two 20-year ExxonMobil integrated agreements support three Delaware plants from the second quarter of 2028.
  • Leverage is moving the right way on my Base path. It falls from 3.4 times to about 2.5 times by the end of 2029.

What could go wrong?

  • Growth costs more than it returns. Growth capex of $5.0 billion in 2026 raised from $4.5 billion, and 2027 spending will stay elevated. A 15% overrun cuts my Base value by about $3.45.
  • Debt keeps rising. Net debt is up about $2.4 billion in nine months to $19.7 billion, and interest of about $940 million in 2026 takes 16% of EBITDA.
  • Marketing margins fade. About $250 million of first-half upside will not repeat, and removing it from my 2026 base lowers the Base value to $216.91.
  • Permian growth slows. Management says out-year growth usually tails off. If producers cut activity, plants fill more slowly, and my Bear case is worth $138.20.
  • The cash tax shield ends. Cash taxes rise toward the full rate as spending slows, and a market that values cash flow after tax will see lower free cash flow growth than EBITDA growth.

Management execution

Management has raised 2026 EBITDA guidance from the February range of $5.4 billion to $5.6 billion to at least $6.0 billion, while pulling projects forward and signing the ExxonMobil agreements. It also raised 2026 growth capex from $4.5 billion to $5.0 billion, and the guidance does not say what the extra $0.5 billion earns. Capital allocation is aggressive: it bought back $642 million of stock in 2025 at an average of $170.45 and $187 million in nine months of 2026 at about $261, and it is raising the dividend 20% to 25% a year while borrowing, which is good for shareholders only if growth projects earn their cost of capital. The long run of beats supports the grade, and I stay careful with a first quarter that was partly marketing windfall. Management credibility scores 15/20.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • 2027 EBITDA guidance at or above $6.5 billion
  • Permian inlet volumes growing 10% or more year over year
  • Net growth capex for 2027 at or below about $4.6 billion with projects on schedule
  • Consolidated leverage at or below 3.5 times
  • Adjusted free cash flow turning positive after dividends in 2028

Yellow

  • 2027 EBITDA guidance between $6.2 billion and $6.5 billion
  • Volume growth between 5% and 10%
  • Growth capex raised again without new EBITDA targets
  • Leverage between 3.5 and 3.9 times

Red

  • 2027 EBITDA guidance below $6.2 billion
  • Volume growth below 5%
  • Leverage above 4.0 times or the dividend plan cut back
  • Project start-ups such as Speedway or the Yeti plants delayed by more than two quarters

Today's readings are green on volumes (14%), yellow on leverage (3.4 times is inside the range but rising) and yellow on free cash flow, which is negative after dividends. The next report is the fourth-quarter release, expected on February 18, 2027, and it should carry 2027 guidance. It should show three things: 2027 EBITDA guidance against my $6.5 billion, a 2027 capex figure and the leverage ratio at year-end.

The case improves if the price falls toward $144.90, or if 2027 guidance comes in well above $6.8 billion with capex no higher than $5.0 billion.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. 2027 EBITDA guidance falls below $6.2 billion, which would show that the 2026 marketing windfall is leaving faster than the new plants are arriving.
  2. Growth capex keeps rising while EBITDA targets stay flat, which would push the build multiple above my 7 times and cut the value of growth.
  3. Leverage rises above 4 times, which would force a choice between the dividend plan and further growth.
