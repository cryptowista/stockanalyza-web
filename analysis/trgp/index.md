## What Targa is

Targa gathers and processes natural gas, mostly in the Permian Basin of West Texas and New Mexico, and moves, fractionates and exports the NGLs that come out of it. Gathering and Processing (G&P) earned an adjusted operating margin of $1,069.9 million in the third quarter, up 22% from a year earlier, and Logistics and Transportation (L&T) earned $888.1 million, up 10%. More than 90% of estimated 2026 adjusted operating margin is fee-based, and management has said a 30% move in commodity prices would change 2026 EBITDA by less than 2%.

Its edge is integration. Targa takes gas at the wellhead, processes it and moves the liquids through its own pipelines and fractionators to Mont Belvieu and its export terminal, which is why year-to-date Permian volumes are up 13% against roughly 7% for the basin. In August it signed two 20-year integrated fee-based agreements with ExxonMobil, which management says came out of a competitive process and a hard negotiation on price. Three new Delaware Basin plants are being built to serve them from the second quarter of 2028.

## Primary valuation: forward EBITDA

Enterprise value (EV) is market value plus debt minus cash. I apply a multiple to forward EBITDA, which is 10% of 2026 and 90% of 2027, and subtract net debt of $19,657 million. Management gave no 2027 EBITDA figure and says formal guidance comes in February, so the 2027 numbers are my estimates. The primary Base value is **$252.00**. The multiple needs a reason. Setting enterprise value equal to after-tax operating profit times one minus growth divided by the return on new capital, all over the 7.5% discount rate minus growth, 11.5x implies growth of 4.9% a year forever, with an 11% return on new capital. The Bear multiple of 9.0x implies 6.2% (at an 8% return) and the Bull multiple of 13.0x implies 4.9% (at 13%). These are not perpetual growth rates I would defend. They are acceptable only because Targa is in a decade of Permian volume growth, which management describes as years of double-digit percentage growth that usually tails off in the out years, and because most of its profit is fee-based. On a steady-state test the multiple is generous, and that is why the second method is lower.

My Base EBITDA path is $6,502 million in 2027, $7,152 million in 2028, $7,652 million in 2029, $8,035 million in 2030 and $8,356 million in 2031, a 8.3% annual growth rate from 2026 to 2029. Growth capex is sized at about seven times each year's EBITDA increase, spent a year ahead (my estimate of the build multiple), which gives $4.6 billion in 2027, falling to $1.8 billion in 2031. Management says 2027 capex will stay elevated and that free cash flow should grow meaningfully in 2028 as the larger projects start up.

## Dividend

The third-quarter dividend was $1.25 a share, up 25% from a year earlier, and management expects to recommend $1.50 a quarter, or $6.00 a year, from the first quarter of 2027, payable in May. At $5.00 a year the yield is 1.8%, and at $6.00 it is 2.2%. The 2026 dividend costs about $1.07 billion a year, which free cash flow does not cover today: nine-month adjusted free cash flow was $466.8 million. The dividend is paid from operating cash flow with the growth spending financed by debt, and it is covered after growth capex only from 2028 on my Base path. Management has a stated payout target of 40% to 50% of free cash flow through dividends and buybacks over several years. The yield is small and I give it no weight in the valuation. What matters is that the dividend is growing 20% to 25% a year while net debt is also rising.

## Reasons to own TRGP

- **Volumes are growing faster than the basin.** Permian inlet volumes are up 14% from a year ago, and year-to-date growth of 13% compares with about 7% for the basin.
- **The earnings are mostly fee-based.** More than 90% of 2026 adjusted operating margin is fee-based, and a 30% commodity price move changes EBITDA by less than 2%.
- **The pipeline of projects is visible.** The Speedway NGL pipeline, Yeti and Yeti II plants, a 19 million barrels a month LPG export capacity target and Train 14 all have dates, and management has pulled Yeti earlier.
- **Long-term contracts add to the base.** The two 20-year ExxonMobil integrated agreements support three Delaware plants from the second quarter of 2028.
- **Leverage is moving the right way on my Base path.** It falls from 3.4 times to about 2.5 times by the end of 2029.

## What could go wrong?

- **Growth costs more than it returns.** Growth capex of $5.0 billion in 2026 raised from $4.5 billion, and 2027 spending will stay elevated. A 15% overrun cuts my Base value by about $3.45.
- **Debt keeps rising.** Net debt is up about $2.4 billion in nine months to $19.7 billion, and interest of about $940 million in 2026 takes 16% of EBITDA.
- **Marketing margins fade.** About $250 million of first-half upside will not repeat, and removing it from my 2026 base lowers the Base value to $216.91.
- **Permian growth slows.** Management says out-year growth usually tails off. If producers cut activity, plants fill more slowly, and my Bear case is worth $138.20.
- **The cash tax shield ends.** Cash taxes rise toward the full rate as spending slows, and a market that values cash flow after tax will see lower free cash flow growth than EBITDA growth.

## Management execution

Management has raised 2026 EBITDA guidance from the February range of $5.4 billion to $5.6 billion to at least $6.0 billion, while pulling projects forward and signing the ExxonMobil agreements. It also raised 2026 growth capex from $4.5 billion to $5.0 billion, and the guidance does not say what the extra $0.5 billion earns. Capital allocation is aggressive: it bought back $642 million of stock in 2025 at an average of $170.45 and $187 million in nine months of 2026 at about $261, and it is raising the dividend 20% to 25% a year while borrowing, which is good for shareholders only if growth projects earn their cost of capital. The long run of beats supports the grade, and I stay careful with a first quarter that was partly marketing windfall. Management credibility scores 15/20.
