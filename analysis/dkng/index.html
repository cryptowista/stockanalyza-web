DraftKings cut its profit guide by $575 million to buy a product that earns nothing this year

DKNG, Nasdaq. Published November 11, 2026. Price as of the November 10, 2026 regular session close. All figures are in US dollars. Latest reported quarter: July to September 2026, released on November 5, 2026.

Hold at $19.36

In February DraftKings guided to $700 million to $900 million of adjusted EBITDA for 2026. On November 5 it guided to $175 million to $275 million. The sportsbook and casino business it calls "core" barely moved, at $875 million to $925 million. The difference is DraftKings Predictions, a new exchange product that will absorb $650 million to $700 million this year and, by the company's own forecast, bring in no material revenue in 2026.

The stock fell 21% the next day to $18.51 and closed at $19.36 on November 10, down 46% this year. My Base value is $25.98 a share, the Bear value $6.55 and the Bull value $42.44. That is a cheap price for the Base and an expensive one for the Bear, and the gap between the two is the story. The required margin of safety is 42.5%, so the buy price is $14.94. The verdict is Hold, with new money waiting.

Valuation route: US online sports betting and casino operator in an investment year, valued with a two-stage cash flow model of owner free cash flow (adjusted EBITDA minus stock pay, capital spending, lobbying and legal costs, interest and cash taxes), cross-checked with cash flow to 2029 plus an exit at 10 times 2030 adjusted EBITDA after stock pay. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What DraftKings is

DraftKings takes online sports bets in 27 US states, runs online casinos where states allow them, and sells daily fantasy, lottery and, since December 2025, event contracts through DraftKings Predictions. It had 4.1 million monthly paying customers in the third quarter, up 13%, each worth $109 of revenue a month on average.

Predictions is a different legal animal. It runs under federal futures rules (DraftKings bought the Railbird exchange in October 2025 and became a futures commission merchant in August 2026), so it can reach customers in states where sports betting is still illegal. Kalshi and Polymarket built that market first. DraftKings is now buying its way in: more than 1.6 million customers have tried Predictions this year, and October gross profit ran at $350 million a year with an 18% promotion rate, by the company's count. Management expects $650 million to $750 million of Predictions revenue in 2027, after an investment of $250 million to $350 million.

Its CEO put the conflict plainly at a September conference: if prediction markets "got shut down by the Supreme Court tomorrow, our share price would pop." DraftKings is spending heavily on a product that its own shareholders might prefer did not exist.

Cash taxes are almost nil. DraftKings paid $4.5 million in nine months because it carries $4.3 billion of federal tax losses, $3.7 billion of which never expire. I model them explicitly: federal tax only on the 20% of profit that losses cannot shelter, plus state tax, until the pool runs out around 2031, then a full 24% rate. Assuming full tax from day one would cost only $0.66 a share in the Base, because most of the shelter is used late.

Primary valuation: two-stage cash flow model after stock pay

DraftKings scores 7 out of 12 on my score for growth companies: strong growth and runway, weak returns on capital and no history of steady free cash flow per share. Its profits are still being built, which a single multiple of today's numbers cannot capture, so the main method is a two-stage DCF, a discounted cash flow model. Stage one runs to 2031 with explicit revenue, adjusted EBITDA margin and stock pay. Stage two fades growth from 7% to 4% by 2036 at a constant margin, then applies 3% terminal growth. The discount rate is 11%, a point above what I use for steadier software, for a regulated business with volatile results and a controlling shareholder. Only fourth quarter 2026 cash flow counts for this year, because the September balance sheet already holds the first nine months.

Secondary valuation: cash to 2029 plus an exit multiple

The second method takes the owner cash flow through 2029 and adds a sale at the end of 2029 at 10 times 2030 adjusted EBITDA after stock pay, all discounted at 11%. Ten times EBITDA after stock pay is about 16 times owner free cash flow once taxes are paid in full: a 6.2% cash yield that, with growth of about 5% a year, returns the 11% I require. The Bear uses 8 times and the Bull also uses 10 times, so the good news in the Bull sits in revenue and margin, not in the multiple as well.

How the buy price is set

The required margin of safety follows the confidence grade, and grade C starts at 42.5%. I add nothing: the Predictions risk, the tax risk and the competition are all in the Bear case, stock pay and lobbying costs are deducted in full, and the controlling shareholder is in the quality and confidence scores. $25.98 times 0.575 is $14.94. On the DCF alone it would be $15.05, on the exit multiple alone $14.77.

A buy at $19.36 would need a Base value of $33.67, which would take a 28% margin by 2031 or a discount rate below 9%. At $14.94 the enterprise value would be $8.77 billion, 8.9 times 2028 Base adjusted EBITDA after stock pay.

Dividend and capital return

There is no dividend. Capital goes back through buybacks, which stopped after the first half of 2026 while cash went into Predictions and the convertible notes. Neither method credits buybacks separately, because stock pay is already deducted in full.

Reasons to own DraftKings

  • The price fits a weaker business than the Base. It needs a 2031 margin of only 20.4%.
  • The core business still grows. Guidance puts core adjusted EBITDA at $875 million to $925 million in 2026 and $1.15 billion to $1.25 billion in 2027, on core revenue up 4% to 9%.
  • Customers are coming. Monthly paying customers rose 13% to 4.1 million and betting volume rose 22%, with more than 1.6 million customers trying Predictions this year.
  • Predictions could end the state-by-state map. It reaches customers where sports betting is not legal, and DraftKings expects its gross margin to be higher than the sportsbook's.
  • $4.3 billion of federal tax losses. They shelter most profit for about five years.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: Predictions never pays back. DraftKings expects payback within three years on marketing spend. Kalshi, Polymarket, Robinhood and FanDuel are fighting for the same customer, and FanDuel's US adjusted EBITDA fell to $3 million last quarter. Each 3 points of margin is worth about $5.60 a share of my Base.
  • Courts and states. Class actions in Massachusetts federal court claim Predictions is illegal sports betting in up to fifteen states, and the Seminole Tribe sued in Florida on October 9. A federal ban would hurt the investment but, by the CEO's own account, might help the share price.
  • Gaming taxes. Higher taxes added $64.4 million to costs in the third quarter alone. Guidance assumes today's rates.
  • Sport outcomes. A run of favorites winning can move a quarter's EBITDA by tens of millions; it hit the second quarter by about $80 million of revenue.
  • Governance. One shareholder controls about 89% of the votes, and the board can keep spending on Predictions whatever minority holders think.
  • Guidance. Two years running, the first profit guide was too high.

Management execution

DraftKings started 2025 with adjusted EBITDA guidance of $900 million to $1 billion, cut it in May to $800 million to $900 million and delivered $620 million. It started 2026 at $700 million to $900 million and now guides $175 million to $275 million. Most of the 2026 cut is a choice to spend on Predictions, not a miss in the core, and the 2027 core guide of $1.15 billion to $1.25 billion is above what analysts expected for the whole company before the report. The pattern still matters: profit forecasts from this team have been too high, and buybacks were done at $28 to $36 a share. I score management credibility at 8 out of 20.

The fourth quarter report, expected in February 2027, is the first test: the guide implies about $259 million of adjusted EBITDA after Predictions spending, the NFL season decides the hold, and management will either confirm or trim the 2027 numbers. A trim below $800 million would move me toward the Bear; Predictions revenue arriving on schedule with lower promotions would lift the confidence grade and could put the stock in the buy zone without the price moving.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Predictions keeps needing heavy promotions and never earns back its marketing.
  2. State taxes and exchange competition hold the margin near 15%.
  3. A court or regulator closes Predictions after the money is spent.

The weakest point is the margin after 2027. Both methods depend on it, and the evidence for it is a guide from a team that has overshot twice. My conservative choices are a 2027 profit below the guide midpoint, a 24% margin against the company's 30% target and deducting lobbying costs; the generous one is letting Predictions turn profitable from 2028. The strongest bull argument is that the price fits a 20% margin. The strongest bear argument is that a 20% margin may be optimistic if exchanges, not sportsbooks, set the price of a bet.

Final verdict: hold at $19.36

DraftKings is spending about $700 million this year to compete with exchanges that never needed a state license. The market prices that spending as if it buys very little. My Base says it buys more than that, but not with enough certainty to pay $19.36 for it.

Verdict: HOLD at $19.36, watchlist for new money. Base $25.98, 34.2% above the price, Bear $6.55, Bull $42.44, Confidence C (13.75/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $14.94, range $14.77 to $15.05 under the two methods.

The buy zone can come to the stock in two ways: the price falls to $14.94, or the 2027 numbers prove the Base and the required margin shrinks. The second would be the better one to wait for.
