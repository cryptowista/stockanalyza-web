Siemens Energy more than tripled its quarterly profit before special items to €1,623 million and booked record orders of €17.9 billion. Its shares fell 6.2% on October 30 to €125.90, which is 34% below the April high of €191.66. Both can be true, because the full-year results on November 11 include a first view of next year, and the market is waiting for it.

At €125.90 on October 30, 2026, Siemens Energy trades 8.1% above my weighted Base of €116.43. The Bear value of €65.42 is 48.0% below the price and the Bull value of €165.94 is 31.8% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. A required margin of safety of 42.5% gives a buy range of €65.16 to €68.74. The verdict is Hold, and no new money at this price.

Valuation route: an industrial group with gas turbines, grid equipment, industrial technology and wind, valued on five-year earnings with an exit multiple and cross-checked by a sum of the parts of its four segments. Net cash is not included. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What Siemens Energy is

Siemens Energy has four segments. Gas Services (turbines and service) earned a 17.3% margin before special items in the quarter. Grid Technologies (transformers and grid connections) earned 19.9%. Transformation of Industry (compressors, electrolysers and related equipment) earned 14.3%. Siemens Gamesa, the wind business, earned 2.7%, its first positive quarter since fiscal 2022.

The fiscal year ends September 30. Management is reviewing options for its portfolio, including the future of Transformation of Industry, and has said no decisions have been made and that it will not act quickly.

Management said data centers were about 20% of gas turbine order capacity, slightly lower than the previous quarter, and that the Middle East was about 30% of capacity orders. In management's words, data centers are important but less dominant than some reports suggest.

## What the cash flow is made of

- **Free cash flow is flattered by customer payments.** The €7.2 billion nine-month figure is before tax and includes down payments and reservation fees on turbines, which are cash received before the work is done. The full-year target is about €8 billion, leaving about €0.8 billion for the fourth quarter, which management says includes significant capital spending.
- **Net income and cash flow differ.** Nine-month net income was €2.8 billion against free cash flow before tax of €7.2 billion; the gap is partly advances and partly tax.
- **Wind still absorbs cash.** Management expects Gamesa to break even on profit in 2026 and on cash in 2028, and says new offshore orders are needed for 2029 and beyond.
- **Special items are not zero.** They were -€59 million in the quarter and +€458 million a year earlier, mostly from the demerger of the Indian energy business.

## Secondary valuation: sum of the parts

I annualize each segment's third-quarter profit before special items, grow it into fiscal 2027 (Gas Services +12%, Grid +20%, Transformation of Industry +7%), and apply 14, 15 and 11 times. Gamesa is worth 0.15 times its revenue because its profit is near zero. I subtract about €0.18 billion of annual corporate cost at 14 times. That gives an enterprise value of about €102 billion, or **€119.54** a share with no adjustment for net cash. The Bear uses 10, 11 and 8 times on 5% lower profit, and the Bull 17, 19 and 13 times on 10% higher profit.

## Growth diagnostic

Growth is structural in grid equipment and gas turbines, where the backlog is booked and capacity is limited, and cyclical in the pace of utility and data center spending. Nine-month revenue growth was 13.4% and the quarter 18.5%. Margins moved from 5.1% to 14.2% in a year, which is a recovery from a low base and not a run-rate I would extrapolate. I classify the growth as strong and the profit as recovering.

## Valuation triangulation

- Primary (five-year earnings value): €113.33, weight 50%.
- Secondary (sum of the parts): €119.54, weight 50%.
- Divergence: 5% of the midpoint, below the 20% warning level.
- Weighted Base: €116.43.

The methods agree because both assume that profit before special items stays above 11% on a larger revenue base. Both exclude net cash. The Bear case is where they differ most, since the earnings method applies a low multiple to flat profit and the sum of the parts still pays for the backlog.

## Dividend and buybacks

The company proposed €0.70 for fiscal 2025 and plans to return up to €10 billion by 2028, including up to €6 billion of buybacks. It has spent €3.6 billion on buybacks at an average of about €153.52, 22% above today's price, and the current tranche has averaged €138.04. The average price paid is above today's price, which is a paper loss on the program so far.

## Reasons to own Siemens Energy

- Gas Services orders rose 62% and Grid Technologies 28% in the quarter, and the €162 billion backlog is made of firm orders.
- Group margin before special items reached 14.2% in the quarter and 12.6% over nine months, above guidance of 10% to 12%.
- Wind earned a positive quarter for the first time since fiscal 2022 and management expects break-even for the year.
- A €6 billion buyback and a dividend are funded by cash flow, and management has ruled out large acquisitions.
- Early 2027 signs are described by management as positive, with more detail promised in November.

## What could go wrong?

- Cycle. Gas turbine and grid orders depend on utility and data center spending that has risen quickly.
- Wind. Gamesa is not sustainably profitable, offshore orders are uneven, and onshore recovery depends on German auctions.
- Competition. Chinese competitors are pressuring prices, and management mentioned antitrust questions over European consolidation.
- Quality of cash flow. Advances flatter free cash flow, and fourth-quarter spending is heavy.
- Portfolio. The review of Transformation of Industry creates uncertainty, and hydrogen demand has not developed as expected.
- Valuation. About 27 times fiscal 2026 net income leaves little room for a miss.

## Management execution

- Guidance was raised after the first half of fiscal 2026 and the nine-month margin of 12.6% is above the 10% to 12% range.
- Wind moved from losses to a positive quarter after years of problems, though the nine-month result is still a small double-digit loss in millions.
- Capital returns began in March 2026 with a €2.0 billion buyback, but the average price paid so far is above the current price.
- Management has not said what comes after fiscal 2026 and has promised the medium-term outlook on November 11.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Orders for gas turbines and transformers slow before the margin gain is locked in.
2. Wind needs more capital than the group earns in grid and gas.
3. The multiple falls to 14 times as the cycle matures.

## Final verdict: hold at €125.90, do not add

The guidance for the year that ended on September 30 is net income of about €4 billion. The number that matters on November 11 is the one for the year that follows.

**Verdict: HOLD at €125.90, do not add. Weighted Base €116.43, 7.5% below the price, cross-check €119.54, Confidence C (14/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, buy range €65.16 to €68.74, conservative buy limit €65.16.**

For an existing holder, the original cost is irrelevant. The price is within the range of my Base and Bull values and the business is in a strong part of its cycle, so I would not sell on valuation alone. I would not add before the annual results and the net cash figure. New money waits for a price at or below €68.74, or for evidence that net cash and fiscal 2027 guidance justify a higher range.
