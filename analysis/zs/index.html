Zscaler is one of the leaders in Zero Trust security: companies route their users' and applications' traffic through its cloud instead of through firewalls. The business is excellent, with 25% growth, 80% gross margins and huge contracted revenue. But in fiscal 2026 it paid its employees $822 million in stock, more than the $779 million of free cash flow it reported. For shareholders, that changes the math.

At $214.64 on September 24, 2026, the shares trade 30.6% above my triangulated Base value of $164.30, a downside of 23.5% to Base. Even the Bear value of $148.40 is below the price, and only the Bull value of $243.49 is above it. The required margin of safety of 40% puts the maximum buy price at $98.58. For existing holders this is a hold. New money should not add at this price.

Route: SF-07 SaaS and technology growth, cloud-native cybersecurity subscription platform. Economic classification: Quality Growth (8/12, capped because of stock compensation and dilution). Primary method: forward EV to fiscal 2028 revenue. Secondary methods: free cash flow after stock compensation, and a growth-normalized historical reference. Zscaler's fiscal year ends in July. What Zscaler is

Zscaler runs a global cloud that inspects and secures traffic between users, devices, applications and the internet. Customers subscribe, so revenue is recurring and highly visible. The platform is expanding from user security into workloads, branches, data security, AI security and security operations. The acquisition of Red Canary added $141 million of annual recurring revenue in fiscal 2026; without it, ARR growth was about 20% rather than the reported 25%.

Fiscal 2026 free cash flow was $779.1 million, a 23% margin. Stock compensation was $821.9 million, about 24.5% of revenue. Free cash flow after stock compensation was therefore about -$43 million, or roughly -1% of revenue. That does not mean the business lacks value. It means investors cannot count the full headline free cash flow while ignoring the equity transferred to employees, which is why the framework's hard metric for software companies is free cash flow after stock compensation.

Cash, debt and shares

Cash and short-term investments were $3.474 billion against $1.725 billion of convertible notes due 2028, which have capped calls to limit dilution, so net cash is about $1.75 billion. Shares outstanding were 163.1 million on August 27, 2026, but fiscal 2027 guidance assumes about 173 million fully diluted shares, which the valuation uses. At $214.64, market value is about $35.0 billion and enterprise value about $33.2 billion.

After $1.75 billion of net cash and 173 million shares, the primary Base value is $187.10. The Base assumes growth continues near the guided 17%, not an acceleration. The 6.7x multiple is well below Zscaler's historical premium because growth is slowing and stock compensation is high.

Secondary valuation: free cash flow after stock compensation

Because today's free cash flow after stock compensation is near zero, this method values a more mature year. Revenue grows 15% a year to about $5.97 billion in fiscal 2030. The headline free cash flow margin rises to 27% and stock compensation falls to 13% of revenue, about half today's level, leaving a hard free cash flow margin of 14%, or about $835 million. At 30x, plus net cash, and divided by 173 million shares, that gives about $155 per share in fiscal 2030, or $116.42 today discounted at 10% a year. These are estimates, and fairly generous ones: they assume stock compensation halves as a share of revenue.

Historical cross-check

Zscaler trades at about 10.6x trailing EV to sales and about 41x headline price to free cash flow, against an estimated five-year average near 14.9x sales. That average came with much faster growth and is not automatically fair today. A growth-normalized historical reference gives about $200, with low weight.

The primary and secondary methods diverge by about 47%, far above the framework's 30% threshold. The flag is raised, confidence is lowered and the margin of safety rises. The divergence is the core finding: on revenue, Zscaler looks roughly fairly priced; on cash left for shareholders after stock pay, it looks expensive. The secondary and historical methods exist only for the Base case, so the Bear and Bull values come from the primary method alone.

What is the market already pricing in?

Enterprise value of about $33.2 billion is about 8.5x fiscal 2027 revenue guidance. The price requires high-teens ARR and revenue growth, about 80% gross margins, further operating leverage, a free cash flow margin of 23% or more, successful AI, data and security operations products and, above all, stock compensation that grows much more slowly than revenue. If growth falls toward the low teens without better owner earnings, the multiple is hard to defend.

How the buy price is set

Dividend

Zscaler pays no dividend.

Reasons to own ZS

  • Zero Trust leadership. A leading position with real switching costs for large enterprises.
  • Visibility. $3.771 billion of ARR and $7.365 billion of contracted future revenue.
  • Quality growth. Revenue and ARR both grew 25% in fiscal 2026, with about 80% gross margins.
  • A wider market. AI security, data security, security operations, workload and branch products expand what Zscaler can sell.
  • Net cash. About $3.5 billion of cash and short-term investments.

What could go wrong?

  • Stock compensation. Fiscal 2026 stock pay exceeded headline free cash flow. If it keeps growing with revenue, per-share value creation stalls. This is the main thesis breaker.
  • Slowing growth. Guidance points to about 17% in fiscal 2027, and part of recent ARR growth came from an acquisition.
  • Valuation. About 8.5x forward revenue requires premium execution.
  • Competition. Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Cloudflare, Microsoft and others compete for consolidating security budgets.
  • Execution. New products and AI must offset maturing seat-based growth; a new chief revenue officer starts on October 1, 2026.

Management execution

Revenue and ARR grew 25%, Q4 non-GAAP operating margin reached 24%, contracted revenue grew 27% and large customers grew 18%. The main caveat is persistently high stock compensation and reliance on non-GAAP profitability. Management credibility scores 16/20, good.

Adversarial review

The first draft had the right diagnosis, that stock compensation consumes all of the headline free cash flow, but its valuation did not follow through. Four corrections were made. Its primary values were rounded up: at its own inputs, the Base is $194.07, not $196, and the Bull $263.14, not $266, and several sensitivity cells were $1 to $2 too high. Its Base revenue of $4.75 billion implied 21% growth in fiscal 2028, an acceleration, while the text described slowing growth; it now assumes 16.5%, close to guidance. Its secondary value of $190 was labeled dilution-aware but had no stated inputs, and it was barely below the primary value, although the draft itself showed free cash flow after stock compensation near zero. It was rebuilt transparently at $116.42. Finally, the draft's twelve-month price-touch probabilities were dropped as not part of the framework. The Base moved from $195 to $164.30, the maximum buy price from $126.75 to $98.58 and the Bull from $265 to $243.49. The verdict remains hold, now with a clear instruction not to add.

The strongest counter-argument is that Zscaler combines about 20% organic ARR growth, 80% gross margins, a 23% free cash flow margin and strong contracted revenue, and that the market has long paid premium multiples for exactly that. The answer is that shareholders own the business after the stock paid to employees, and on that measure the price already assumes stock compensation falls sharply.

Final verdict: hold at $214.64, do not add

Zscaler is a high-quality recurring revenue security platform, but stock compensation currently consumes all of its headline free cash flow, and the price assumes that changes.

Verdict: HOLD at $214.64, do not add. Triangulated Base value $164.30, downside 23.5%, Confidence B (18/25). Required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $98.58.

For an existing holder, business quality supports holding, though a position well above Base value deserves a sizing review. New money waits for $98.58 or below, or for clear evidence that stock compensation is falling relative to free cash flow.

The main thesis breaker is failure to maintain high-teens recurring growth while stock compensation stays near or above headline free cash flow.
