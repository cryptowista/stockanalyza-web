# Ares Capital earned $0.46 a share in the quarter and paid $0.48

ARCC, Nasdaq. Published November 22, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close ($18.43). The fiscal year is the calendar year, so "FY26" is 2026. Ares Capital is a business development company, or BDC: a listed lender that must pay out nearly all of its taxable income. NII is net investment income, the interest and fees it earns after its own costs and fees to its manager. NAV is net asset value per share, the reported value of its loans and other assets less debt.

Ares Capital reported net investment income of $0.46 a share for the third quarter and declared a dividend of $0.48. The dividend yields 10.4% at $18.43, the shares trade at 0.95 times a net asset value of $19.31, and my model puts the Base value at $17.59, so the price is 4.7% above it. The gap between $0.46 and $0.48 is small, which is the point: the stock is priced for it to stay small.

Three things moved in the quarter. NII per share fell from $0.48 a year earlier to $0.46, NAV per share fell from $19.94 at the end of 2025 to $19.31, and non-accrual loans, which have stopped paying interest, rose to 2.4% of the portfolio at cost from 1.8%. On the other side, the company refinanced debt more cheaply, launched a commercial paper program and agreed to buy MidCap Financial Investment Corporation. Weighted average shares were 720 million against 709 million a year earlier.

My Base value is **$17.59**, which is 4.7% below the price. The Bear value of $12.66 is 31.3% below the price and the Bull value of $20.86 is 13.2% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is $17.18. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of **$10.12**, or $10.18 to $10.02 depending on the method. That is about 0.52 times NAV, so the rule will almost never allow a purchase of a lender that holds first lien loans. It is a discipline for new money, not a forecast that the shares fall there. The verdict is Hold, do not add.

Valuation route: a credit-focused BDC, so I value it on adjusted NAV and sustainable NII after normal credit losses as the primary method, and on normalized earnings times a justified multiple as the second. ## What Ares Capital is

Ares Capital is the largest listed BDC. At September 30, 2026 it held $29.2 billion of investments at fair value in 636 portfolio companies. First lien senior secured loans are 58% of the portfolio, second lien 4%, senior subordinated loans 7% and preferred equity 8%. Subordinated certificates in its senior direct lending program are 4%. Two parts are equity-like and carry more risk: its stake in Ivy Hill Asset Management, which is 10% of the portfolio, and other equity at 9%. Floating-rate securities are 70% of fair value, so falling short-term rates lower its income.

The weighted average yield was 9.6% on total investments and 10.8% on debt investments at fair value. The company is externally managed by an Ares affiliate. In the quarter the income-based fee was $82 million, which is 20.0% of NII before that fee, and there was no capital gains incentive fee.

## Capital, debt and share count

Debt was $15.1 billion at carrying value, or $15.5 billion of principal, against stockholders' equity of $13.9 billion. Debt to equity is 1.11 times, or 1.06 times net of available cash, which is below the 1.12 times at year end. Total liquidity is about $7 billion, of which $6.9 billion is borrowing capacity. Interest and credit facility fees of $221 million a quarter are about 5.7% a year on quarter-end principal (my arithmetic), against a yield of 10.6% on debt investments.

In July the company repaid $1.0 billion of 2.150% unsecured notes and in September it issued $750 million of 6.250% notes due 2033, swapped to floating at SOFR plus 1.8525%. In the same month it refinanced a $709 million CLO at a spread of SOFR plus 1.51% instead of 1.86%. It also launched what it describes as the first commercial paper program in the BDC sector, and on November 20 it amended a credit facility with BNP Paribas to extend the maturity and lower the margin. The maturity schedule for all debt was not in the text I read, so that is an open data gap. Spillover income, the taxable income carried into the next year that can support the dividend, was not disclosed in the release either.

I use 722 million shares and a market value of $13.3 billion at the current price.

## The MidCap Financial deal

On October 26 Ares Capital agreed to buy MidCap Financial Investment Corporation, a BDC managed by Apollo, in a cash and stock deal worth about $2.6 billion including debt. MidCap holders receive $10.88 a share: about $1.21 in cash and about $9.67 in Ares Capital stock. The stock part is calculated at the greater of Ares Capital's NAV and its share price, and MidCap holders share half of any premium of the price over NAV, up to 104.5% of NAV. Ares Capital may issue up to about 41 million shares, or 5.7% of the 722 million now outstanding, though the number is expected to be lower. The close is expected in the first half of 2027 and needs shareholder and regulatory approvals. Management says the deal should add to core earnings per share, and the combined portfolio would be over $31 billion.

MidCap reported non-accruals of 4.6% of cost in its June report, against 2.4% at Ares Capital, according to a summary of the filings, and it will pay a special dividend of undistributed income before closing. Paying $10.88 for a business that carried a NAV of about $12.86 in its preliminary September figure is a discount, which helps Ares Capital's NAV if its own shares are valued at NAV. I do not put the deal into the numbers, because the marks, the integration costs and the share price at closing are unknown.

## Primary valuation: NAV and sustainable income

A BDC pays out almost all of its income, so its value is the income it pays plus the NAV that remains. I model 10 years. FY27 NII is $1.86 a share, a yield of 9.6% on NAV, and it is paid out each year. Credit losses reduce NAV by $0.20 a share a year, or about 0.5% of the $40 of assets behind each share. That is well below the $0.63 fall in NAV over the last nine months, which I treat as partly mark-to-market and recoverable, so this input is not conservative: the Bear case uses $0.60. NII falls with NAV, so it declines slowly. I discount at 10.0%, my estimate for a lender with leverage and credit risk, and I assume the shares trade at NAV at the end of year 10.

The model gives **$17.70**. Income over 10 years contributes $11.00 and the exit value $6.71, which is 38% of the total and below my 75% attention level for a 6 to 10 year horizon. The $1.86 is my estimate: it assumes lower short-term rates and tighter spreads are offset by cheaper funding and a steadier fee income. The Q4 consensus of $0.47 supports it, but that is a third-party estimate and not an input.

## Secondary valuation: normalized earnings

The second method takes FY27 NII of $1.86 less $0.20 of normal credit losses, which is $1.66 of normalized earnings per share, and applies 10.5 times. A lender that pays out all its earnings and does not grow is worth about 1 divided by the cost of equity, which is 10 times at 10%. I add half a turn for scale, and access to the unsecured bond market. The multiple is my judgment and not company guidance. The result is $17.43.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $17.70 and $17.43, a divergence of 1.6%, well below the 20% warning level. They are not independent: both start from the same NII forecast and the same credit loss assumption, so agreement is not independent evidence. I weight the NAV and income model 60% and the earnings method 40%, because the first captures the NAV drift directly. The weighted value is $17.59. ## What is the market already pricing in?

Holding everything else at Base, the price of $18.43 requires a cost of equity of 9.3% instead of 10.0%, or an NII yield of 10.3% of NAV ($1.98 a share in FY27) instead of 9.6%. The market is therefore paying for slightly better income than my Base, or accepting a return of 9.3% instead of 10.0%, and it is not pricing in a NAV decline as large as in my Bear case.

On a one year view, if NII is $1.86, NAV falls $0.20 and the price stays at 0.95 times NAV, the shares return about 9.1%, mostly the dividend. This is a model return for information, not a forecast.

## Reasons to own

- It is the largest BDC, with $29.2 billion in 636 companies, 58% in first lien loans, and it declared $0.48 a quarter in both the third quarter of 2025 and 2026.
- Leverage of 1.11 times and about $7 billion of liquidity leave room to absorb losses and to fund the MidCap deal.
- Funding is getting cheaper: the CLO spread fell 35 basis points, the 2033 notes swap to SOFR plus 1.85% and the BNP facility margin was cut.
- The shares trade at 0.95 times NAV and yield 10.4%, and the price is only 4.7% above my Base value.

## What could go wrong?

- Credit. Non-accruals rose to 2.4% of cost from 1.8% in nine months. Credit losses of $0.60 a share a year take the Base value to $14.67.
- Rates. 70% of the portfolio floats, so lower rates cut income faster than the cost of fixed-rate debt falls. NII of $1.74 a share gives a Base value of $16.67.
- Marks. Most of the portfolio has no market price, so its marks are the company's own estimates, and $1.2 billion of exits in the quarter went to Ivy Hill or its vehicles.
- Dividend. NII was below the dividend in the third quarter, and a cut would likely lower the share price.
- The deal. MidCap carries more non-accruals, and the share count rises by up to about 41 million.

## Management execution

The company launched a commercial paper program, repriced a CLO, issued 2033 notes and agreed to a large merger within one quarter, which is active capital management. The results are less clear. Core EPS missed consensus by $0.02, NAV is down 3.2% since December, and NII per share has fallen for two quarters, from $0.50 to $0.46. The next report, expected February 3, 2027, should show how much of the NII decline came from lower fee income and how much from lower yields.

## Final verdict: hold at $18.43, do not add

Ares Capital is a good lender priced for a good outcome. At $18.43 it yields 10.4% and trades near NAV, but the income is covering the dividend by a thin margin and my Base value is slightly below the price. My maximum buy price is $10.12.

**Verdict: HOLD at $18.43, do not add. Triangulated Base value $17.59, upside -4.5%, Confidence C (17.5/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by method $10.18 to $10.02, maximum buy price $10.12.**

The date I would mark is February 3, 2027: if NII is back at $0.48 the dividend is earned again, and if it is $0.45 the market will have to choose between the yield and the NAV.
