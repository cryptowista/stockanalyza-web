## What MGIC is

MGIC is one of the largest private mortgage insurers in the U.S., with an estimated 19.4% share of the private mortgage insurance market in 2025. It insures lenders and government-sponsored entities against borrower default on eligible low-down-payment loans, manages risk transfer through reinsurance, and has to maintain capital under GSE eligibility requirements known as PMIERs. The business is long-lived and recurring, but it is concentrated in one correlated exposure: the health of the U.S. housing and employment cycle.

Credit quality is currently a real strength. 45% of primary risk in force carries borrower credit scores of 760 or above, with only 2% below 660. Persistency fell to 83.3% from 84.7% a year earlier as refinancing picked up, and net premium yield declined to 31.3% from 33.0%, partly reflecting reinsurance costs and portfolio mix. Nothing here signals a business-model change; it remains a mortgage-credit insurer with a strong current book.

## Which numbers matter for a mortgage insurer

Operating cash flow or generic free cash flow isn't the right lens for an insurer. The hardest honest metric here is **reported common book value per share**, read together with normalized earnings and return on equity. Q2 GAAP net income of $182.1 million was very close to adjusted net operating income of $183.7 million, so this analysis gives priority to the GAAP figure and treats the adjusted number as a cross-check rather than the primary input. Tangible book value per share, after the AOCI adjustment, was $25.08.

Simple annualization of H1 diluted EPS gives $3.24; annualizing Q2 alone gives $3.44. A conservative normalized EPS range of **$3.00 to $3.50** is used, midpoint **$3.25**, allowing for policy runoff, premium yield pressure and claims variability against a strong recent run.

## Shares and capital return

Shares outstanding fell 11.4% year over year to 206.6 million at Q2-end, driven by real buybacks rather than a share-class quirk: 6.6 million shares for $176.6 million in Q2 alone, plus another 1.5 million for $42.4 million through late July. The board authorized a further $750 million of repurchases through the end of 2028. The company also carries $650 million of 5.25% notes due 2028. Buybacks support per-share book value and EPS today, but future pace depends on capital needs and board discretion, so it shouldn't be extrapolated indefinitely.

## Historical cross-check

A third-party historical reference puts MGIC's five-year median price to book near 1.02x, with a range of roughly 0.77x to 1.30x. Applied mechanically to $24.27 book value per share, the median implies about $24.75 and the top of the range about $31.55. At $28.48, MTG trades at 1.17x reported book and 1.14x tangible book, inside the historical band but above the median, without adjusting for today's higher profitability, capital rules or portfolio mix.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $28.48 the market pays about 8.76x normalized earnings and 1.17x book. Those multiples say investors expect MGIC to keep earning above a distressed insurer's return and to preserve the current capital cushion, while still assigning some discount for cyclical housing and regulatory risk. No reverse DCF is used, since price-to-book and normalized earnings are the sector-correct valuation engines for a regulated, capital-dependent insurer.

## How the buy price is set

A 25% margin of safety fits a profitable insurer with a strong current capital position but concentrated exposure to housing, employment, borrower credit and long-tail claims: higher than a low-volatility compounder would need, lower than a distressed or opaque insurer would require. Applied to the $29.50 Base value, that gives a **maximum buy price of $22.13**, with the strongest opportunity below $22. At $28.48 the shares trade about 28.7% above that ceiling.

## Reasons to own MTG

- **Scale and share.** An estimated 19.4% share of the U.S. private mortgage insurance market as of 2025.
- **Capital strength.** $2.7 billion of PMIERs excess and $930 million of holding-company liquidity at quarter-end.
- **Strong recent profitability.** $182.1 million of Q2 net income and 9.8% year-over-year book value growth.
- **Credit mix.** 45% of risk in force carries 760+ credit scores, with meaningful reinsurance protection.
- **Shrinking share count.** Buybacks that reduce shares outstanding can compound per-share value when executed below intrinsic worth.
- **Earnings support.** The stock trades below 9x estimated normalized earnings.

## What could go wrong?

- **Housing and employment stress.** A U.S. recession, rising unemployment or falling home prices can increase delinquencies and claims.
- **Persistency risk.** Refinancing and housing turnover can reduce policy persistency, premium yield and new insurance written.
- **Competition.** FHA pricing and eligibility, along with other private insurers, can intensify competitive pressure.
- **Regulatory risk.** Changes to GSE charters, PMIERs rules or reinsurance eligibility could reduce distributable capital.
- **Concentration.** Mortgage insurance is one correlated U.S. housing-credit system; benign historical loss experience may not predict a severe stress period.
- **Capital allocation.** Buybacks at an excessive valuation, or while capital buffers are actually needed, can destroy value.

## Management execution

Management has sustained profitability, grown book value per share, returned capital through buybacks and dividends, and extended reinsurance protection, including a $400 million subsidiary dividend to the holding company this quarter that supported parent liquidity. The framework does not independently verify a multi-year guidance track record, and a high buyback pace can destroy value if executed above intrinsic value or if capital is later needed for stress. **Assessment: positive but not exceptional; moderate confidence.**

## Adversarial review

The first draft's arithmetic mostly reconciled: the price to book and price to earnings multiples, the justified base values, and the triangulation all check out. One number was wrong. The probability-weighted scenario value was stated as $28.33, but the draft's own scenarios and probabilities ($22 × 25% + $29.50 × 55% + $36 × 20%) actually sum to $28.93. The corrected figure is used throughout this article; it doesn't change the conclusion, since it's still close to, and now slightly above, the current price.

The strongest counter-thesis is that current earnings may be closer to a cyclical peak than the framework's normalized $3.25 EPS assumes, and that the market's roughly 1.17x book valuation could understate losses that would emerge in a severe housing downturn. At normalized EPS of $3.00 and 8x earnings, value would be $24; combined with a book multiple near 1x, that points to a $22 to $24 stress range, which challenges the Base case but is already substantially represented in the stated Bear scenario.

## Final verdict: hold at $28.48, watchlist for a better entry

MGIC is a profitable, well-capitalized mortgage insurer with strong capital returns, but a small estimated upside doesn't compensate for housing-cycle risk at the current price. The main thesis breaker is a meaningful deterioration in delinquencies, book value or PMIERs capital headroom.

**Verdict: HOLD at $28.48. Triangulated Base value $29.50, upside 3.6%, Confidence C (17/25). Required margin of safety 25%, maximum buy price $22.13.**

For an existing holder, the covered dividend and ongoing buybacks support holding while book value and credit quality stay intact. New money waits for $22.13 or below, or for a meaningful discount to book value without deterioration in credit or capital.
