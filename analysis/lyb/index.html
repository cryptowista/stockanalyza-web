At $55.84, screeners put LyondellBasell at about 14 times trailing earnings. Remove the one-time items and it is 7.9 times. Neither number is right, because the trailing year contains two trough quarters, with EBITDA of $417 million and $615 million, and two windfall quarters of $2,127 million and $1,512 million, produced by a conflict that damaged petrochemical plants in the Middle East. Add them up and you get $4.67 billion, a figure that looks like normal earnings only because the swings cancel.

That is the whole analysis in one sentence: what is LyondellBasell's normal EBITDA? The company earned $9.31 billion in 2021, $6.53 billion in 2022, about $5.2 billion in 2023, $4.19 billion in 2024 and $2.54 billion in 2025. The stock rose to $83.94 in March when the supply shock began and has since fallen 33.5%, to a price that treats most of the windfall as temporary.

My triangulated Base value is $75.98, which is 36.1% above the price. The Bear value of $42.07 is 24.7% below it and the Bull value of $101.03 is 80.9% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 30% on Bear, 50% on Base and 20% on Bull, is $70.82. The price is attractive against those values. It is not attractive enough for my rules, which at Confidence C require a 42.5% margin of safety and give an entry range of $42.23 to $45.14. The stock would need to fall another 19.2% to 24.4% to reach it, so this is a hold, and a watchlist name for new money.

What LyondellBasell is

LyondellBasell makes polyethylene and polypropylene, the plastics in packaging and car parts, along with propylene oxide and gasoline blending components (oxyfuels) in its Intermediates and Derivatives business. Its North American plants run on cheap natural gas liquids, which is the cost advantage management points to, and its European business has been the weak spot. In May the company sold four European sites to AEQUITA, taking a $734 million loss on the sale. It had closed its Houston refinery in 2025, and it agreed to sell the former refinery site on the day of the third-quarter release.

The business is a price taker. A tight market adds hundreds of millions of dollars of EBITDA in a quarter, and a loose one removes them. The conflict that started this year damaged about 6 million tons of Middle East polyethylene capacity, which management estimates at 20% to 25% of the region's supply, and the company expects it to stay out until at least 2027. That is a real supply gap, and it is also a reason to be careful: the profits that follow it are borrowed from a market that will rebalance.

Cash, debt and shares

At September 30 LyondellBasell held $3,227 million in cash against $12,331 million of debt, made up of $247 million of short-term debt, $891 million of current maturities and $11,193 million of long-term debt. That is a net debt of $9,104 million, or 1.9 times trailing EBITDA. At the end of 2025 it was $9,495 million, or 3.7 times that year's EBITDA, so the balance sheet has improved only a little in dollars and a lot in ratio, because EBITDA recovered. The company repaid a 500 million euro note in September, and it has $7.7 billion of available liquidity.

Operating leases are left out of net debt because rent is already deducted in EBITDA. The share count was 323.24 million at October 28, to which I add 0.8 million for dilutive awards, which gives 324.0 million shares, a market value of $18.09 billion and an enterprise value of $27.20 billion. There were no share repurchases in the first nine months of 2026, against $201 million in the same period of 2025. Pension obligations and joint venture details were not in the sources I used, and I list them as a data gap.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $55.84 the enterprise value of $27.20 billion is 5.8 times trailing EBITDA, 5.8 times my mid-cycle EBITDA and 5.0 times a 2026 estimate of $5.40 billion (nine months of actuals plus about $1.15 billion for the fourth quarter, my estimate). Solving backwards with the rest of the Base case fixed, the price needs one of these: mid-cycle EBITDA of $3.89 billion at 7.0x (against my $4.70 billion), a multiple of 5.8x on my EBITDA (against 7.0x), a discount rate of 10.5% (against 8.5%), terminal growth of negative 1.4% (against 1.5%), or EBITDA about 14% below my Base path in every year. Each of these sits between my Bear and Base cases.

LyondellBasell earned $3.88 billion in 2020, the pandemic year, so a 7.0x multiple on that figure is the price. The market is paying for a normal year at roughly the level of its worst recent one. How good is the growth?

There is no growth here to grade. Earnings per share excluding items fell from $12.46 in 2022 to $1.70 in 2025 and rebounded to $7.34 in nine months of 2026, which is a cycle and not a trend. The growth-adjusted valuation ratio (PEG) is not meaningful, and the free cash flow comparison is distorted for the same reason. My Base case assumes terminal growth of 1.5% and no improvement in mid-cycle EBITDA beyond the cost program. Nine-month free cash flow of $1,357 million is a rebound from a low base, helped by the Middle East supply gap, and I do not extrapolate it.

Dividend

The board cut the quarterly dividend by half in February, from $1.37 to $0.69 a share, after a year in which it paid out far more than it earned. At $2.76 a year the yield is 4.9% and the cost is about $892 million a year. At mid-cycle, free cash flow after interest is about $2.07 billion, so the dividend is covered 2.3 times. In the first nine months of 2026 free cash flow of $1,357 million covered dividends of $672 million 2.0 times.

Management lists deleveraging ahead of the dividend and has repurchased no shares this year. The stated goal is to return 70% of free cash flow through the cycle. With the cut in place the payout is sustainable on my Base case, and on my arithmetic a second cut would need EBITDA near the 2025 level of $2.5 billion.

Reasons to own LYB

  • The price assumes a bad normal. It needs $3.89 billion of mid-cycle EBITDA at 7.0x, below the 2022 to 2025 average of $4.61 billion.
  • Costs have come down. The Cash Improvement Plan beat its 2025 goal by $200 million and targets $1.3 billion in total by the end of 2026, and the sale of the European assets removes a segment that lost money.
  • Supply is tight into 2027. About 6 million tons of Middle East polyethylene capacity is damaged, and integrated polyethylene margins in the Americas finished the third quarter about 50% above 2025 levels.
  • Cash conversion is real. Free cash flow covered the dividend 2.0 times in nine months, and cash was $3.2 billion at quarter-end.
  • Leverage is moving the right way. Net debt is 1.9 times trailing EBITDA against 3.7 times at the end of 2025.

What could go wrong?

  • The windfall fades. Second-quarter EBITDA of $2.13 billion came from a supply shock, and by the third quarter it was already $1.51 billion.
  • Oversupply returns. If the damaged plants restart on schedule or new Asian capacity arrives, EBITDA could settle near $3.6 billion, my Bear case at $42.07.
  • Oxyfuels margins normalize. The record $795 million in Intermediates and Derivatives rested on supply disruptions and an unusually strong gasoline market, and management expects some margins to normalize.
  • Leverage is high at the bottom. Net debt of $9.1 billion was 3.7 times EBITDA a year ago, and the dividend was already cut once.
  • Europe is still weak. The remaining European polymer plants are guided to a 60% operating rate in the fourth quarter, and closure costs recurred in the third quarter.

Management execution

Management beat its own cost targets, with the Cash Improvement Plan ahead by $200 million in 2025 and the Value Enhancement Program above its original $750 million goal, and it sold the European assets and closed the refinery. It also paid a dividend that earnings did not cover until the board cut it, bought back $201 million of shares in the first nine months of 2025 while profit fell, and lost about $250 million of second-quarter EBITDA to unplanned downtime at Bayport. Management credibility scores 13/20, fair to good, helped by the cost record and held back by the capital allocation before the cut.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • EBITDA excluding items at or above $1.0 billion a quarter
  • Net debt falling toward $7 billion
  • Free cash flow at least 1.5 times the dividend
  • Capex held near $1.4 billion to $1.6 billion without cutting turnarounds
  • Integrated polyethylene margins staying above 2025 levels

Yellow

  • EBITDA between $0.7 billion and $1.0 billion a quarter
  • Net debt flat near $9 billion
  • Free cash flow between 1.0 and 1.5 times the dividend
  • Oxyfuels margins back to normal while polymer margins stay flat

Red

  • EBITDA below $0.7 billion a quarter
  • Free cash flow below the roughly $0.9 billion annual dividend
  • Net debt above the $9.5 billion of the end of 2025
  • Middle East capacity restarting early with polyethylene margins falling

Today's readings are green on EBITDA ($1.51 billion in the third quarter) and free cash flow cover (2.0 times), and yellow on net debt ($9.1 billion). Fourth-quarter results should come in late January or early February 2027, and the date is not yet confirmed. They should show four things: EBITDA near $1.1 billion to $1.2 billion with lower operating rates (my estimate), net debt falling on the seasonal working capital release, the Cash Improvement Plan at its $1.3 billion target, and a capex plan for 2027 that does not rise above $1.6 billion.

The case improves if the price falls toward $45 with EBITDA holding above $1.0 billion a quarter, or if a full year of cost savings lifts my estimate of mid-cycle EBITDA above $5.0 billion.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. EBITDA drops below $0.7 billion a quarter once the supply gap closes, which would show that mid-cycle is nearer $3 billion than $4.7 billion.
  2. Free cash flow falls below the dividend while net debt stays above $9 billion, forcing a second dividend cut or a leverage problem.
  3. Damaged Middle East capacity returns early or new Asian capacity floods the market, and polyethylene margins fall back to 2025 levels.

Final verdict: hold at $55.84, watchlist for new money

LyondellBasell is cheaper than my estimate of what it earns in a normal year, on a balance sheet that is improving and with a dividend that is covered. At $55.84 it is 26.5% below my Base value and 24% to 32% above the entry range, and the whole case rests on an EBITDA figure that the cycle can move by $500 million either way.

Verdict: HOLD at $55.84. Triangulated Base value $75.98, upside 36.1%, Confidence C (17.5/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range $42.23 to $45.14, maximum buy price $42.23.

At 7 times EBITDA, the price asks for $3.9 billion of normal earnings, which is what the company made in 2020, the pandemic year. That is why it belongs on the watchlist. It is also why the entry range sits right next to my Bear value: the discount I require is the one the Bear case already shows.
