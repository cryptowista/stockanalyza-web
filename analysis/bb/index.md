# BlackBerry is finally profitable and growing, but at $8.90 the shares are priced above my Bull case

BB, NYSE (also TSX). Published November 15, 2026. Price as of the November 13, 2026 regular session close. Values in US dollars. Latest results: second quarter of fiscal 2027, to August 31, 2026 (reported September 24, 2026). Fiscal year ends in February.

## Verdict: REDUCE at $8.90

BlackBerry's QNX unit grew 27% last quarter and the company has reported six profitable quarters in a row. At the start of the year BlackBerry traded at $3.79. Since then the company has beaten its own guidance twice, raised it twice and shown a sixth straight quarter of GAAP profit, and the shares have gone to $8.90. The business deserves part of that move. It does not deserve all of it, because even my Bull case is worth $6.50.

My Base value is $4.91 a share, the Bear value $2.89 and the Bull value $6.50. The Base blends a segment discounted cash flow ($4.26) with a sum of the parts ($5.89). I require a 40% margin of safety, which sets the maximum buy price at $2.95. At $8.90 the market pays 8.4 times fiscal 2027 revenue and 35 times adjusted EBITDA for a company whose owner cash flow, after stock pay, is about $75 million this year.

Valuation route: embedded software and secure communications company with positive cash flow, valued with a discounted cash flow built segment by segment (QNX, Secure Communications, Licensing and corporate costs), and cross-checked with a sum of the parts on fiscal 2028 EBITDA and with multiples of free cash flow. The convertible notes are treated as shares because the stock trades far above their $3.88 conversion price.

## What BlackBerry is

BlackBerry now earns its money from three businesses. QNX makes the safety-certified operating system and related software inside vehicles, medical devices and robots, and the company says it runs in more than 275 million cars. QNX had record revenue of $80.3 million in the second quarter, up 27%, with an adjusted gross margin of 87% and adjusted EBITDA of $29.0 million, a 36% margin. It earns revenue from development licenses, services and royalties paid when a car ships.

Secure Communications sells government-grade mobile and messaging software (SecuSUITE and the older UEM product). Revenue was $60.9 million, up 2%. Annual recurring revenue is $221 million, but dollar-based net retention fell to 91%, from 94% at the end of last fiscal year, and management cut its full-year outlook for this segment to $260 to $270 million because of a more cautious view of some government deals. Licensing, the third segment, collects intellectual property fees. It brought in $22.1 million in the quarter against $6.6 million a year earlier, and management says that is not a new run rate: it expects about $6 million in each of the next two quarters.

The forward story is the royalty backlog, the company's estimate of future royalties on designs already won. QNX said on October 20 that it had passed $1.2 billion, up from about $950 million at the end of last fiscal year, helped by the first design win for Alloy Kore, a broader automotive operating system, with Coretura (the software venture of Volvo Group and Daimler Truck) worth more than $100 million of estimated future royalties. The backlog is a lifetime estimate based on projected vehicle volumes, not annual revenue, and the filing itself warns it may not indicate actual future revenue.

## Cash flow: what the owner keeps

Adjusted EBITDA of about $150 million for fiscal 2027 is not cash that belongs to shareholders. It leaves out stock pay of about $29 million a year (SBC was $14.0 million in the first six months), restructuring and other cash items, taxes and a decline in deferred revenue (down 5% from a year ago, to $128.4 million). Management guides operating cash flow of about $115 million, which after roughly $4 million of capital spending is about $111 million of free cash flow. I subtract stock pay of about $29 million and, because the valuation adds the cash balance separately, about $7 million of net interest income that sits inside that cash flow. The result is owner free cash flow of about $75 million, 12% of revenue. The interest figure is my estimate.

Two points keep me from paying up for the growth in this number. Free cash flow was only $46.5 million in fiscal 2026, so the jump is large from a low base. And cash taxes are low because of past losses, so they rise as those run out: my Base raises the tax rate from 7% to 22% over seven years.

## Primary valuation: discounted cash flow by segment

I valued the owner cash flow from September 1, 2026 and rolled the result to November 13. The model covers the second half of fiscal 2027, fourteen full years to fiscal 2041 and a terminal value. I use a 10.5% cost of equity: the stock's beta is 1.27 on one quote page, and I treat that as a judgment, not a measurement. Terminal growth is 3.0%. QNX grows 17% in fiscal 2028 and fades to 3.5% by fiscal 2041 with a margin of 34% rising to 37%, which is just above this quarter's 36%. Secure Communications grows 2% a year at a 21% margin. Licensing fades from $20 million to $10 million. Corporate costs of about $40 million grow 3% a year.

## Secondary valuation: sum of the parts

The second method values each segment on a multiple of its fiscal 2028 EBITDA and subtracts corporate costs. QNX gets 26 times because it grows 17% in the Base, has a 34% margin, a royalty backlog and high switching costs, which together fit a Rule of 40 score near 50. Secure Communications gets 9 times because it is flat, retention is below 100% and government demand is uneven. Licensing gets 7 times because it is lumpy and fading, and corporate costs are capitalized at 12 times. In the Bear the QNX multiple is 20 times and in the Bull 32 times.

## Valuation triangulation

| Method | Bear | Base | Bull | Weight |

The methods differ by 32% in the Base, above the 30% flag level, and by 66% in the Bear because the sum of the parts gives a high multiple to a shrunken QNX. The gap comes from how each method treats the multiple. The sum of the parts pays 21.5 times fiscal 2028 EBITDA in total, and the discounted cash flow pays 13.9 times, because the discounted cash flow deducts stock pay, restructuring and taxes before it values anything, and the multiple method starts from adjusted EBITDA that excludes them.

| Base case | Enterprise value, $ million | To FY28E EBITDA | To FY28E owner free cash flow | Value per share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discounted cash flow | 2,230 | 13.9x | 24.5x | $4.26 |
| Sum of the parts | 3,443 | 21.5x | 37.8x | $5.89 |
| Market at $8.90 | 5,250 | 32.8x | 57.6x | $8.90 |

Both methods depend on the same QNX growth and margin, so their agreement would not raise my confidence even if the gap were smaller. I weigh the discounted cash flow at 60% because it counts every cost and shows the fade, and the sum of the parts at 40% because it reflects what the market pays for embedded software. The weighted Base of $4.91 is a point inside a $4.26 to $5.89 range; the probability-weighted value, with 25% Bear, 50% Base and 25% Bull, is $4.80. Since the gap is above 30%, I lowered the confidence grade and did not add a separate margin of safety for it.

## How the buy price is set

The required margin of safety is 40%: the middle of the 35% to 50% range for grade C confidence. I add nothing for the divergence because it already lowered the confidence grade, and nothing for the auto cycle because the Bear case contains it. $4.91 times 0.60 is **$2.95**, 67% below today's price. The discounted cash flow and the sum of the parts give $2.55 and $3.54 separately. Below the $3.88 conversion price the notes would stay as debt, which lowers the per-share value slightly, so the maximum buy price is an approximation at that level.

If you think in required returns, the Base earns 8% a year from $6.17, 10% from $4.53 and 12% from $3.60.

## Dividend and buybacks

BlackBerry pays no dividend. It bought back $10 million of shares in the first six months, renewed its buyback program in May and says it is also considering selective acquisitions. The buybacks roughly offset new shares from stock pay, so they do not shrink the share count. I would rather see the cash used on QNX than on an acquisition at a high price.

## Reasons to own BlackBerry

- QNX is growing 27% with an 87% adjusted gross margin, and the royalty backlog above $1.2 billion gives some visibility into future royalties.
- The company beat and raised guidance in two consecutive quarters and reported a sixth straight quarter of GAAP profit.
- The balance sheet is solid: $447 million of cash and investments against $200 million of notes that will probably convert.
- Physical AI, robotics and medical devices could widen the market beyond cars, and QNX Everywhere has passed 30,000 free licenses.

## What could go wrong?

- **Price:** the shares are at 8.4 times revenue after a 135% gain this year. A guidance miss or a slower QNX quarter could move them sharply either way;
- **Automotive cycle:** QNX royalties follow vehicle production, and design wins turn into revenue only years later. The backlog is an estimate.
- **Secure Communications:** retention of 91% means existing customers are paying less, and government deals can slip.
- **One-offs:** the second quarter had $22.1 million of licensing revenue, and quarterly comparisons will be harder from here.
- **Taxes and stock pay:** low cash taxes depend on past losses. Stock pay of about $29 million a year is a real cost.
- **Acquisitions:** management is looking at tuck-in deals, and a bad one could waste the cash.
- **My own numbers:** owner cash flow, the 14-year path, the 10.5% cost of equity and the sum of the parts multiples are my estimates.

## Management execution

CEO John Giamatteo and CFO Tim Foote have turned a loss-making company into a profitable one. Revenue of $549.1 million in fiscal 2026 grew 3%, and GAAP net income moved from a loss to a profit. QNX delivered $80.3 million against a guidance of $70 to $75 million and the outlook was raised twice. On the other side, Secure Communications guidance was cut in the same report, the CEO sold stock in July and management sets guidance that it then beats, which is reassuring but tells you the target is cautious, not that growth is accelerating. I score management 7 out of 10.

## The three most important thesis breakers

These are the events that would make the Reduce call wrong, or confirm it faster.

1. QNX growth stays above 25% for several years with margins near 40%, and Alloy Kore turns into royalties early. That moves the value toward and above my Bull of $6.50.
2. A takeover offer from a larger technology or automotive company, which would price the shares on a strategic basis and not on cash flow.
3. Automakers cut production or delay programs, QNX growth drops below 15% and Secure Communications keeps shrinking. That moves the value toward the Bear of $2.89.

## Final verdict: reduce at $8.90

BlackBerry is a better company than it was two years ago. QNX is a high-margin software franchise with a growing backlog, the balance sheet is solid and the profit is real. The problem is the price. At $8.90 the shares trade 81% above my Base, 37% above my Bull, and the market needs QNX to grow about 8.6 points a year faster than I model for fourteen years. The Base earns 6.4% a year from here.

The call is Reduce because the price is far above the Base (-44.8% downside) and above the Bull case, and the two methods are on the same side of the price. The maximum buy price is $2.95. A takeover is the main way I can be wrong in the short run.

The company can keep beating its guidance and the shares can still fall, because the price already assumes it will.

**Verdict: REDUCE at $8.90. Base $4.91, -44.8% against the price, Bear $2.89, Bull $6.50, Confidence C (16/25). Required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $2.95, range $2.55 to $3.54 under the two methods.**
