Robinhood reported record revenue of $1.42 billion in the third quarter, up 12%, and net income fell 14% to $476 million. The shares were already down about 51% from their 52-week high of $139.75, and they fell again after the report.

The gap between the two numbers is the story. Revenue grew because customers brought more money, traded more options and bet on event contracts. Profit fell because crypto revenue dropped 55%, net interest revenue fell 9%, credit losses rose and the tax rate went from about 19% to about 24%.

My triangulated Base value is $43.71, which is 36.2% below the $68.46 reference price. The Bear value is $18.50 and the Bull value is $80.71. Confidence is grade D (10/25), which requires a 55% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at $19.67. The verdict is a reduce. At $68.46 the shares trade at about 31 times my 2027 earnings estimate of $2.21 a share, and my Bull case is worth only +17.9% against the price.

What Robinhood is now

Robinhood is a retail brokerage that has grown into a bundle of financial products. It had 28.7 million funded customers and $396 billion of platform assets at the end of September, 5.2 million Gold subscribers (up 34%), a margin book of $22.1 billion (up 59%) and 1.3 million Gold Card holders spending more than $19 billion a year. It says 14 business lines have reached $100 million or more of annualized revenue. Rothera, its prediction market venture with Susquehanna, passed $100 million of annualized revenue in its first full quarter.

The company reports no 2027 guidance. It plans to begin rolling out its product suite in the Dubai region in 2027. In October, net deposits topped $13 billion by the CEO's count, including $6 billion from one family's contribution to Trump Accounts, and the October operating data showed 28.9 million funded customers and $383 billion of platform assets.

Which earnings to value

Operating cash flow was $4.0 billion in the first nine months of 2026, but it includes movements in customer cash, so I do not use it as owner cash. I value net income after stock pay, with a conversion of 90% to allow for capital that a brokerage and a credit card lender must retain. Third quarter annualized net income is about $1.9 billion, and adjusted net income about $2.2 billion.

The model starts from 2026 revenue of about $5.5 billion (my estimate, after record revenue of $4.5 billion in 2025), grows it 12% in 2027 and fades the growth to 8% by 2031, holds the pre-tax margin at 42% (against 44% in the third quarter), and taxes it at 22%. The result is net income of $2.0 billion in 2027 and $2.9 billion in 2031. Every one of these is my estimate.

Secondary valuation: earnings multiple

I apply 22 times my 2027 earnings of $2.21 a share, a multiple that reflects growth of about 10% a year, earnings that depend on trading activity and interest rates, and a business that must keep spending to grow. That gives $48.52, and adding $0.92 of net cash a share gives $49.44. The two methods share the same earnings path, so their agreement is partial confirmation: it tests the multiple and the terminal value, not the earnings.

Growth check and history

Revenue was $4.5 billion in 2025 (up 27% in the fourth quarter alone) and grew 12% in the third quarter of 2026, while net income fell 14%, so growth is slowing and profit is lagging it. A growth-adjusted check is not meaningful when earnings fall. I did not verify a history of valuation multiples from a consistent source, so it carries no weight. The 52-week range is $63.52 to $139.75.

What is the market already pricing in?

At $68.46, holding everything else at Base, the market needs revenue growth about 16 points a year higher than my Base in every year through 2031 (so about 28% in 2027, fading to 24% in 2031), or a pre-tax margin of about 66% held for five years (against 44% today), or a discount rate of about 7.1%. The growth case is the plausible one: platform assets, margin lending and Gold subscribers are growing 19% to 59% a year. The margin case is not, because operating expenses are growing and credit losses are rising. What I cannot verify is how much of the growth comes from trading, which can fall in a weak market, and how much from subscriptions and banking, which does not.

Dividend and capital return

Robinhood pays no dividend. It has repurchased about $1.3 billion of shares since the third quarter of 2024, mostly at far lower prices than today. Buybacks in the third quarter were only $25 million, against $476 million of net income, and the share count is down only 0.8% from a year ago.

Reasons to own Robinhood

  • Customer growth. Funded customers grew 7% to 28.7 million, platform assets grew 19% to $396 billion, and net deposits were $15.7 billion in the quarter.
  • Diversification. Options and equities revenue grew 31% and 41%, event contracts reached $173 million a quarter, and 14 business lines are above $100 million annualized.
  • Subscription base. 5.2 million Gold subscribers, up 34%, and 1.3 million Gold Card holders.
  • Margins. Adjusted EBITDA margin of 57% and a pre-tax margin of 44%.
  • Cost control. The top end of the 2026 expense outlook was lowered by $25 million.

These support the Base. They do not support the price.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: trading activity. Crypto revenue is down 55% from a year ago and options and event contracts depend on active customers. A fall in trading would hit revenue directly while expenses keep growing.
  • Net interest. Net interest revenue fell 9% on lower securities lending and short-term rates. Lower rates would cut it further.
  • Credit. The provision for credit losses was $71 million against $26 million a year ago, as the card book grows. I did not read the delinquency data.
  • Expense growth. The implied fourth quarter expense is up to 26% above the third. Operating expenses grew 16% in the third quarter against revenue growth of 12%.
  • Regulation and litigation. The Supreme Court agreed on November 13 to hear the bid by Robinhood to block a lawsuit over interim data omitted from its 2021 IPO. The outlook excludes potential regulatory matters.
  • Volatility. The shares have had many daily moves above 5% in the past year and are exposed to retail risk appetite.

Management execution

Management has expanded products fast, added 14 lines above $100 million of revenue, grown deposits and cut the top of the expense outlook. It also faces a CFO transition, a pending acquisition whose costs are excluded from guidance, and a record of profit growth lagging revenue growth this quarter. Assessment: 12/20, strong product execution and a profit lag. The next tests are fourth quarter expenses against the $763 million to $838 million implied range, and profit growth keeping pace with revenue.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Net income growing at least as fast as revenue
  • Fourth quarter operating expenses and stock pay at or below $763 million
  • Net deposits annualized above 15% of platform assets
  • Event contracts and Gold revenue continuing to grow while crypto stabilizes
  • Net interest revenue stabilizing as the margin book grows

Yellow

  • Fourth quarter expenses near the top of the $838 million range
  • Provision for credit losses above $80 million a quarter
  • Net interest revenue falling again
  • Tax rate staying near 24%

Red

  • Revenue falling for a quarter while expenses rise
  • Funded customers falling
  • Credit card delinquencies rising sharply
  • An adverse regulatory ruling or large settlement
  • Net deposits falling below 10% annualized

Today's readings: the expense outlook cut and deposit growth are green, the credit provision and net interest are yellow, and the fall in crypto revenue is a red point I already count in the Base. The next point is the fourth quarter report and the monthly operating data.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Profit keeps falling while revenue grows, because expenses and credit losses outrun revenue.
  2. Trading volumes and crypto fall together in a weak market, which hits transaction revenue.
  3. A regulatory or legal ruling changes how the company earns on order flow, prediction markets or credit.

The strongest case against my Reduce: the platform is still adding customers and products at a pace that is hard to model, and a company that grows platform assets 19% and margin lending 59% can lift revenue by much more than my 12%. My answer is that profit fell this quarter while revenue grew, expenses are guided up for the fourth quarter, and trading-driven revenue does not compound steadily. Main data limits: no 10-Q, no credit delinquency data, no pending acquisition details, no verified history of multiples, no view of the call. Revenue for 2026, the growth path, margin, tax rate, discount rate, cash conversion, share of cash counted as excess and the multiple are my estimates.

Final verdict: reduce at $68.46

Robinhood is growing customers, assets and product lines faster than almost any broker, with a 44% pre-tax margin. But net income fell 14% in a record revenue quarter, and at $68.46 the price is 57% above my Base and 248% above the maximum buy price.

Verdict: REDUCE at $68.46. Triangulated Base value $43.71, downside 36.2%, Confidence D (10/25), required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $19.67.

The price needs revenue growth of roughly 25% a year for five years at today's margins. Last quarter's 12% came with falling profit.
