What Collegium is

Collegium sells branded specialty drugs in two groups. The pain group is Belbuca, Xtampza ER and the Nucynta franchise. The ADHD group is JORNAY PM, which came with the 2024 purchase of Ironshore, and AZSTARYS, bought from Corium Therapeutics on May 12, 2026 for about $650 million in cash, with up to $135 million more if milestones are met. In the second quarter pain contributed $140.9 million, or 70.5% of $199.9 million of sales, and ADHD $59.0 million, or 29.5%. Belbuca alone was $57.7 million, 28.9% of the company. Three wholesalers accounted for 97% of fiscal 2025 revenue, according to the annual report, which is a channel concentration and not a statement about how many patients buy the drugs.

The company spends nothing on research: R&D expense was zero in fiscal 2025, because it has been a buyer of commercial assets since 2022. That makes cash generation easy and replacement hard. Physical capital spending is under 1% of sales, so the real reinvestment is the next drug purchase, and my model assumes none.

The dates when generics can arrive matter more here than the last patent date. As the company's own January 2026 presentation sets them out:

  • Belbuca: generic entry is permitted from January 2027 under a settlement with Teva. Other patent litigation can run to 2032. In Base I keep $150 million of residual brand and authorized generic sales in 2027 and $120 million in 2028, against $230 million this year (my allocation).
  • Nucynta: authorized generics launched in the first quarter of 2026. Lower net prices on them are the reason the company gave for cutting guidance in August.
  • Xtampza ER: Teva may enter in September 2033.
  • JORNAY PM: Orange Book patents run to March 2032 and the presentation lists no generic filers yet.
  • AZSTARYS: six Orange Book patents, most to December 2037. This is the longest runway in the portfolio.
  • Symproic: patents to November 2031. A small asset.

A permitted entry date is not a launch date, since a generic still needs approval and supply. That uncertainty is why my three scenarios differ so much.

Primary valuation: hard EBITDA with a two year transition

Normal earnings are more than two years away and differ from today's, so multiplying them by a multiple and calling the result today's value would be a trick. The model runs forward to December 31, 2028, 2.2 years from the valuation date. Cash generated before then pays down debt or builds liquidity. Nothing is paid to shareholders, since there is no dividend, and cash is counted once: as lower debt at exit. Owner cash each year is (hard EBITDA - interest) x (1 - 23%) - capital spending - working capital.

Secondary valuation: franchise cash runoff

The second method drops the multiple and values each drug's cash to 2035. For every product it takes sales times a contribution margin after manufacturing, non-financing royalties and direct selling costs (JORNAY 65%, AZSTARYS 60%, Belbuca and Xtampza 72%, Nucynta 55%, Symproic 45% in Base). It subtracts central platform and stock pay costs of $99 million rising to $103 million and then falling to $75 million, $10 million a year of legal costs and the JORNAY royalty, applies 23% tax, and deducts $3 million of capital spending and $5 million of working capital each year.

  • Shared by both methods: the drug portfolio, 2027 and 2028 sales, the patent and generic dates, the starting cash and debt and the conversion rule.
  • Different in each: the first capitalizes consolidated 2028 EBITDA at a multiple I choose, the second builds product margins, central costs and a full runoff. The second has no perpetuity.
  • Treated the same: all capital spending is deducted, rent is inside costs, and neither credits any minority or joint venture cash.
  • What agreement would prove: little. More than half of the value in each method depends on the same drug franchises, so the methods cannot confirm that the sales forecasts are right. The two do not agree, and that lowers my confidence.

History, peers and growth diagnostics

Normalized hard cash has to be compared with where the market already is. At $23.19 the diluted equity is $739.8 million and enterprise value $1,675.8 million. That is 3.66 times the guided adjusted EBITDA and 4.43 times my hard EBITDA. The only close peer I could price on the same date is ANI Pharmaceuticals (ANIP) at $72.28, about 6.2 times its guided 2026 EBITDA, with revenue growing 22% to 29% (the ANIP figures are from its own release and a balance sheet at June 30, so this is a partial check). A growing peer deserves a higher multiple than a company with eroding legacy sales, and I do not claim that 6.2 times applies to a harder metric. I left out Supernus because its announced merger distorts its price. I have no full peer median, and I did not make one up.

The growth-adjusted check does not work either. Third-party consensus adjusted EPS is roughly $7.4 to $7.5 for 2026 and a few percent lower for 2027, so the 2026 adjusted P/E is about 3.1 times and the growth rate is negative. A PEG ratio on negative growth is not meaningful, and a vendor PEG of 0.66 uses a growth window I cannot reconcile, so I discard it. Consensus does not enter my Base.

How the buy price is set

A Confidence score of 13 is grade C, whose base margin of safety runs from 35% to 50%. I use 35%, the low end, because the operating erosion is already inside the scenarios, and add 10 points for a legal tail I cannot model: an arbitration claim of more than $500 million (see the risks). The required MOS is 45% and the maximum buy price is $27.22 times 0.55, or $14.97. That is 35.4% below the price. The leading one-input tests move it between $11.84 and $17.75. The price is above that whole band, and a price below it would call for a check for fresh impairments before any purchase.

Dividend and buybacks

Collegium has never paid a cash dividend and says it has no intention to, so this is not an income stock and the payout ratio is not applicable. Cash goes to debt and to buying back shares. In the 2024 to 2025 program it bought 2.70 million shares for $85.0 million, an average of $31.43, per the filings, 35% above today's price. The new $50 million repurchase paid for shares at about $25.70. Buybacks help the shares that stay, and they are not income for holders who sell.

Reasons to own Collegium

  • The cash is reported, not adjusted. Cash from operations was $329.3 million in 2025 and $128.4 million in the first half of 2026. The question is how much of it survives the generics.
  • JORNAY has real demand. Prescriptions rose 13.1% in the second quarter, and its patents run to March 2032.
  • Deleveraging is built into the Base. Cash can take net debt from $936 million to $450 million by the end of 2028 without a dividend competing for it.
  • AZSTARYS has the longest life. Most of its patents run to December 2037, which offsets the near-term pain erosion, though the $650 million price and up to $135 million of milestones need real returns.
  • The Bull case has room. It pays the whole milestone cap and the convert dilution and still reaches $51.88.

These justify watching the stock. At $23.19 they do not remove the risk that the residual equity gets small if the pain cash falls quickly.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: pain cash falls faster than ADHD cash grows. Belbuca can face generics from January 2027 and Nucynta authorized generics are already selling at lower net prices, while promotion and manufacturing cannot fall in step. My ordinary Bear leaves $1.81 a share after debt.
  • Legal exposure. A claim by North Sound Partners over alleged pre-acquisition conduct seeks damages above $500 million, according to the 10-Q as I have it, and the company says it cannot estimate a loss. A $100 million payment at exit would take the Base to $24.82 and $500 million to $14.65, which is below today's price. These are tests, not estimates, and an earlier payment would be worse. Patents, payer access, controlled-substance supply rules and opioid litigation are separate risks.
  • Refinancing. The credit facility matures in December 2030, but I understand it can spring forward to November 18, 2028 if more than $50 million of the convertible notes remains and liquidity is under $350 million. The notes themselves mature on February 15, 2029. My Base holds $250 million of cash and assumes the $100 million revolver is available, subject to the actual definitions. A rate 2 points higher on about $700 million of debt for two years takes the Base to $26.73, and that does not test closed credit markets.
  • Capital allocation. Acquired growth has to earn a return on $650 million. The first half of 2026 added back $30.3 million of acquisition costs and $25.4 million of SBC, and the annual report says adjusted EBITDA affects incentive pay, so repeated exclusions deserve scrutiny.
  • Receivable and rebate accruals. Accrued rebates, returns and discounts were $406.9 million at June 30, up from $318.3 million at year end. I assume no release of that working capital.

The 2027 interest and repayment schedule is my reading of the credit terms. Scheduled term repayments of $11 million in the stub and $66 million in each of 2027 and 2028 are covered even in the Bear, where cash is about $117 million and $97 million.

Management execution

The record is good in selected years and weaker lately. Fiscal 2023 revenue of $566.8 million landed within the late-year guidance of $565 to $570 million. In 2024 revenue of $631.4 million beat the initial $580 to $595 million, but about $37.2 million of that was acquired JORNAY revenue, so legacy sales of roughly $594.2 million were at the top of the range, not above it. In 2025 revenue of $780.6 million and EBITDA of $460.5 million beat initial ranges of $735 to $750 million and $435 to $450 million with the same perimeter. Then in 2026 the May guidance after AZSTARYS ($865 to $895 million) was cut in August to $825 to $855 million. The refinancing in December lowered borrowing costs and pushed out maturities, and the company then borrowed another $300 million while buying back stock, which makes visible debt reduction more important. The Ironshore earn-out hurdle was not met, a reminder that revenue growth is not the same as every deal milestone. Assessment: 11/20, good delivery in selected years with a real 2026 warning; capital allocation average.

Final verdict: hold at $23.19

Collegium makes a lot of cash and the price already allows for a lot of erosion, but the discount does not pay for the uncertainty about what remains after the generics and how long the remaining drugs last. For an existing holder this is not an automatic sell signal, but the cash and refinancing thesis should be checked before adding, and a low forward P/E alone is no reason to average down.
