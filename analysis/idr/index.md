## What Idaho Strategic is

Idaho Strategic Resources, based in Coeur d'Alene, Idaho, has about 51 employees. Its producing asset is the Golden Chest mine in Murray, Idaho, an underground gold mine whose ore is processed at the majority-owned New Jersey Mill and sold as gold concentrate and doré. In 2025 it produced 12,538 ounces from ore grading 10.14 grams per tonne, a high grade that is the heart of the story, and in the third quarter of 2026 it produced 3,069 ounces. Hydroelectric power supplies about 60% of the mine's electricity, according to the CEO, which holds down one cost line.

Around the mine the company has been consolidating a land package of more than 20,000 acres in the Murray Gold Belt, and it holds rare earth and thorium prospects at Lemhi Pass and Mineral Hill. First-phase drilling at Lucky Horseshoe and Cardinal is finished, but assay results are not out and there is no resource estimate, so I give the rare earth projects no value. The company is also building a new mill at Murray, on schedule according to management, with commissioning described as the next milestone and a tailings facility 75% complete at September 30. Management has said it wants to reach 20,000 ounces a year by 2028, which would be about 60% more than the 2025 output of 12,538 ounces (the target comes from a summary of a June 2026 conference presentation).

## Why I use owner free cash flow after exploration

I build owner free cash flow from the mine: ounces sold at the gold price, less cash production costs, corporate costs and exploration (which the company expenses and which I treat as a real recurring cost, because it is what replaces the ounces mined), less tax at a full 21%, plus depreciation, less capital spending. The company's reported tax charge is low because it has loss carry-forwards, and I do not rely on them. The release gives no operating cash flow and no capital spending, so the capital spending path is my estimate.

Base assumptions, all my own estimates: a gold price of $3,700 in 2027 rising 2% a year, which is 6% above the spot price; production of 12,500 ounces in 2027, 15,000 in 2028 and 17,000 in 2029, then 17,500, a little below management's 20,000 goal; cash costs of $1,350 an ounce rising 3% a year; exploration of $9 million a year; corporate costs of $3 million; and capital spending of $20 million in 2027 as the mill is finished, $14 million in 2028 and $12 million a year afterward. That gives EBITDA of $17.4 million in 2027 and $31.1 million in 2031, and owner free cash flow of -$5.5 million in 2027, $5.5 million in 2028, $11.8 million in 2029 and $13.8 million in 2031. I assume a fourth-quarter 2026 outflow of about $3 million.

## Primary valuation: ten years of owner free cash flow

The primary method discounts a fourth-quarter 2026 stub and ten years of owner free cash flow, 2027 to 2036, at 10%, the framework default, and I test 9% and 11%. Beyond 2036 I assume no growth, because one underground mine with undisclosed reserves is not a business I can credit with growth in perpetuity, and the exploration spending is already in the cash flow. Owner free cash flow reaches $15.8 million in 2036.

The ten years are worth $3.74 a share, the terminal value $3.73 and the net cash adds $4.20. The primary value is **$11.67**.

Terminal value share: 50% of enterprise value, at a ten-year horizon, below the 75% level where I would check that the terminal value is supported by sustainable flows. The weak point is not the terminal share but the reserve life, which the company has not disclosed, so a mine that runs out before 2036 would make this value too high.

## Secondary valuation: five years of cash flow and an exit at 5 times EBITDA

The cross-check takes the stub and five years of cash flow to the end of 2031 and values the company at that date at 5 times that year's EBITDA of $31.1 million, or $155.6 million. Discounted at 10%, the cash flow adds $1.49 a share, the exit adds $5.90 and the net cash adds $4.20. The secondary value is **$11.59**.

**Why 5 times.** The sector guide puts commodity producers at 5 to 8 times, and I take the bottom because this is a single underground mine with no published reserve life, a high exploration cost and a customer base of one concentrate broker. In 2031 my Base turns 44% of EBITDA into owner free cash flow, because tax and capital spending take the rest, so 5 times EBITDA is 11.2 times free cash flow. At a 10% return that implies growth of about 1% a year afterward, roughly no growth, which is what I assumed in the primary method. The market pays 9.6 times my 2027 EBITDA today, a multiple that only works if production keeps growing.

Exit value share: 80% of enterprise value comes from the exit, at a horizon of five years, below the 85% level where I check that the exit is supported by sustainable flows.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Run backward on the Base case, $14.61 needs one of these: a gold price of about $4,070 in 2027, 10% above my Base and 17% above spot, with the same 2% yearly rise; or production about 16% above my Base in every year; or a discount rate of 6.7% instead of 10%; or an exit multiple of about 10 times EBITDA in the cross-check. When I replace my production path with management's goal of 20,000 ounces by 2029 and keep gold at $3,700, the value is $14.32. In other words, the price pays for the company reaching its production goal at a gold price that is 6% above where gold trades today.

That is why the verdict is Hold and not Reduce. The 25.6% premium to Base is below the 30% level at which the framework calls a stock overvalued enough to reduce, and the company has a plausible route to the target. But the verdict depends on the gold deck: at the spot price of $3,476 the Base is $9.83, the premium is 49%, and the verdict would be Reduce. That is a close call and I want readers to see it.

As a return, buying at $14.61 and holding the Base case to the end of 2031 gives about **2.0% a year**, with the cash that accumulates added to an exit at 5 times EBITDA, well below the 10% I require. The same math gives -15.4% a year in Bear and 17.1% in Bull. The company pays no dividend, so the return comes from the gold price, the ounces and the exit.

On earnings, the shares trade at about 21 times my 2027 operating profit after tax and 9.6 times my 2027 EBITDA, which is not cheap for a mine that has to spend $20 million on capital next year and produces negative free cash flow in 2027. A growth-adjusted ratio (PEG) would reward the earnings rebound of the last two years, but that rebound was the gold price, so I do not rely on it. Growth durability: ounce growth from the new mill and the Paymaster area is the plan, but it is not yet shown in the results, since third-quarter output was flat. The cash flow growth in my Base comes from higher ounces and capital spending falling after the mill is finished, not from a rising gold price beyond 2% a year.

## Reasons to own IDR

- **High-grade ore.** Feed grade was 9.26 grams per tonne in the third quarter and 10.14 for 2025, with recovery of 92.6%.
- **A strong balance sheet.** Net cash is $67.2 million, 29% of the share price, with about $1.9 million of notes.
- **A growth plan with work done.** The mill is being finished, the tailings facility is 75% complete and the Paymaster ramp is under development. Reserves grew 53.2% in 2025.
- **Profit even at lower gold.** Operating income was $14.1 million in nine months, and by my arithmetic the third quarter would still have been marginally profitable at spot gold.
- **Leverage to gold.** A 10% rise in gold lifts the Base by about 26%, to $14.61.
- **Free options.** The rare earth and thorium prospects and the Murray Gold Belt land package carry no value in my scenarios.

## What could go wrong?

- **The gold price.** Gold fell about 23% in eight months, and with a cost of $2,450 an ounce the margin disappears quickly. This is the Bear case.
- **One mine.** All of the revenue comes from the Golden Chest, and the company has not disclosed reserve ounces or mine life, so ounces could run out or the grade could fall further. The third-quarter grade was 6.8% lower.
- **Rising costs.** The all-in sustaining cost rose 28% in 2025 and is flat at about $2,450 in 2026, and the cash cost per ounce rose 13% in the quarter.
- **Dilution.** Shares rose 15% in 2025, and an at-the-market program of up to $20 million is available.
- **Execution on the mill.** The release gives no commissioning date and no cost.
- **Customer concentration.** One broker, H&H Metals, was the sole concentrate buyer, according to the quarterly report.
- **Liquidity and size.** Market value is about $230 million and the stock is volatile, so prices can gap in either direction.

## Management execution

Management, led by President and CEO John Swallow, has taken the company from a small explorer to a profitable producer, grew reserves 53.2% in 2025, is on pace for a record number of drilled meters (about 25,500 through the third quarter of 2026) and kept the net cash position while building a mill with its own labor. Against that, it has issued shares aggressively, the all-in cost has risen, the new mill has no stated date or budget, there is no production or cost guidance, and the shares fell 73% from the high. I did not review pay or related-party items in detail.
