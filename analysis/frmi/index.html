Fermi's third quarter release reads like a lease: a hyperscaler, 600 megawatts, $200 million paid in advance. The quarterly report calls it a non-binding letter of intent. The shares have gained +61% since November 10 on that news and still trade 88.2% below the $21.00 IPO price, which makes the open question how much to pay for a signature that has not happened.

At $2.48 the shares trade 63.1% above my weighted Base value of $1.52, so the Base is 38.7% below the price. The Bull value of $5.59 is 125.2% above the price and the Bear value is zero. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade D, which sets a required margin of safety of 60% and a maximum buy price of $0.61. The verdict is Reduce: even if the lease is signed on the terms I assume, the price is already above what it is worth, and the dates that decide the funding fall before any rent does.

Valuation route: a pre-revenue developer of a private power and data center campus, so it is valued as a path from the money still to be spent to a first lease and the value of that lease once it is running, less everything owed ahead of shareholders. Primary method: those project cash flows discounted at 12%, with a stabilized exit value. Cross-check: book value per diluted share. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Fermi is

Fermi (operating as Fermi America) is a Dallas developer of Project Matador, a private power and data center campus near Amarillo, Texas, on land leased from the Texas Tech University System. It plans to generate its own electricity on site and lease "powered land" to customers who build the data halls. It listed on Nasdaq in October 2025 at $21.00 a share. It has no revenue. Management cites 2.2 gigawatts (GW) of current capacity and a plan that reaches about 17 GW if customers sign binding agreements.

Valuation triangulation

  • Primary (project cash flows, 12% rate): $1.58, weight 60%.
  • Secondary (book value per diluted share): $1.43, weight 40%.
  • Divergence between the two: 10.3% of their midpoint, inside the 20% limit.
  • Weighted Base: $1.52.

The two methods agree, but they share a diluted share count and the same spending to date, and the primary method includes the cost still to come. They differ in what they measure: one prices the lease I assume, the other the capital already sunk. I weight the primary method higher because its assumptions can be read and changed. The agreement is partly luck, since a $0.60 million rent happens to return about what has been spent. A growth-adjusted valuation ratio is not meaningful with no earnings.

Dividend and buybacks

Fermi pays no dividend and has no buyback. It has paid for the build with debt, equity and shares used as pay: stock compensation was $184.5 million in nine months, and the charter limits holders to 2.5% of the shares, which the company discloses may inhibit large investors from buying.

Reasons to own Fermi

  • A counterparty is spending money. An investment-grade hyperscaler has committed a $200 million prepayment and $15 million for early construction ahead of a lease, and the Financial Times reported it is Amazon, which is expected to build its own data centers on the site.
  • The site is far along. Net property is $1,669.6 million, and nearly $1.7 billion has been invested. First power is targeted for mid 2027.
  • The customer's money matters. The $200 million prepayment and the $90 million steel sale together would fund about $290 million of the $1.0 billion still to be spent in my Base.
  • Overhead is smaller than the loss suggests: 54.9 million of cash costs in the quarter, against $142.2 million of reported expense.
  • Some legal weight has come off. The Firebird case was dismissed with prejudice on October 29, and a related party paid the settlement. The claim of more than $415 million over Class B units was sent to arbitration on November 12 with no liability recorded.
  • The option is large. A second phase of about 704 MW on my terms would justify today's price, and management cites 2.2 GW of capacity. My Bull value is $5.59.

What could go wrong?

  • The lease is not signed. The letter of intent is non-binding and needs definitive documents. TensorWave signed in August and was terminated in November, so a signature is not a closing.
  • Financing dates come first. Keystone's $91.5 million needs an approved customer agreement by December 31, MUFG's $444.9 million is in default without committed financing for 400 MW by February 10, 2027, and it matures on August 10, 2027. The filing's going concern language rests on these plans working.
  • The rent is unknown. Every value here rests on my assumption of $0.60 million a MW. At $0.52 million the weighted Base is $1.17.
  • Dilution. Shares are used as pay and for fees, 22.7 million more are contingently issuable to an advisor and a new raise is likely if debt markets wait. My Bear case has 780 million shares.
  • Governance and legal. The founder and former CEO has fought the board in public filings and sold 5 million shares at $1.52 on November 5. A securities class action is pending, a Bayonne claim of about $8.5 million is in court, and the Class B claim above $415 million is in arbitration. A related party paid the Firebird settlement, and the law firm of the general counsel, who joined in July, received $579 thousand in legal fees.
  • The land. The Texas Tech lease allows termination if the notice to proceed is not given by December 31, 2026, unless extended. The chancellor has reaffirmed the partnership.
  • Power plant costs. A contingent Siemens obligation could reach about $240 million undiscounted, a lease for seven GE mobile units runs to 2045 from the third quarter of 2027, and 2.5% of income from the first 1 GW is owed under a profits interest.

Management execution

  • Spending has run ahead of contracts. Almost $1.7 billion is invested, no lease is signed, and the one lease that was signed has been terminated.
  • The team is new at the top. Lee McIntire became chief executive in August and has since produced a customer commitment, which is credible progress and still a letter.
  • Costs are under control only in the cash line. Cash overhead was 54.9 million in the third quarter, up from about $42.0 million in the second by a third-party calculation, and stock and share-settled fees were 61% of reported expense.
  • The quarterly report is detailed on debt, commitments and covenants, which makes the deadline table above possible. It does not give the rent, the term, the total cost to finish the first phase or a financing plan for it.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • A signed long-term lease for the 600 MW with a disclosed term and rent at or above my $0.60 million a MW.
  • Committed financing for at least 400 MW before February 10, 2027, or a waiver, and approval of the customer agreement for Keystone by December 31.
  • Definitive Hillcore documents by December 18 and the Texas Tech notice to proceed on time.

Yellow

  • Deadlines are met only through amendments, extensions or new fees.
  • The lease is signed at a rent below about $0.52 million a MW, or with a shorter term.
  • Cash overhead rises above the $54.9 million of the third quarter.

Red

  • The letter of intent lapses or the customer pays less than the $200 million.
  • A lender deadline passes without a waiver, or the company sells shares below the market to meet it.
  • Texas Tech exercises its right to terminate the ground lease.

The hard data are the December 1 mobilization, the December 18 and December 31 dates, and the February 10 financing test.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The rent comes in at $0.52 million a MW and not $0.60 million. The weighted Base falls to $1.17, 52.9% below the price.
  2. The lease and first power come a year late. The weighted Base falls to $1.23, 50.4% below the price.
  3. Share count rises to 780 million, through new equity or share-settled fees. The weighted Base is $1.31, 47.2% below the price.

Final verdict: reduce at $2.48

The next hard dates are December 18, December 31 and February 10, and they arrive before any rent does.
