Rheinmetall's sales rose 38% in nine months and its operating free cash flow was minus €1.7 billion

RHM, Xetra (Frankfurt). Published November 24, 2026. Price as of the Monday, November 23, 2026 Xetra regular session close (€725.20). Values in euros. Rheinmetall is a German maker of armoured vehicles, ammunition, air defence, digital command systems and, since 2026, warships. Operating result is the company's own adjusted profit measure, before purchase accounting effects and special items. EBITDA is operating result plus depreciation. Free cash flow to the firm is the cash left after operating costs, taxes, capital spending and working capital, before paying lenders and owners. Working capital is the money tied up in inventory and receivables, net of customer prepayments. Cash conversion is operating free cash flow as a share of operating result. Backlog is signed orders plus expected call-offs under signed framework agreements. SAFE is the EU loan programme for defence purchases. Arminius is the German order for Boxer armoured vehicles.

In the first nine months of 2026 Rheinmetall grew sales 38% to €8.4 billion and earned a 16.0% operating margin. Its operating free cash flow was minus €1.7 billion. The company then cut its 2026 sales guidance for the second time this year, and the shares have fallen from €1,966 in January to €725.20.

My Base value is €931, 22% above today's price. The Bear value of €375 is 48% below the price and the Bull value of €1,756 is 142% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is €998. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, so the maximum buy price is €535, or €519 to €560 depending on the method. The upside to Base is 28.4%. I call it Hold: there is real value here, but not enough cushion for a business that has not yet shown it can turn growth into cash.

Valuation route: a defence prime contractor, so I value it on ten years of discounted free cash flow to the firm as the primary method and on an exit multiple of FY31 EBITDA as the second. Customer prepayments are treated as working capital, not as income. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets. This analysis is conditional on the capital markets day in Hamburg on November 25 to 27, where management says it will give 2027 and longer-term guidance.

What Rheinmetall is

Rheinmetall makes the armoured vehicles, ammunition, air defence systems and digital command networks that European armies are buying in large numbers. Its main customer is the German armed forces, followed by other European NATO members. The group now reports five segments: Vehicle Systems (Boxer, Puma, Lynx and military trucks), Weapon and Ammunition, Air Defence, Digital Systems and Naval Systems. Naval Systems came with the purchase of the military shipbuilder NVL at the end of February 2026, and the buyer and seller agreed not to disclose the price.

The civilian car parts business, Power Systems, has been classified as discontinued since the fourth quarter of 2025. On June 3, 2026 Rheinmetall signed its sale to the Munich group Aequita for a provisional €350 million, with closing targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026. A second piece, a joint venture, stays in the group for the medium term. CEO Armin Papperger has led the company since 2013 and, according to press reports, bought about €0.7 million of shares on November 5 and 6.

Why the guidance fell

On November 5 Rheinmetall cut its 2026 sales outlook to about €13.2 billion. In March it had guided to €14.0 billion to €14.5 billion, and in August to €13.7 billion to €14.2 billion. The reasons given are Arminius, which cannot be commissioned before next year, and delayed customer acceptance tests of artillery ammunition lots. The shares fell 16% on the day.

Net financial debt went from €369 million of net cash at the end of 2025 to €2.9 billion of debt in nine months. The company drew €1.0 billion of a €1.5 billion club loan in March, placed a €500 million bond at 3.375% due 2031 in May, took short-term deposits of €810 million, and paid €535 million of dividends to its own shareholders on May 15. It also spent about €1.1 billion on acquisitions and investments in consolidated companies, which includes NVL. After the quarter it raised its syndicated credit line from €750 million to €1.5 billion.

Two features of this bridge are estimates. The balance sheet does not show lease liabilities separately, so I assume they sit inside financial liabilities. The minority interests, which sit in partly owned subsidiaries and joint ventures, took €115 million of nine-month profit, or about 15% of net income. I deduct 13% of value for them, because the group's profit is shifting toward wholly owned segments.

Shares and ownership

Rheinmetall had 46.79 million shares after the last convertible bond tranche converted in July 2026. The convertible was called in May and the last €4 million of it converted into 12,344 shares, so there is no meaningful dilution left. At €725.20 the equity is worth €33.9 billion. Counting net debt, pensions, the Power Systems proceeds and the minorities, the enterprise value is about €41.8 billion, or 11.5 times my FY27 Base EBITDA of €3.6 billion.

Primary valuation: discounted free cash flow to the firm

Sales. The 2026 guide is €13.2 billion. I take €17.0 billion for FY27, which includes the Arminius shift and a full year of NVL, then €20.5 billion, €24.0 billion and €27.5 billion, reaching €30.0 billion in FY31. After that growth fades from 6% to 3% a year. My FY30 figure of €27.5 billion is 55% of the €50 billion that management targets for 2030, and I use it because the company has cut its own 2026 sales number twice this year. The €78.0 billion backlog is 5.9 times 2026 sales.

Margin and cash. The operating margin is 19.0% in FY27 and 20.0% from FY29, against 18.5% guided for 2026 and a target of more than 20% for 2030. Taxes are 28% of operating result, close to the 27% the group paid on pre-tax profit in the first nine months. Depreciation rises from 2.4% to 3.3% of sales. Capital spending is 7.6% of sales in FY27, above €1 billion again, and falls to 4.2% by FY32 as the new plants are built. Working capital absorbs 12% of each extra euro of sales, which gives a pre-tax cash conversion of about 50% in FY27, between the 40% floor management gave in March and the 50% multi-year average it targeted in 2025. Q4 2026 free cash flow is €1.5 billion, close to what the guidance needs: nine-month operating free cash flow was minus €1.7 billion, so a positive full year needs at least €1.7 billion before taxes in Q4. I discount at 9%.

Secondary valuation: exit multiple on FY31 EBITDA

The second method keeps the cash flows for five years and then values the group at 10 times FY31 EBITDA of €7.0 billion. Today the group trades at 11.5 times my FY27 Base EBITDA, so 10 times is a discount to the current multiple once growth has slowed to 9% in FY31 and capital spending still takes 20% of EBITDA. The exit value is 81% of the five year enterprise value, below the 85% level that needs close checking. The result is €974.

What is the market already pricing in?

At €725.20 the enterprise value is €41.8 billion. Holding margins at my Base path, the price needs 2030 sales of about €21.2 billion, which is 77% of my Base out-year path and 42% of management's target. Holding my Base sales instead, the price needs margins about 4 points lower, near 16% against 20%. My Base cash flows would earn about 10.5% a year at today's price, against the 9% I discount them at, and my Bull case would earn 15%. On rough FY27 Base earnings of about €38 a share, after purchase accounting of €200 million and interest of €170 million (my estimates), the price earnings ratio is 19.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is grade C because the sales and margin path from FY27, the Q4 cash flow, the minority share and the lease liabilities are my estimates, the company gave no 2027 guidance, and the capital markets day is still ahead. The margin of safety is the standard 42.5% for grade C. I add nothing for the method gap, which is small, or for defence cyclicality, which the Bear case already carries. The maximum buy price is €931 times 0.575, or €535, with an entry range of €519 to €560 by method. At a 10% discount rate the Base value is €838 and the maximum buy price €482. The price is 35% above my maximum buy price, so this is a Hold and not a Buy.

Dividend

The dividend for 2025 was €11.50 a share, up from €8.10, and cost €535 million. At €725.20 that is a yield of 1.6%. The payout was 45.5% of 2025 earnings. My Base FY27 free cash flow to the firm of €986 million would cover a dividend of that size about 1.8 times before interest, but the nine months to September did not cover it, so for now the dividend is funded by new debt. The yield is not a reason to own the shares.

Reasons to own

  • The backlog is €78.0 billion, 5.9 times 2026 sales, and 70% of it is firm orders against 58% a year ago.
  • Margins are rising: Q3 operating margin was 17.5%, and Air Defence earned 23.2% on 65% faster sales in the quarter.
  • European defence budgets and the EU SAFE loans are funding orders that Rheinmetall has already won, such as the 298 Lynx vehicles for Romania.
  • The sale of Power Systems for €350 million turns the group into a pure defence business.
  • The price is 63% below the January high and the 2026 earnings are higher than 2025, so the multiple has compressed sharply.

What could go wrong?

  • Cash. Operating free cash flow was minus €1.7 billion in nine months, net debt is €2.9 billion, and the full-year guide of above 0% depends on a very large Q4 inflow.
  • Orders. Arminius has slipped from year end 2026 to the first quarter of 2027, and a further delay would push sales and prepayments out again. Q3 order intake fell 13%.
  • Peace talks. Ukraine negotiation headlines have pushed European defence shares down on several days this autumn, and a ceasefire would test how much of the order book is tied to the war.
  • Price audits. The customer is checking Arminius prices before signing, and a lower price would reduce margin on the group's largest vehicle contract.
  • Execution. New plants, ammunition acceptance tests and the shipyards bought in March all have to work at once.
  • Concentration. Germany is the largest customer, so a change in its budget or procurement rules matters more than any single order.

Management execution

Management has delivered margin: operating margin was 13.1% in the first nine months of 2025 and 16.0% in 2026, and the 18.5% guided for the year equals what it earned in 2025. It has not delivered its sales and cash numbers. The 2026 sales guide fell from €14.0 billion to €13.2 billion within eight months, and cash conversion from above 40% to above 0%. It also spent about €1.1 billion on acquisitions and investments while operating cash flow was negative. Against that, it sold the car parts business, and the CEO is reported to have bought shares after the price fell.

What I would watch from here

The capital markets day in Hamburg on November 25 to 27 is the first test. Management has said it will give 2027 and longer-term guidance there. The next dated events are the Arminius signing, expected in the first quarter of 2027, and the 2026 annual report on March 11, 2027. I could not find a verified options-implied move for the capital markets day, so I do not use options pricing.

Green: 2027 guidance of €16 billion or more in sales with cash conversion above 40%, an Arminius contract with a down payment, and a price at or below €535.

Yellow: 2027 sales guidance below €16 billion, margin guidance below 18%, capital spending above €1.3 billion again, or net debt above €3.5 billion at year end.

Red: a lower 2030 target with no cash conversion path, a further Arminius delay beyond the first half of 2027, or negative operating free cash flow for the full year 2026.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Orders do not convert. If out-year sales are 23% below my Base, at about €21 billion in 2030, the value falls to today's price. With sales 15% lower and margins 2 points lower together, my Base becomes €709.
  2. Margins break. If margins settle about 4 points below my path, near 16%, the Base value is the price and there is no upside left.
  3. Cash stays negative. Working capital taking 20% of extra sales instead of 12% lowers the Base only to €907, so the cash conversion fear is a timing risk for the stock more than a value risk, unless it forces equity or a dividend cut.

Final verdict: hold at €725.20

Rheinmetall is a defence company growing sales 38% with a €78 billion backlog, and at €725.20 the market is paying for about 42% of the sales it targets for 2030. That is a reasonable price for a business with that backlog, but not a cheap one for a business whose cash flow has gone the wrong way for three quarters. I would not sell here, and I would not buy until the price reaches €535 or the company shows the cash.

Verdict: HOLD at €725.20, watchlist for new money below €535. Triangulated Base value €931, upside +28.4%, Confidence C (15.0/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by method €519 to €560, maximum buy price €535. Conditional on the capital markets day on November 25 to 27, 2026.

The shares are priced for a company that reaches about €21 billion of sales by 2030, and in Hamburg management will say whether it still believes in €50 billion.
