CIBC: a 16.9% adjusted return on equity, and a price that needs 17.8% for ten years

CM, Toronto and New York. Published November 21, 2026. Price is the November 20, 2026 Toronto close of C$147.36, equal to about US$102.67 on the NYSE. Values in Canadian dollars. The fiscal year ends October 31, so "FY27" is the year to October 2027. ROE is return on common equity.

Hold at C$147.36, do not add

The price is 12.0% above my Base value of C$131.61, inside the 15% band where I call a stock fairly valued. Earnings are strong, but the price needs a return on equity that I do not expect to last. My maximum buy price is C$95.42.

In the nine months to July 31, 2026 CIBC reported net income of C$7,974 million, which is C$170 million more than its adjusted figure of C$7,804 million. The reason is a C$422 million income tax recovery that outweighed the Caribbean sale charge, so the 17.3% reported return on equity flatters the bank, and the adjusted 16.9% is the number to use. At C$147.36 the shares trade at 2.26 times book value, and my model says that price needs a return on equity of about 17.8% for ten years straight.

CIBC is one of Canada's big five banks and it has had a very good year. Third-quarter revenue grew 15% to C$8,368 million, adjusted net income grew 26% to C$2,648 million, and the common equity tier 1 ratio, the main regulatory measure of capital strength, was 13.4%. The stock then lost about 11% from its July close of C$166.04, including a 3.2% drop on November 20 as the Toronto index fell to a six-month low.

My Base value is C$131.61, which is 12.0% below the price. The Bear value of C$96.97 is 34.2% below the price and the Bull value of C$156.40 is 6.1% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is C$129.15. At Confidence B my rules require a 27.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of C$95.42, or C$88.95 to C$105.12 depending on the method. The verdict is Hold for existing holders, and new money should wait.

Valuation route: a large Canadian bank, so I value it on residual income (earnings above the cost of equity, built on book value) as the primary method and on forward earnings as the second. Economic classification: Quality Growth (6/12). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

What CIBC is

CIBC has C$1,177.8 billion of assets, C$611.4 billion of loans and C$852.2 billion of deposits. It earns money in four groups. In the third quarter Canadian Personal and Business Banking earned C$948 million, Capital Markets C$722 million, Canadian Commercial Banking and Wealth Management C$619 million and the U.S. commercial and wealth business C$320 million, with Corporate and Other costing C$200 million. Canadian mortgages are a large part of its lending, so household debt is the risk that matters most.

In May the bank announced the sale of most of CIBC Caribbean, for about US$1.6 billion, and a new reporting structure with a leadership reshuffle. The sale costs C$232 million after tax in the third quarter, or C$0.26 per share, and is excluded from the adjusted figures.

Two parts of the quarter deserve a second look. The provision included a reversal on performing loans and an allowance release tied to the sale of a U.S. commercial real estate loan, so the credit charge of 0.40% of loans is helped by one-offs. And non-interest income, up 20%, depends on market-related fees, which rise and fall with markets. Net interest margin on interest-earning assets, excluding trading, was 2.07% against 1.94% a year earlier, which is the more durable part of the improvement.

Capital and share count

Common equity is C$59,146 million, or C$65.14 per share, on 907.9 million shares. Risk-weighted assets are C$369.3 billion. The common equity tier 1 ratio is 13.4%, the tier 1 ratio 15.6%, the total capital ratio 17.5%, the leverage ratio 4.3% and the liquidity coverage ratio 127%. The dividend is C$1.07 a quarter, or C$4.28 a year, a payout of 38.8% of adjusted earnings in the quarter. I use 915 million diluted shares for per-share earnings and book value per share as reported.

At 13.4% the capital ratio is high enough, and the Caribbean sale will add more when it closes, that I assume that total payout, dividends plus buybacks, reaches 60% of earnings in the Base case, which lets book value grow about 5.6% a year at a 14% return.

Primary valuation: residual income

Residual income values a bank as book value plus the present value of earnings above the cost of equity. I use a 9.0% cost of equity, which is my estimate: a Canadian long bond yield near 3.5% plus a 5.5% premium for equity risk at a beta close to 1. Base return on equity starts at 15.0%, which is a little below the adjusted 16.9% of the last nine months, fades to 14.0% by year five and stays there for ten years. Terminal return is 12.5% and terminal growth 3.0%. The 14% sits above the cost of equity because the franchise is large and efficient, with an adjusted efficiency ratio of 52.7%, and below the current level because the last nine months include a strong capital markets quarter and credit releases.

The cost of equity is the assumption that decides the verdict, and I cannot verify it from a filing.

Secondary valuation: forward earnings

Adjusted earnings per share over the last four quarters were C$10.24. For FY27 my Base estimate is C$11.60, which is 7.5% above the FY26 run rate of about C$10.80, helped by loan growth in commercial banking and a stable margin, and held back by a higher provision. I apply 12.5 times, in the range where the large Canadian banks have usually traded on forward earnings, and which fits a bank that earns 14% on equity with a growing book. The result is C$145.00. The multiple implies that earnings grow about 6% a year with a return on equity near 14%, which matches my residual income path.

The EPS estimates and multiples are mine and are not company guidance. Management said it expects continued positive operating leverage and will give more guidance at its December Investor Day.

Valuation triangulation

The two methods give C$122.69 and C$145.00, a divergence of 17%, which is below the 20% warning level. They are not fully independent, because both rest on the same earnings path. I weight residual income 60% and forward earnings 40%, because the first charges for the cost of equity explicitly and the second reflects what the market has paid for Canadian banks. The weighted value is C$131.61, a point inside the range. Which method is right matters here: on the second method alone the price is almost fair, and on the first it is 20.1% too high. The verdict is Hold on either, so the choice does not change the decision.

What is the market already pricing in?

Holding the cost of equity at 9.0%, the price of C$147.36 requires a return on equity of about 17.8%, held flat for ten years, or an equivalent combination of a lower cost of equity and a high return. The bank has earned 16.9% adjusted over nine months and 14.2% a year earlier, so 17.8% for a decade is above anything in the recent record. At the price, the bank trades at 2.26 times book value and 14.4 times trailing adjusted earnings. A year ago the market paid 1.65 times book, so most of the rerating is already in the price.

How the buy price is set

The base margin of safety is 30% at Confidence B. I subtracted 2.5 points for the strength of the capital position and the stability of a deposit-funded franchise, which gives 27.5%. I did not add points for cyclicality, because the Bear scenario already carries the credit cycle. The maximum buy price is C$131.61 times (1 - 27.5%), which is C$95.42, or C$88.95 to C$105.12 by method. The price is 54% above it. The stock would need to fall by 35% to reach the buy zone, which has happened to Canadian banks in recessions.

Dividend

The dividend is C$4.28 a year, a 2.9% yield, and covered by earnings with a payout of 38.8% of adjusted earnings. Even in my Bear case, EPS of C$9.20 covers it twice. It is not a reason to buy at this price: a 2.9% yield against a 9% cost of equity relies on growth to do the work.

Reasons to own

  • Adjusted return on equity of 16.8% in the quarter, up 2.6 points in a year, with an adjusted efficiency ratio of 52.7%.
  • Capital of 13.4% common equity tier 1 and a leverage ratio of 4.3%, with proceeds from the Caribbean sale still to come.
  • Nine consecutive quarters of double-digit revenue growth, from both net interest income (up 11%) and fees (up 20%).
  • A dividend that has stayed at C$1.07 a quarter with a payout under 40%.

What could go wrong?

  • Credit: Canadian household debt and mortgage renewals at higher rates, and U.S. commercial real estate, where the bank has been selling loans.
  • Quality of the earnings: provisions were helped by releases, and market-related fees are cyclical.
  • Valuation: the price is 2.3 times book against 1.65 a year ago, so a lower multiple hurts even if earnings hold.
  • Execution: the new reporting structure, the leadership changes announced in May and the Caribbean exit all happen in the same year.

Management execution

Management has delivered: adjusted EPS of C$2.73 in the third quarter, up from C$2.16 a year earlier, and an efficiency ratio that improved by 2 points. The weak point is how much of the improvement is repeatable. The nine-month reported figures include a C$422 million tax recovery, and the third-quarter provision includes releases. The December Investor Day is the place where the bank will say how much of this it expects to keep.

Confidence score

Confidence is B at 18.0 out of 25. Data quality scores 4 out of 5 because the results come from the bank's own filings, but gross impaired loans, the allowance and the mortgage detail were not in the material I could read. Valuation robustness scores 3, because the cost of equity moves the result by more than 20%. Business durability scores 4, management and governance 4, and the stability of the earnings base 3 given the one-offs.

What I would watch from here

Green: fourth-quarter results on December 3 show adjusted ROE near 16% with a provision ratio of 0.40% or lower and no new releases, and the Investor Day sets a medium-term ROE target above 15%.

Yellow: the provision ratio rises above 0.45% or gross impaired loans rise for two quarters, or the fourth-quarter margin falls below 2.0% on the measure excluding trading.

Red: adjusted ROE falls below 14%, the CET1 ratio drops below 13%, or the bank guides to negative operating leverage.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. A rise in mortgage or commercial real estate losses that takes the provision ratio above 0.60%.
  2. A drop in market-related fees that removes the capital markets gain in the third quarter.
  3. A cost of equity above 10%, which on my Base path lowers the value to C$103.61.

Final verdict: hold at C$147.36

CIBC is an efficient, well-capitalized bank with returns that have risen for two years. At C$147.36 the market already pays for a return on equity above anything it has delivered, and a year ago the stock traded at only 1.65 times book. My maximum buy price is C$95.42.

Verdict: HOLD at C$147.36, do not add. Triangulated Base value C$131.61, downside -10.7%, Confidence B (18.0/25). Required margin of safety 27.5%, entry range by method C$88.95 to C$105.12, maximum buy price C$95.42.

The date I would mark is December 3, when CIBC reports its fourth quarter, followed by the Investor Day. Both give a view of what return on equity management thinks is sustainable.
