# Unum reported a $419.7 million net loss and a $349.0 million adjusted profit in the same release

UNM, NYSE. Published November 17, 2026. Price as of the November 16, 2026 regular session close.

The catch is that group disability benefit costs are sharply higher than a year ago, and my Base value of $71.73 is 8.8% below the price. The maximum buy price is $41.24.

Unum reported a net loss of $419.7 million and an adjusted operating profit of $349.0 million in the same release. The $768.7 million between them is mostly an $853.0 million investment loss on assets Unum agreed to move to Fortitude Re, the reinsurer taking over a large block of its long-term care policies.

The loss is the less important number. Unum's original 2026 outlook assumed a group disability benefit ratio (benefits paid as a share of premium) of 62% to 64%, and it now targets about 65%. The third quarter came in at 67.1% as reported and at 70.6% once notable items are removed, against 61.3% a year earlier. Long-term disability claim submissions ran about 10% above management's expectations in the quarter, and early fourth-quarter submissions had not come down. Group disability profit fell 60.4% to $94.8 million, and the stock is down 14.7% in a month.

My triangulated Base value is **$71.73**, which is 8.8% below the $78.68 price (the November 16, 2026 close). The Bear value of $48.68 is 38.1% below the price and the Bull value of $87.42 is 11.1% above it. Confidence is grade C, which requires a 42.5% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at **$41.24**. The verdict is a hold, and new money should not add at this price.

Valuation route: Insurance, group benefits (disability, life, voluntary) with a closed block of long-term care policies. Economic classification: Standard (3.6/12, rescaled). Primary method: justified price to book value excluding unrealized investment losses. Secondary method: sum of the parts, with the core business on normalized earnings and the Closed Block as a separate claim. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

## What Unum does and why the headline profit misleads

Unum sells workplace insurance. Employers buy group disability (short and long term) and group life coverage for their staff, and employees buy extra protection through the same channel. Colonial Life is the voluntary benefits arm, and Unum International is mostly the United Kingdom. Quarterly premium income was $2,800.7 million, up 4.2%.

The sixth segment is the Closed Block, older long-term care and other policies that are no longer sold. Unum changed its headline adjusted operating income this year so that it excludes the Closed Block, and it recast last year's number to match. The Closed Block lost $135.6 million before tax in the third quarter. That loss is in the net loss and not in the $349.0 million, so I treat it as a cost of owning the stock and value it separately below.

The $349.0 million also includes about $0.33 per share of favorable notable items, mostly the annual reserve review. Without them the quarter earned about $1.87 per share, or $296.7 million.

## Which numbers matter for Unum

For an insurer, free cash flow is the wrong headline metric. Book value excluding unrealized investment losses, the return on it, and the benefit ratio that drives that return are the right ones. Equity excluding accumulated other comprehensive loss (AOCI, mostly unrealized bond losses) is $11,985.6 million, or **$76.73** a share on 156.2 million shares. I use it because Unum holds most of its bonds to pay claims and not to sell.

My normalized core earnings start from the third-quarter run rate without notable items, $296.7 million a quarter or $1,187 million a year. One point of group disability benefit ratio is worth $26.0 million after tax, or about $0.17 a share. In the Base case the ratio recovers 3.1 points, to about 67.5%, as double-digit price increases on short-term disability and paid family leave renew over the next year and long-term disability follows more slowly. That gives core earnings of **$1,267 million** a year, or $8.11 a share, about the 2026 guidance without the $0.33 notable item ($7.97 to $8.17). The Bear case holds the ratio near 72%, and the Bull case reaches management's long-term target of about 65%.

The Closed Block drag is my estimate. The Q3 loss without the $54.9 million reserve update was about $80.7 million, in line with the $90 to $100 million quarterly accounting cost management gave. I use a pre-tax drag of $280 million a year in the Base case ($360 million in the Bear case and $200 million in the Bull case), taxed at 21%. Base consolidated earnings are **$6.70** a share, a return of 8.7% on book excluding unrealized losses.

## Primary valuation: justified price to book value

The justified price to book multiple is (ROE minus growth) divided by (cost of equity minus growth). Growth of 3% and a cost of equity of 9.5% are my estimates. The book value is $76.73 per share in every case.

| Case | Sustainable ROE | P/B | Value per share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear | 7.1% | 0.63x | $48.33 |
| Base | 8.7% | 0.88x | $67.60 |
| Bull | 9.8% | 1.05x | $80.22 |

The multiple comes from the formula, not a tier. With a return below the cost of equity, a business is worth less than book, and with a return just above it, about book. The market price of $78.68 is 1.03x book excluding unrealized losses and 1.14x reported book value.

## Secondary valuation: sum of the parts

Here I value the core business on normalized earnings and subtract the Closed Block as a separate claim. The Closed Block drag is capitalized as a stream that declines 10% a year in the Base case (5% in the Bear case, 15% in the Bull case) and discounted at 9.5%.

| Case | Core earnings / share | P/E | Closed Block / share | Value per share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear | $7.26 | 8.5x | -$12.56 | $49.19 |
| Base | $8.11 | 10.5x | -$7.26 | $77.92 |
| Bull | $8.53 | 12.0x | -$4.13 | $98.21 |

I chose 10.5x because the core business pays out nearly all of what it earns (statutory operating earnings of about $299 million in the quarter match adjusted operating income), so an earnings yield of 9.5% equal to my cost of equity implies no growth in distributable earnings. Premium growth of 4% to 7% is assumed to offset claims inflation. The **secondary Base is $77.92**.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence C score carries a base margin of safety of 42.5%, the discount to estimated value I require before calling a stock a buy. I add nothing on top, because the claims uncertainty is in the Bear case and the Confidence grade. Applied to the $71.73 Base, the **maximum buy price is $41.24**, which is 15.3% below the Bear value. At a 30% margin the reference price would be $50.21. ## Reasons to own UNM

- **Repricing power.** A significant majority of the group block is outside multiyear rate guarantees, so short-term disability and paid family leave prices can reset within about one annual cycle.
- **Colonial Life.** Income rose 35% and sales 6.4% in the quarter, with a benefit ratio of 47% excluding notable items.
- **Less long-term care risk.** Exposure is down more than 40% after two Fortitude Re deals, and Fairwind, the long-term care subsidiary, holds about $2 billion of excess capital.
- **Capital strength.** A risk-based capital ratio of 485% and $1.38 billion of holding company liquidity.
- **A low multiple.** 9.4x guided earnings and 1.03x book excluding unrealized losses, with about $1.3 billion a year returned to shareholders.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: disability claims stay high.** Long-term disability submissions were about 10% above expectations (about 5 points of benefit ratio) and short-term disability and paid family leave added about 2 points. Early fourth-quarter submissions had not fallen, and management does not know yet whether the rise lasts.
- **Pricing takes time.** Short-term disability and paid family leave get double-digit increases that renew annually, so relief comes through 2027. Long-term disability uses a longer experience window and renews more slowly.
- **No 2027 outlook.** Management will say more after fourth-quarter results. The 2026 earnings guidance was cut to $8.30 to $8.50 a share.
- **The Closed Block.** Earnings stay volatile as group long-term care cases terminate and the Fortitude Re deals add $90 to $100 million a quarter of accounting costs at first.
- **The United Kingdom.** The benefit ratio was 80.5% without notable items and international sales fell 41.6%.
- **Earnings quality.** The reserve review released $83.7 million before tax in the core business and added $0.33 to the quarter. The headline definition now leaves out the Closed Block.

## Management execution

Management moved long-term care risk off the balance sheet twice, kept the core premium growth guidance at 4% to 7%, and returned more than $1 billion in nine months. It also lowered earnings guidance, and it paid an average of about $96 for the shares it bought in the third quarter. **Assessment: 13/20, acceptable. The next test is whether fourth-quarter submissions fall and whether the pricing actions show up in the 2027 outlook.**

## Final verdict: hold at $78.68, do not add

Unum has a profitable core, a strong risk-based capital ratio and a lower exposure to long-term care. It also has a group disability line whose benefit ratio is sharply higher than a year ago, an earnings headline that now leaves out a loss-making block, and a stock that needs the recovery to arrive on schedule. At $78.68 the price is 9.7% above my Base value and 90.8% above the maximum buy price. Existing holders have no thesis breaker to act on yet. New money should wait.

**Verdict: HOLD at $78.68. Triangulated Base value $71.73, downside 8.8%, Confidence C (15/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $41.24.**

The case improves if the price falls toward $41 with the balance sheet intact, or if fourth-quarter results show the benefit ratio falling toward 65% and the 2027 outlook confirms the pricing actions. Management bought shares at about $96 and the benefit ratio for the next two quarters will tell whether that was a mistake.
