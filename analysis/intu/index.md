## What Intuit is

Intuit, based in Mountain View, California, runs four main businesses. TurboTax sells tax preparation to consumers and has about 39 million federal filers; QuickBooks sells accounting and payments software to small and mid-sized businesses; Credit Karma earns fees from lenders and insurers that it matches with consumers; and Mailchimp is an email marketing service. Together they serve about 100 million customers. Global Business Solutions, which holds QuickBooks and Mailchimp, brought in $12.9 billion of the $21.4 billion of revenue in fiscal 2026.

Fiscal 2026 was a record on paper: revenue up 14%, free cash flow of $8.6 billion and $6.8 billion returned to shareholders (Investor Day summary, Quartr). It was also a hard year for the shares. In May Intuit announced that it would cut about 17% of its staff, and the shares fell 20% in one day, the largest fall since 2003 (Bloomberg Government). In August it guided fiscal 2027 growth down to 9% to 10% from 14% and said it would accept slower revenue to win new customers; the chief executive said that AI will be a disruptor and Intuit intends to be the disruptor, and that the company may lower prices to gain share (CoinCentral). At the Investor Day in September management said it had missed its new-customer targets in do-it-yourself tax filing and in the core QuickBooks Online business (Cerbat Gem).

The pieces are unequal. In the first quarter, QuickBooks Online Accounting revenue grew 19% and Global Business Solutions grew 15%, while TurboTax grew 1% and Mailchimp fell 1%. Intuit has now agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion, of which $3.31 billion is cash and $368 million is Bending Spoons stock, with the sale expected to close in early calendar 2027 and the proceeds earmarked for share repurchases. Guidance excludes the sale. That is about 2.9 times Mailchimp's guided revenue of $1.26 billion.

## Owner free cash flow

I start from GAAP operating profit. Intuit's own adjusted figures used to add back stock pay, which made earnings look larger; since August 1 it no longer does, which is why the new non-GAAP guidance looked so weak against old estimates (Proactive Investors). My measure was already after stock pay. I apply the company's 24% long-term tax rate, add depreciation and amortization of acquired intangibles (3.2% of revenue; $212 million in the first quarter, of which $165 million was amortization of acquisitions, which is not a cash cost), subtract capital spending of 3.0% of revenue (my estimate, including capitalized software, which I did not retrieve separately; purchases of property were $75 million in the quarter) and subtract 2% of every extra dollar of revenue for working capital.

- Profit is growing fast after costs were cut: GAAP operating income up 63% in the quarter and guided up 26% to 27% for the year, on revenue growth of 8% to 9%.
- QuickBooks and the mid-market business are strong: Global Business Solutions grew 15% and QuickBooks Online Accounting 19% in the quarter.

- The price assumes a lot of damage: my Base cash flows reach it at a discount rate of 17% or a margin 15 points lower.

## What could go wrong?

- AI disruption. Tax preparation and small-business accounting are the kind of products AI tools target, and the chief executive said that Intuit may cut prices to gain share. My Bear case, at $148.96, is 47% below the price.
- Consumer weakness. TurboTax grew 1% in the quarter, federal filers fell 2% in fiscal 2026 and Credit Karma guidance was cut from 11% to 13% growth to 3% to 7%.
- Customer growth. Management missed its new-customer targets in do-it-yourself tax and QuickBooks Online and is guiding slower revenue to fix it.
- Guidance resets. Intuit has lowered expectations in May, August and now November; the next reset would hurt a share price that already moves 5% or more on news.
- Accounting change. Non-GAAP figures now include stock pay, so old consensus numbers are not comparable; my valuation was not affected, because it already deducted it.
- Mailchimp. The sale needs regulatory approval, and part of the price is paid in Bending Spoons stock.
- Data gaps. Capitalized software, the principal of each note, the effective tax rate beyond fiscal 2027, competitor data and the detail of the Credit Karma change were not available to me.

## Management execution

The offsets: the company cut 17% of its staff in May, missed new-customer targets, reset guidance twice this year and now trimmed it again. He is chairman and chief executive. **Assessment: 15/20, strong on cost, capital return and the business platform, weaker on consumer growth and forecasting. The next test is the second-quarter report, in late February, which includes the first read of the tax season.**

- TurboTax and Credit Karma growth at or above the new guidance, and customer growth restored.
- GAAP operating margin above 33% in fiscal 2028, with stock pay falling as a share of revenue.
- A price at or below $268.61, or Mailchimp sold on schedule and the proceeds spent on shares.

### Yellow

- Consumer revenue below its 2% to 4% guide.
- Another cut to Credit Karma or TurboTax guidance.

### Red

- QuickBooks Online growth falling below 15% or small-business customers leaving.
- TurboTax prices cut to defend share, with units still falling.
- A new guidance reset that lowers operating income.

Today profit, balance sheet and capital return are green; consumer growth and AI are yellow, and the price is yellow, because it is below my Base but above my buy price. That is why the verdict is hold: the business is worth more than the price, and my rules still ask for 5% more.

## The three things that would make this verdict wrong

1. AI damage worse than my Bear. At 10 points lower margin in every year the value is $344.33, still above the price; a combination of lower margins and falling revenue as in my Bear gives $148.96.
2. Slower growth than my path. At 3 points less growth every year the Base is $394.72.
3. A higher discount rate. At 17% my Base equals the price; at 12% it is $391.77. A rate somewhat above 10% is possible, but not 17%.

The verdict could also be wrong in the other direction: a quick fall of 5% would put the shares in the buy zone, and my rules would then say buy.
