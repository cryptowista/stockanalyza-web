## What Alight is

Alight runs benefits administration for large employers: health, retirement and leave programs, plus the technology and call centers behind them. In the second quarter, revenue was $511 million and 92.2% of it was recurring. That recurrence is the franchise. Employers do not move such a program lightly, and a failed migration is painful for the client.

It is not a software subscription business, and I do not value it as one. Only part of revenue is subscription-based, the reported gross margin was 27.8% in the second quarter, and I could not verify the annual recurring revenue, retention or customer acquisition figures that a software multiple would need. Recurring does not mean growing: recurring revenue fell from $492 million to $471 million in the second quarter, and project revenue rose from $36 million to $40 million, which cushioned the total. Adjusted EBITDA fell from $127 million to $92 million over the same comparison. Management reaffirmed the 2026 outlook on October 14: revenue of $2,078 million to $2,098 million and adjusted EBITDA of $400 million to $415 million.

The company also changed shape. In 2024 it sold its payroll and HCM business, so older revenue and cash flow figures are not comparable, and 2025 included large goodwill impairments. Those are not cash, but they tell you that earlier expectations were too high.

## Which cash to value

Reported free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital spending. It leaves out two real claims: finance lease principal, and the payments under the tax receivable agreement (TRA). The TRA is a contract that passes part of certain tax savings to other parties. Alight classifies those payments as financing, so they never touch free cash flow, but the cash leaves all the same. Stock compensation is the third item: it costs nothing in cash but dilutes owners, so I charge it at a cash equivalent of $22 million a year going forward.

The same bridge for earlier years: owner cash of minus $64 million in 2023, minus $93 million in 2024, plus $109 million in 2025 and minus $55 million in the first half of 2026. A growth rate across those years is not meaningful, because the endpoints change sign, and I do not print one.

Cash is counted once. Of the $215 million of corporate cash I treat $100 million as the working minimum, which leaves $115 million as a credit in the valuation, before whatever Abett cost. The $233 million of fiduciary cash is client money with an equal liability, so it adds nothing.

## The secondary valuation: enterprise value

The second method starts from the business and then pays all claims. Economic EBITDA is first-year adjusted EBITDA less compensation and restructuring cost: 407.5 minus 22 minus 35, or 350.5. At 7.5 times, my assumption for a mature service business, the enterprise is worth $2,628.75 million. Subtract $2,000 million of bank debt, $45 million of finance leases and the $463 million TRA, add $115 million of cash and the $45 million note, and common equity is $280.75 million, or **$9.83** a share. The 7.5 times is my estimate, equal to 6.45 times the guidance midpoint of adjusted EBITDA, not a peer median. A complete peer panel is not something I could verify, so I use no peer-derived target.

The two methods are built differently but are not independent. Both rely on the business holding its revenue, both pay the same TRA and both assume the same share count. When they agree, it shows my own inputs are consistent, and it earns no confidence. A balance sheet check is worse: common equity is mostly intangibles and tangible common equity is negative, so there is no asset floor under the shares.

## Growth and yield checks

The check that makes Alight look cheapest is the forward earnings multiple. A third-party forecast page, last updated October 5 and so older than the refinancing news, shows adjusted earnings per share of $10.00 for 2025 (split adjusted), $4.77 for 2026 and $5.22 for 2027. That is a 52.3% fall this year, so a growth-adjusted multiple on 2026 is not meaningful. Using only the 2027 rebound, the P/E is 1.65 and the PEG would be 0.175. I do not use it: the adjusted figure ignores the TRA, the basis of the estimates could not be verified, and a PEG of 0.175 on a falling business is a rebound distortion, not a bargain. I show it only to say why I disregard it.

On my Base, owner cash before TRA is $77.5 million in year one, a yield of 31.5% on the $246 million market value, 35.8% in year four and 44.6% in year five. These are before the remaining TRA payments and are not a dividend yield. My growth rate over the three annual steps from year one to year four is 4.3% a year, from $77.5 million to $88.0 million, or $2.71 to $3.08 a share. Over four steps to year five it is 9.0%, but the terminal figure is $89.5 million after higher taxes, which is why I do not extend it. A growth rate after TRA payments, and a yield after them, are unavailable, because the payment schedule is not public in a form I can use. Reliability: low.

## How the cap is set

Confidence D calls for a margin of safety of 50%, and I add nothing on top. Sales decline, refinancing, the TRA and the acquisition are already handled in the scenarios, the Bear and the confidence score, and charging them a second time would count one risk twice. The arithmetic cap is $10.75 x (1 - 50%) = **$5.37**. I do not call this a buy zone. The framework I use also allows a no-buy call when a financial risk cannot be measured, and that applies here: a lower quote alone does not repair unknown refinancing terms or acquisition funding. I also do not give a return to a buyer at $8.60, because that needs the timing of TRA payments and the funding plan, and I do not have either. A pre-TRA yield of 31.5% is not that return.

## Reasons to own

- **A franchise that is hard to replace.** 92.2% of second quarter revenue was recurring, and large employers change benefits administrators slowly.
- **Real operating cash.** The business produced $152 million of operating cash flow in six months, and the large impairments in 2025 did not use cash.
- **A thin equity layer with big upside.** If recovery, refinancing and disciplined deal funding arrive together, the Bull is $44.61, 418.7% above the price.
- **The TRA may shrink.** If the payments end up smaller or later than my Base, the stock gains: at a $350 million deduction the value is $14.70.
- **An honest disclosure on October 14.** The company separated its reaffirmed 2026 outlook from stale lender materials instead of blurring the two.

## Main risks

- **Refinancing.** About $2,000 million is due in August 2028, the interest hedges expire in December 2026 and no satisfactory deal existed on October 14. A failed rollover can leave nothing for common shareholders.
- **The TRA.** It is not in reported free cash flow, its payment schedule is unclear, up to $40 million of 2026 payments are disputed, and a change of control can accelerate it. A takeover is not automatically a premium for shareholders.
- **Falling revenue.** Recurring revenue fell from $492 million to $471 million in the second quarter, and the second quarter gross margin was 27.8%.
- **Capital spending and contract costs.** Costs of implementing clients can be spread over seven or fifteen years, so adjusted profit can run ahead of cash.
- **Abett.** No price, funding or earnings are disclosed. Even a sensible deal can use liquidity the refinancing needs.
- **Dilution.** Awards, a rescue financing or a debt conversion can change ownership materially. A reverse split creates no value.
- **Client cash.** The $233 million of fiduciary funds cannot repay corporate debt, and the seller note is not cash on demand.
- **Model risk.** A small change in enterprise value can wipe out or multiply the common stake. No asset floor protects it.

## What I would watch from here

### Supports the thesis

- A funded extension of the 2028 debt on affordable terms, and disclosure of the Abett price, funding and acquired earnings.
- Recurring revenue flat or growing for two quarters, and owner cash positive after capital spending, leases and TRA over a full payment cycle.
- A TRA payment schedule that reconciles without surprise acceleration, and cash that covers the $100 million working buffer after Abett.

### Watch closely

- Refinancing talks without a commitment, or a decline in recurring revenue that is slowing but not stopped.
- Adjusted profit improving only through add-backs, or owner cash positive only before the TRA.
- A narrow cash buffer, rising reliance on the revolver, or more awards with unclear funding.

### Breaks the thesis

- No workable refinancing of the 2028 debt, or distressed terms.
- Accelerating contraction in recurring revenue or continued erosion of gross margin.
- TRA payments that exceed the liquidity plan, or Abett consuming cash or debt without a credible return.

## The most important thesis breaker

Failure to refinance while keeping a business and a TRA cash bridge that still add up. The next dated test is the November 3 report: I would reconcile its cash, debt and TRA figures before changing any value here.

The strongest case against Hold is the Bull of $44.61, a durable franchise with lower TRA cash and an affordable refinancing. I keep it in the table at full size and do not dismiss it because the stock is cheap on the chart. My answer is that it requires three things at once, a recovery, a refinancing and a sensibly funded deal, and none of the three is yet visible in the filings. The strongest case against owning at all is the Bear, in which lenders and the TRA take everything. The main limits: I do not know the October cash, debt, share count or TRA balance, the Abett terms, or how much of the first half cash flow was timing. Those are why confidence is D.

## Final verdict: hold at $8.60

Alight may well be cheap if the business holds, if the lenders cooperate and if Abett is paid for sensibly. The data does not yet say that any of those will happen. A 96% fall on the chart is not a margin of safety, because in this capital structure a small change in operating value can erase common equity or multiply it. The $101 million of headline free cash flow is not cash for shareholders, the October 14 refinancing update was a negative, and the numbers above describe the company before Abett.

**Verdict: HOLD at $8.60, watchlist for new money, no new buy. Conditional Base value $10.75, upside 25.0%, Confidence D (10/25), required margin of safety 50%, indicative cap $5.37, no actionable buy zone.**

For holders, the model gives no reason to add and no instruction to sell; the right size depends on how much of the position you can afford to lose entirely, which I cannot know. For new money, it means waiting for a funded refinancing, the Abett terms and owner cash that stays positive after the TRA. The price allows for a weaker business.
