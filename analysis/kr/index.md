In October Kroger's chief executive told investors that the company had lost share, through uneven store execution, costs that grew faster than sales and a value offer that slipped. The shares have gone from $66.77 on October 26 to $70.43 today.

That is a market paying for a turnaround that has been described but not yet delivered. Identical sales without fuel grew only 0.2% in the second quarter, the company cut its full-year guide for them to 0.2% to 0.8%, and adjusted earnings per share still rose 5% on cost savings and 10% fewer shares than a year ago.

My triangulated Base value is **$76.94**, which is 9.2% above the $70.43 reference price. The Bear value is $52.93 and the Bull value is $109.43. Confidence is grade C (17/25), which requires a 42.5% margin of safety and puts the maximum buy price at **$44.24**. The verdict is a hold: the price is close to what I think the business is worth, and nowhere near a price where the margin of safety rule would say buy.

## What Kroger is now

Kroger runs about 2,700 stores in 35 states, an eCommerce business of roughly $18 billion in sales and a private label business of about $40 billion, according to its chief executive. In the second quarter eCommerce sales grew 20% on an adjusted basis and profit at Kroger Precision Marketing, its retail media unit, grew 24%.

At an Investor Update on October 20 the company set targets for fiscal 2027 to 2029: about 3% comparable sales growth excluding fuel and pharmacy in 2029, 3% to 6% a year growth in adjusted EBITDA, and 7% to 11% a year growth in adjusted EPS. These targets exclude the pending Giant Eagle acquisition. The plan includes about 110 new stores by 2029 and, according to local reports, about 3,000 corporate job cuts over three years, around 37% of the corporate workforce.

## Which cash to value

For a grocer the useful measure is free cash flow after capital spending and interest, because rent is already in operating costs. I value free cash flow to shareholders of about $2.8 billion in fiscal 2026 (the middle of the guide), rising to $3.2 billion in fiscal 2031 in the Base (about 3% a year, below the company's 3% to 6% target for EBITDA growth, because the plan needs $3.8 billion to $4.0 billion of capital spending and price investment). This is my estimate.

Operating cash flow fell in the first half and capital spending rose, which is why I do not extrapolate the company's EPS target to cash flow. The Giant Eagle purchase ($1.25 billion in cash) is outside the model, and I treat it as value-neutral.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $70.43, holding everything else at Base, the price is consistent with free cash flow and earnings about 8% below my Base in every year (2027 earnings of about $5.08 a share), or a discount rate of about 9.6%. The market is paying for roughly the cash flow Kroger guides to now, plus a little of the plan, not for the full 7% to 11% EPS growth the company targets.

## Reasons to own Kroger

- **Cash generation.** Free cash flow guided to $2.7 billion to $2.9 billion, against a market value of about ${mcap:.0f} billion.
- **Shrinking share count.** Shares outstanding are down 10% in a year, which lifts EPS even with flat profit.
- **Margin levers.** Retail media profit grew 24%, eCommerce is growing 20% and operating costs fell to 17.2% of sales.
- **A defined plan.** Targets for 2027 to 2029 and a self-funded price investment, with leverage of 1.91 against a target of 2.30 to 2.50.
- **Dividend record.** 20 consecutive annual increases.

These support the Base. They do not create a margin of safety.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: sales momentum.** Identical sales grew 0.2%, and the company lowered its guide for the year. A price investment that does not bring traffic would cut margins without sales.
- **Competition.** The chief executive named lost share, and Walmart and Amazon are named as competitors in press reports.
- **Execution.** About 3,000 corporate job cuts, a new store format and 110 new stores in three years all happen at the same time.
- **Giant Eagle.** $1.65 billion of value, expected to close in 2027 and to add to adjusted earnings only in the second full year.
- **Albertsons dispute.** A news report says Albertsons is seeking $8 billion from Kroger after the failed merger. I did not verify the claim or its merit, and I have not included any amount.
- **Earnings quality.** Adjusted earnings exclude transformation costs ($119 million over 26 weeks), merger litigation charges and a large 2025 impairment of the automated fulfillment network.

## Management execution

The new chief executive has set specific targets, admitted share loss in public and kept the cost base and the buyback under control. He has also lowered the sales guide in September and has yet to show growth in stores. **Assessment: 11/20, a credible plan with an unproven result. The next tests are the third quarter report, expected about December 3, identical sales against the new guide, and the first Giant Eagle approvals.**

- Net total debt to adjusted EBITDA staying below 2.3

### Yellow

- Identical sales below the 0.2% to 0.8% guide range
- Gross margin falling as price cuts start
- Capital spending above $4.0 billion
- Another round of restructuring or transformation costs

### Red

- Identical sales negative
- Guidance for operating profit or EPS cut
- A large adverse ruling or settlement in the Albertsons dispute
- Leverage above the target range because of buybacks or acquisitions
- Free cash flow guide cut below $2.5 billion

Today's readings: the reaffirmed EPS and operating profit guides and the leverage are green, the lowered sales guide and rising capital spending are yellow. The next point is the third quarter report, expected about December 3.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Price cuts reduce margin without lifting identical sales, and free cash flow falls below $2.5 billion.
2. The cost savings are reinvested in price faster than they arrive, and adjusted operating profit declines.
3. An adverse outcome in the Albertsons dispute or a failed Giant Eagle integration drains cash or management attention.

## Final verdict: hold at $70.43

Kroger generates steady cash, is shrinking its share count and has a plan, but sales are flat and the plan is three years long. At $70.43 the price is 9% below my Base and 59% above the maximum buy price, which is the right place to hold and watch, not to add.

**Verdict: HOLD at $70.43, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $76.94, upside 9.2%, Confidence C (17/25), required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $44.24.**

If Kroger reaches the low end of its own EPS target, $5.20 becomes about $6.37 by fiscal 2029, which at my 14 times is about $89.18. That is the price of patience, not a margin of safety.
