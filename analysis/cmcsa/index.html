Comcast: the price assumes the cable business shrinks about 5% a year forever, and the verdict is hold at $23.67

CMCSA, Nasdaq. Published November 4, 2026. Price as of the November 3, 2026 regular session close. Latest reported quarter: July to September 2026. Fourth quarter results are expected on January 28, 2027.

Hold at $23.67

At $23.67 Comcast trades 37.7% below my Base value of $38.01, but 8.3% above my maximum buy price of $21.86. The gap to the Base is real, and so is the reason for it: broadband customers are leaving, connectivity profit fell 4.4% last quarter, and the value depends heavily on how fast that continues. Under nearby assumptions the buy price ranges from $19.02 to $25.41, so this is a closer call than the Base suggests. Confidence is C, so the required margin of safety is 42.5%. The shares traded near $24.58 on November 4, which does not change the verdict.

Comcast lost 144,000 broadband customers in the September quarter and produced $4.2 billion of free cash flow. The share price has decided which of those two numbers matters. At $23.67 it fits a connectivity business whose cash flow shrinks about 5% a year, every year, forever. Last quarter that business's profit fell 4.4%.

At $23.67 on the November 3, 2026 close, Comcast trades 37.7% below my Base value of $38.01. The Bear value is $16.97, 28.3% below the price, and the Bull value is $57.25, 141.9% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The verdict is Hold, with new money waiting for $21.86 or less.

Valuation route: a company with two very different businesses that it plans to split into two listed companies in mid-2027, valued as a sum of the parts. The connectivity business is valued from its long-run cash flow after investment; theme parks, media and studios at earnings multiples; corporate costs, investments and net debt are added or deducted. The cross-check capitalizes normalized free cash flow to shareholders. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Comcast is, and what it is about to become

Comcast has two halves. Connectivity & Platforms is Xfinity broadband, mobile and TV in the United States, Comcast Business, and Sky's broadband and TV in the UK and Italy. In the September quarter it earned $7.6 billion of adjusted EBITDA, 83% of the total. Content & Experiences is NBCUniversal: the NBC and Telemundo networks, Bravo, the Peacock streaming service, Universal's film and television studios, and the Universal theme parks in Orlando, Hollywood, Japan and Beijing.

That shape is about to change for the second time this year. The cable networks went to shareholders as Versant on January 2, 2026, and Sky's German business was sold on May 31. On June 29 Comcast announced a tax-free spin-off of NBCUniversal together with all of Sky, expected in mid-2027. Mike Cavanagh will run NBCUniversal and Michael Angelakis, a former Comcast finance chief, will run what remains: US broadband, mobile and business services. Comcast keeps up to 19.9% of NBCUniversal for up to a year and plans to sell it to pay down debt. Both companies keep the dual-class structure that gives the Roberts family control. Sky has also agreed to buy ITV's media and entertainment business for up to £1.6 billion, closing in the second half of 2027, and that business will go with NBCUniversal.

I value the businesses, not the two future share certificates. The total is the same either way, and the split of Sky and of the debt between the two companies has not been published yet.

Domestic broadband lost 144,000 customers, against 91,000 a year earlier, and video lost 274,000. Mobile added 368,000 lines, and about 70% of customers on the free line offered with broadband are moving to paid service, according to management. Theme park profit fell 17.1%, because of soft demand in Orlando since June and Chinese travel restrictions that hurt attendance in Japan. Management expects parks to return to growth in the second half of 2027.

Management called some rival broadband pricing irrational: fiber costs $1,500 or more per home to build and was being sold at $30 to $40 a month in some markets. It also announced the largest cost overhaul in the company's history and a charge of more than $1 billion in the December quarter.

Which numbers to trust

  • Reported revenue fell 6.1%, but most of that is the portfolio. Without Versant and Sky Germany, revenue was up 0.5% and adjusted EBITDA up 0.8%. The underlying problem is narrower: domestic broadband.
  • The first nine months of 2026 carry one-off sports. The Winter Olympics, the Super Bowl and the World Cup added $2.8 billion of Media revenue but little profit; Media lost $426 million in the March quarter. I use a normalized Media profit of $2.6 billion a year.
  • Free cash flow is flattered twice. Share-based pay of about $1.5 billion a year is a real cost not deducted, and deferred taxes added $1.5 billion in nine months. I deduct both in the cross-check, about $2.5 billion in total.
  • Adjusted EPS excludes amortization of acquired intangibles, mostly from Sky and NBCUniversal, $524 million before tax in the quarter. It is not a cash cost, so I ignore it, but I do not count it as earnings power either.

Primary valuation: sum of the parts

The connectivity business is most of the value and most of the uncertainty, so I value it from cash flow rather than a multiple. For 2026 I estimate $30.95 billion of EBITDA: nine months reported plus $6.0 billion from residential and $1.5 billion from business services in the December quarter. Capital spending including capitalized software is about $11.1 billion. After a 23% tax, that leaves about $15.3 billion of cash a year. I let it decline 1.5% a year forever and discount at 7.5%. The other businesses get earnings multiples. The Base value is $38.01.

Cross-check: free cash flow to shareholders

The second view ignores the segments and looks at the cash left for shareholders. I estimate 2026 free cash flow at $15.7 billion: $12.7 billion for nine months and $3.0 billion for the December quarter, lower than usual because September's timing benefit reverses. Less $1.5 billion of share-based pay and $1.0 billion of tax deferral I do not expect to last, that is $13.2 billion.

If that cash shrinks 1% a year and Comcast repays debt at the same pace to keep leverage steady, about $0.8 billion a year, then at a 9% cost of equity the shares are worth $34.71. That is 8.7% below the sum of the parts, close enough that I do not need to explain the difference beyond the assumptions themselves.

Valuation triangulation

  • Primary (sum of the parts, Base): $38.01. This is the central value.
  • Cross-check (free cash flow to shareholders): $34.71, 8.7% below.
  • Nearby assumptions (connectivity -2% to -1% a year, discount rate 7% to 8%): $33.07 to $44.18.
  • Connectivity at the Bear's -4% a year, everything else Base: $26.80, 13% above the price.

Both methods put fair value in the mid to high $30s if the connectivity decline slows. Neither can tell you whether it will. That is why the buy price is far below both.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is C, which carries a default margin of safety of 42.5%. I add nothing to it, because the competitive risk already sits in the connectivity growth rate and the scenarios. That gives a maximum buy price of $21.86.

With connectivity growth between -2% and -1% a year and a discount rate between 7% and 8%, the same margin gives buy prices from $19.02 to $25.41. The price of $23.67 sits inside that range, so here the decision does depend on the assumption: at -1% a year and 7.5%, the buy price would be $23.60, almost exactly today's price. I keep the point estimate, because the September quarter was worse than the June quarter in broadband customers, and the evidence for a slowing decline is one quarter of narrower EBITDA losses. The 52-week low is $20.48.

With no change in valuation, the return over the next few years is roughly the 5.6% dividend plus debt reduction that slowly accrues to shareholders. Anything more needs the market to stop extrapolating today's decline forever.

Dividend

Comcast pays $0.33 a quarter, $1.32 a year, a 5.6% yield at $23.67, unchanged since early 2025. It costs about $4.7 billion a year, against my $13.2 billion of normalized free cash flow to shareholders, so it is covered almost three times. The dividend policy of each company after the split has not been set, and the buyback, which used to return more than the dividend, is paused.

Reasons to own Comcast

  • It produces about $13 billion a year of free cash flow to shareholders after the adjustments above, against a market value of $83.8 billion.
  • The price already assumes connectivity cash flow falls about 5% a year forever, so the stock does not need growth to work, only a slower decline.
  • Mobile is growing quickly on a network Comcast mostly does not have to build: about 90% of Xfinity Mobile traffic runs over Wi-Fi, and lines reach only 7% of the addressable market in its footprint.
  • Business services earn a 56% margin and still grow.
  • The separation may let investors value the parks, studios and Peacock on their own, rather than at a cable company's multiple.

What could go wrong?

  • Broadband competition. Fiber builders and fixed wireless from the mobile carriers keep taking share, and some fiber is priced at $30 to $40 a month. Each half point of yearly connectivity decline is worth about $3 a share.
  • Price versus volume. Simpler pricing and free mobile lines lowered average broadband revenue per customer, and customers moving to the new prices raised churn in the September quarter.
  • Credit and the split. Moody's and Fitch are reviewing the rating, and the remaining Comcast will be a more leveraged, broadband-only company. Debt allocation and dividend policy are not yet public.
  • Theme parks. Orlando demand is soft, Epic Universe still has capacity problems, and a UK park will need years of spending before it earns anything.
  • Governance. The Roberts family controls the vote through Class B shares, and both future companies keep that structure.

Management execution

  • Capital returns were disciplined: $11.7 billion returned in 2025 and the share count down 5%; the dividend was raised each year through 2025 and held in 2026.
  • The broadband strategy changed twice in two years, from price increases to simpler national pricing with free mobile lines, and the results so far are mixed: more mobile lines, more broadband losses in the latest quarter.
  • The portfolio is being reshaped quickly and in the right direction: Hulu sold, Versant spun, Sky Germany sold, and NBCUniversal and Sky to be separated.
  • The theme park bet, Epic Universe, opened on schedule but has not yet delivered steady growth.
  • Disclosure is detailed by segment, but the cost program and the separation terms have not been quantified beyond the charge of more than $1 billion.

On my ten-point management check (two points each), Comcast scores 12 of 20: credit for capital returns, portfolio moves and disclosure, little for the broadband record and per-share growth.

What I would watch from here

The December quarter report on January 28, 2027 should show four things for the Base to hold: connectivity EBITDA falling less than the September quarter's 4.4%, broadband customer losses back below 100,000, the size and payback of the cost program, and the first details of how debt and dividends will be split between the two companies. Michael Angelakis is due to present the connectivity plan early in 2027.

Green

  • Connectivity EBITDA decline narrowing toward 2% a year.
  • Broadband revenue per customer stabilizing as free mobile lines convert to paid.
  • Theme park profit growing again in the second half of 2027, as management expects.

Yellow

  • Broadband losses above 150,000 a quarter for two more quarters.
  • A credit downgrade that raises borrowing costs for the remaining Comcast.
  • A separation plan that leaves most of the debt with the connectivity company.

Red

  • Connectivity EBITDA falling 6% or more a year.
  • A dividend cut at either company after the split.
  • A large acquisition paid with debt before the separation.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Fiber and fixed wireless keep connectivity cash flow falling 4% to 5% a year, which is what the price already assumes and leaves no upside.
  2. The cost program does not offset lower broadband prices, so margins keep falling with revenue.
  3. The separation leaves the connectivity company with too much debt and no buyback, so the cash flow serves lenders rather than shareholders for years.

The weakest point is the connectivity growth rate, because half a point a year moves the value by about $3 and the buy price by about $1.75, and the latest quarter went the wrong way on customers. The strongest bull argument is that the price needs the current rate of decline to last forever, with no credit for mobile, business services or the cost program. The strongest bear argument is that the incumbent rarely wins a price war against new fiber, and Comcast is still losing customers while cutting prices.

Final verdict: hold at $23.67

Comcast is priced as if the September quarter will repeat forever. If the decline in connectivity profit only slows to the pace of a quiet bad year, the shares are worth well into the $30s, and the separation may help the market see the parks and studios separately. The problem is the one number that decides the value is the one with the least evidence behind it.

Verdict: HOLD at $23.67, watchlist for new money. Base $38.01, 60.6% above the price, Bear $16.97, Bull $57.25, cross-check $34.71, Confidence C (16.25/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $21.86, range $19.02 to $25.41 under nearby assumptions.

New money buys only at or below $21.86. For an existing holder the original cost is irrelevant, and a Base value well above the price supports holding while the dividend is covered and the decline slows. The next tests are the December quarter report on January 28, 2027 and the separation terms.
