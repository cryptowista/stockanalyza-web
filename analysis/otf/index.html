Blue Owl Technology Finance (OTF) pays a regular dividend of $0.35 a quarter, a 15.5% yield at $9.01, and trades at about 55% of its reported net asset value (NAV) of $16.48 a share. That looks like a bargain, and it may be one. But in the second quarter net investment income (NII), the interest and dividends left after all costs, was $0.30 a share, so 86% of the regular dividend. About 12.5% of investment income was paid in kind (PIK), meaning added to the loan balance instead of paid in cash, and the manager's fees rose after the June 2025 listing.

At $9.01, the last trade at 7:15 pm New York time on October 19, 2026 (after hours; an XTB screen on October 20 shows $9.07), the shares trade 10.4% below my triangulated Base value of $10.06, an upside of 11.7% (10.9% at $9.07). The Bear value is $4.24, 52.9% below the price, and the Bull value of $14.76 is 63.8% above it. Confidence is grade C (13/25), the required margin of safety (MOS) is 40%, and the maximum buy price is $6.04. The price is below my Base but far above the buy ceiling, so the verdict is Hold.

Valuation route: a listed, externally managed business development company (BDC), a lender. Primary method: what the shares are worth given the return the lender can sustainably earn on its book value, with a discounted path to normal earnings. Secondary method: a five-year runoff of the loan portfolio after haircuts. Classification: quality growth on a sector scorecard, but with no growth premium.

What OTF is

OTF lends mostly to private software and technology companies and is managed by Blue Owl. On June 30, 2026 it held $14.7 billion of investments at fair value in about 205 portfolio companies, 77.8% first lien loans and roughly 56% of the first lien book unitranche. Application software was 15.3%, systems software 17.5% and health care technology 12.4% of the portfolio. About 93% of it is valued with unobservable inputs, so the marks are the manager's and its valuation firms' estimates rather than market prices.

The company changed shape twice. In March 2025 it merged with a sister fund and nearly doubled its share count, from about 212 million to 464 million shares. In June 2025 it listed, and with that the fee schedule rose: the management fee from 0.90% to a 1.50% / 1.00% framework on gross assets and the income incentive fee from 10% to 17.5%. Investment income grew 5.8% in the second quarter against a year earlier, but adjusted NII per share fell from $0.36 to $0.30.

Funding is diversified but not cheap or permanent. Since June the company sold a $400 million tap of 6.50% notes, a $150 million 7.60% note due September 2032 (the coupon steps up by 1.00% to 2.00% if its rating falls below investment grade or secured debt passes a limit), and added a $250 million securitization facility and upsized another by $100 million. This is not a reported number. A $100 million error in unmodeled asset values moves it by about $0.22.

The primary valuation: what the book can earn

Management wants NII of $0.35 a share a quarter by mid-2027. The path to it is plausible but not done. In my Base the quarterly NII rises from $0.31 to $0.35 by the third quarter of 2027, six months later than management says:

Borrowing more adds only $5.2 million a quarter; most of the improvement has to come from repricing and moving non-income equity into loans. That takes execution.

To value it I do not simply multiply $0.35 by a multiple. I reduce $1.40 a year for the PIK that may not be collected (85% collected, two years late), for 0.35% a year of principal losses and for a small allowance for discount timing. That leaves sustainable economic NII of $1.14 a share, or $1.108 after the capital eroded while the dividend exceeds earnings. On $16.20 of NAV that is a return of 7.0%, below the 12% I use as the cost of equity.

A lender earning below its cost of equity deserves a discount to book: the justified price to NAV is (7.0% - 0%) / (12% - 0%) = 0.586 times. The terminal value at the end of 2027 is $1.108 / 12% = $9.23 on economic NAV of $15.75. Discounted for 1.2 years it is $8.06, and the five quarterly distributions I assume (the $0.35 regular rate) add $1.62 in present value. The primary Base is $9.68. The market price of 0.55 times NAV is therefore not irrational.

I take June fair values and apply haircuts by category, for example 2% on first lien loans, 8% on second lien, 10% on unsecured, 15% on preferred equity and 20% on common equity. Both methods start from the same marked assets and the same 12%, so their agreement is not independent proof that the marks are right.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C calls for a margin of safety of 35% to 50%. I use 40%: PIK collections, private marks and the interim capital are not independently certain, but I do not stack another penalty on risks already in the numbers (credit losses, PIK, leverage, technology exposure). The maximum buy price is $10.06 x (1 - 40%) = $6.04. Changing the cost of equity from 10% to 14% moves the ceiling between about $6.90 and $5.40.

Four numbers answer different questions: what the shares are worth ($10.06), the return from buying at $9.01 and holding on the path above (14.1% a year, or 13.9% at $9.07), the price that gives a 12% return ($9.68) and the price that leaves a 40% margin of safety ($6.04). The return path pays $0.35 a quarter to the end of 2027, then my sustainable $1.108 a year, and sells at $9.23 in 2031. It is not a forecast that the board will cut the dividend.

Reasons to own

  • A large discount to book. At 55% of NAV, with non-accruals at 0.6% of cost, the shares have recovery upside if the marks and future collections hold.
  • A solid credit record. NAV total returns of 11.1%, 9.3% and 11.8% for 2023 to 2025 and a platform with wide deal access.
  • Visible earnings levers. Repricing, funded growth and moving non-income equity into loans can lift NII toward the dividend, and I charge the funding and fee costs for them.
  • Buybacks below NAV and funding access. The company bought back $105 million of stock in the first half and has raised and extended funding.

Main risks

  • Dividend and cash quality. NII is below the dividend and a large part of income is PIK. Paying out more than recurring earnings consumes NAV.
  • Technology credit risk. About a third of the portfolio is direct software lending, and AI can both help and disrupt borrowers. Low non-accruals can coexist with marks that are slow to fall.
  • Funding and reinvestment. More leverage needs profitable deployment, borrowing base eligibility and refinancing of cheap old debt, starting with the January 2027 note.
  • Fees, governance and realization. External fees, share issuance below NAV and no shareholder control over asset sales can keep the price below NAV for long.

What I would watch from here

Supports the thesis

  • NII of $0.35 a share or more that is repeatable, without more reliance on PIK.
  • Hard cash-like income moving toward the dividend, and non-accruals around 1% of cost or less.
  • The January 2027 note refinanced at an acceptable cost and asset coverage comfortably above 175%.

Watch closely

  • NII of $0.30 to $0.34 with a funded ramp, or the PIK share rising from 12.5%.
  • Non-accruals at 1% to 3% of cost or more loans rated 3 to 5 by the manager.
  • Share issuance below NAV offsetting buybacks.

Breaks the thesis

  • NII below $0.28 after the target period, or NAV falling again after counting dividends.
  • Non-accruals above 3% of cost, or correlated write-downs in software.
  • Asset coverage near 150%, a borrowing base shortfall or a failed refinancing.

The most important thesis breakers

  • Overdistribution. Dividends and principal losses keep running ahead of recoverable income while the extra leverage does not earn more than its funding, fee and credit cost.
  • Credit. A worse set of marks or a borrowing base problem would force me to re-underwrite rather than lean on the $6.04 ceiling.
  • Earnings target. NII fails to reach $0.35 after mid-2027, so the stock is a 7% return on equity business at a fair discount, not a bargain.

How I checked the model

I rebuilt the model from its stated inputs and reproduced: the economic NAV bridge ($16.202); the five-quarter distribution present values ($1.6187) and the terminal value ($9.23 and $8.06); the primary Base of $9.68 and the justified 0.586 times; the haircut loss of $663.2 million, the $14.75 recoverable NAV and the runoff value ($11.96, $10.77 after the 90% factor); the 65/35 blend, divergence of 10.6%, upside and buy price; the Bear and Bull blends; the return of 14.1% and the 12% entry of $9.68; the hard income bridge and its history; the yields and coverage ratios. I did not independently rebuild the sensitivity grids, the earnings bridge inputs (a 9.5% asset yield and a 5.9% debt cost) or the reverse solves, but the grid values are consistent with the Base and the reverse NII of $0.297 sits where the grid puts $9.01.

From the company's documents I confirmed: second quarter NII, adjusted NII, NAV (and the $16.49 at March 31), investment income, non-accruals, leverage, buybacks and PIK share; fair value and cost of investments, carrying debt, net assets and share counts from the 10-Q; first half NII and PIK; the dividend calendar; the 7.60% note and its step-ups; and the November 4 and 5 dates. The $9.01 price is also the latest price on a price history page. The fee schedule, debt principal of $7,254.5 million, unfunded commitments, asset coverage, sector weights, the swaps, the August note tap and facility sizes, the first-half unrealized depreciation, the share of PIK agreed at origination, year-ago comparisons, peer figures and the history for 2023 to 2025 come from the supplied analysis and I could not re-read them. The analysis uses cash of $213.5 million where the balance sheet shows $210.3 million; it only affects a peer comparison and I could not reconcile the difference.

Main data limits: the October NAV and share count are estimates, there is no data on PIK collections by loan, and the loan marks are private. These are why Confidence is C and not higher.

Final verdict: hold at $9.01

OTF has a real discount to book, low non-accruals and a credible way to raise income. But income is below the dividend, a large share of it is not cash, fees are higher and the return on equity is below its cost. At $9.01 the shares are modestly below my Base, but 49% above my buy ceiling. The verdict is the same at $9.07.

Verdict: HOLD at $9.01, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $10.06, upside 11.7%, Confidence C (13/25), required margin of safety 40%, maximum buy price $6.04, range $5.40 to $6.90.

For holders this means keeping the position and watching the third quarter report: the Bull case at $14.76 is real. For new money it means waiting for a lower price or proof that NII covers the dividend in cash terms.
