ADT went public in January 2018 at $14 a share, and almost nine years later it agreed to be bought for $10.00 in cash by Thrive Holdings, a company backed by Joshua Kushner. The shares closed at $6.87 on the last trading day before the news, so $10.00 is a 45.6% premium, and at $9.88 the market already prices almost all of it. The remaining 12 cents is a gain of 1.2% for waiting somewhere between two and four months, about 3.6% a year if the deal closes at the end of March. If it breaks, the unaffected price was $6.87, a fall of 30% from here, and my own standalone Bear value is lower still at $5.29.

The standalone Base value is $10.99, +11.2% above the price, so the business looks worth about what the buyer is paying. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 42.5%, so the maximum buy price is **$6.32**. The shares are 56% above it. The verdict is Hold: for an existing holder the cash price is the exit, and for new money the risk and reward are lopsided.

Valuation route: a subscription security business with an announced all-cash takeover. ## What ADT is

ADT sells and monitors home and small-business security and smart-home systems in the United States, with more than 12,000 employees. Monitoring and related services produced $1,084 million of the $1,349 million of third-quarter revenue, about 80%, and recurring monthly revenue (RMR) was $360 million at September 30. That figure was $362 million a year earlier, so the subscription base is flat to slightly down: RMR fell 1%, gross customer revenue attrition was 13.1% against 13.0% a year ago, and revenue payback, the years of customer revenue needed to recover the cost of winning a customer, held at 2.3 years.

The growth that does exist comes from the other line. Security installation, product and other revenue rose 32% in the quarter to $265 million, while monitoring fell 1%. ADT is adding products and services around the core: the ADT+ platform, an AI-based presence-sensing technology from the Origin AI acquisition ($164 million of cash paid in the nine months), and a new service to install and maintain select products for Apple Retail customers beginning this fall. Apollo, which took the company public, sold its remaining shares in May, and State Farm is now the largest shareholder.

The buyer is Thrive Holdings, a permanent-capital holding company with more than 75 businesses in accounting, IT services and the built environment. The sale price per share is $10.00, valuing the equity at about $7.6 billion and the whole company, with debt, at $15.3 billion. Apollo-managed funds provide the debt and preferred financing. Thrive's engineering teams are meant to find technology gains inside ADT, which is the same argument management made for ADT+.

Three features stand out. First, financing is committed and is not a condition, so the usual way a leveraged deal fails is closed off, and Thrive owes $473 million in certain failure cases. Second, the proxy statement shows Thrive moved from $8.60 on August 13 to $9.00, then $9.85, then $10.00 on September 27, while ADT asked for $10.35. The board then agreed to $10.00 with no exclusivity for Thrive and relied on the go-shop instead of a broader search before signing. ADT had approached one strategic party, which declined on October 27, and one existing stockholder, which declined on October 14. Third, the quarterly dividend stops once the agreement is signed, so a holder gets no payout while waiting.

The go-shop ends in two days. The $114 million fee is 1.5% of the equity value, low enough not to deter a serious rival, but the earlier outreach found no interest and I put the chance of a higher bid at a few percent at most. The shareholder vote has not yet been scheduled in the documents I reached. Management believes regulatory approvals can come by year-end 2026 with no assurance, and the proxy uses February 1, 2027 as an assumed closing date for compensation disclosures.

## From reported cash flow to owner free cash flow

ADT's free cash flow is operating cash flow minus all capital spending, including the purchase of customer accounts from dealers, subscriber system equipment and property. Subscriber acquisition costs that ADT capitalizes sit inside operating cash flow, so the figure is already after the cost of winning customers. It is also after interest, which matters because ADT carries $7.8 billion of debt. I make three adjustments to turn it into an owner figure.

| Estimate for 2026, $ million | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Free cash flow, nine months reported | 1,007 |
| Fourth quarter: management plan unlevered cash flow of 247 less about 77 of after-tax interest (my estimate) | 170 |
| Free cash flow, full year estimate | 1,177 |
| Stock compensation, not disclosed in the release (my estimate) | -60 |
| Deferred tax, nine-month figure of 24 annualized | -32 |
| Owner free cash flow, 2026 run rate | 1,085 |

The run rate is about **$1,085 million, or $1.43 a share**, a 14.4% yield on the market value at $9.88. That looks cheap, and the market did not believe it: before the offer the stock traded at $6.87, a yield of about 21% on the same figure. The reason is in the plan that ADT gave Thrive. It projects unlevered free cash flow, meaning before interest, of $913 million in 2027. Take away roughly $300 million of after-tax interest and a shareholder is left with about $613 million, 44% below the 2026 run rate. I could not reconcile the two from the documents I reached. Candidates are the lower dealer account spending in 2026, which may not repeat, higher cash taxes in the plan, and the release's own adjustments for receivables facility flows and restructuring. I did not obtain the 10-Q, so the stock compensation and the cash tax figure remain estimates.

Because I cannot say which number is right, I use both. **Bear starts from the plan's $613 million, Base from $850 million, close to the midpoint and weighted toward the plan, and Bull from $1,000 million, the 2026 run rate with an 8% haircut.** This is the largest single assumption in the analysis, and the robustness table shows what it does.

## Balance sheet and shares

At September 30, ADT had $228 million of cash and $7,781 million of debt, of which $1,242 million is current, so net debt is $7,553 million excluding $26 million of restricted cash. Nothing was drawn on the revolving credit facility, and the company borrowed another $100 million on a first-lien term loan in August. Net debt is about 2.8 times the adjusted EBITDA run rate of roughly $2.65 billion (my estimate from the third quarter), but about 5.0 times my measure of earnings after reinvestment, which I use below. Interest of $102 million a quarter is about 19% of the roughly $542 million of quarterly operating cash flow before interest (440 plus 102). Equity on the balance sheet is $3,640 million, and goodwill and intangibles are $9.8 billion of the $15.95 billion of assets, so book value says little.

The leverage triggers for a three-tier cash flow analysis are met (interest above 15% of operating cash flow before financing). I handle that by valuing cash flow after interest and holding debt flat, with the current maturities assumed to be refinanced at today's cost. ADT repurchased 89 million shares for $617 million in nine months, an average of about $6.93, which was cheap against the $10.00 now on offer, and it also paid $130 million of dividends. Share repurchases stop in practice with the deal, and the dividend has ceased.

I use **760 million diluted shares**, derived by dividing the $7.6 billion deal equity value by $10.00. The third-quarter weighted diluted count was 737 million, and the proxy implies about 731 million basic shares including 54.7 million Class B, so 760 million is a conservative fully diluted figure and an estimate, not a reported number. Directors and executive officers hold 30.5 million shares, 4.17% of the stock.

## Primary valuation: ten years of owner free cash flow

The primary method discounts ten years of free cash flow to shareholders at 11.5%, a cost of equity above the 10% I use for unlevered businesses because debt is 1.0 times the market value of the equity, and I test 10.5% and 12.5%. Base starts at $850 million in 2027, grows 2.5% a year for five years and 2.0% for the next five, which reaches $962 million in 2031 and $1,062 million in 2036, with terminal growth of 1.5%. That is below the plan's growth, in line with revenue +2% in 2026, and it assumes the monitoring base stops shrinking.

I do not subtract net debt in this method. The cash flow is after interest and the debt is assumed to stay at today's level, so subtracting it would count the debt twice. The primary value is **$11.98**. Terminal value share: 40% of the value at a ten-year horizon, well under the 75% level at which I would check that the terminal value is supported.

## Secondary valuation: five years of cash flow and an exit at 9 times earnings after reinvestment

The cross-check uses a different base. Operating cash earnings after reinvestment is adjusted EBITDA minus all capital spending and minus net subscriber acquisition costs (deferred costs paid less their amortization). For the third quarter that is 665 - 235 - 44 = $386 million, or about $1,544 million a year. I use **$1,500 million** for 2026, rounded down, and grow it 2% a year to $1,656 million in 2031. At 9.0 times, the enterprise is worth $14,905 million at the end of 2031, and after subtracting today's net debt of $7,553 million (held flat) and adding the first five years of cash flow, the secondary value is **$9.99**.

**Why 9.0 times.** At $6.87 the market valued ADT at about 8.3 times the current $1,544 million figure, and the $10.00 price is 9.9 times. A midpoint of 9.0 sits between the two. At an enterprise discount rate of 8.5% and cash conversion of about 77% of this earnings figure, 9.0 times implies growth of -0.1% a year forever, essentially no growth, which suits a base that is shrinking 1% in recurring revenue. Bear uses 7.0 times (implied growth -2.3%), below where the market valued the company before the offer, and Bull uses 10.0 times, the deal multiple (implied growth 0.7%). Exit value share: 56% of the secondary value comes from the exit at a five-year horizon, under the 85% level at which I would test it.

## What is the market already pricing in?

For the shares as a standalone business, $9.88 needs one of four things on the Base case: owner free cash flow growth 1.6 points a year lower than my Base, an exit multiple of 7.3 times instead of 9.0, a discount rate of 13.1% instead of 11.5%, or a starting free cash flow of $735 million instead of $850 million. The market price is therefore consistent with my central assumptions and a little more cautious, which is what a price at a 10% discount to Base should look like.

For the deal, the question is different. The shares at $9.88 are priced for completion with a probability of about 98% to 100%, if the alternative is the October 27 price of $6.87 and cash earns about 4% (my assumption), depending on whether the deal closes on February 1 or at the end of March. A holder is paid for the time value of money and nothing for the risk of a failed deal. The gain is 3.6% a year at a March 31 close and 6.8% at a February 1 close.

At a 95% probability, which is my estimate given committed financing, a near-expired antitrust clock, a 45.6% premium and a board that negotiated up from $8.60, the expected value is $9.84, below the price, before even counting the time value. The shares are only fairly priced for someone certain the deal closes. I do not give a single probability a precision it does not have: the main open risks are the stockholder vote, which needs a majority of every outstanding share and has no support agreements behind it, and the antitrust review, which has no reported second request so far.

On earnings the shares trade at 10.3 times annualized third-quarter adjusted EPS of $0.24, a diagnostic only. The growth-adjusted ratio is not meaningful with adjusted EPS up 4% and revenue growth driven by one-time installation sales. As a stress test, taking 20% off both the terminal and exit values lowers the Base to $9.37, 14.7% below.

## How the buy price is set

Confidence C starts at a 42.5% margin of safety. I added nothing for the cash flow gap, deal risk or leverage, because each is charged elsewhere: the gap in the three starting cash flows and the Bear scenario, leverage in the 11.5% discount rate, and deal risk in the explicit completion table above. A 42.5% discount to the $10.99 Base gives a **maximum buy price of $6.32**. That is below the unaffected price of $6.87 by 8%, and it is a price at which the shares would be worth owning without the offer. The offer is not a floor. If the deal fails, the standalone value stays, and I would regard $5.29 as the realistic bad case.

## What the options market implies

I did not obtain a current options chain for ADT, so I draw no conclusion from implied volatility. With a cash deal pending, option prices would mostly show the market's view of the completion risk, and the share price itself already says that view is very confident.

## Dividend and buybacks

ADT paid $41 million of dividends in the third quarter, about $0.055 a share, and $130 million in the nine months. The merger agreement stops the regular quarterly dividend before closing, and in the negotiation Thrive asked for the dividend to end as a condition of its higher proposals. A holder therefore gets $10.00 and nothing else. Buybacks, $617 million in nine months at an average of about $6.93, ended in practice when the offer was signed.

## Reasons to own ADT

- **A committed cash price.** $10.00 is 45.6% above the unaffected price, the financing has no condition attached, and Thrive owes $473 million in certain failure cases.
- **A short clock.** The antitrust waiting period expires on December 14, the go-shop ends on November 28, and the company expects to close in the first quarter of 2027.
- **A small chance of more.** The go-shop break fee is only $114 million, and a rival could still bid, although earlier outreach found no interest.
- **Real cash generation.** Free cash flow was $1,007 million in nine months, interest costs are falling, and the company retired 89 million shares.
- **A subscription base.** About 80% of revenue is monitoring, revenue payback is stable at 2.3 years and attrition has held near 13%.

## What could go wrong?

- **The deal fails.** A failed stockholder vote, an antitrust problem or a material adverse change would send the stock toward its standalone value, with $6.87 as the reference point and my Bear at $5.29.
- **The vote.** The deal needs a majority of all outstanding common shares, and the final terms contain no voting agreements. Directors and officers hold 4.17%.
- **The cash flow gap.** Free cash flow is running at roughly $1.1 billion in 2026, while the plan for 2027 implies about $0.6 billion to shareholders after interest. If the plan is right, ADT is worth less than my Base.
- **A shrinking base.** Recurring monthly revenue fell 1% and monitoring revenue is down 1% in the quarter and the nine months. Growth now depends on installation and product sales, which earn less.
- **Leverage.** Net debt of $7.6 billion is about 5.0 times earnings after reinvestment, and $1.2 billion falls due within a year.
- **Little upside and a long wait.** The outside date is July 29, 2027. If a review drags on, the annualized return on 12 cents shrinks further.

## Management execution

With guidance withdrawn there is nothing current to grade, so I look at what happened. Management cut net interest expense 13% in nine months, lifted free cash flow 71%, repurchased 89 million shares at an average near $6.93, and negotiated Thrive from $8.60 to $10.00, although I cannot tell whether a higher price was available. Against that, recurring revenue is shrinking, margin fell from 52% to 49% in the third quarter, and the board chose to sign with one bidder and a go-shop after approaching only two other parties. Management credibility is **12/20**: strong on cash and capital allocation, weaker on recurring growth, and the plan it gave the buyer contradicts the 2026 cash flow.
