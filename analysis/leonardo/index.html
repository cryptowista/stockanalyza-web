Leonardo raised its 2026 EBITA outlook to €2.21 billion, but its nine-month free cash flow is still negative

LDO, Euronext Milan. Published November 24, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close (€47.83). Values in euros. Leonardo is an Italian maker of helicopters, military aircraft, defence electronics, cyber security systems and space equipment. EBITA is Leonardo's operating profit before amortisation of acquired intangibles and one-off items. EBITDA adds depreciation to it. Free cash flow to the firm is the cash left after operating costs, taxes, capital spending and working capital, before paying lenders and owners. Free operational cash flow is Leonardo's own cash flow measure, after interest and tax and before dividends and acquisitions. Working capital is the money tied up in inventory and receivables, net of customer prepayments. Net debt is borrowings minus cash. The order backlog is signed orders not yet delivered. Leonardo DRS is the group's US-listed defence subsidiary.

Leonardo raised its 2026 EBITA outlook from €2.03 billion in March to about €2.21 billion, a level it confirmed in November, and nine-month EBITA grew 37.7%. Yet free operational cash flow for the nine months was minus €68 million, so the guidance of about €1.37 billion for the year needs roughly €1.44 billion in the fourth quarter alone. At €47.83 the shares are 5% below my Base value, which is not cheap enough to buy.

My Base value is €50.35, 5% above today's price. The Bear value is €27.36 and the Bull value is €79.96. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is €52.00. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, so the maximum buy price is €28.95, or €27.13 to €31.68 depending on the method. I call it Hold: Leonardo is growing orders, margins and backlog, but the price already pays for about what I can support, and the share is 28% below its 52-week high of €66.26, reached on March 12.

Valuation route: a defence prime contractor, so I value it on ten years of discounted free cash flow to the firm as the primary method and on an exit multiple of FY31 EBITDA as the second. Leonardo gives guidance for 2026 and an industrial plan to 2030, and I use both as a check, not as inputs. Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

What Leonardo is

Leonardo reports Helicopters, Aeronautics, Defence Electronics and Security, Cyber and Security, and Space. Electronics brought in nearly half of the nine-month orders. The group also owns about 71% of Leonardo DRS, a US defence company listed on Nasdaq. In March 2026 Leonardo closed the purchase of Iveco Defence Vehicles, called IDV, which cost about €1.6 billion and was paid from the group's own resources.

Leonardo has 578,150,395 shares, and treasury shares are about 0.21% of them, so I use about 576.9 million. That count is my own derivation, not a figure Leonardo reports. The dividend for 2025 was €0.63 a share, up 21%, and was paid after the June 22 ex-date.

Shares and ownership

At €47.83 the equity is worth €27.6 billion. Adding net debt, an estimated pension deficit and the outside holders of Leonardo DRS lifts the enterprise value to about €33.7 billion, or 9.4 times my FY27 Base EBITDA of €3.6 billion. Outside investors own about 28.7% of Leonardo DRS, so I deduct about €2.5 billion for them, my estimate from DRS's market value of roughly US$10 to 11 billion. The 2025 dividend cost about €364 million, a yield of 1.3%.

Primary valuation: discounted free cash flow to the firm

Sales. I start from the 2026 guidance of €22.1 billion and assume fourth quarter sales of about €6.7 billion. FY27 is €24.2 billion, up 9.5% with a full year of IDV, then €26.2 billion, €28.0 billion and €29.8 billion in FY30, reaching €31.4 billion in FY31. After that growth fades from 4% to 2.5%. Leonardo's own plan, which excludes IDV, targets revenues of €30 billion in 2030. IDV adds about €1.5 billion a year on its April to December run rate, so my FY30 figure excluding IDV is about €28.3 billion, 6% below the plan.

Margin and cash. The EBITA margin is 10.6% in FY27 and 11.5% from FY30, against 8.5% over nine months and about 10.0% implied for 2026. The plan targets EBITA of €3.59 billion in 2030, a 12.0% margin, and my FY30 EBITA of about €3.43 billion is below it. Taxes are 25% of EBITA, depreciation is 4.3% of sales, and capital spending is 5.0% of sales in FY27, falling to 4.3% by FY32. These three are my estimates. Working capital absorbs 15% of each extra euro of sales. Q4 2026 free cash flow is €1.4 billion, close to the €1.44 billion implied by guidance. I discount at 9%.

Secondary valuation: exit multiple on FY31 EBITDA

The second method keeps the cash flows for five years and then values the group at 9 times FY31 EBITDA of €4.96 billion. By FY31 growth has slowed to about 5%, capital spending takes 28% of EBITDA and the return on new capital is 12%. A price of 9 times EBITDA against FY31 free cash flow of €2.41 billion is equivalent to growing at about 3.4% a year forever at a 9% discount rate, which is above my 2% terminal growth, so it is not a harsh multiple. The exit value is 76% of the five year enterprise value, because the five-year cash flows are small next to the value at the end. The result is €55.09.

Valuation triangulation

The two methods give €47.19 and €55.09, a divergence of 15.4%, under the 20% warning level. Both use the same sales, margins, capital spending and net debt bridge, so they do not test those inputs against each other. They test the end point: the discounted model's cash flows after FY31 are worth about 7.6 times FY31 EBITDA, against the 9 times I use in the exit method. I weight the discounted model 60% and the exit multiple 40%. The weighted value is €50.35.

What is the market already pricing in?

At €47.83 the enterprise value is about €33.7 billion. Holding margins at my Base path, the price needs 2030 sales of about €28.3 billion, 95% of my Base path. Holding my Base sales instead, the price needs an EBITA margin near 11.0% in FY30, against my 11.5%. Alternatively my Base cash flows reach the price at an exit multiple of about 7.9 times EBITDA. At today's price my Base cash flows earn about 8.9% a year, just under the 9% I discount them at, and my Bear and Bull cases earn 6.0% and 11.5%. On rough FY27 Base earnings of about €3.33 a share, before interest, the price earnings ratio is 14.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is grade C because the pension deficit, the lease balances and the share count are my estimates, the joint ventures are not valued, and the 2027 to 2031 path is mine. The margin of safety is the standard 42.5% for grade C. The maximum buy price is €50.35 times 0.575, or €28.95, with an entry range of €27.13 to €31.68 by method. At a 10% discount rate the Base value is €45.13 and the maximum buy price €25.95. The price is 65% above my maximum buy price, so the call is Hold, not Buy.

Dividend

The dividend of €0.63 a share for 2025 was up 21% and cost about €364 million. At €47.83 that is a yield of 1.3%. Guided free operational cash flow of about €1.37 billion for 2026 would cover it about 3.8 times, so the payout is safe if the fourth quarter delivers, but at this yield the dividend is not a reason to own the shares.

Reasons to own

  • Orders grew 27.8% in nine months and the backlog is €60.4 billion, about 2.7 times the 2026 revenue guidance.
  • EBITA grew 37.7% and the 2026 EBITA outlook was raised from €2.03 billion in March to about €2.21 billion.
  • Net debt is about 1 times 2026 EBITDA, and guidance has it falling to about €2.2 billion by year end.
  • Leonardo DRS grew third quarter revenue 11% and holds a record funded backlog of US$5.6 billion.
  • The share is 28% below its 52-week high of €66.26.

What could go wrong?

  • The fourth quarter. Nine-month free operational cash flow was minus €68 million, and the year's guidance needs about €1.44 billion in three months.
  • Margin. The nine-month EBITA margin was 8.5%, and my Base needs 10.6% in FY27 and 11.5% by FY30.
  • Leverage. Net debt rose from €1,001 million to €3,325 million in nine months, partly on the IDV purchase.
  • Integration. IDV is a new vehicle business that is guided to add €1.1 billion of revenues from April to December, and its margin and cash flow are unproven inside the group.
  • Concentration in the order book. Electronics orders grew 44.2%, so a delay in a few large contracts moves the whole year.
  • Valuation. The price is 5% below my Base, so there is no margin of safety if sales or margins fall short.

Management execution

Management has raised its 2026 guidance more than once. Orders guidance went from €25 billion in March to €29.5 billion, EBITA from €2.03 billion to €2.21 billion, and free operational cash flow from €1.11 billion to €1.37 billion. The weak spot is cash flow, which is guided to come entirely from the fourth quarter, and the guidance for it is the one number I cannot check until February.

What I would watch from here

The next test is Leonardo's full-year 2026 result, which I expect in the first quarter of 2027, although I have not verified the date. I could not find a verified options-implied move, so I do not use options pricing.

Green: full-year 2026 free operational cash flow at or above €1.37 billion, net debt at or below €2.2 billion, and a price at or below €28.95.

Yellow: free operational cash flow between €1.0 and €1.3 billion, a 2026 EBITA margin below 10%, or net debt above €2.5 billion.

Red: a cash flow miss of more than €0.5 billion against guidance, a cut to the EBITA outlook, or a further acquisition funded with debt.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Margins. With margins 2 points higher every year my Base becomes €60.22, and with margins 2 points lower it is €40.48, so margin alone moves the call from fair to a 18% overvaluation.
  2. Sales. With sales 15% higher from FY28 my Base becomes €57.96, and with sales 15% lower it is €42.74.
  3. The exit multiple. At 7 times EBITDA the Base is €45.92 and at 11 times it is €54.78, against the 9 times I use.

Final verdict: hold at €47.83

Leonardo is a growing defence group with a record backlog and rising margins, and the market has priced most of that in. At €47.83 the shares trade at 5% below my Base value, with a Bear value of €27.36 that is 43% below the price. I would not buy new shares until the price is at or below €28.95, and I see no reason to sell at this price, because the Base value is above it.

Verdict: HOLD at €47.83. Triangulated Base value €50.35, upside +5.3%, Confidence C (16.25/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by method €27.13 to €31.68, maximum buy price €28.95.

The guidance asks the fourth quarter to produce all of the year's cash, and the price already assumes it will.
