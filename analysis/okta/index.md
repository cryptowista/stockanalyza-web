## What Okta is

Okta sells the software that decides who can log in to what: single sign-on, multi-factor authentication, and identity governance for employees (Workforce Identity, 59% of annual contract value) and login for a company's own customers (Customer Identity, 41%). It calls itself the leading independent identity provider and is now selling Okta for AI Agents, which manages the access of software agents and was scheduled for general availability on April 30, 2026 (Rallies). Todd McKinnon is chief executive and co-founder; Brett Tighe is chief financial officer.

The customer base is large and slow-growing. There are 5,255 customers with $100,000 or more of annual contract value, up 6%, and they are about 85% of the total; 605 customers pay $1 million or more, up 22%. Trailing 12-month net retention is 107%, international revenue is 21% of the total and the average contract is about 2.5 years. Remaining performance obligations (RPO, contracted revenue not yet recognized) are $4.858 billion, up 17%, and the part due within 12 months (cRPO) is $2.585 billion, up 14%.

The business model turns revenue into cash well. Customers are billed ahead, so current deferred revenue is $1.75 billion, and free cash flow was 28% to 35% of revenue in each of the last three quarters I read. The same company has headcount of about 6,570 and stock-based pay of $114 million a quarter.

- The cash is real: $498 million of free cash flow in six months, a 32% margin, and a guide of $910 million to $930 million for the year.
- There is no debt and $2.3 billion of cash, about $12.49 a share, after the convertible notes were settled in cash.

## What could go wrong?

- Valuation. The price is 3.1 times my Base and about twice my Bull; it leaves no room for growth below the Bull path.
- Slow growth. Revenue is growing 10% to 11%, net retention is 107%, customers above $100,000 grew only 6%, and the guide includes a one-point headwind from moving services to partners.
- Stock pay. At about 14% of sales it is half of free cash flow, with $739 million of unrecognized compensation, and 2 points of sales higher in every year takes the Base to $63.22.
- Tax. Cash taxes are low because of a valuation allowance; my 21% is a normalized rate and is conservative while the shield lasts. At zero tax the Base is $84.41.
- AI cuts both ways. Agents that log in to software need identity controls, which is Okta's pitch, but software stocks fell earlier in 2026 on fears that AI agents erode subscription pricing (Barchart).
- Security. An identity provider is a high-value target, and a breach would hit trust in the product that every customer relies on.
- Data gaps. Cash taxes paid, interest income apart from other income and a peer table were not available to me.

## The three things that would make this verdict wrong
