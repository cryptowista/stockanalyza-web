# NNN REIT has 99.2% occupancy and AFFO up 5.8%, but its own 8.9% AFFO yield is above the 7.3% it earns on new properties

NNN, New York Stock Exchange. Published November 22, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close ($40.30). NNN is a real estate investment trust (REIT) that owns single-tenant retail properties on long net leases, where tenants pay most property costs. FFO is funds from operations and AFFO is adjusted funds from operations, the cash earnings measures REITs report. ABR is annual base rent. A cap rate is net operating income divided by property value.

## What NNN is

NNN owns 3,817 properties in 50 states, Washington D.C. and Puerto Rico, with 40.8 million square feet, mostly single-tenant retail buildings let on long net leases. Annual base rent was $974.0 million, up 6.8% from a year earlier. Occupancy was 99.2%, up 170 basis points from a year ago, and the weighted average remaining lease term was 10.1 years. The company has raised its annual dividend for 37 consecutive years.

Liquidity was $1.4 billion, including $1.2 billion of undrawn credit line.

For net asset value I use 198.45 million shares and add the $204 million of proceeds.

## Primary valuation: net asset value

I start from annual base rent of $974.0 million and subtract $14 million of unreimbursed property costs, the middle of the 2026 guidance, which gives net operating income of about $960 million. I apply a cap rate of 7.25%, which is close to the 7.3% NNN earned on acquisitions in nine months and the 7.3% on income-producing properties it sold, below the 7.8% on third-quarter sales. The portfolio has a shorter remaining lease term than the new purchases, 10.1 years against 19.6, and I give no credit for development or other assets, which the release does not quantify.

That gives a real estate value of $13.2 billion. I subtract net debt of $5.07 billion, add the $204 million of forward equity proceeds and subtract corporate costs of about $550 million, which is 10 times the $55 million of guided G&A (my estimate of how the market capitalizes overhead). Divided by 198.45 million shares, the value is **$39.45**. The cap rate and the overhead multiple are my estimates.

## Secondary valuation: multiple of AFFO

The second method applies 12.5 times a normalized AFFO of $3.50 a share. That is below the $3.55 to $3.59 guidance by about $0.07 at the midpoint, because I deduct the refinancing cost described above and leave out any further occupancy gain from 99.2%. The multiple implies an AFFO yield of 8.0% and, with a 68% payout, a dividend yield of about 5.7%. With baseline growth of 3% to 4% a year, that is a total return of about 9%, my cost of equity for this REIT. NNN has leverage of 5.6 times and no growth from acquisitions at the current share price, so I do not pay a premium multiple.

The model gives **$43.75**.

## Valuation triangulation

They share the same view of rent and occupancy, so agreement is not independent evidence, but the asset method looks at property prices and the AFFO method at the stock market's own yields. I weight net asset value 60% and the AFFO multiple 40%, because the first ties to observable sale prices. The weighted value is $41.17. The table puts both methods on one basis.

## Reasons to own

- Occupancy is 99.2% and AFFO per share grew 5.8% in the quarter.
- Leases that came up renewed at 105% of prior rent, and re-let properties at 132%.
- Debt is unsecured, 9.8 years long, at 4.2%, with 2.3% floating and $1.4 billion of liquidity.
- The dividend has risen for 37 years and is covered with a 68% payout.

## What could go wrong?

- Cost of capital. The AFFO yield is above the 7.3% acquisition yield, so growth from buying properties is limited, and guidance implies only about $90 million of fourth-quarter investment.
- Refinancing. $750 million of debt at about 3.5% comes due in 2026 and 2027, and new debt costs more.
- Cap rates. A cap rate of 7.75% gives a net asset value of $35.15.
- Tenant concentration by industry. Automotive service and convenience stores are about 35% of rent.
- Other claims. The release did not show development, other assets or other liabilities, and $200 million of other claims would lower net asset value to $38.44.

## Management execution

Management delivered record occupancy, kept the dividend growth record and slowed acquisitions as the share price fell, which I read as protecting per-share results. It also issued stock in the third quarter, with forward sales at an average of $47.28, well above today's price, which will help if the forwards settle at those prices. The held guidance and the lower acquisition pace are consistent with a company that is waiting for a better share price.

## What I would watch from here

**Green:** acquisition volume back above $200 million a quarter at 7.3% or better, AFFO guidance raised, and refinancing of the December 2026 notes at or below 5%.

**Yellow:** occupancy below 98.5%, net debt to EBITDAre above 5.8 times, or tenant rent coverage problems in automotive service.

**Red:** AFFO guidance cut, the AFFO payout above 80%, or sales of income-producing properties at cap rates above 8%.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Cap rates rising to 7.75% or more, which lowers net asset value to $35.15.
2. Vacancy rising so that net operating income falls about 2%, which lowers the Base value to $39.87.
3. Refinancing at rates above 5.25%, or a share price that keeps acquisitions at zero.
