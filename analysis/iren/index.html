In the quarter ended September 30, IREN reported revenue of $228 million, up 66% from the quarter before, and an operating loss of $186 million. Over the same three months it spent about $4.9 billion on data centers, graphics processors and other equipment, 21 times its revenue. The shares cost $14.39, down from $22 two weeks ago. I value what is left for owners after the equipment bill, the stock pay and the debt, and that number is small and very sensitive.

At $14.39 the shares trade 172% above my weighted Base value of $5.28, which is 2.7 times it. The Bull value of $43.96 is 205% above the price, and in the Bear case owners get nothing. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade D and the two valuation methods differ by 32%, which adds 5 points to the margin of safety, so the framework requires 60% and the maximum buy price is $2.11. The framework calls a stock highly overvalued at $6.87, 30% above Base; the price is 2.1 times that line. This is a knife-edge valuation: small changes in what the equipment earns move the Base from zero to above the price, and I show that below.

Valuation route: a capital-intensive AI cloud and data center owner, valued on owner free cash flow before interest: EBITDA less stock-based pay, cash tax and all equipment spending (growth and replacement), with debt, finance leases and prepaid customer revenue counted as claims and interest left out of cash flow. Primary method: a ten-year path with a terminal value, discounted at 10%. Cross-check: a multiple of owner free cash flow at the end of fiscal 2032, discounted back. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What IREN is

IREN, formerly Iris Energy, is an Australian company that owns land, grid power and data centers, and sells access to graphics processors for artificial intelligence work. Until 2025 most of its revenue came from mining bitcoin. In the September quarter bitcoin mining brought in $28 million and AI cloud $200 million, and the company said it plans to substantially finish its mining transition by December (crypto.news). Average mining hashrate fell to 8.8 exahashes a second from 45.3.

The assets are large. IREN says it has secured more than 30,000 acres and 5 gigawatts of power, against a development pipeline of more than 35 gigawatts, which it calls speculative; its target is 0.8 gigawatts of data center capacity by the end of calendar 2027. Horizon 1, built for Microsoft, was accepted by the customer in August 2026 and is in service on NVIDIA's latest rack design. Horizons 2 to 4 are in late construction. Three unnamed customers made up 28%, 24% and 18% of first-quarter revenue.

Contract pricing is rising: new three-year and five-year deals are priced 11% and 12% higher. After the quarter the company said it signed a short-term contract at $40 million per megawatt of IT load and a five-year contract at about $22 million per megawatt, where the customer prepays 90% of the graphics processor cost. Operating annualized revenue doubled to about $1 billion by the end of September. The target of more than $4 billion annualized revenue, fully contracted and, according to the company, fully funded, was moved from the end of 2026 to January 2027 and may slip to February after a customer-requested design change and supplier timing (CNBC headline via StockAnalysis, the release).

Dividend and capital return

IREN pays no dividend and does not buy back shares; it issues them. It sold 12.3 million shares for $541 million in the September quarter and 17.4 million for about $630 million in October, which raised the share count to 424.4 million, and it paid for the Mirantis acquisition in August with 12.6 million shares valued at $502 million. Stock pay was $106 million in the quarter. Every dollar of the $6 billion of unfunded spending that is raised from shares dilutes holders, and at $14.39 a $6 billion raise would be about 417 million shares, roughly doubling the count; I do not model that, because at a fair price the raise would be neutral for value.

Reasons to own IREN

  • Revenue is rising fast: up 66% in one quarter, AI cloud revenue up 182%, and operating annualized revenue doubled to about $1 billion.
  • The backlog is large and relatively safe: $20.3 billion, two-thirds with investment-grade customers, and new contracts are priced 11% to 12% higher.
  • The company owns scarce assets: more than 5 gigawatts of secured power, 30,000 acres and a first building accepted by Microsoft.
  • AI cloud gross margin is 86% before depreciation, and adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 14% to 29% in one quarter.
  • The Bull value of $43.96 is 205% above the price, and the price needs only equipment costs of $1.40 per dollar of revenue, instead of my $1.90, to be fair.

What could go wrong?

  • Financing. $6 billion of the $15 billion remaining fiscal 2027 spending is not financed and depends on market conditions; the company's graphics processor loans cost 9.0% to 9.5% and peer bonds have traded at much higher yields in the sell-off.
  • Delivery. The $4 billion target slipped from December to January or February, and some capacity targeted for December 31 moved to the following quarter.
  • Customer concentration. Three customers were 28%, 24% and 18% of revenue, and the 10-Q says losing or defaulting by one could hurt debt service.
  • Replacement spending. Graphics processors need to be bought again; at 24% of sales it takes most of the margin, and the Base falls to zero at $2.30 of spending per dollar of added revenue.
  • Dilution. The share count rose by about 10% in one month through the at-the-market program and the Mirantis deal, and a $6 billion raise at today's price would roughly double it.
  • Costs. Cash operating expense is guided up another $50 million to $60 million in the December quarter, and stock pay was 46% of sales.
  • Data gaps. Convertible note terms by series, restricted cash purposes, whether the $2.5 billion of unpaid equipment is inside the $15 billion, shares sold since October 30, contract terms by customer and a peer table were not available to me.

Management execution

Daniel Roberts and Willy Pinto, the co-founders, lead the company. Management turned a bitcoin miner into an AI cloud with a Microsoft-accepted building, raised $3 billion of convertible notes in the June quarter, signed a $20.3 billion backlog and kept raising money at rising share prices. The offsets are a delayed $4 billion target, a quarterly loss larger than expected, a $450 million impairment in the June quarter, heavy dilution and $6 billion of spending without a financing plan. Assessment: 11/20, strong on contracting and construction speed and unproven on funding, margins after depreciation and delivery dates. The next test is the arrival of the January revenue target.

Today the contract, pricing and backlog numbers are green; delivery timing and financing are yellow, and one of my red triggers, a large equity raise, is partly in progress through the at-the-market program. That is why the verdict is reduce and not hold: the business is growing, but the price already assumes the equipment earns more than I can show.

The three things that would make this verdict wrong

  1. Equipment that costs $1.40 per dollar of annual revenue or less. The company's own contract price implies $1.80 to $1.90, and at $1.50 my Base is $12.62, close to the price.
  2. Margin and stock pay both better than my Base. At 4 points more EBITDA margin the Base is $14.84; at 4 points less stock pay it is the same, and at both the Bull case of $43.96 is the result.
  3. Cheaper money. At a 9% cost of capital the Base is $12.49, and the price needs 8.8%. IREN's graphics processor loans cost 9.0% to 9.5% and its convertible notes less than 1.2%, so a mix of them could get there.

Each of the three alone takes the Base near the price, and two together take it above. That is the case for a smaller position and not for ignoring the stock; my rules ask for a margin of safety that this price does not give.
