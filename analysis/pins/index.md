## What Pinterest is

Pinterest is an advertising business built on visual search. People use it to plan a kitchen, a wedding or a wardrobe, and advertisers pay to appear next to that intent. It reports one segment and had 661 million monthly users in the third quarter, up 10%. Management says about 57% of them belong to Gen Z. Users are not revenue, though, and the split by region shows why.

The U.S. and Canada produced about $894 million of third quarter revenue, or 76.2% of the total, at $8.38 of revenue per user. Europe produced $193 million (16.4%) at $1.21, and the rest of the world $87 million (7.4%) at $0.23. About 394 million users sit in the rest of the world. If they ever monetize at even a fraction of the North American rate, revenue rises a lot. They have not done that so far, and more users in a low-revenue region do not repair weak advertiser demand.

The product list is long: AI visual search, a shopping assistant, automated performance ad tools, app-install ads and connected-TV inventory from the tvScientific acquisition (about $465 million, per the filing). They are options on higher revenue per user. I do not value any of them as a separate profit stream, because the filings do not show their returns yet.

Stock pay is 24.85% of third quarter revenue and 25.2% over nine months. The last row is not a valuation input, because the pay was earned over past and future service, shares are withheld for taxes and vesting timing differs from the accounting charge. It does show that "record free cash flow" and "profit for owners" are two different statements here.

My model treats the two parts of stock pay separately. Shares already granted and unvested (58.2 million) are claims on the equity, with a finite credit for the service still to come: $1,185 million of unrecognized cost over about 2.0 years at September 30. Future grants are replaced by a cash cost, so I do not add a dilution factor on top, which would count the same labor cost twice. In the Base that cost starts at 21% of revenue and falls to 12% by year ten, against about 25% in 2026. That decline is the single largest bet in the valuation.

Taxes get the same caution. Pinterest carries a $1,603 million deferred tax asset on its balance sheet. I let it shield only $500 million of tax, used at no more than 15% of positive taxable income a year, and charge the full 23% after that.

## Capital, shares and debt

At September 30 there were 499.4 million Class A and 73.3 million Class B shares, or 572.6 million in total. Adding the 58.2 million unvested restricted units gives 630.8 million economic units, down 3.7 million from 634.6 million at June 30. The legal count rose 7.1 million and the unvested pool fell 10.9 million. Third quarter grants were 5.5 million units against 12.4 million released and 4.1 million forfeited, which is real progress. One quarter of fewer grants does not prove a permanently lower cost.

There are also 8.55 million employee options with an average strike of $19.96, priced with a Black-Scholes model at 45% volatility. The $1,000 million of 1.75% convertible notes mature on March 1, 2031 and convert at about $22.72 a share (44.0 million shares), with a capped call up to $30.59. I put the coupons and the $1,000 million repayment into the cash flows once, at their real dates, and price only the net premium above $30.59 as a separate claim. I do not subtract the notes again as debt.

Cash and marketable securities were $1,470 million. I treat $500 million as the working cash the business needs and count the other $970 million as excess. A new $700 million secured revolver was undrawn at filing. It adds flexibility, not cash, and it comes with a net leverage covenant of 3.5 times.

Buybacks changed the picture this year. In nine months the company repurchased 111.4 million shares for $2,039.8 million, an average of $18.31 (my arithmetic), against a Base value today of $14.30. That is a comparison with today's model and not proof that anyone overpaid when the shares were bought. The company bought no shares under its programs in the third quarter, and $1,948 million of authorization remains, which is permission and not cash. I model no future buybacks.

## Primary valuation: free cash flow to common shareholders

The method values the cash that can reach common shareholders after the business has paid for its growth, its taxes and its notes. I forecast ten rolling years ending October 30 and discount at a 12.5% cost of equity: the 4.98% ten-year Treasury yield (October 29) plus a 4.75% equity premium times a risk loading of 1.6 gives 12.58%, rounded to 12.5%. The 1.6 is my loading, not a measured beta. The starting point is a 2026 revenue anchor of $4,811 million, which is nine months of reported revenue plus the midpoint of the fourth quarter guidance. Base assumptions, all estimates:

- Revenue growth of 10% in years one and two, fading to 3% by year ten.
- A cash EBITDA margin before stock pay of 28.5% rising to 33%, and capex of 1.5% of revenue.
- Stock pay as a cash cost of 21% of revenue falling to 12%, with a finite credit for service on awards already granted.
- Terminal growth of 3% after year ten, with the same investment and cash-buffer needs as the forecast years.

## Secondary valuation: five explicit years

The cross-check runs the same model for five explicit years and then holds year-five economics forever: growth of 3%, a 32% cash margin and stock pay at 17% of revenue. It gives **$11.10**, which is 22.4% below the primary value and 44.3% below the price.

This is not an independent confirmation. It uses the same revenue path, tax assumptions, financing and claims, and differs only in how much of the later improvement it believes. The $3.20 gap is the price of that belief: the primary value needs stock pay to keep falling from 17% to 12% of revenue and margins to keep rising after year five, and the cross-check does not. The terminal part is 60.7% of the cross-check value, in a five-year model. I found no independent long-horizon forecast of cash to common shareholders to blend in, and no analyst target enters this analysis.

## Historical cross-check and growth diagnostic

At $19.92, the 572.6 million shares counted at September 30 are worth $11,407 million. Adding the $1,000 million notes and subtracting all $1,470 million of cash and securities gives an enterprise value of about $10,937 million, or 2.34 times trailing revenue of $4,680 million. This leaves out the unvested awards and the options, so it flatters the multiple and does not replace the bridge above. I did not refresh peer multiples for this update, and a consistent owner-cash history for 2019 to 2026 is not available to me.

A price to earnings growth ratio is not meaningful. Reported earnings are depressed and include the stock pay, and mixing them with a forecast on a different definition would give a number that looks precise and means nothing. Growth is valued through the explicit revenue and cost paths instead.

The test that matters is whether the cash flow keeps growing after the notes are repaid. From year six, once the awards already granted have been expensed and the notes are gone, Base cash to common shareholders grows from $817 million to $1,302 million in year ten, or 12.4% a year. That is a structural path with execution risk, and it depends on the falling stock pay. In the Deep case the same ratio is optical: cash rises from $8.8 million to $16.2 million, from almost nothing.

## Dividend and buybacks

Pinterest pays no common dividend and has no stated policy for one, so this stock does not suit anyone who needs income. The $1,000 million of convertible notes pays 1.75% to its holders and not to shareholders. Buybacks are discretionary: none were made under the programs in the third quarter, and the remaining $1,948 million is authorization, not cash. The valuation counts cash that could reach shareholders, not cash that will be paid out.

## Reasons to own PINS

- **Reach and intent.** 661 million monthly users, up 10%, using the service to plan purchases. That is a useful position for advertisers if monetization catches up.
- **A strong North American engine.** Revenue in the U.S. and Canada grew 14% and revenue per user 10% to $8.38, in the largest and most profitable region.
- **Large international headroom.** Europe earns $1.21 per user and the rest of the world $0.23, against $8.38 in North America. Even partial convergence adds revenue, if local advertisers show up.
- **Specific product levers.** Automated performance ads, measurement tools, app-install ads and connected-TV inventory are testable paths to higher revenue per user, though none has shown separate returns yet.
- **Real balance sheet progress.** $1,470 million of cash and securities, an undrawn $700 million revolver, and unvested awards down 10.9 million in one quarter.

## Management execution

Revenue of $1,173.9 million fell below the $1,190 to $1,210 million range management itself set, by $26.1 million against the midpoint. Adjusted EBITDA met its range. On the call the chief executive acknowledged the miss and named the international sellers, and the company described changes to its go-to-market team. A new CFO, James Dibbo, joined shortly before the call, according to the transcript, so I do not credit a new capital allocation policy that has not been tested.

On the numbers, cash conversion is strong and accounting is consistent: the 10-Q disclosed the notes, the award flows and the credit facility clearly. The weaker points are the missed guidance, a stock pay bill near a quarter of revenue and a year of buybacks partly funded with debt at an average price above my current value. I have no verified return on the tvScientific acquisition yet. Giving credit for communication is fair. It does not cancel a forecast miss.

## Final verdict: hold at $19.92, do not add

Pinterest is a large, growing, cash-generating advertiser whose owners pay for that cash in shares. At the October 29 close the price was above my Base and above every entry case, and the results released after the close add to the doubts: a revenue miss, a weaker fourth quarter outlook and flat revenue in Europe. The lower extended-hours quote is closer to my value, but I will not buy a stock because it fell, and the first regular session after the results has not happened.
