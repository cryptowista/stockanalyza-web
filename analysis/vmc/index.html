Vulcan Materials reported a third quarter in which revenue fell 4.7% to $2,185 million and shipments of aggregates fell 3.1% to 62.7 million tons. Cash gross profit on each ton still rose 0.9% to $11.95, and the adjusted EBITDA margin edged up to 32.3% from 32.1%, even though unit cash costs rose 7%, or 3% without diesel, according to a third-party recap. That is the whole investment case in one line: price is beating cost in a year when volume is not helping. At $274.84 the market pays 15.7 times my 2027 EBITDA estimate, and the shares are 17.0% below their 52-week high.

At $274.84 the shares trade 18.3% above my weighted Base value of $232.34. The Bear value of $166.46 is 39.4% below the price and the Bull value of $346.86 is 26.2% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. My two methods disagree sharply, $271.21 from EBITDA and an exit multiple against $174.02 from ten years of cash flow, and the price is within 1.3% of the higher one. Confidence is grade C, which sets a required margin of safety of 47.5% and a maximum buy price of $121.98. The verdict is Hold, do not add: at this price the Base case earns 9.6% a year, just below my 10% hurdle, and the Bear case loses about 39.4%.

Valuation route: a US aggregates producer with low debt and a moderately cyclical, pricing-led business. Primary method: EBITDA for 2027 to 2030, with debt paid down from cash flow, sold at an exit multiple at the end of 2030 and discounted at 10%. Cross-check: ten years of free cash flow before interest, discounted at 9.3% with a terminal value. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. What Vulcan is

Vulcan, based in Birmingham, Alabama, with about 11,200 employees, quarries and sells construction aggregates (crushed stone, sand and gravel) for roads, buildings and data centers. Quarries are heavy and cheap to ship only a short distance, so each one is close to a local monopoly, and that is why price per ton can rise faster than cost. Management says data centers are a strong driver of nonresidential demand, projects move quickly from quote to shipment, and residential building stays weak until affordability improves.

The exit multiple needs a reason. Today the market pays 15.6 times my 2027 estimate, and a third-party page shows a five-year average near 19 times. In 2030 my Base has EBITDA growing 5% a year with price doing the work, capital spending at 27% of EBITDA and no net debt, so I take 16.5 times, below that five-year average. The test of that choice is what it implies: at a 9.3% cost of capital and my 2030 cash flows, 16.5 times only works if the business grows at about 5.7% a year forever, well above the 3.5% in my cross-check. That gap is the premium the market has paid for pricing power, and the next sections show what it is worth.

The methods disagree by 43.7%, and the whole gap comes from one question: what the business is worth after 2030. The two methods share the same EBITDA forecast, the same capital spending and the same net debt. If I replace the cash flow method's perpetuity with the same 16.5 times exit in 2030, discounted at 9.3%, it gives $275, almost exactly the price. The perpetuity version values the business at 10.1 times EBITDA in 2036, because at 9.3% and 3.5% growth, with capital spending at 27% of EBITDA and a 22% tax rate, a dollar of EBITDA is worth about ten times. To justify the market's multiple you need either growth near 5.7% forever or a cost of capital near 7.4%.

Both views are defensible. Aggregates have traded at 15 to 19 times for years because pricing has beaten inflation, and a perpetuity model cannot capture a premium that depends on pricing power continuing. The choice of method does not change my decision, though: the primary value of $271.21 is also close to the price, so neither method points to a buy. I weight the primary method at 60% because it matches how the market prices the stock, and I show the full range so that the weighted figure does not look more certain than it is.

Dividend and buybacks

The dividend is $2.08 a share a year, a yield of 0.76%, and the company paid $202.3 million of it in nine months. The next dividend is payable on November 24 after a November 9 ex-dividend date, according to MarketBeat, and the quarterly rate is $0.52 by my arithmetic from the $67 million paid in the third quarter. Buybacks are the bigger payout: $749.8 million in nine months against $38.1 million a year earlier, which cut the diluted share count by 2.6%. Management called the dividend steadily growing and said buybacks will continue with consistent returns. I assume none in my Base, and all spare cash goes to debt.

Reasons to own Vulcan

  • Price is beating cost. Cash gross profit a ton rose to $11.95 in a quarter when shipments fell 3.1% and unit cash costs rose 7%.
  • 2027 pricing has a head start. Management expects mid-to-high single-digit price growth including mix, with increases already announced for January 1 and a midyear round to follow.
  • Data-center demand is strong, and quote-to-shipment times are short, so Vulcan sees it early.
  • Leverage is low. Net debt is 1.9 times EBITDA, the first note maturity is $400 million in 2027, and management says it has ample capacity for acquisitions.
  • Capital is being returned and redeployed: $749.8 million of buybacks and $290.6 million of acquisitions in nine months, funded partly by $572.1 million from business sales.
  • In my Base, net debt falls to zero by 2030 without any change in policy.

What could go wrong?

  • The multiple. 97% of the primary value is the exit multiple, and a cash flow method that assumes no premium gives $174.02. If aggregates stop earning 16 times or more, the price has little to stand on.
  • Cash conversion. Operating cash flow fell 24% in nine months while full-year EBITDA is guided to grow about 3%. I do not have the reason, and my 2027 conversion of about 78% is a recovery I am assuming.
  • Fourth quarter. Even the $2.4 billion guide needs about $592 million of EBITDA, roughly 14% more than last year's fourth quarter according to a third-party recap.
  • Energy. About $120 million of diesel and energy cost in 2026, with management assuming diesel stays high into 2027.
  • Volume. Shipments fell 3.1% in the third quarter, residential building is weak with no recovery date, and management gave no 2027 volume figure.
  • Capital allocation. Buybacks funded partly by commercial paper, and "several more" bolt-on deals expected, mean the net debt I model may be too low at year end.
  • Unverified inputs. Cash taxes, tax depreciation, the 2027 capital spending and the covenant terms are my estimates or unread.

Management execution

  • The operating record this year is solid on price: cash gross profit a ton is above last year in a down-volume quarter, and trailing EBITDA is $2,325.8 million.
  • Guidance has been hard to meet. A third-party recap says 2025 ended near $2.32 billion of EBITDA, below the range given for that year, and the 2026 energy headwind estimate rose from "over $100 million" to about $120 million.
  • Portfolio choices are consistent: sell concrete and asphalt, buy aggregates quarries. Two bolt-ons closed in the third quarter and one in early October, in Texas, Tennessee, southeastern Colorado and southern Ohio, typically half a million to one million tons each.
  • Management's comment that diesel may ease in mid-to-late 2027 came with the admission that it is a guess, which is the right way to say it.
  • Buybacks accelerated to $350 million in the third quarter, at prices that were all above my weighted Base (the 52-week low is $237.03).

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Fourth-quarter EBITDA near $592 million and a 2027 guide that implies growth of at least 6%.
  • Operating cash flow recovering toward 70% of EBITDA, with the company explaining the 2026 drop.
  • Cash gross profit a ton above $12.50 in the first half of 2027, which would show the January 1 increases sticking.

Yellow

  • Fourth-quarter EBITDA below $560 million, or shipments down more than 3% again.
  • Buybacks at the third-quarter pace of $350 million while commercial paper keeps rising.
  • Diesel staying high through the first half of 2027 with the price-cost gap narrowing.

Red

  • A 2027 guide that implies EBITDA below 2026.
  • Unit cash costs rising faster than price for two quarters in a row.
  • A large acquisition at more than 15 times EBITDA while operating cash flow is still falling.

The next hard data are the fourth-quarter results with 2027 guidance, which management says will come in February (I have not confirmed the date).

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Cost catches up with price. EBITDA grows only 2% in 2027 and 3% in 2028, then 6%, 5% and 4%. The weighted Base falls to $214, 22.1% below the price.
  2. The market stops paying a premium for aggregates and values the business at 14 times at the exit. The weighted Base falls to $208, 24.1% below the price.
  3. Cash conversion stays weak, costing an extra $200 million a year (about 40% of the gap between my 2027 conversion and 2026). The weighted Base falls to $221, 19.6% below the price.
