About $0.52 of that comes from refunds of tariffs the company paid earlier, which it discloses and which I do not expect to repeat. Take them out and the midpoint is about $7.36, and the operating margin the company guides drops from about 23% to about 21.5%.

At the $85.28 close the shares trade 13.2% below my triangulated Base value of **$98.28**. The Bear value is $65.13, 23.6% below the price, and the Bull value is $124.51, 46.0% above it. Weighting the three cases 25%, 55% and 20% gives $95.86. After the release the shares fell to $79.36 in after-hours trading, a 6.9% drop, and even that is 24.2% above the buy price. The Base value is the weighted result of two methods; the Bear and Bull come from the scenario model alone, because I did not build a separate Bear and Bull for the multiple method.

Valuation route: a premium footwear brand owner (HOKA and UGG) that outsources production and sells through wholesale and its own stores and websites. Primary method: normalized earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization (EBITDA) with the tariff refunds removed, multiplied by 9. Secondary method: a four-step cash flow model of owner cash (cash flow after stock compensation and all capital spending) with a terminal multiple. Net cash is added once. I add no value for the buyback authorization, because buying shares is a use of cash and not an asset.

## What Deckers is

Deckers owns two brands that make up almost all of its sales. HOKA, running shoes, sold $677.2 million in the second quarter of fiscal 2027 (the quarter ended September 30), and UGG sold $786.4 million. Together they were 98.9% of the $1,480.6 million total. About 74% of sales go through wholesale partners and 26% through Deckers' own direct-to-consumer (DTC) channel, which includes online sales.

The quarter looked fine on the surface: sales up 3.5% (2.8% at constant currency), gross margin of 60.5% and an operating margin of 26.3%. The details are less comfortable. DTC comparable sales, the cleanest read on consumer demand for the brands, fell 6.4%. U.S. sales were down 0.4%, and international carried the quarter with growth of 9.0%. HOKA grew 6.8%, respectable but a slowing pace for the brand that carries the growth story. Management gave no outlook for the third quarter.

## Which cash to value

I value owner cash: operating cash flow minus all capital spending minus stock compensation. Deckers' stock compensation is small, about 4% of free cash flow, so owner cash is close to reported free cash flow. The harder problem is timing. Almost all of the year's free cash flow arrives in the December quarter ($1,020 million of the $1,097 million in fiscal 2026), so only full fiscal years are comparable.

The normalized figure starts from the guidance midpoint of $5,712.5 million of sales and a 21.5% margin, which gives operating profit of $1,228 million and EBITDA of $1,301 million (depreciation and amortization of about $73 million is my estimate). Taxed at 23% that is $946 million, less about $40 million of working capital, or roughly $905 million of owner cash. The twelve-month figure of $1,071 million is 18% higher because it contains the tariff refunds and a stronger gross margin. That is inside my 25% limit, so a simple multiple is allowed and the valuation is a today value.

Net cash is the other moving part. Cash swung from $1,603 million at June 30 to $1,210 million at September 30, and it refills by roughly $900 million by December. I use the average, $1,406.5 million, and test the full range from $1,210 million to $1,907 million (the March 31 level). Operating leases, roughly half a billion dollars, are not deducted, because the rent is already inside EBITDA and cash flow.

## Shares and buybacks

Shares outstanding fell from 148.5 million in June 2025 to 136.7 million in June 2026, a drop of 8.0%. The weighted diluted count was 135.3 million in the second quarter against 147.7 million a year earlier. I use 134.5 million diluted shares, the Q2 figure less shares bought since (my estimate), and I do not assume any further reduction. The second-quarter repurchase was 3.2 million shares for $299.3 million ($93.65 each), and the year to date total is 6.5 million shares for $637.5 million, an average of $98.77. Both averages are above today's $85.28, so part of the buyback was paid for at a price that has not held. The plan for the year is $1.0 billion, about 8.7% of the market value at the close, and the remaining authorization is $4.4 billion.

Owner cash of about $905 million is already after stock compensation, and the company plans to spend $1.0 billion on buybacks this year. The September cash of $1.21 billion and the December inflow cover that, but I treat repurchases as a use of the cash I already value and not as extra growth.

## The primary valuation: EBITDA times a multiple

For a branded consumer company with stable demand I use a multiple of EBITDA in the 8 to 12 times range. A premium range of 12 to 18 times needs same-store growth above 3%, and Deckers' DTC comparable sales are falling, so that range is out of reach. I use 9 times, below the midpoint of 10, because the DTC channel is soft and U.S. sales are flat. FY2027 EBITDA of $1,301 million (tariff refunds excluded) times 9 is $11,711 million. Adding net cash of $1,406.5 million and dividing by 134.5 million shares gives **$97.53** a share.

| Value per share | Cash $1,210 million | Cash $1,406.5 million | Cash $1,907 million |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 times EBITDA | $86.39 | $87.85 | $91.57 |
| 9 times EBITDA | $96.06 | $97.53 | $101.25 |

All the value in this method sits in the capitalized run rate. There is no explicit forecast period, so the continuation value is 100% of the total, and the second method puts a calendar on the same business.

## The secondary valuation: a four-step cash flow model

The second method forecasts owner cash for the rest of fiscal 2027 and for fiscal 2028 to 2030, then applies a terminal multiple to the last year. Owner cash each year is sales times operating margin times (1 minus 23% tax), less 15% of any increase in sales to fund working capital. The first flow is the normalized $905 million plus $23 million that I add back for the first half (estimated first-half cash flow excluding refunds was about -$23 million). Flows are discounted at 10%, with 8% and 12% as tests, and net cash of $1,406.5 million is added once. All of the inputs are my assumptions.

## Triangulation: how the two values combine

The two Base values are $97.53 and $99.42, a gap of 1.9%. That closeness is partly a coincidence of 9 times EBITDA and 12 times terminal cash, and both lean on the same driver: the 2027 margin and the growth rate afterwards. Their agreement therefore does not raise my Confidence. At 8 times EBITDA and a 10 times terminal multiple the two are still close ($87.85 and $88.58). I weight the EBITDA method 60% and the cash model 40%: 0.6 times $97.53 plus 0.4 times $99.42 = **$98.28**.

## What the price already assumes

At $85.28 the market value is $11,470 million. Less net cash of $1,406.5 million, the enterprise value is $10,064 million, which is 7.7 times FY2027 EBITDA without refunds and 11.6 times earnings per share without refunds ($7.36). At the after-hours $79.36 the EBITDA multiple is 7.1. That is already below the bottom of the 8 to 12 times range I use for a stable consumer company. The shares offer a normalized owner cash yield of 7.9% at the close.

Put differently, the price assumes the brands do not grow at all, or that margins fall materially. It does not assume a collapse: in my Bear case sales shrink 4%, 3% and then stay flat while the margin drops to 17.5%, and that case is still worth $65.13. Several different stories fit $85.28, which is why I treat this as a check and not a forecast.

A quick look at growth quality: price to earnings without refunds is 11.6 and revenue growth is about 4%, so this is not a growth-adjusted valuation case and I use the growth ratio (PEG) as a diagnostic only. Owner cash was $920 million in FY2025 and $1,052 million in FY2026. Both years had working capital swings of up to $300 million, so I do not read that increase as a trend. My Base is flat to slightly up.

## How the buy price is set

Confidence C calls for a margin of safety of 35% to 50%. I use 35%, the low end, because the company has no debt, a lot of cash and high cash conversion. Revenue and margin risk is already in the scenarios, the discount rate is tested, the terminal multiple is tested between 9 and 14 times, tariff and consumer risk sit in the Bear case, and data limits sit in the Confidence score, so none of them is charged twice. The maximum buy price is $98.28 times (1 minus 35%) = **$63.88**. At 30% it would be $68.80 and at 40% $58.97. It is a decision reference, not an order to place.

## Dividends and buybacks

Deckers pays no cash dividend, so there is no income to wait on. Returns to shareholders come through repurchases, which are discretionary. The $1.0 billion planned for fiscal 2027 is about 110% of my normalized owner cash, so it can only continue without drawing down cash if the December quarter cash flow comes in strong.

## Reasons to own

- **Two strong brands.** HOKA and UGG together earned a gross margin of 60.5% and an operating margin of 26.3% in the quarter, and the brand, not the factory, holds the economics.
- **A net cash balance sheet.** Cash of $1.21 billion at the seasonal low and no borrowings leave room for seasonal working capital and for buybacks.
- **A low expectation in the price.** At 7.7 times EBITDA the market already prices the brands as if growth had stopped.
- **International growth.** International sales grew 9.0% and carried the quarter while U.S. sales were flat.
- **Cash returned.** The share count is down 8% in a year, helped by a strong December quarter of cash flow.

These reasons justify research and monitoring. They do not show that $85.28 offers the downside buffer I require.

## What could go wrong?

- **Consumer weakness at full price.** DTC comparable sales of -6.4% suggest shoppers are less willing to pay full price, and that is where brand fatigue would show first.
- **HOKA slowing.** Growth of 6.8% is already slowing, and a fall below 5% with DTC still negative is one of my thesis breakers.
- **Fashion cycle at UGG.** UGG is more than half of sales and is seasonal and trend driven.
- **Tariffs.** The refunds flatter this year and will not repeat. Future tariff costs and sourcing changes could pressure the gross margin from the guided 58.5%.
- **Buyback timing.** The company bought stock at an average of $98.77 this year. A lower share count only helps if the price paid is below value.
- **Wholesale concentration.** About 74% of sales depend on retail partners and their inventory decisions.

## Management execution

Management delivered a strong year in fiscal 2026 and shrank the share count by 8%, but the story this quarter is less clean. The profit guidance went up while the cause is a refund, and the company declined to give a third-quarter outlook. I do not have the earlier guidance to measure how much the sales outlook moved, which limits how far I can judge credibility. The chief executive called the consumer environment challenging. I give management and capital allocation 7 of 10 in business quality. What I want to see is guidance that does not rely on one-offs and buybacks done at lower prices.

## What I would watch from here

### Supports the thesis

- HOKA growth above 10% and DTC comparable sales back above zero.
- Gross margin without refunds staying above 58%.
- Share count down more than 4% over a year, paid for from operating cash.

### Watch closely

- HOKA growth between 5% and 10% (it is 6.8% now) and DTC comparable sales between -5% and 0%.
- Gross margin without refunds between 56% and 58% (the guided 57.0% sits here).
- Net cash at the September low between $0.5 billion and $1.0 billion.

### Breaks the thesis

- HOKA growth below 5% together with negative DTC comparable sales, or gross margin without refunds below 56%.
- UGG sales falling year on year, or net cash at the September low below $0.5 billion.
- Share count rising. DTC comparable sales are already in red territory today, at -6.4% against a threshold of -5%, though it is one quarter.

The thresholds are my monitoring triggers, not company guidance. The next review is the December quarter report, expected in late January 2027.

## The three most important thesis breakers

- **HOKA stalls while DTC stays negative.** The 9 times multiple already assumes a stable business, and a stalled HOKA would take it to the Bear case.
- **Margin without refunds below 21.5%.** Every point of margin is worth about $4 a share in my model.
- **Buybacks outrunning cash.** If the company keeps buying at these paces while the September cash level keeps falling, the balance sheet advantage shrinks.

## Final verdict: hold at $85.28

Deckers is a good business at a lower price, but the close is still 33.5% above where I would buy. The refund-boosted guidance is the kind of number that looks better than it is, and the DTC data is what I would look at next.

**Verdict: HOLD at $85.28, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $98.28, upside 15.2%, Confidence C (15/25), required margin of safety 35%, maximum buy price $63.88. Main thesis breaker: HOKA growth below 5% while DTC comparable sales stay negative.**

For holders this is a review, not an instruction to sell. For new money it means waiting for a price near $64, or for evidence in the December quarter that DTC comparable sales are turning and the margin holds without refunds.
