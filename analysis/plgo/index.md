# Pelagos paid another company $254 million in nine months to underwrite its business, and the stock still trades near book value

Pelagos Insurance Capital lists 108 employees. Much of the business it insures is originated and underwritten by The Fidelis Partnership, a separately owned managing agency that earned $254.1 million of commissions from Pelagos in the first nine months of 2026, 13.1 points of the combined ratio. The arrangement has worked: book value per diluted share has risen from $16.24 at the January 2023 separation to $26.60, and the third quarter combined ratio was 91.5%.

My Base value is $29.95 a share, the Bear value $21.98 and the Bull value $39.48. The stock is up 57% over the past year according to XTB and sits about 4% below my Base. At that price the Base earns about 12% a year over five years, above the 10% I require, but the Bear is 24% below the price, and earnings in this industry swing with catastrophes: the annualized operating return on equity was 15.2% in the first quarter, 5.1% in the second and 15.3% in the third.

Valuation route: property and casualty reinsurer and specialty insurer, valued with a model of the excess return on equity over a 10% cost of equity, built from book value and a normalized combined ratio, cross-checked with a price to book multiple. Free cash flow is not used, because an insurer's cash flow follows premiums and claim payments, and a growth-adjusted multiple is not meaningful when earnings swing with catastrophes.

## What Pelagos is

Pelagos calls itself a capital allocator and risk selector in specialty insurance and reinsurance. It was founded in 2014, separated and listed on the NYSE in 2023, and renamed from Fidelis Insurance Holdings in May 2026. It writes more than 100 products in two segments, Insurance and Reinsurance, and offers quota share and excess of loss cover, and it also supports Lloyd's syndicates through funds at Lloyd's.

The structure matters for the valuation. The Fidelis Partnership is separately owned and managed and receives a ceding commission of 11.5% of net premiums on open-market business (3.0% on business from third-party underwriting agents), a portfolio management fee of 3.0%, and a profit commission of 20% of operating profit above a 5% underwriting return on equity. The 10-year framework agreement was rolled on September 18, 2026. The upside is access to business Pelagos could not source alone. The cost is a fee that is paid whether or not the business earns a profit, and an underwriter whose interests are close to, but not the same as, shareholders'.

Each case starts from a normalized year, with premiums of $2.75 billion (my estimate, a little above the 2026 run rate) and a combined ratio, then the return on equity fades over ten years toward a terminal level and equity grows at a set rate, with the rest distributed. ## Reasons to own Pelagos

- Book value per diluted share has grown from $16.24 to $26.60 since the 2023 separation, with cumulative dividends on top.
- A combined ratio of 92.4% for nine months, 91.5% in the quarter, and a 15.3% annualized operating return on equity.
- Aggressive and so far well-timed buybacks: 17.7 million shares at an average of $20.47, which cut the share count about 7% in nine months.
- A 47% dividend increase to $0.22 a quarter.
- Premium growth of 5% to 6% with Insurance up 13% in the quarter, through underwriting partners that give access to specialty business.

## What could go wrong?

- **Catastrophe and large losses:** $115.9 million in one quarter, 14.8 points of premiums. A larger event can erase a quarter's profit, as in the second quarter, when the annualized return was 5.1%.
- **Reserves:** prior-year development was adverse by $4.7 million in the quarter, and the Insurance segment was adverse by $10.3 million over nine months (Baltimore bridge, political risk, property). Aviation claims cost $287 million in 2024.
- **Underwriter alignment:** The Fidelis Partnership earns commissions of 13.1% of premiums, larger than the whole 7.6 point underwriting margin of the first nine months.
- **Softening market:** Reinsurance premiums fell 20% in the quarter and 36% of gross premiums are ceded, so results depend on the price and credit of reinsurers ($1.3 billion recoverable on reserves).
- **Leverage and capital:** debt to total capital is 25.2% and I found no solvency ratio in the text I read. A $340 million letter of credit program is in place, with one facility expired on September 18.
- **Markets:** a 1 point rise in yields cuts the bond portfolio by about 2.9%, hedge funds are $364.2 million, and the credit loss provision rose $15.7 million in the quarter.
- **Buying above book:** at $28.83 repurchases dilute book value per share rather than raise it.

## Management execution

Management has grown book value per share by 64% since the separation and returned capital steadily, including $95.2 million in the third quarter. The weaker record is underwriting results in 2024 and the first half of 2025, when adverse development on aviation claims and heavy catastrophe losses pushed the combined ratio above 100%. The first nine months of 2026 are the first sustained evidence of better results, and nine months is short.

## Final verdict: hold at $28.83

Pelagos has done what a specialty insurer is supposed to do: grow book value, buy stock below it and raise the dividend. The price of 1.08 times book assumes that continues at a return on equity of about 11%, which the last nine months support and the two years before them did not. The Base earns 12% a year, but confidence is C and a Bear year costs about 24%.

**Verdict: HOLD at $28.83, watchlist for new money. Base $29.95, +3.9% against the price, Bear $21.98, Bull $39.48, Confidence C (17.5/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, maximum buy price $17.22, range $16.82 to $17.48 under the two methods.**

The buy zone can come to the stock in two ways: a price near $17.22, or evidence of a steady combined ratio under 93% with a disclosed solvency ratio, which would lift the buy price to about $20.96. At 1.08 times book you are paid for the 15% quarters and charged for the 5% ones, and the second kind arrived as recently as June.
