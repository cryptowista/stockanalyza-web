## What Cloudflare is

Cloudflare runs a global network that sits in front of websites, applications and AI services, and sells security, speed, zero trust access and a developer platform on top of it. Matthew Prince, the co-founder, is chief executive; Thomas Seifert is chief financial officer. About 10 million developers are active on the Workers developer platform, and requests from AI agents grew more than 1,700% from a year earlier (Zacks). The company has about 4,700 employees after a plan announced on May 7, 2026 that cut about 20% of the workforce.

The customer numbers are strong. There are 5,006 customers paying more than $100,000 a year, up 25%, dollar-based net retention is 122%, and current remaining performance obligations (cRPO, contracted revenue due within 12 months) grew 37%. The company says its annualized revenue passed $3 billion in the quarter.

The business model is capital intensive. Purchases of property and equipment were $121.8 million in the quarter, 15.6% of sales, and the company guides network capital spending at 14% to 15% of revenue for the year. That is why free cash flow, at 10% of sales, runs well below the 17.5% non-GAAP operating margin.

## Reasons to own Cloudflare

- Growth is accelerating: revenue up 39% in the quarter, the fastest in nearly four years, and the fourth-quarter guide implies 38%.
- Margins are moving the right way: non-GAAP operating margin 17.5% against 15.3% a year earlier, and management expects GAAP profit in the fourth quarter.
- Demand is broad: cRPO up 37%, large customers up 25% to 5,006, net retention 122% and about 10 million developers on Workers.
- The balance sheet is large: $5.1 billion of cash against $4.5 billion of zero-coupon notes that mature in 2030 and 2031.
- Management raised the 2026 revenue guide by about $97 million at the midpoint in one quarter and cut costs with a 20% reduction in staff in May.

## What could go wrong?

- Valuation. The price is 6.7 times my Base and 3.2 times my Bull; it leaves no room for a slower path or a lower multiple.
- Stock pay. At 22% of sales it is 27% larger than non-GAAP operating profit and grew 45% in a year. A reduction to 13.5% by 2031 is in my Base, and 2 points of sales higher in every year takes the Base to $38.85.
- Gross margin. GAAP gross margin fell to 71.2% from 74.0% and the company lowered the floor of its long-term range to 70%; the 10-Q warns that component shortages could compress margins further.
- Capital intensity. Network capital spending is 14% to 15% of revenue, and free cash flow margin fell to 10% from 13% while profit margin rose.
- The notes. $4.5 billion falls due in 2030 and 2031 unless converted; today's price is above the 2030 conversion price, which shifts the settlement toward shares and cash together.
- Market sentiment. The stock fell 8.3% on November 5 in a sell-off in which the Nasdaq fell nearly 5%, and its multiple leaves it exposed to another one in AI-linked companies (Crypto Briefing).
- Data gaps. Free cash flow for the fourth quarter, capped call cap prices, cash taxes paid and a peer table were not available to me.

## The three things that would make this verdict wrong
