Yelp produced $179.4 million of owner free cash flow over the last twelve months, after stock pay and all capital spending. That is 15.6% of its $1.15 billion market value, but most of it arrived in the second half of 2025. In the first half of 2026 owner cash was $49.9 million, taxes paid in cash were a quarter of the tax charge, and earnings are falling. On a full-tax, guidance-based footing I value $107.9 million, 9.4% of the market value.

At the $20.70 close my triangulated Base value of $21.54 is 4.1% above the price, inside the 15% band I treat as fair value. The Bear value is $10.31 and the Bull value $33.91. Weighting the three cases 30%, 50% and 20% gives $20.64. Confidence is grade C (14/25), the required margin of safety (MOS) is 45%, and the maximum buy price is $11.85, so the price is 74.7% above it. The two valuation methods give $22.41 and $20.23, a 10% gap, and the shares are fairly priced in either case.

Valuation route: a local advertising marketplace that is shrinking slightly in its core. Primary method: a five-year discounted cash flow on normalized owner cash. Secondary method: enterprise value to cash earnings (adjusted EBITDA minus stock pay and capital spending). Net debt of $23.9 million, including the unpaid retention payments from the Hatch acquisition, is counted once.

What Yelp is

Yelp sells advertising to local businesses, mostly through its listings and search results. Services advertisers such as contractors and home services bring in the most revenue, while restaurants, retail and other categories are smaller and shrinking. It has about 510,000 paying locations, an 85.6% gross margin and a 16.6% return on invested capital. In 2026 it bought Hatch, an artificial intelligence tool for service businesses, for $271 million plus about $30 million of retention payments over two to three years.

The risk is how customers find local businesses. Yelp relies on search engines and app stores for traffic, says so in its filings, and does not quantify the share. If AI assistants and agents become the first place people ask for a plumber or a dinner reservation, a listing business loses ground. Advertising clicks fell 5% in the June quarter.

Shares, debt and buybacks

Cash of $94.1 million less the $100.0 million revolver and about $18 million of Hatch retention still to be paid (my estimate) is net debt of $23.9 million. I do not deduct operating leases of $23.3 million, because rent is already in the profit I value. Shares were 54.98 million at June 30, plus 0.6 million of dilution, so I use 55.58 million. Cash and shares are both taken at June 30. The share count has fallen from 82 million in 2018, but the sequence in 2026 was a $174 million first-half buyback, 164% of free cash flow and partly funded by drawing $165 million on the revolver, at an average $24.92 in the second quarter, followed by a debt-funded acquisition at 7.7 times revenue and a pause in repurchases until 2027. At $20.70 that buyback looks expensive, and I count it against capital allocation. $339 million of authorization remains.

Where the value sits

The value after year five is 56% of the Bear, 67% of the Base and 71% of the Bull primary value. That is well below the 85% level at which a five-year model deserves a second look, so the answer is not mostly a bet on far-off years.

The secondary valuation: a multiple of cash earnings

The second method takes adjusted EBITDA minus stock pay and capital spending: $129 million in the Bear, $164 million in the Base and $204 million in the Bull. It applies 5.0, 7.0 and 8.5 times and subtracts net debt, which gives $11.18, $20.23 and $30.77 a share.

These multiples are before tax, so I translated them to see what they assume. After 28% tax, 7.0 times is 9.7 times earnings, which at a 10.5% required return means earnings that stay flat, about +0.2% a year forever. That is my Base view: the listings business is not growing, the core is shrinking slightly and the guide is down. The Bear multiple of 5.0 times is 6.9 times after tax, earnings shrinking about 2.1% a year at 12%. The Bull multiple of 8.5 times is 11.8 times after tax, +1.4% a year at 10%. None of these requires heroic growth, and a company with falling clicks should not get more. I have no peer table, so the multiples are my assumptions.

What the price already assumes

At $20.70 the market value is $1,151 million and the enterprise value is $1,174 million: 3.7 times adjusted EBITDA, 7.2 times EBITDA after stock pay and capital spending, and 10.9 times my normalized owner cash. Owner cash of $99.8 million, about 8% below my $107.9 million, justifies today's price at 2% growth and a 10.5% discount rate. The market has already priced in the earnings decline and the tax normalization, and is paying for a flat-to-shrinking franchise.

The shares are down 32% this year and close to the low end of a 52-week range of $17.26 to $34.49.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C calls for a margin of safety of 35% to 50%. I use 45%: 40% as the base for this grade, plus 5 points for concentration in search engines, app stores and a single advertiser category. AI substitution sits in the Bear case, tax timing sits in the cash flow, and the leverage is small (net debt is 0.07 times EBITDA), so I add nothing for them here. The maximum buy price is $21.54 times (1 minus 45%) = $11.85. At a 35% margin it would be $14.00. It is a decision reference, not an order to place.

Reasons to own

  • High margins and returns. An 85.6% gross margin and a 16.6% return on invested capital, with little debt.
  • Services advertising is holding. Revenue from the largest category was flat at $241.0 million.
  • New revenue lines. AI data licensing (including local results in ChatGPT) and Hatch add revenue outside the core.
  • A smaller share count. From 82 million shares in 2018 to about 55 million.

These reasons justify keeping the company on a list. They do not close the gap between $20.70 and the $11.85 I would want to pay.

What could go wrong?

  • AI intermediating local search. Assistants and agents could answer local questions without sending people to Yelp. Clicks are already down 5%.
  • Dependence on search engines and app stores. The filing names them as sources of traffic and does not say how much.
  • A shrinking restaurant, retail and other business. Down 10% in the quarter, with restaurant closures noted in the news.
  • Stock pay at 7% of revenue. Management targets below 6% by the end of 2027.
  • Selling and short interest. The chief financial officer sells shares every month at about $20 to $23, the chief executive sold about 400,000 shares in early 2026 at $24 to $29, and 21.5% of the float is short. JP Morgan has an Underweight rating from September 2026.

Management execution

Yelp has returned a lot of cash and cut its share count, but the 2026 sequence matters more to me than the history: heavy buying near $25, then borrowing to buy Hatch at 7.7 times revenue, then stopping repurchases to repay debt. It is a sign of weak discipline in capital allocation, not a disaster, and I did not lower the verdict for it. I give management credibility 6 of 10 and capital allocation 5 of 10.

What I would watch from here

Supports the thesis

  • Services advertising growing year on year and paying locations rising.
  • Adjusted EBITDA above $325 million for the year.
  • Stock pay falling toward 6% of revenue.

Watch closely

  • Adjusted EBITDA between $315 million and $325 million, as guided.
  • Revenue in the third quarter near $365 million to $370 million.
  • Revolver balance and the timing of buybacks restarting in 2027.

Breaks the thesis

  • Services advertising turning negative, or clicks falling faster than 5%.
  • Adjusted EBITDA below $315 million, or Hatch missing its payback.
  • Cash taxes rising to the full charge with no earnings growth to absorb it.

The thresholds are my monitoring triggers, not company guidance. The date of the third-quarter report has been announced, and I will review the numbers when it arrives.

The three most important thesis breakers

  • Services advertising turns down. It is the only part of the core that is holding.
  • AI assistants take local discovery. This would hit both the traffic and the multiple.
  • The Hatch purchase does not pay back. It adds debt and retention cost and leaves a smaller core.

Final verdict: hold at $20.70

Yelp is a profitable, lightly indebted listings business that the market already values as flat. The headline cash flow overstates what it will earn once taxes normalize and the strong second half of 2025 drops out of the twelve-month total.

Verdict: HOLD at $20.70. Triangulated Base value $21.54 (range of the two methods $20.23 to $22.41), Confidence C (14/25), required margin of safety 45%, maximum buy price $11.85. Main thesis breaker: AI assistants and search engines taking over local discovery.

For holders this is a review, not an instruction to sell. For new money it means waiting for a price near $11.85 to $14.00, or for evidence that clicks and Services advertising have stabilized.
