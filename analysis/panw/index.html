Palo Alto Networks reported net income of $482 million for the quarter ended October 31. Before tax it earned $25 million; the rest is a $457 million tax benefit, which the release says includes a one-time item tied to a historical transaction. The headline numbers are better: sales up 39% to $3.44 billion, next-generation security recurring revenue up 66% to $9.73 billion, a guidance raise. The number I value is smaller than all of them. After $622 million of stock-based pay in a single quarter, non-GAAP operating profit of $1,059 million becomes $437 million.

At $352.88 the shares trade 289% above my weighted Base value of $90.73. The Bull value of $155.02 is 56% below the price and the Bear value of $36.35 is 90% below it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade C, which gives a required margin of safety of 42.5% and a maximum buy price of $52.17. My rules say reduce when the price is more than 30% above the Base, which is $118 here, and the shares are about three times that level.

Valuation route: a cybersecurity platform company with recurring subscription revenue and large acquisitions, valued on owner free cash flow: non-GAAP operating profit less stock-based pay, taxed at 21%, less capital spending above depreciation, less acquisition payments, plus the working capital that growing prepayments bring in. Primary method: a ten-year path with a terminal value, discounted at 10%. Cross-check: a multiple of owner free cash flow at the end of fiscal 2031, discounted back. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Palo Alto Networks is

Palo Alto Networks sells cybersecurity as a platform: network and AI security (firewalls and cloud security), Cortex (security operations) and Idira, the identity platform the company reports alongside them. In the fourth-quarter release the company named strength across all three. It also owns Chronosphere, an observability business bought for $3.35 billion, and on November 18 it announced its intent to acquire Applied Compute to deliver secure open weight AI models; I did not find the price.

The key number the company reports is next-generation security annual recurring revenue (NGS ARR): $9.73 billion at October 31, up 66% from a year earlier. Remaining performance obligations (contracted revenue not yet recognized) were $21.0 billion, up 35%. The growth is not all organic. Goodwill rose from $4.57 billion to $22.0 billion in fiscal 2026 and to $22.56 billion at October 31, and weighted diluted shares rose from 709 million to 838 million in a year. The CFO said on the fourth-quarter call that last year's net new ARR included a nine-figure benefit from one large AI model customer moving to Chronosphere. The release does not split growth into organic and acquired, and I did not find the split elsewhere.

Guidance for fiscal 2027 (August 2026 to July 2027) was raised on November 18: revenue to $14.52 to $14.57 billion from $14.10 to $14.20 billion, NGS ARR to $11.475 to $11.525 billion, non-GAAP earnings a share to $4.26 to $4.30 from $4.16 to $4.19, with a 29.5% non-GAAP operating margin and adjusted free cash flow of 38% of sales.

Historical cross-check

The shares have moved violently. The 52-week range is $139.57 to $432.33. The stock closed at $394.49 on October 28 and at $313.84 on November 6, a fall of 20.4% in seven sessions, and it was up 3.92% at $352.88 on the morning after the results. After the fourth-quarter report in September it fell about 13% in two sessions, the worst two-day move since February 2024, according to Sentisense.

Non-GAAP earnings guidance for fiscal 2027 is $4.28 at the midpoint, so the price is 82 times it. That figure adds back stock pay of about $2.4 billion a year; deducting it after a 21% tax would leave about $2.0 a share (my rough arithmetic), and the price would be about 175 times that. I do not value the shares on non-GAAP earnings, and I do not use a historical multiple, because the business has changed size and shape: diluted shares are up 18% and goodwill is almost five times higher than a year ago. A peer table on a matched basis is not available to me from free sources, so I do not use one.

A growth-adjusted ratio gives no help: guided non-GAAP earnings a share grow 11%, from $3.84 to $4.28, while sales grow 27%, and the gap between the two is stock pay and acquired growth. I give it no weight.

Reasons to own Palo Alto Networks

  • Recurring revenue is growing fast: NGS ARR is up 66% to $9.73 billion, and the company guides $11.5 billion at July 31, 2027.
  • Contracted revenue is rising: remaining performance obligations are $21.0 billion, up 35%, and total deferred revenue is $14.6 billion.
  • Cash generation is real: $1.8 billion of free cash flow in a quarter and $7.2 billion of net cash after $1.9 billion of convertible notes.
  • Management has raised guidance in one quarter: fiscal 2027 sales from $14.10 billion to $14.52 billion at the low end, and EPS from $4.16 to $4.26.
  • The shares are 18% below their 52-week high.

What could go wrong?

  • Stock pay. $622 million in one quarter is 18.1% of sales and 58.7% of non-GAAP operating profit. If it does not fall toward 10% of sales, the Base is worth about $83 (2 points higher in every year) or less.
  • Dilution. Weighted basic shares are up 21% in a year, mostly from CyberArk, and the company just announced another acquisition.
  • Acquired growth. The fiscal 2027 guide of 27% includes a full year of CyberArk and Chronosphere; I could not find the organic rate, and last year's net new ARR had a nine-figure one-off from a single customer's migration.
  • Gross margin. It fell from 74.2% to 68.0% in a year; the release does not say how much is acquired businesses and how much is hardware or hosting cost.
  • The multiple. At 108 times my fiscal 2027 owner free cash flow, a fall to 60 times with no change in the business takes the shares to about $332 in 2031.
  • Convertible notes. $1.93 billion moved from long-term to short-term; settlement in cash or shares is a use of the cash I count, and I did not check the terms.
  • Cash quality. Part of the cash flow is prepayment: more than half of my fiscal 2027 owner free cash flow is working capital, which stops when growth slows.

Management execution

The chief executive is Nikesh Arora and the chief financial officer is Dipak Golechha. Management has beaten its own quarterly guide and raised the year's guide, and it has absorbed two large acquisitions, CyberArk and Chronosphere. The offsets are the size of the stock pay, the share count that rose 18% in a year, and a reporting style that favors adjusted measures: the free cash flow measure adds back acquisition payments, and the operating margin leaves out stock pay. Assessment: 13/20, good on execution and weaker on capital allocation. The next test is whether stock pay as a share of sales falls from 18%.

What I would watch from here

The next results are for the fiscal second quarter, expected in February 2027. They need to show sales of $3.53 billion to $3.55 billion, NGS ARR of $10.20 billion to $10.22 billion and non-GAAP EPS of $1.01 to $1.03, and, more important for me, stock pay below 17% of sales.

Today one of my red triggers is close: stock pay was 18.1% of sales in the first quarter, and the red line is a second quarter above 18%. Two readings are yellow: gross margin of 68.0% and an acquisition announced with no price. Growth, guidance and net cash are green.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Stock pay that stays at 17% to 18% of sales. Two points higher in every year take the Base to $83.45; stock pay at 13% in the Bear is part of its $36.35.
  2. Acquired growth that fades without being replaced. My Bear has sales of $18.4 billion in fiscal 2031 against $23.5 billion in the Base.
  3. A fall of the multiple. At 60 times owner free cash flow in 2031 the shares return -1.3% a year; at 22 times, -18% a year.

At $352.88 the shares cost 108 times that cash flow and 3.9 times my Base value.
