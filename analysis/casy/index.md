Casey's calls itself the fifth-largest pizza chain in the United States, and the stock still trades on gasoline. In the fiscal year that ended April 30, 2026, fuel gross profit was $1,496.6 million, slightly more than the company's entire EBITDA of $1,483.6 million. Each cent per gallon of fuel margin on 3.5 billion gallons is worth about $35 million, or 2.4% of EBITDA. The margin rose 3.9 cents that year, from 38.7 to 42.6, and fuel gross profit supplied $260 million of the $284 million increase in EBITDA.

The market has paid for that story. The stock closed at a high of $914.72 on June 11, 2026 and fell 14.2% in one day on September 9, after the first-quarter report. At $691.14 it is 24.4% below that high and still 17.8 times trailing EBITDA. My Base value is **$478.11**, which puts the price 44.6% above it. The Bear value of $264.57 is 61.7% below the price, and even the Bull value of $622.87 is 9.9% below it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is $460.91.

This is a good business at a price my method cannot support. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of **$274.91**, or $214.72 to $315.04 depending on the method. The stock would need to fall 54.4% to reach the top of that range. The verdict is Hold, and I would not add at this price.

Valuation route: a convenience store retailer that earns most of its profit from food, groceries and fuel, so I value it on enterprise value to EBITDA, normalized for fuel margin, with a ten-year cash flow model as the second method. Economic classification: Compounder (11/12). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

## Why a pizza chain trades on cents per gallon

Casey's operated 2,944 stores in 19 states at April 30, 2026, about half of them in Iowa, Missouri and Illinois. Fuel is about 60% of revenue, but revenue is not where the profit comes from. The table shows where fiscal 2026 gross profit of $4,321.0 million came from.

| Category | Gross profit ($ million) | Share of total | Gross margin |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prepared food and dispensed beverage | 1,040.9 | 24.1% | 58.6% |
| Grocery and general merchandise | 1,635.4 | 37.8% | 35.8% |
| Fuel | 1,496.6 | 34.6% | 14.1% |
| Other | 148.1 | 3.4% | 24.5% |
| Total | 4,321.0 | 100.0% | 24.6% |

Operating expenses were $2,837.4 million, which leaves EBITDA of $1,483.6 million. The bridge from gross profit to EBITDA is the key to this valuation: fuel provides 34.6% of gross profit but would cover 100.9% of EBITDA on its own. The food business earns more per dollar of sales, and the inside business, meaning everything sold in the store, grew gross profit steadily. Inside same-store sales rose 4.2% in fiscal 2026 and inside margin reached 42.2%, up 0.7 points. Those are the durable numbers. The fuel number is the one that moves the stock in a single quarter.

Fuel margin has no fixed level. It was 38.7 cents per gallon in fiscal 2025 and 42.6 in fiscal 2026, and in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 it was 47.8, with margins during the quarter that management said ranged from the 30s to the 60s, mostly in the 40s. In August it was in the low 40s. The table converts those margins into EBITDA, using fiscal 2026 gallons of 3,515.2 million and my normal level of 41.5 cents (my estimate: a little below the fiscal 2026 actual and above the two-year average of 40.7, consistent with the August reading).

| Fuel margin | Cents per gallon | Difference from 41.5 | EBITDA effect ($ million) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fiscal 2025 actual | 38.7 | -2.8 | -98 |
| Fiscal 2026 actual | 42.6 | +1.1 | +39 |

The first-quarter figure is the one to be careful with. At 47.8 cents, a year of fuel would add $221 million to EBITDA against my normal level, which is 15% of fiscal 2026 EBITDA. Management left full-year guidance unchanged after that quarter, saying it normally updates after the seasonally strong second quarter. I treat the guidance, not the quarter, as the anchor.

## Cash, debt and shares

At July 31, 2026, Casey's held $524.1 million of cash against $2,430.5 million of debt and finance lease obligations ($104.3 million current and $2,326.2 million long-term), which gives a net debt of **$1,906.5 million**, or 1.2 times trailing EBITDA of $1,554.4 million. At April 30 it was $1,908.6 million, or 1.3 times fiscal 2026 EBITDA. Liquidity was about $1.4 billion including about $900 million of undrawn credit lines. Reported debt includes finance leases, and the sources I used do not split them out. Operating leases are left out of net debt because rent is already deducted in EBITDA.

The share count was 37.02 million at the latest count, to which I add about 0.18 million for dilutive awards (my estimate, based on the 37.14 million diluted weighted average in the first quarter), which gives 37.2 million shares, a market value of $25.71 billion and an enterprise value of **$27.62 billion**. The board raised the repurchase authorization to $1 billion in June 2026. Casey's bought back $200.5 million of stock in fiscal 2026, after $0.7 million the year before.

## Primary valuation: normalized forward EBITDA

Enterprise value (EV) is market value plus debt minus cash. I apply a multiple to forward EBITDA and subtract net debt of $1,906 million. Forward EBITDA is 45% of fiscal 2027 and 55% of fiscal 2028, because that is how much of each year falls in the next twelve months, and each year is restated to a normal fuel margin of 41.5 cents (my estimate). Management guides fiscal 2027 EBITDA up 8% to 10%, which is $1,602 million to $1,632 million, and my Base case uses $1,615 million. Values are in $ million unless stated.

| EV / EBITDA multiple | 11.0x | 14.0x | 16.5x |
| Enterprise value | 15,917 | 22,288 | 27,323 |

| Equity value | 14,010 | 20,382 | 25,417 |
| Value per share (37.2 million shares) | $376.62 | $547.90 | $683.25 |

The primary Base value is **$547.90**. The multiple needs a reason, because a multiple of 14 is only a number until it is tied to growth. Setting enterprise value equal to after-tax operating profit times one minus growth divided by a 13% return on new capital, all over the 7.5% discount rate minus growth, 14.0x implies growth of 5.3% a year forever. The Bear multiple of 11.0x implies 4.6% and the Bull multiple of 16.5x implies 5.7%. None of these is a perpetual growth rate I would defend. The multiples can only be right if Casey's grows well above inflation for a decade, which is what the plan says and what the last three years delivered (a 16% EBITDA growth rate from fiscal 2024 to fiscal 2026, 504 stores added). They are generous on a steady-state test, and that is the reason the second method comes out so much lower.

My Base EBITDA path is $1,615 million in fiscal 2027, $1,644 million in 2028, $1,753 million in 2029, $1,852 million in 2030 and $1,940 million in 2031. The 1.8% increase in 2028 reflects fuel margin falling from my 43.9 cents estimate for fiscal 2027 to 41.5, which costs about $88 million. Fiscal 2029 EBITDA of $1,753 million is a 5.7% annual growth rate from fiscal 2026. The new three-year plan (fiscal 2027 to 2029) targets 8% to 10%, which would be $1,869 million to $1,975 million, so my Base sits $116 million to $222 million below the plan. That gap is mostly fuel: the plan starts from a year in which margin was above its long-run level.

Taxes are 25% of operating income, inside management's 24% to 26% guide. Depreciation is $490 million in fiscal 2027, as guided, rising to $610 million. ## Valuation triangulation

The two methods differ by 37.9%, above the 30% level at which I lower the confidence grade and widen the required margin of safety. They share the same EBITDA path, so they are not independent on the operating forecast, and they answer different questions: the multiple method asks what a buyer pays for normal earnings, and the cash flow model asks what those earnings are worth after the capex that produces growth. The gap comes mostly from three sources: the multiple method treats a decade of growth as if it were permanent (the steady-state test above), the cash flow model charges $740 million to $800 million a year of capex against a business whose depreciation is $490 million to $610 million, and the cash flow model fades growth to 3% after fiscal 2031. I weight the multiple method at 60% because that is how convenience store chains are priced and sold, and the cash flow model at 40% because the steady-state test shows the multiple is generous. The unweighted range is $373.42 to $547.90, and the weighted value is a point inside it, not a narrower claim.

## How good is the growth?

The growth is real. Inside same-store sales averaged 4% from fiscal 2024 to 2026, Casey's added 504 stores in three years, and EBITDA grew 16% a year. Fiscal 2026 EBITDA growth of 23.6% was flattered, though: fuel gross profit supplied $260 million of the $284 million increase, and 3.9 cents of margin was worth $137 million on its own. Net income grew 30.7% and EPS 30.9%, with the diluted share count down only 0.2%, so almost none of the per-share growth came from buybacks. The plan for fiscal 2027 to 2029 is 8% to 10% EBITDA growth, at least 400 new stores, mid-single-digit inside same-store sales growth and about $2 billion of free cash flow over three years. Operating expenses are guided up 5% to 7% in fiscal 2027, below EBITDA growth, and the plan says efficiencies should keep opex growth below EBITDA growth. Free cash flow growth of 23.4% in fiscal 2026 came with capex up 29.6%, so Casey's is paying for growth as it goes. I do not extrapolate 23.6% and I treat 8% to 10% as the upper half of what I will credit.

## Dividend

The quarterly dividend was raised 14% to $0.65 a share in June 2026, the 27th consecutive annual increase. At $2.60 a year the yield is 0.4% and the cost is about $97 million a year, against free cash flow of $721.6 million in fiscal 2026, so the dividend is covered 7.4 times, and the payout is 13.6% of fiscal 2026 EPS. ## Reasons to own CASY

- **The inside business compounds.** Inside same-store sales grew 4.2% in fiscal 2026 on inside margin of 42.2%, and prepared food earns a 59.3% gross margin.
- **Store growth is funded.** The plan adds at least 400 stores over three years, 120 a year this year, about half new builds and half small acquisitions, and management calls the acquisition market favorable.
- **The balance sheet is light.** Net debt is 1.2 times trailing EBITDA, liquidity is about $1.4 billion and interest costs about $95 million a year.
- **Remodels are paying back.** Remodeled CEFCO stores averaged about a 30% lift in prepared food and beverage sales, and the construction drag should ease by the fourth quarter of fiscal 2027.
- **Cash conversion is solid.** Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was 1.9 times net income, and free cash flow was $721.6 million after $655.9 million of capex.

## What could go wrong?

- **Fuel margin normalizes.** The first quarter averaged 47.8 cents and August was in the low 40s. Every cent below my normal level removes about $35 million of EBITDA, and a return to 38.7 cents would remove about $98 million.
- **The price already assumes the plan.** At 17.3 times forward EBITDA a miss on store growth, inside margin or opex costs more in multiple than in earnings.
- **Inside growth is slowing.** Same-store sales were 3.2% in the first quarter against 4.2% for the year, grocery was up 2.7% and national-brand snacks and beer were weak.
- **Remodel drag lasts.** The CEFCO program takes about 25 basis points off inside same-store sales and 50 off fuel gallons, and management does not expect a turn before the fourth quarter.
- **Capex is heavy.** Guided capex of about $800 million is 1.6 times guided depreciation of $490 million, so free cash flow stays near 3% of today's market value.

## Management execution

Management delivered its previous three-year plan and beat it: 504 stores added against a target of 350, a 16% EBITDA growth rate against 8% to 10%, and about $1.7 billion of free cash flow. It also sold the 10 Mississippi stores that came with an acquisition, calling the market the wrong place to operate once it had reviewed the returns, and it is spending through a remodel program that costs inside sales growth and fuel gallons for several quarters. The fiscal 2027 outlook leaves room: EBITDA up 8% to 10% on an opex increase of 5% to 7%. After a quarter at 47.8 cents of fuel margin management did not raise it, which is either discipline or a view that August's low-40s margin is closer to normal, and I take it as the second. Management credibility scores 15/20. The deduction is for a plan whose last three years were helped by fuel margin that management does not control.
