What AXT is

AXT grows crystals and slices them into wafers, the base layer on which other companies build lasers and light detectors. Indium phosphide (InP) wafers go into the lasers and detectors inside AI data center optical transceivers. Gallium arsenide (GaAs) wafers serve vertical-cavity lasers (VCSELs), robotics and radio chips. In the second quarter InP revenue was $30.7 million, GaAs $6.6 million, germanium $0.3 million, and a group of consolidated raw material joint ventures added $10.0 million.

The operating base is in China. Of $176.7 million of long-lived assets, $175.8 million sit there, and China took $30.8 million of the $47.6 million of second-quarter revenue, 64.8%. Management said China was above 50% of revenue in the quarter and expects a 40% to 60% range. Exports from China need permits, which is why the third-quarter guide is worded as revenue that already has a permit or needs none.

The ownership structure matters for valuation. AXT's main operating subsidiary, Tongmei, has outside investors, and noncontrolling interests in Tongmei total about 14.5%. Those holders took 14.6% of second-quarter net income ($1.9 million of $13.0 million). I treat that as a claim on the operating business. Tongmei withdrew its Shanghai STAR Market listing application (the exchange accepted the withdrawal in July), which created redemption rights on about $49 million invested by private equity funds in 2021. Management says those investors currently want to stay in, and that AXT has the cash to redeem them in full.

Why trailing earnings and headline cash flow cannot be used

Trailing earnings blend a loss-making 2025 with one very good quarter, and the company's quarterly profit includes interest on the $632.5 million it raised in April. Cash flow tells a different story from the income statement. Operating cash flow in the first half was -$0.9 million and free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capital spending) was -$9.5 million. That figure was helped by $12.0 million of customer advances, which rose from $0.1 million at the end of 2025. Excluding the advances and deducting $1.8 million of stock-based compensation (SBC, a real cost paid in shares), the hard figure is -$23.3 million. Another $6.4 million of equipment was paid for with notes receivable, a non-cash item.

Inventory rose to $96.3 million from $81.7 million at the end of 2025 and accounts receivable to $36.7 million from $26.8 million, so growth is absorbing cash before it produces any. I did not reopen the 2021 to 2025 filings for cash flow history. The reported revenue history is $137 million in 2021, $141 million in 2022, $76 million in 2023, $99 million in 2024 and $88 million in 2025, which shows how short the profitable record is.

My normalized 2027 scenarios are EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) of $106 million in the Bear case, $162 million in the Base and $201 million in the Bull. They rest on revenue of $330 million, $415 million and $470 million. A straight line between the capacity targets management gave on the call, roughly $60 million of quarterly InP revenue capacity at the end of 2026 and about $130 million at the end of 2027, plus about $20 million a quarter from other lines, implies about $495 million of 2027 revenue if every wafer is made and sold. The Bull case is 95% of that path, the Base 84% and the Bear 67%. That path is my estimate, not company guidance. For comparison, a third-party aggregator shows 2026 revenue of about $218 million, close to my $217 million (first half $74.5 million, third quarter $66 million, fourth quarter about $77 million).

Secondary valuation: ten-year free cash flow model

Free cash flow is operating profit after 14% tax (China's rate is 15%, and the second-quarter effective rate was 13.9%), plus depreciation, minus capital spending, minus working capital at 25% of each revenue increase, minus SBC. Capital spending is $100 million in 2027, the plan an analyst cited on the call and management confirmed, then $90 million, $80 million and $70 million, and 6% of revenue after that. Cross-check and growth diagnostics

Two checks are not available. A historical multiple comparison would need profitable years, and AXT lost money in 2023, 2024 and 2025, so the history gets no weight. A peer comparison needs synchronized multiples for the few other indium phosphide suppliers, which I could not source. I use a forward P/E check instead: 2027 Base EPS (earnings per share) is $1.86 (operating profit plus about $21 million of net interest income, 14% tax, 14.5% minority share, 66.5 million shares), and I apply 20x in the Bear, 32x in the Base and 40x in the Bull. That gives $25, $59 and $91. For reference a third-party aggregator shows 2027 EPS consensus near $2.15, 16% above my Base EPS and close to my Bull EPS of $2.28. It is one source, it moved a lot over the summer, and I do not use it in the Base.

The growth valuation cross-check (SM-19) is diagnostic only. The price is 42.9 times my 2027 Base EPS (37.1 times the $2.15 consensus), and my Base EPS grows 42.5% in 2028, a PEG of about 1.0 that supports nothing on its own. Growth durability: structural demand with execution risk and a strong rebound element, since 2025 was a loss. Cyclical distortion: high, because gross margin sits at a shortage level and the comparison base is a trough. GAAP and non-GAAP are close ($0.17 against $0.19 in the second quarter) because SBC is only $1.8 million a half year. The framework does not allow SM-19 to set fair value.

The free cash flow growth quality check (SM-20) says the same from the cash side. Free cash flow in the first half was -$9.5 million, so a compound growth rate is not meaningful and I use a path to free cash flow instead. What is the market already pricing in?

At $79.79 and 66.5 million economic shares, market value is about $5.31 billion. After removing $628.7 million of net cash and adding back the 14.5% minority share, the operating business is valued at about $5.47 billion. That is 33.8 times my 2027 Base EBITDA, 22.8 times my 2028 Base EBITDA and 20.7 times the annualized third-quarter guide of $264 million. At my Base multiple of 24x, the price requires EBITDA of about $228 million in 2027, 41% above my Base and 13% above my Bull. At the Bull multiple of 32x it needs about $171 million, 5.6% above my Base and below my Bull.

In cash flow terms, the price equals about 2.4 times my entire Base free cash flow path at an 11% discount rate. Alternatively, it equals my Base path at a discount rate of about 6.4%, or my Bull path at about 9%, which the model values at $81. A buyer today needs five things to be true: indium phosphide demand keeps outrunning supply for years, AXT fills a capacity target that doubles twice, export permits keep flowing, prices and margins hold near today's levels as competitors add capacity, and the new capacity turns into free cash flow after the 2027 spending. The market is not pricing a good outcome. It is pricing something close to my best case.

How the buy price is set

A Confidence D score carries a base MOS of 50% or more, and I start at 50%. I add 5 points for export permits and geopolitical exposure, which is a binary risk to shipments, 5 points for cyclicality and operating leverage, and 5 points for the valuation divergence, and subtract 5 points for the net cash balance sheet. The required MOS is 60%, the top of what the framework allows before a stock becomes a plain no-buy. Applied to the $53 Base, the maximum buy price is $21, and a stronger 70% margin marks a strong-buy reference near $16. There is no customer concentration add-on, because no customer exceeds 10% of revenue. The shares would need to fall about 73% from $79.79 to reach $21.

Dividend and capital return

AXT pays no common dividend, so the income check does not apply. The Series A preferred stock accrues a 5% cumulative dividend, which is $2.9 million unpaid and included in the $8.3 million preferred claim above. There is no active buyback and the company says it does not plan one.

Reasons to own AXTI

  • A genuine bottleneck. Management says demand outpaces supply however fast capacity is added, backlog is above $100 million and orders are being rationed to what can be built.
  • Visible contracts and cash. Long-term supply agreements carry $98.5 million of remaining obligations through the first half of 2029, and customers are paying deposits to reserve capacity.
  • Operating leverage is proven in one quarter. Gross margin went from 8.0% to 44.9% in a year, and the third-quarter guide implies EPS of $0.30 to $0.32.
  • A funded balance sheet. About $629 million of net cash after the April raise covers the 2027 capital spending plan several times over.
  • Low customer concentration. The top five customers are 30% of revenue and none exceeds 10%.
  • Technology headroom. Management says its 6-inch indium phosphide capability is progressing and larger diameters support margins.

What could go wrong?

  • Thesis breaker: export permits and restrictions. China needs a permit for shipments abroad, and the CFO said AXT cannot predict the timing or success of permits for any order. The guide of $66 million is only the revenue that already has a permit or needs none.
  • Pricing and margin normalization. Gross margin of 45% is a shortage margin. Management says it is roughly tripling its own capacity in 2026, and capacity added by other suppliers can bring prices down.
  • China concentration. About 65% of second-quarter revenue shipped to China, nearly all long-lived assets are there, the company holds its China business through partly owned subsidiaries, and pledged cash backs local bank loans.
  • Cash conversion. First-half free cash flow was -$9.5 million, inventory is $96.3 million, and 2027 capital spending of about $100 million comes before any proof of free cash flow.
  • Dilution. Shares rose 18.5% in six months, authorized shares were raised from 70 million to 120 million in June, and the company has just shown it will sell stock at a high price.
  • Tongmei minority holders. Redemption rights on about $49 million exist after the withdrawn listing, and a Hong Kong listing is expected to take about a year.
  • Litigation. A securities class action covering March 2021 to April 2024 was dismissed with leave to amend on July 7, 2026, and a third amended complaint was filed on August 4.

Management execution

Dr. Morris Young is chief executive and Gary Fischer chief financial officer. In 2026 they raised $632.5 million at $64.25 a share, brought InP capacity forward faster than first planned, signed long-term supply agreements with prepayments, and guided far above consensus. The offsets are a record of losses from 2023 to 2025, a securities lawsuit that is still alive, a Shanghai listing that was withdrawn after a long process and a Hong Kong attempt that will take another year. Management also declines to give the backlog number, saying the order book depends on what it chooses to accept, which makes the most-quoted demand metric a management decision. Assessment: 11/20, adequate to good. The next test is whether margins and cash conversion hold as the capacity comes online.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. Export permits or new restrictions cut shipments of indium phosphide wafers.
  2. Indium phosphide prices and gross margin fall as industry capacity arrives, taking gross margin below 38%.
  3. The capacity build absorbs the cash: free cash flow stays negative after 2027 spending, or AXT sells more stock to fund it.

Final verdict: reduce at $79.79

AXT is a small crystal grower that sits on one of the tightest bottlenecks in AI optics, and the second quarter shows what that is worth. The shares, though, trade at 33.8 times my 2027 Base EBITDA and just under my Bull value, and the cash flow has not turned positive yet. In April the company sold 9.84 million new shares at $64.25. A buyer today pays 24% more than that, for the same business with one more quarter of results.

Verdict: REDUCE at $79.79. Triangulated Base value $53, downside 33.3%, Confidence D (9/25). Required margin of safety 60%, maximum buy price $21.

The main risk to this verdict is that the shortage lasts much longer than I assume, so that capacity fills as planned, prices hold and revenue passes $1.5 billion with margins intact.
