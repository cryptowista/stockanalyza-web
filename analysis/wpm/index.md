Wheaton Precious Metals does not mine anything. It pays mining companies money up front, and in return it gets the right to buy a share of their gold and silver for years at a fixed low price. That model is one of the best in the resource sector, and the third quarter showed it: revenue up 62% to $772 million, net earnings of $410 million and operating cash flow of $645 million. The problem is what happened to the metals afterward. Gold fell to $3,446 on November 25, its lowest since August 2025, and silver, which brings in nearly half of Wheaton's revenue, is now about a third of its January record above $120. The shares still closed at $122.63. My Base case uses gold at $3,700 and silver at $44 next year, production growing to about 1.1 million gold-equivalent ounces by 2030, and a 10% required return, and it is worth $50.51 a share.

The Bear value is $24.17 and the Bull value is $80.07, so the price sits 407% above Bear and 53% above Bull. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 42.5%, so the maximum buy price is **$29.04**. The shares are 322% above that and would need to fall 76% to reach it.

Valuation route: a precious metals streaming company, valued on the free cash flow that belongs to shareholders after tax and after the spending needed to keep production from shrinking. The primary method is a ten-year discounted cash flow. The cross-check is five years of cash flow plus a discounted exit at a multiple of EBITDA. Economic classification: Standard, with commodity price scenarios. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What Wheaton is

Wheaton holds streaming and royalty agreements on 57 mining assets: 22 operating mines, 20 development projects and 15 at the exploration or other stage. At the biggest mines it gets a fixed share of the gold or silver produced and pays a low price per ounce that is fixed or a small share of the spot price, so the cost of mining, such as wages, diesel and a rising cost of ore, falls on the operator and not on Wheaton. In the third quarter its cash cost was $548 for each gold-equivalent ounce (GEO) sold, against an average sale price of $4,014, a cash margin of $3,466 per ounce. Revenue was 51% gold, 46% silver, and the rest palladium and cobalt.

The company reports silver as gold by a fixed ratio. From its own figures that ratio is 60 silver ounces to one gold ounce (91,753 gold ounces plus 8.18 million silver ounces at 60 to one plus 2,846 other GEOs gives the reported 230,965). That matters, because silver trades at about 85 to one against gold today. Each silver ounce counted as 1/60 of a GEO is therefore worth only about 70% of a gold ounce, and the realized price per GEO falls when silver falls faster than gold.

The biggest change this year is Antamina. On April 1 Wheaton paid $4.3 billion to buy BHP's share of the silver stream, which doubled its silver entitlement at the mine from 33.75% to 67.5%. It funded this with cash, a $1.5 billion term loan and a draw on its revolving credit facility, and it has since repaid $1.1 billion of bank debt.

## Why I deduct the cost of replacing the ounces

A stream is a wasting asset. Every ounce Wheaton delivers is an ounce it no longer owns, and the mines behind the oldest streams run out. To keep production at 1.1 million GEOs it has to keep buying new streams. Reported operating cash flow ignores that: it counts the cash from selling ounces and treats the next purchase as optional. Wheaton's own accounts show the cost of the ounces already delivered, as depletion of $127.9 million in the quarter, or $665 for each GEO sold.

I treat this as a real cost. In my Base case, from 2029 I deduct 25% of revenue every year as the spending needed to hold production flat. That is $818 per GEO in 2029, against the $665 of depletion in the third quarter, and I picked a higher number because new streams are bought at today's metal prices and are not cheap: the Antamina purchase alone cost $4.3 billion. The 25% is my estimate, not a company figure, and it is the second most uncertain input after the metal price. Before that, I deduct the upfront payments already agreed or likely: $116 million for the fourth quarter ($51 million paid since September 30, $40 million to Vale for Salobo and $25 million for Cozamin) and my estimate of $250 million in 2027 and $150 million in 2028. Tax is 16% of operating profit, the rate Wheaton paid in the quarter and for nine months ($288 million of tax on about $1.82 billion of profit before tax).

On that basis my Base owner free cash flow is $1,896 million in 2027, or $4.17 a share, falling to $1,649 million in 2029 when replacement spending starts. The company's reported operating cash flow in the third quarter was $645 million, which annualizes to $2.58 billion, but it was earned at a gold price 20% and a silver price 50% above today's.

## Primary valuation: ten years of owner free cash flow

The primary method discounts ten years of owner free cash flow at 10%, the framework default, and I test 9% and 11%. The valuation date is the September 30 balance sheet, so the fourth quarter of 2026 counts as a stub ($429 million, on my estimate of 235,000 GEOs sold at gold $3,800 and silver $46) and 2027 to 2036 follow.

The Base metal prices are gold $3,700 and silver $44 in 2027, 3.6% and 6% above the spot prices of about $3,570 and $41.50 on November 26, then gold rising 2% a year and silver 3%. Production is 960,000 GEOs in 2027, 1,020,000 in 2028, 1,075,000 in 2029 and 1,120,000 from 2030, which is 7% below the company's 1.2 million because of the labour disputes, a short record on the new projects and my view that the target will be hard to reach. It stays at 1.1 million until 2033 and then fades to 1,070,000 in 2036 as the oldest mines deplete. Revenue is $2.99 billion in 2027, rising to $3.85 billion in 2031 and $4.16 billion in 2036. EBITDA is $2.43 billion in 2027 (an 81% margin) and $3.14 billion in 2031. After tax and the spending above, owner free cash flow is $1,896, $2,189, $1,649, $1,763 and $1,807 million for 2027 to 2031, reaching $1,952 million in 2036. Terminal growth is 2.5%.

The stub and the ten years are worth $26.14 a share, the terminal value $22.39 and the net debt takes off $3.19. The primary value is **$45.34**.

Terminal value share: 46% of enterprise value, at a ten-year horizon, below the 75% level where I would check that the terminal value is supported by sustainable flows.

## Secondary valuation: five years of cash flow and an exit at 10 times EBITDA

The cross-check takes the stub and the next five years of owner free cash flow and values the company at the end of 2031 at 10 times that year's EBITDA of $3,144 million, or $31.4 billion. Discounted at 10%, the five years add $16.39 a share, the exit adds $42.49 and net debt takes off $3.19. The secondary value is **$55.69**.

**Why 10 times.** The sector guide for miners treats anything above 8 times as expensive, but a streamer has no mining costs and an 80% margin, so it deserves more. In 2031 my Base turns 57% of EBITDA into owner free cash flow after tax and replacement spending, which makes 10 times EBITDA equal to 17.4 times free cash flow. At a 10% return that implies growth of about 4% a year after 2031, well above the 2.5% in my terminal value. So the cross-check is the more generous of the two methods. The market pays 23.6 times today, which is the whole difficulty with this stock, and I do not set the cross-check at that level because a multiple that rich only holds if investors keep accepting a return well below 10%.

Exit value share: 72% of enterprise value comes from the exit, at a horizon of five years, below the 85% level where I check that the exit is supported by sustainable flows.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Run backward on the Base case, $122.63 at a 10% return needs metal prices 2.1 times my Base from 2027, which is gold at about $7,750 and silver at about $92, with the same growth after that. At an 8% return it needs gold near $6,550 and silver near $78, and at 6% gold near $5,130 and silver near $61. Left at my Base metal prices, the price needs a discount rate of 4.5%, which is close to or below what long Treasuries have been paying and not what an equity needs, with the Fed expected to raise rates in December. Production at the company's 1.2 million GEOs target adds only $4.09 to the Base.

That is the central finding. The market is not disagreeing with my model about the next two years. It is accepting a return near 4.5% on a stock whose cash flow moves one for one with two commodity prices, or it is betting on a gold price far above spot. Both are possible, and gold has gone from highs above $4,900 in January to $3,446 in November, so the second bet has not been working. The shares fell from about $151 on November 9 to $109.91 on November 20, then rose 11.6% in three sessions as the metals bounced from their lows.

As a return, buying at $122.63 and holding the Base case to the end of 2031, with the cash that builds up (all of it kept, since Wheaton pays out little) added to an exit at 10 times 2031 EBITDA, gives about **-6.1% a year**, against the 10% I require. The same math gives -19.7% a year in Bear and 3.6% in Bull. It is positive only if the market keeps paying a rich multiple: on Base earnings an exit at 15 times gives 0.1% a year, 20 times gives 5.1%, and today's 23.6 times gives 8.1%. Even then it falls short of 10%.

On earnings, the price is 27 times the first nine months' earnings annualized, a period in which Wheaton sold at gold $4,273 and silver $62 in the latest quarter. A growth-adjusted ratio (PEG) is not useful here because earnings move with metal prices, not with the business. Growth durability: structural for volume (the pipeline of 20 development projects), cyclical for revenue. Free cash flow growth is mostly the Antamina purchase and last year's price rise, so I do not extrapolate it.

## Reasons to own WPM

- **Costs the operators carry.** A cash cost of $548 per GEO against a sale price of $4,014 left a margin of $3,466 in the quarter, and the margin would still be about 80% at spot prices.
- **Visible growth.** The company guides to about 1.2 million GEOs by 2030, with Koné pouring its first gold on September 26, Hudbay approving construction at Copper World and Salobo getting a coarse particle flotation upgrade for which Wheaton pays nothing.
- **A clean balance sheet.** Net debt is 0.55 times EBITDA after $1.1 billion of repayments this year, and the dividend takes 17% of earnings.
- **Diversification.** 57 assets across gold, silver, palladium and cobalt, so no single mine carries the company.
- **Metals could recover quickly.** The Silver Institute projects a sixth annual silver deficit of about 35 million ounces, and a Fed that stops raising rates would help both metals.

## What could go wrong?

- **The metal price.** Almost all of the value moves with gold and silver. Silver fell from above $120 in January to about $40, and gold has fallen below $3,500 before bouncing.
- **Rates.** Core PCE inflation was 3.3% in October and markets nearly fully price a Fed hike in December. Higher real rates have driven the sell-off and a higher discount rate hits long-life cash flows hardest.
- **Operators and countries.** Wheaton does not run any mine. Labour disputes are open at San Dimas and Stillwater, and production comes from Peru, Mexico, Brazil, Canada and now Côte d'Ivoire.
- **Replacement cost.** The company has to buy new streams in a market where rivals compete for the same deals, and the return on them is not guaranteed. The $4.3 billion Antamina purchase was made with gold above $4,000, and its return depends on silver recovering.
- **The multiple.** At 23.6 times my 2027 EBITDA the stock needs investors to keep accepting a low return. If the multiple falls to 15 times on a Base outcome, the return to 2031 is about zero.
- **Timing of sales.** Sales lagged production by 17% in the quarter. The gap should close, but it makes single quarters hard to read.

## Management execution

Management, led by President and CEO Haytham Hodaly and CFO Vincent Lau, delivered the Antamina deal on time, started cutting the debt that paid for it within a quarter, kept the 2026 guidance and said it is on track to meet it. It also reported record nine-month revenue, earnings and operating cash flow. Against that, the biggest purchase of the year was made close to the top of the metals cycle, the Q3 gold output fell 8% from last year, and the long-term target of 1.2 million GEOs depends on projects run by other companies, so management does not control the date. I did not review pay or governance in detail for this analysis.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Gold and silver stay at or below today's levels while the price of the stock stays where it is, which means the market has to accept the 4.5% return it is now pricing, or the stock falls.
2. Replacement spending is higher than my 25% of revenue, because new streams are bought at high metal prices and then earn less when the prices fall.
3. The company's target of 1.2 million GEOs by 2030 slips, because the projects behind it belong to other companies.

**Stacking audit.** Combining every generous choice (9% discount rate, terminal growth of 3.5%, a 12x exit, metal prices 20% higher, production at the company's 1.2 million GEOs and replacement spending of 15% of revenue) gives $92.53. Combining every conservative one (11%, terminal growth of 1.5%, an 8x exit, metal prices 10% lower, production 10% lower and replacement spending of 35%) gives $29.28. The price of $122.63 is above the top of that range by 33%, and the Base of $50.51 sits 34% of the way up it. The choices that lean conservative are the 10% discount rate for a company that the market values at about 4.5%, a Base production 7% below the company's plan and a replacement charge that is higher than depletion. The one that leans generous is the 10x exit in the cross-check. I cannot rule out that my Base is too low, but it would have to be wrong by more than a factor of two.

**The strongest counter-argument** is that a 10% required return is not how streaming companies are valued, and that is true: the shares have long traded on a multiple of cash flow that implies a return of 5% or 6%, because the contracts are long, the costs are fixed and the exposure to gold is a feature. If you accept 6%, my Base becomes $82, which is 33% below the price, and it still needs metal prices about 40% above my Base (gold near $5,100 and silver near $61) to reach $122.63. That case is credible if gold goes back to $5,000, and it sits above my Bull, but it is a bet on the metal price and on investors' willingness to pay up again, and the price already pays for it.

## Final verdict: reduce at $122.63

Wheaton is one of the best businesses in its sector: an 80% cash margin, low leverage, a growth pipeline and a record year. At $122.63 the shares already pay for gold near $7,750 at a 10% return, for gold near $5,100 at 6%, or for a 4.5% return at my Base prices.
