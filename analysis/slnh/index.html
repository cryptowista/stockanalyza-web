Soluna Holdings builds power-backed data centers, mainly for bitcoin miners, and wants to turn its energy sites into AI data centers. In the first half of 2026 it sold about $135 million of new common stock to build the balance sheet, which is why it has cash. The shares have still lost 78% of their value in twelve months. On October 26 the stock closed at $0.52, and three days later at $0.70, a rise of 34% that followed an announced AI partnership with Applied Digital, though I cannot confirm the cause, and I do not think the assets behind the shares justify that move.

At $0.70 on October 29, 2026, the shares trade at 3.3 times my Base value of $0.21, so the Base is 70.0% below the price. The Bear value of $0.04 is 94.3% below the price and the Bull value of $0.81 is 15.7% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The required margin of safety of 60% gives a buy range of $0.08 to $0.09 and a conservative limit of $0.08. For existing holders this is a sell, because the price rewards plans that are not yet contracts. For new money there is nothing to buy at this price.

What Soluna is

Soluna develops data center sites next to wind and other power sources in Texas and elsewhere. About 192 megawatts are operating at three energized sites. Customers such as Bitdeer and Galaxy Digital rent space and power to mine bitcoin, and Soluna earns hosting fees. It also owns the 150 megawatt Briscoe Wind Farm, bought on April 1, 2026 for a base price of $53.0 million, and has bought larger stakes in the Dorothy 1A and 1B projects.

The company's pitch is the next step: AI computing. It has plans for a 300 megawatt campus at Dorothy 3, where a partner is expected to hold 51% of a joint venture, a 100 megawatt first phase at Kati 2 with Metrobloks, and a partnership with Applied Digital for a wind-powered AI data center in Texas. It describes a development pipeline of about 6.3 gigawatts, of which about 1.6 gigawatts is in planning and development and 4.5 gigawatts is still in assessment with power partners.

None of the AI projects has a disclosed customer contract with a price, a start date or a cost, so the pipeline is a set of options and not a backlog.

What the earnings are made of

  • Reported earnings are driven by non-cash items and financing costs. Adjusted EBITDA was a loss of $1.56 million, while EBITDA was a loss of $14.3 million, so about $12.7 million of the difference is adjustments that the release does not break out.
  • Hosting is a thin-margin business. One project, Dorothy 1A, produced $2.9 million of revenue and $0.8 million of gross profit in the quarter, a margin of 28%, but the consolidated margin was about 5%.
  • The company is funded by selling shares. In the first half, it raised $113.5 million through its at-the-market program, $18.9 million through an equity line with YA II PN, $2.6 million from warrants and $24.5 million of notes, and about $23.6 million more under the at-the-market program after June 30.

There are no earnings to capitalize, no free cash flow and no dividend on the common shares, so I value the company on its assets and its options.

Secondary valuation: common book value

This check is not independent of the first: both start from the same cash and the same assets and both deduct the same preferred stock. It confirms the arithmetic of the balance sheet and nothing more, and I give no extra Confidence for the agreement.

Dividend and buybacks

The common shares pay no dividend and there is no buyback. The company pays about $11 million a year to the preferred holders. The share count rose by about 21 million between June 30 and today, and I expect further issuance.

Reasons to own SLNH

  • 192 megawatts of operating capacity and a wind farm that gives the company control of part of its power supply.
  • About $100 million of cash and debt principal of only $9.2 million after the October repayment.
  • A large, power-backed development pipeline, with partners named for Dorothy 3, Kati 2 and a Texas AI data center.
  • Hosting revenue is growing, and Kati 1's 48 megawatts were filled by Galaxy Digital, with a further 35 megawatts agreed with Bitdeer.

What could go wrong?

  • More shares are issued at low prices, which dilutes holders. The company may issue more than 20% of its shares under the equity line.
  • The AI projects do not turn into signed customer contracts, or they need more capital than the company can raise.
  • Hosting remains a thin-margin business, and the customers are bitcoin miners whose demand depends on the bitcoin price.
  • The $123 million of preferred stock and its 9% dividend ranks ahead of the common shares in any wind-down.
  • The share price has been below $1. I found no Nasdaq deficiency notice, but a notice or a reverse split is possible.

Management execution

  • Management raised large amounts of equity and used part of it to repay $28.7 million of Generate Capital debt, which removes liens on the Dorothy assets.
  • It consolidated ownership of Dorothy 1A and 1B and bought the Briscoe wind farm, which simplifies the structure.
  • Gross profit fell from $1.9 million to $0.77 million between the first and second quarters, as revenue grew.
  • Announcements of partnerships and joint ventures are frequent, but contract terms, prices and capital costs are not disclosed. I give neutral credit until a first AI contract is signed.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • A signed AI customer contract with a price, a term and a capital cost, for Dorothy 3 or Kati 2.
  • Hosting gross margin rising above 15% as capacity grows.
  • A quarter with no new share issuance and cash above $100 million.

Yellow

  • The third-quarter report, due in mid-November, shows the cash balance after the debt repayment and the share count.
  • The Dorothy 3 joint venture, expected to close by December 31, 2026, slips into 2027.
  • A Nasdaq notice about the price below $1.

Red

  • Large issuance under the equity line at prices below $0.50.
  • Bitcoin hosting customers reduce or cancel capacity.
  • Preferred dividends suspended or a reverse split announced.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The AI projects fail to produce a signed contract in the next two quarters, which removes most of the value that the price assumes.
  2. Further issuance at falling prices, which increases the share count faster than asset value.
  3. A fall in bitcoin that reduces hosting demand and leaves the operating assets worth less than the $75 million I assume.

The weakest point of the model is the value of the AI pipeline, which the company does not disclose and which is the whole difference between the Base and the price. The strongest bull argument is that scarce, power-backed sites are worth far more than I credit as AI demand grows, and that a first signed contract could move the value past $1. The strongest bear argument is that the common shares are a small slice behind $123 million of preferred stock, funded by dilution, and that the recent rally was a reaction to a partnership headline.

Final verdict: sell at $0.70

Soluna has real assets, a clean debt position and a story that fits where power and computing are going. At $0.70, though, the common shares are priced for AI contracts that do not yet exist, behind a preferred stock that takes the first $123 million of value.

Verdict: SELL at $0.70. Weighted Base $0.21, 70.0% below the price, cross-check $0.19, Confidence D (6/25). Required margin of safety 60%, buy range $0.08 to $0.09, conservative buy limit $0.08.

For an existing holder, the original cost of the shares is irrelevant. The three-day rally is a chance to exit at a price well above my Base, and anyone who keeps the shares should size the position for a total loss. A review is warranted when a signed AI contract with a price and capital cost is published, or when the third-quarter report shows the cash and share count.
