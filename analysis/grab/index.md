In the September quarter all three of Grab's businesses, deliveries, rides and financial services, made an adjusted profit in the same quarter for the first time, and management raised its 2026 profit guidance by about 9%. The shares closed at $2.86 the day before, 54% below the 52-week high, after the stock had hit a three-year low over the $1.49 billion Atome deal.

At $2.86 on the November 2, 2026 close, Grab trades 34.3% below my weighted Base of $3.84. The Bear value is $2.25, 21.4% below the price, and the Bull value is $5.65, 97.4% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The verdict is Hold, with new money waiting for a lower price.

Valuation route: a Southeast Asian platform that combines delivery, ride-hailing and lending, valued on five-year after-tax operating profit with an exit multiple and cross-checked on forward earnings before interest, tax and depreciation after share-based pay. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

## What Grab is

Grab runs the main food-delivery, grocery and ride-hailing app in much of Southeast Asia, and it is adding consumer lending. Deliveries (food and grocery) are about half of revenue, Mobility (rides) is the most profitable segment, and Financial Services (loans, digital banks, payments) is the smallest and fastest growing. Revenue was $1,112 million in the September quarter, with 57 million monthly users.

The company has spent years losing money to win users, and the question now is how much of the profit it can keep. Adjusted EBITDA in the September quarter was $237 million, but incentives paid to drivers and customers were $737 million, or 10.7% of on-demand sales, which is the part of the bill that can swing the margin.

## What the cash flow is made of

- **Adjusted free cash flow is $286 million in the quarter and $533 million over twelve months.** That is the company's definition. Reported operating cash flow in the quarter was only $55 million, so I treat the adjusted figure with caution.
- **Share-based pay is a real cost.** It ran at $45 to $80 million a quarter in the last six quarters, about 6% of revenue, and adjusted EBITDA excludes it. I deduct it in every case.
- **Capital spending is light.** It was $96 million in 2025, about 3% of revenue, so most of the profit converts to cash if margins hold.
- **Reported profit is noisy.** Net income was $136 million in the March quarter and $253 million in the June quarter, mostly from items outside operations, and then $18 million in September after a fair value loss. I use operating profit and adjusted EBITDA less share-based pay, not net income.
- **The profit depends on incentives.** They were $737 million in the quarter. A move of one point in the incentive rate on $6.8 billion of on-demand sales is about $68 million of profit a quarter.

The Atome purchase is $1.49 billion in cash for 60%, of which $260 million is new capital for Atome, with closing expected by the third quarter of 2027. Grab has also agreed to buy the other 40% about two years after closing, at a price set by Atome's earnings, with an equity value of at least $2 billion and at most $4.5 billion. I treat the first payment as value-neutral, because Grab receives a 60% stake for the cash, and I do not model the second purchase, which is a commitment of at least $0.8 billion. The 551 million potentially dilutive securities are an overhang that I cover through the share-based pay deduction, not through a larger share count.

## Primary valuation: five-year after-tax operating profit

I project revenue and adjusted EBITDA to 2031, deduct share-based pay and depreciation, tax the result at 22%, divide by 4.04 billion shares, apply an exit multiple to 2031 and add today's net cash of $4.0 billion, or $0.99 a share. I discount at 9% for 5.16 years. The Base value is **$4.14**.

- **Margin:** adjusted EBITDA margin rises from about 19% in 2026 to 27% in 2031. My 2028 adjusted EBITDA is $1.49 billion, below management's $1.7 billion target, which includes Atome.
- **Deductions:** share-based pay at 4% of revenue (6% today) and depreciation at 3.5%, which leave an operating margin of 19.5% in 2031.
- **Exit multiple:** 15 times 2031 after-tax operating profit, for a business with a 27% EBITDA margin, net cash, thin capital needs and revenue still growing about 9%. Lending adds credit risk, which keeps the multiple below a pure platform.
- **Terminal share:** the exit value is 76% of the Base value, with the rest from today's cash, because Grab pays no dividend.

## Growth diagnostic

Revenue grew from about $2.4 billion in 2023 to $2.8 billion in 2024, $3.4 billion in 2025 and a guided $4.2 billion in 2026. The recent acceleration comes from Financial Services, which more than doubled, and from higher user numbers, while Mobility grew 10%. I classify the growth as structural, with margin quality still being built: the profit growth is real, but incentives and the loan book are the two levers that can reverse it.

## Dividend and buybacks

Grab pays no dividend. It has a $900 million buyback program, of which $400 million was done in September and October and $500 million remains, to be finished by September 2027, with shares cancelled. At about $3 that is about 170 million shares, around 4% of those outstanding. Press reports said the chief executive and other executives also bought shares after the stock hit a three-year low.

## Reasons to own Grab

- All three segments earned an adjusted profit in the same quarter, and guidance has been raised twice this year.
- Adjusted EBITDA has grown for 19 straight quarters, and margin rose 5.65 points in a year to 21.3%.
- Net cash liquidity of $5.0 billion is large against a market value of about $11.6 billion, and capital spending is only about 3% of revenue.
- Financial Services is built on a deposit base of $2.7 billion, with reserves of 6.5% against overdue loans of 2.6%.
- The buyback retires about 4% of the shares at the current price, and management's own buying is a signal that it sees value.

## What could go wrong?

- Incentives. They are 10.7% of on-demand sales, and fuel prices and competition can force them higher.
- Lending. The loan book is $2.7 billion, to reach about $3 billion by year-end and above $6 billion by 2028, funded by deposits in a region with weaker credit data. A rise in overdue loans from 2.6% would cut profit.
- The Atome deal. It costs $1.49 billion in cash, needs regulatory approval, and carries a second purchase of the other 40% at a price of at least $0.8 billion.
- Regulation. Indonesia is reviewing delivery rules, and management does not expect a material effect on guidance.
- Quality of the adjusted figures. Adjusted EBITDA and adjusted free cash flow exclude share-based pay and other items, and reported operating cash flow was $55 million in the quarter.
- Capital allocation. The foodpanda Taiwan deal was blocked and costs a $60 million break fee, and a second large deal follows quickly.
- Dilution. Potentially dilutive securities rose to 551 million from 415 million in a year.

## Management execution

- Guidance has been raised twice in 2026, and the 2028 target of $1.7 billion adjusted EBITDA was reiterated.
- The company discloses new data on loan quality (overdue loans, reserves), which makes the lending claim testable.
- Management is spending in the December quarter on driver supply and new products, at the cost of flat quarterly profit, which I read as a choice for growth.
- Capital allocation is active: a $900 million buyback and two acquisitions in a year, one blocked by a regulator and one pending.
- The chief executive's purchase of about $29.9 million of shares, reported after the stock hit a three-year low, aligns him with holders.

## What I would watch from here

### Green

- December quarter adjusted EBITDA above $240 million with revenue above $1.14 billion.
- Overdue loans staying below 3% as the loan book passes $3 billion.
- Regulatory approval for Atome with no change in price.

### Yellow

- Incentives above 11.5% of on-demand sales.
- Reported operating cash flow staying far below adjusted free cash flow.
- Potentially dilutive securities rising above 600 million.

### Red

- Overdue loans above 4% of the portfolio.
- Deliveries margin falling below 2%.
- A larger acquisition funded from cash or new shares.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Incentives rise and the adjusted EBITDA margin stalls near 20%, as in my Bear case.
2. Lending losses rise as the loan book doubles, and bank funding or regulators limit growth.
3. Atome or another deal absorbs cash and management attention while the core margin slips.

## Final verdict: hold at $2.86

The question is not whether the quarter was good. It was. It is whether a 10% jump on one good quarter leaves any margin of safety for a company that is about to spend $1.49 billion on a lender.

**Verdict: HOLD at $2.86, watchlist for new money. Weighted Base $3.84, 34.3% above the price, cross-check $3.54, Confidence C (16/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, buy range $2.04 to $2.38, conservative buy limit $2.04.**

New money buys only at or below $2.38. For an existing holder the original cost is irrelevant, and a Base value above the price supports holding. The price after the results is about $3.15, further from the buy range, so I would not add. The next tests are the December quarter result and the first approval news on Atome.
