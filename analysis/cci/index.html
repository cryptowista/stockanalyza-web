Crown Castle rose 31% in a month on a SpaceX report, and the price now sits above my Bull value

From October 2 to November 2, Crown Castle shares rose 31% on reports that SpaceX wants cell towers, and no SpaceX lease has been confirmed. The shares closed Friday at $82.40, 24% above the October 2 price of $66.44 and 0.8% above my Bull value of $81.71. At that price the stock trades at 17.9 times FY26 AFFO, the cash earnings measure for real estate investment trusts, and the market is pricing in a cost of equity of 7.9% against the 9.0% I use.

The quarter explains why the shares moved on hope and not on results. Site rental revenues for the third quarter fell 5% to $966 million, Adjusted EBITDA fell 6% to $673 million, and AFFO per share rose 11% to $1.24, because net interest expense dropped to $177 million from $247 million and shares fell to 426 million from 437 million after about $1 billion of buybacks. Both came from the sale of the fiber and small cell business, which closed on May 1 for $8.4 billion of net proceeds. In other words, the per-share growth was financed by selling the other business, and the tower revenue base is still shrinking because of DISH.

My Base value is $70.06, 15.0% below the price. The Bear value of $48.63 is 41.0% below the price and the Bull value of $81.71 is 0.8% below it too. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is $67.61. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of $40.28, or $39.00 to $42.21 depending on the method. The verdict is Hold, do not add.

Valuation route: a US tower real estate investment trust, so I value it on owner cash flow per share (AFFO less stock-based compensation and discretionary capital spending) in a ten year model as the primary method, and on EV to EBITDA as the second. Economic classification: Quality Growth (6.0 out of 12 on the REIT scorecard). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

What Crown Castle is

Crown Castle owns about 40,000 cell towers in the United States and leases space on them to wireless carriers. Towers average 2.0 tenants each. In the third quarter T-Mobile paid 42% of annualized site rental revenue, AT&T 28% and Verizon 23%, so the three carriers are 93% of the business. About 69% of the towers, 27,425, sit on ground leased from landowners, and ground lease costs rise about 3% a year. The company is now a pure-play tower business after the fiber sale.

Two events matter now. DISH, the fourth carrier, stopped paying and Crown Castle terminated its agreements in January 2026, asserting that DISH owes more than $3.5 billion of remaining payments. The 2026 outlook includes no DISH contribution, and the full-year cost of the DISH terminations is $220 million of revenue. American Tower settled with DISH Wireless for $525 million, according to the Wall Street Journal as relayed by Inside Towers on November 20, and Crown Castle has not reported a settlement. The second event is SpaceX, which has been reported to be seeking tower access for a mobile network. Management said on November 18 that satellite service is complementary and did not comment on any negotiation, and no lease has been confirmed.

Organic growth in site rental billings, excluding the DISH and Sprint effects, was 3.5%, down from 3.9% in the second quarter. Core leasing was $16 million against $20 million a year earlier, and management now expects it near the bottom of its $60 million to $70 million range. Churn was 80 basis points, at the low end of the 50 to 150 basis point guidance. Capital spending was $66 million, of which $58 million was discretionary. The company paid $275 million to buy out a residual economic interest in some towers from a major customer, which extended leases and limited churn on those towers and adds about $16 million a year of margin, a 5.8% yield on the payment.

Capital and share count

Net debt is $17,599 million, from $17,904 million of debt at face value and $305 million of cash including restricted cash. That is 6.5 times annualized third-quarter Adjusted EBITDA, at the top of management's 6.0 to 6.5 times target range. The weighted average interest rate is 3.9%, 97% of the debt is fixed rate, the average maturity is about seven years, and $4.0 billion of the $4.5 billion revolver is undrawn. Shares outstanding are 426 million, with 11 million held in treasury. I use 426 million, the third-quarter diluted count, although the company's full-year outlook assumes 430 million weighted over the year.

The balance sheet carries a repricing risk. Debt due in 2027 and 2028 was issued at low coupons and will be refinanced at higher rates.

Primary valuation: owner cash flow per share

Reported AFFO adds back stock-based compensation, which is a real cost to owners, about $80 million a year or $0.19 a share. It also does not charge for discretionary capital spending, mostly ground lease buyouts, which management sizes at $150 million to $250 million a year and which I set at $200 million for FY27. My owner cash flow in FY27 is therefore $4.72 of AFFO less $0.19 less $0.47, or $4.06 per share. It grows 4.0% a year for years 2 to 5, which assumes organic tower growth above the 3.4% trough of 2026 plus cost savings ($65 million of run-rate savings so far and $20 million more identified), then 3.5% for years 6 to 10 and 2.5% in perpetuity. I discount at 9.0%: a 10 year Treasury yield near 5% plus an equity premium of about 4%. That growth is durable because tower leases are long and carry fixed escalators, it needs little reinvestment, and leverage of 6.5 times stays within management's range.

The model gives $67.83. Terminal value is 55% of it, over a ten year horizon and below my 75% attention level. Years 1 to 10 contribute $30.25 and the terminal value $37.58. The FY27 AFFO of $4.72 is my estimate: the company has issued no 2027 outlook.

Secondary valuation: EV to EBITDA

I value the company at 18.0 times FY26 Adjusted EBITDA of $2,715 million, the midpoint of guidance, which is an EV of $48,870 million. Less net debt of $17,599 million, that is $73.41 a share. I use 18.0 times and not the 20 times or more that towers have often traded at, because growth is at a trough of 3.4%, the largest tenants are pushing back on lease terms and leverage is at the top of the range. The multiple is my judgment and is not company guidance.

Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $67.83 and $73.41, a divergence of 7.9%, well below the 20% warning level. They are not independent: both rest on the same tower cash flows and the same net debt, so their agreement is not independent evidence. I weight the owner cash flow model 60% and the EV method 40%, because the first charges for stock compensation and growth spending directly. The weighted value is $70.06. What is the market already pricing in?

Holding everything else at Base, the price of $82.40 requires a cost of equity of 7.9% instead of 9.0%. Holding the cost of equity at 9.0% instead, it requires owner cash flow growth of 7.1% a year for years 2 to 5 and 6.3% for years 6 to 10, against 4.0% and 3.5% in Base and above the 3.4% the company reports as its 2026 trough. In both cases the market asks for something close to my Bull case, which assumes a DISH recovery of $800 million and organic growth above 5%. The move from $66.44 to $87.03 in October came before any lease was announced.

On a one year view, if AFFO per share grows from $4.60 to my $4.72 and the multiple stays at 17.9 times, the shares return about 7.8% including the 5.2% dividend. If the multiple moves to the 15.2 times that my Base value implies, the return is about -7.6%. These are model returns for information, not forecasts.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is grade C, so the base margin of safety is 42.5%. I add no separate margin for leverage or concentration, because both are already in the Bear inputs and the stress tests. The maximum buy price is $70.06 times 0.575, or $40.28, with an entry range of $39.00 to $42.21 by method. The price is 105% above my maximum buy price, so this is not a buy. A cost of equity of 8.0% would raise the maximum buy price to $44.63, which is still far below the price.

Dividend

The dividend is $1.0625 a quarter, $4.25 a year, a yield of 5.2%. That is 92% of the FY26 AFFO outlook of $4.60. Management says it expects to raise the dividend only after the payout ratio falls to 75% to 80%. Against owner cash flow, the picture is tighter: after stock compensation, sustaining capital and the ground lease buyouts, the cash left is below the dividend. Reasons to own

  • Towers lease on long contracts with fixed escalators, and churn was 80 basis points in the quarter.
  • Management calls 2026 the low point for organic growth, and catalysts it cites include AT&T's 600 MHz deployment, Verizon's AWS-3 spectrum and an upper C-band auction.
  • Restructuring has delivered $65 million of run-rate savings, with $20 million more identified, and a target of 200 basis points of margin expansion from 2026 to 2030.
  • A DISH recovery, or reuse of the DISH equipment on about 12,000 built sites, would add cash that my Base excludes.

What could go wrong?

  • Valuation. The price is above my Bull value. If the market returns to a 9.0% cost of equity with no new lease, the shares would be worth about $70.06.
  • Concentration. Three carriers pay 93% of revenue, and the largest tenants are pushing back on lease terms.
  • DISH. Revenue of $220 million is gone and the recovery is a court outcome. American Tower reached $525 million with DISH Wireless, and Crown Castle has not announced an outcome.
  • Leverage and refinancing. Net debt is 6.5 times EBITDA, $2,197 million matures in 2027, and a refinancing rate of 6.5% instead of 5.5% would add about $22 million of interest on the 2027 maturities alone.
  • Dividend. The payout is 92% of AFFO and above owner cash flow, so a weak year would leave no room.
  • Rates. A cost of equity of 10.0% gives a Base value of $64.53.

Management execution

The company raised its 2026 AFFO outlook again, AFFO per share rose 11% in the quarter, the fiber sale closed on time at $8.4 billion, and restructuring savings are ahead of plan. Against that, the company cut its dividend when it announced the fiber sale, bought back about $1 billion of stock while leverage sits at the top of its range, and has lowered its services outlook repeatedly. The record is mixed, leaning positive on cost control. I would like to see 2027 guidance, which management has not given.

What I would watch from here

Green: a signed SpaceX or other new carrier lease, a DISH settlement at or above $800 million, or 2027 guidance showing organic growth above 4% with AFFO per share above $4.75.

Yellow: core leasing below $60 million, churn above 100 basis points, or net debt to EBITDA above 6.5 times.

Red: a DISH recovery near zero, 2027 guidance with AFFO per share below $4.60, or a refinancing of the 2027 maturities above 6.5%.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. A cost of equity of 10.0% or more, which lowers my Base value to $64.53.
  2. Organic growth that stays at the 2026 trough of 3.4% or below, which takes growth in owner cash flow to 3.0% and the Base value to $67.48.
  3. A DISH outcome near zero together with no new carrier, which leaves the Bear case of $48.63 in view.

Final verdict: hold at $82.40, do not add

Crown Castle is a durable tower franchise at a trough in growth, and the shares have already paid for the recovery and for a SpaceX lease that does not exist yet. My maximum buy price is $40.28.
