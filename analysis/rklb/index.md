# Rocket Lab's backlog doubled in a quarter, and at $61.53 the shares still price in a rocket that has not flown

RKLB, Nasdaq. Published November 16, 2026. Price as of the November 13, 2026 regular session close. Values in US dollars. Latest results: third quarter 2026, reported November 9, 2026.

## Verdict: SELL at $61.53

Rocket Lab grew revenue 73% to $267.8 million and added $1.2 billion of backlog in one quarter. At $61.53 the market values the company at 38 times 2026 revenue, 41% above my Bull value of $43.68 and 59% above my Base of $25.16. The 50% margin of safety puts the maximum buy price at $12.58.

Rocket Lab told investors on November 9 that it had signed nearly $1 billion of new launch contracts in a single quarter, including more than $515 million for Neutron, a rocket that has not yet flown and that slipped to 2027 four weeks earlier. The contracts are real. So is the delay. The shares have since fallen to $61.53 from a 52 week high of $151.

My Base value is $25.16 a share, the Bear value $12.19 and the Bull value $43.68. The Base blends a multiple of forward revenue ($31.79) with a discounted free cash flow ($15.22). The two methods differ by 70%, so I treat the range as the result, and the price sits above both ends. The required margin of safety is 50% and the maximum buy price is $12.58. This is a call on the price, not on the business: backlog, margins and growth are all moving the right way.

Valuation route: growth company that is not yet profitable, valued on enterprise value to forward sales (with the large cash pile added back) and cross-checked with a ten year discounted free cash flow. The Bear case assumes Neutron is late again and more shares are sold. The pending Iridium acquisition is not modeled.

## What Rocket Lab is

Rocket Lab launches small satellites on its Electron rocket and builds the spacecraft, solar panels and other parts that go on them. Space Systems is now the larger business: it produced $214.8 million of the $267.8 million of third quarter revenue, which is about 80%, and launch about $53 million by my subtraction. Space Systems revenue rose 13.3% from the second quarter on satellite platforms and a full quarter of laser communications and robotics acquisitions, and launch revenue rose 19%.

The next step is Neutron, a reusable medium rocket meant to compete with SpaceX's Falcon 9. Rocket Lab also agreed on June 29 to buy Iridium, a satellite communications operator, for about $8 billion including debt. That would add recurring service revenue and a satellite network that Rocket Lab could launch itself.

## Primary valuation: EV/sales on forward revenue

With no operating profit, the first method values the company on forward sales. My Base revenue for 2027 is $1,450 million, 48% growth, backed by a $3.57 billion backlog, and I add the $4.70 billion of cash. I use 12 times in the Base, which pays for growth near 50% and a gross margin that guidance puts at 34% to 36% on a GAAP basis. The Bear uses 6 times on $1,250 million and an extra 68 million shares sold to fund a longer Neutron delay, and the Bull uses 20 times on $1,600 million. A multiple of forward sales says nothing about when profit arrives, and the market's 25.6 times is more than twice my Base.

## Dividend and buybacks

Rocket Lab pays no dividend and does not buy back shares. The company has done the opposite, selling about $3.9 billion of stock through at-the-market programs in nine months. Anyone owning the stock should expect a rising share count until the business pays for itself.

## Reasons to own Rocket Lab

- Revenue grew 73% to a record $267.8 million and the fourth quarter guidance of $270 to $290 million points to another record.
- Backlog is $3.57 billion, 3.6 times 2026 revenue, with about $1 billion of new launch contracts signed in the third quarter alone.
- There is $4.70 billion of cash and almost no debt, so a delay does not threaten the company's survival.
- The company controls both the rockets and the satellite parts, and the Iridium deal would add recurring communications revenue.

## What could go wrong?

- **Neutron:** the first flight moved to 2027 after voids were found in a carbon composite dome, and testing runs to the first quarter of 2027. Earlier targets were 2024, 2025 and late 2026.
- **Valuation:** at 38 times 2026 revenue a missed schedule can take 10% off the price in a day, as it did in October.
- **Dilution:** $3.9 billion of stock was sold in nine months and cash flow is still negative, so more may follow.
- **Iridium:** a $8 billion deal at about 15 times EBITDA for a company growing 8% a year, financed partly with Rocket Lab stock that trades below the collar.
- **Competition:** SpaceX is the incumbent in medium launch and Starship has reached orbit.
- **My own numbers:** the ten year path, margins, 11% cost of capital and the 12 times multiple are my estimates.

## Management execution

Management has delivered on revenue: the third quarter beat its own guidance and the backlog rose 52% in the quarter. It has not delivered on Neutron, whose first launch has slipped repeatedly, and the company now says that cash flow depends on it. Cash is high, but it was raised through heavy dilution. I score management 6 out of 10.

## What I would watch from here

### Green

- Neutron reaches orbit in 2027 and a second launch follows within months
- GAAP gross margin above 38% with the operating loss below $50 million a quarter
- Operating cash flow positive for two quarters in a row without interest income

### Yellow

- Neutron testing slips past the first quarter of 2027
- Revenue growth below 40% in 2027
- Share count rising by more than 3% in a quarter

### Red

- A failed Neutron qualification test or a launch failure
- Backlog falling for a quarter or Neutron customers cancelling
- New equity or debt raised on top of the Iridium financing

Fourth quarter results are due in early 2027, and the Neutron test results around the first quarter are the next hard evidence.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. Neutron flies and launches at a pace that makes Rocket Lab the second medium launch provider. That moves the value toward the Bull of $43.68 and the market already assumes part of it.
2. The market keeps paying 25 times sales. My value does not move, but the shares can stay above it for a long time.
3. Neutron fails or slips into 2028 and more shares are sold, which moves the value toward the Bear of $12.19.

## Final verdict: sell at $61.53

Rocket Lab is growing fast, has the cash to survive a delay, and has a backlog that supports the next two years. The price already counts a successful Neutron, a multi-year growth run and a margin the company has never shown. At $61.53 the shares trade 59% above my Base and above my Bull.

For someone who owns the stock, selling or cutting the position is the cleaner call on these numbers. New money should wait for a price near $12.58 or for a flown Neutron, whichever comes with a lower price.

**Verdict: SELL at $61.53. Base $25.16, -59.1% against the price, Bear $12.19, Bull $43.68, Confidence D (12/25). Required margin of safety 50%, maximum buy price $12.58.**
