# Leidos raised earnings guidance for a third time and cut revenue guidance in the same release

LDOS, NYSE. Published November 17, 2026. Price as of the November 16, 2026 regular session close.

Watchlist for new money below $106.74. Leidos trades at 8.9x normalized EBITDA with $11.06 billion of funded backlog, up 22%. The catch is that the stock is 21.8% above my maximum buy price, and the margin and the third-quarter cash flow both came in lower than a year ago.

Leidos raised its 2026 earnings guidance for the third time this year and lowered its revenue guidance in the same release. Both are true. About $625 million of projected 2026 revenue left the company on October 5, when Leidos put a 1,500-person business into a joint venture with Altaris.

The third quarter was a good one for the numbers that pay for a stock. Non-GAAP earnings per share rose 19% to $3.62, funded backlog (contracts with money already appropriated) grew 22% to $11.06 billion, and free cash flow for the first nine months reached about $1.5 billion. Leidos also bought back $504 million of its own stock in the quarter. At $130.02, the November 16, 2026 close, the shares trade 5.2% above where they stood in my September analysis.

My triangulated Base value is **$152.48**, which is 17.3% above the price. The Bear value of $111.27 is 14.4% below it and the Bull value of $199.51 is 53.4% above it. A Confidence B score requires a 30% margin of safety, which puts the maximum buy price at **$106.74**. The stock would have to fall 17.9% to get there. Until then this is a hold: a company I would be comfortable owning, at a price that does not yet pay for being wrong about federal funding.

## What changed since September

My September analysis put the Base value at $155.00 and the maximum buy price at $116.25. The Base is now $152.48, $2.52 lower, because I trimmed normalized EBITDA to $2.40 billion and kept the 10x multiple. The maximum buy price is $9.51 lower for a different reason. The framework now requires a 30% margin of safety at Confidence B, up from the 25% I used in September, and that is a rule change, not bad news about Leidos. Confidence improved from grade C (17/25) to grade B (18/25) now that the balance sheet is reported, and the valuation score fell from 76 to 66 because the price rose.

The price moved the other way. In September the stock was 20.3% below my Base value and 6.3% above the maximum buy price. Now it is 14.7% below the Base and 21.8% above the maximum buy price. The business improved, the stock improved faster, and the buy limit moved down, which is how a good quarter ends up with a worse entry point.

## What Leidos does and where the value comes from

Leidos sells information technology, engineering and technical services, mostly to the U.S. federal government. Revenue is tied to contracts that run for years, so the numbers that matter are backlog and how fast it converts into cash. At the end of the third quarter total backlog was $49.55 billion and funded backlog was $11.06 billion, which means about 22 cents of every backlog dollar already has an appropriation behind it.

The mix is shifting. Homeland revenue grew 32% in the third quarter, while Health, where profit is expected to shrink into 2027, is the soft spot. The customer is a single one: the federal government. The risk that comes with that is timing, not demand. A continuing resolution or a shutdown delays funding for work that is still wanted.

Entrust, a $2.388 billion acquisition that closed in March 2026, is inside the numbers now. The October 5 joint venture with Altaris takes the opposite direction: Leidos contributed its SES business (about 1,500 employees, about $625 million of projected 2026 revenue) in return for a 41.5% equity stake, with a preliminary non-operating gain of about $100 million expected in the fourth quarter.

## Which numbers matter for Leidos

For a services contractor, the metrics that carry the valuation are adjusted EBITDA and the free cash flow it turns into. Guided 2026 adjusted EBITDA is about $2.44 billion (a 13.5% margin on $18.1 billion of revenue). I use **$2.40 billion** as the Base, about 2% below guidance, because Health profit is expected to shrink, a continuing resolution can delay awards, and the margin was 15.2% a year ago against 13.9% now. I assume no structural margin recovery. The Bear case is $2.25 billion and the Bull case $2.50 billion.

Normalized free cash flow is **$1.55 billion**: guided operating cash flow of about $1.9 billion, less $0.15 to $0.2 billion of capital spending and capitalized software. That is above the three-year average of $1.30 billion and below the nine-month pace. At the current price the Base free cash flow is a 9.8% yield on a $15.86 billion market value.

## Shares and capital structure

The share count fell from 125.49 million on July 3 to 121.997 million on October 2, a drop of 2.8% in one quarter, almost entirely from the $504 million of repurchases. Net debt is $5.397 billion, which is the $6.027 billion carrying value of debt less $630 million of cash (restricted cash of $144 million is excluded, and the principal amount of debt is not disclosed in the release). That is about 2.2 times EBITDA. There are no preferred shares, material minority interests or convertible debt. Stock compensation is treated as a cash cost inside normalized EBITDA and free cash flow, so there is no separate dilution layer.

## Primary valuation: enterprise value to EBITDA

Enterprise value is what the whole business is worth before the claims of lenders. I take EBITDA times a multiple, subtract net debt, and divide by the 121.997 million shares.

| Case | EBITDA ($B) | Multiple | Enterprise value ($B) | Net debt ($B) | Value per share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bear | 2.25 | 8.5x | 19.1 | 5.5 | $111.68 |
| Base | 2.40 | 10.0x | 24.0 | 5.4 | $152.49 |
| Bull | 2.50 | 12.0x | 30.0 | 5.3 | $202.46 |

I chose 10x, and not a tier from a table, for specific reasons. It implies about 3% to 4% organic growth (Leidos reported 4% organic growth in the second quarter and 4% reported growth in the third), a mid-13% margin, moderate leverage at 2.2x, low capital intensity and earnings that last five to seven years. Getting above 10x needs growth above 5% or a margin back at 15%. The Bear multiple of 8.5x reflects a federal slowdown and Health erosion, and the Bull multiple of 12x needs both margin recovery and the defense mix to work. The primary Base is **$152.49**.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $130.02 the market value is $15.86 billion and the enterprise value is $21.26 billion, which is **8.9x** my $2.40 billion of normalized EBITDA. That equals one of two things: flat EBITDA at a multiple 1.1 turns below my Base, or EBITDA about 11% lower than my Base at the full 10x. The market is paying for roughly flat earnings with some Health erosion, and it gives no credit for the margin recovery that the Bull case needs. It is one price and two unknowns, so I cannot say which of the two it is.

## How the buy price is set

A Confidence B score carries a base margin of safety of 30%, the discount to estimated value I require before calling a stock a buy. I add no extra margin, because federal timing risk is already in the Bear case and leverage is in the multiple. Applied to the $152.48 Base, the **maximum buy price is $106.74**. If the Base multiple were 9x the limit would be $93, and at 11x it would be $120.50. The current price of $130.02 is above the limit in every one of those cases. The maximum buy price sits 4.1% below the Bear value, so at that price the downside to the pessimistic case would be nearly gone.

## Dividend

The quarterly dividend is $0.45, or $1.80 a year, a yield of 1.4% at $130.02. That is about 14% of Base earnings per share and about 14% of Base free cash flow, covered many times over. Income is not part of this thesis. The $504 million of buybacks in one quarter is more than twice the $220 million a year the dividend costs.

## Reasons to own LDOS

- **Backlog with money behind it.** Funded backlog is up 22% to $11.06 billion and the quarterly book-to-bill was 1.2x.
- **Cash generation above plan.** Free cash flow reached about $1.5 billion in nine months, against a normalized $1.55 billion for the full year.
- **A shrinking share count.** About 3% of the shares were bought back in one quarter.
- **Guidance that keeps rising.** The 2026 EPS range has been raised three times, to $12.50 to $12.70.

## What could go wrong?

- **Thesis breaker: federal funding delays.** Continuing resolutions and shutdowns delay awards and cash conversion. Funded backlog is only 22% of the total, so timing matters.
- **Health profit erosion.** Profit in the segment is expected to shrink into 2027. If it drags EBITDA to $2.25 billion, the value at 8.5x is about $112.
- **Cash conversion.** Q3 free cash flow was $476 million, against $680 million a year earlier, and operating cash flow fell 26%. The fourth quarter needs to show the guided $1.9 billion for the year.
- **Margin.** The adjusted EBITDA margin was 13.9%, against 15.2% a year ago, and I assume no recovery.
- **Joint venture uncertainty.** The EBITDA and fair value of the SES stake are not disclosed. My bounded estimate is a net effect between $0 and -$4 a share, a Base of $148 to $152, which is under 3% of value.
- **Integration and leverage.** Entrust must deliver, and net debt of 2.2x EBITDA limits how much more stock Leidos can buy back without adding debt.

## Management execution

Management raised EPS guidance three quarters in a row, cut the revenue guidance for a reason that has nothing to do with demand (the joint venture), returned $504 million in one quarter, and raised the dividend. Against that, free cash flow dipped year over year and operating cash flow fell 26%, so the claim that this is only timing is still unproven. **Assessment: 15/20, good. The test is whether the fourth quarter delivers operating cash flow of about $1.9 billion for the year.**

## Stress test and independent check

I recomputed every value in Python: per-share values, the free cash flow method, the triangulation and the maximum buy price. The weakest point in the model is the October 5 joint venture. The 10-Q (Note 12) discloses the SES contribution and the 41.5% stake but not SES EBITDA or the fair value of the stake. I estimate SES EBITDA at $50 to $60 million (an 8% to 10% margin), which at 10x is $500 to $600 million of enterprise value, partly offset by a stake I value at $0.3 to $0.5 billion. The net effect is $0 to -$4 a share, which I disclose and do not put into the Base. My normalized EBITDA already sits about 2% below guidance, and the guidance includes SES only until October 5. Net debt uses the carrying value of debt because the release does not give the principal.

| Robustness check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Base at 9x EBITDA | $133, maximum buy price $93 |
| Base at 11x EBITDA | $172, maximum buy price $120.50 |
| EBITDA $100 million lower at 10x | About $8 a share lower |

| Altaris joint venture effect | $0 to -$4 a share, Base $148 to $152 |
| Maximum buy price versus the Bear value | $106.74 is 4.1% below $111.27 |

The strongest counter-thesis for the bulls: Leidos has guided up three times, holds $11.06 billion of funded backlog and trades at 8.9x EBITDA, a multiple that already assumes no recovery. The strongest counter-thesis for the bears: the margin has fallen 1.3 points in a year, free cash flow dipped 30% in the latest quarter, and the market is paying a higher price than in September for a company with a lower Base. None of the stress tests puts the price below the maximum buy price, so the Hold verdict is stable. Two inputs are my estimates, not company data: the SES figures and the normalized EBITDA. Options data was not available and plays no role.

## Final verdict: hold at $130.02, watchlist for new money below $106.74

Leidos is a profitable, cash-generating contractor with a growing funded backlog and a management team that keeps raising the earnings range. At $130.02 the stock is priced 14.7% below my Base value, but 21.8% above the maximum buy price, so the discount is not large enough to cover a funding delay or a Health surprise. Existing holders have no thesis breaker to act on. New money should wait.

**Verdict: HOLD at $130.02. Triangulated Base value $152.48, upside 17.3%, Confidence B (18/25). Required margin of safety 30%, maximum buy price $106.74.**

The case improves if the price falls to the $100 to $110 range with funded backlog and the margin intact, or if the margin holds above 14% for two quarters, which would raise the Base itself.
