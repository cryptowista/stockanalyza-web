On September 25 I called AeroVironment a Hold at $158.55, put the Base value at $143.50 and said the buy zone started at $86.10. The shares now trade at $85.80, 0.3% inside that zone. It would be a tidy ending if it were a buy, and it is not, because the September Base paid about 23 times fiscal 2028 EBITDA after stock pay and treated that future value as if it were worth the same today. Rebuilt with discounting and a multiple I can defend, the Base is $76.56.

At $85.80 the shares trade 12.1% above my triangulated Base value of $76.56, a downside of 10.8% to Base. The Bear value is $32.65 and the Bull value is $140.76, so the range is wide: the price sits 62% above Bear and 39% below Bull. The required margin of safety (MOS), the discount to fair value I need before buying, is 42.5%, so the maximum buy price is $44.02. The shares are 94.9% above that and would need to fall 48.7% to reach it. In September they were 84% above the buy price, so in percentage terms the gap has widened, not closed.

Valuation route: Industrials, aerospace and defense, valued on a mid-cycle EBITDA after stock compensation with a free cash flow cross-check on the same assumptions. Because normal earnings power is more than a year away, the primary method adds the cash flow until fiscal 2028 to a discounted exit value, and a ten-year discounted cash flow runs alongside it. What changed since September

Little in the business moved. On November 9 AeroVironment announced a multiyear contract with a $940 million ceiling, of which about $315 million is an initial funded award, for an undisclosed U.S. customer in its Autonomous Systems segment. Ceilings are not backlog, and orders beyond the initial award depend on funding. The shares jumped about 10% that day to near $124 and have since fallen to $85.80. I found no new filing that explains the fall, and the Q2 report is still ahead of us. What changed is my model, and the next table shows where.

What AeroVironment is

AeroVironment, or AV, supplies small drones, loitering munitions and counter-drone systems to the U.S. and allied militaries. BlueHalo, which closed on May 1, 2025 for about $3.485 billion, added space, directed energy, cyber and electronic warfare. After the U.S. cancelled the BADGER/SCAR space program in March 2026, AV recorded a $240.7 million goodwill impairment. This year it also won a $464.8 million Army order for LOCUST laser weapons, its first high-energy laser production contract, and a potential $500 million counter-drone award in July.

A securities class action over the SCAR statements was filed in May in the Eastern District of Virginia, an amended complaint followed on October 5, and a hearing on the motion to dismiss is scheduled for December 9. I do not model a payout, but it sits in the same quarter as earnings.

Why adjusted EBITDA overstates owner cash

Adjusted EBITDA adds back stock compensation, which was $38.3 million in fiscal 2026, up from $21.5 million. I treat it as a cash cost, at 1.8% of revenue, because the shares it creates dilute the owners just as a cash wage would drain them. Guidance midpoint EBITDA of $315 million becomes about $276 million after stock pay.

Cash conversion is the weaker point. In fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was -$78.4 million, capital spending $62.5 million and capitalized intangibles $23.7 million, so free cash flow was about -$165 million, and Q1 fiscal 2027 was negative again. My model assumes another -$30 million between August and April, then positive cash flow of $84 million in fiscal 2028. Headline growth is also flattered by the acquisition: fiscal 2026 revenue of $1.977 billion included about $919 million from BlueHalo.

Balance sheet and shares

Cash and investments total about $675 million against $747.5 million of face value in 0% convertible notes due 2030, so net debt is $72.5 million. September's $55 million used the book value of the notes; at face value, which is what has to be repaid, it is $17.5 million higher. The conversion price is $322.40, far above today's price, so I treat the notes as debt and do not add shares for them.

BlueHalo was paid for with about $2.64 billion of AV stock, and in July 2025 AV raised about $968.5 million of equity and $726.9 million of convertibles. Shares outstanding are 50.8 million and the valuation uses 52 million. At $85.80, market value is about $4.46 billion and enterprise value about $4.53 billion, which is 2.1x the fiscal 2027 revenue guidance midpoint.

I use it because normal earnings are not here yet: fiscal 2027 guidance sits mid-ramp and capex is at 13% of revenue. The Base path follows.

Why 14x. I pay 14x EBITDA after stock pay in Base, which is 12.4x on the enterprise value today. The reasons: growth of about 12% in fiscal 2028 that fades to mid-single digits, earnings that depend on U.S. defense budgets and on programs that can end abruptly, as SCAR did, reinvestment of 5% to 7% of revenue in capex, and a clean balance sheet with debt at a 0% coupon. That is a good business with a short track record, so I place it in the lower half of the 12x to 18x range for quality industrials. Bear gets 9x, because a business that stalls at 2% growth and 14% margin does not deserve more, and Bull gets 19x, which assumes growth near 24% in fiscal 2028 and a 21% margin.

Exit multiple share of value: 98.8% of the primary value comes from the exit, because only 17 months of cash flow precede it. That is how a transition model works, so I rely on the ten-year cross-check below to test it.

What is the market already pricing in?

Run backward, the price of $85.80 needs one of three things on top of my Base: a steady-state adjusted EBITDA margin of 18.8% instead of 17.5%, or an exit multiple of 15.3x instead of 14x, or a discount rate of 8.9% instead of 10%. None is extreme, which is why the shares are close to fair value and not far above it. The price does not ask for a miracle, but it also leaves no room for the cash conversion to disappoint.

If the shares kept today's multiple of 16.5x EBITDA after stock pay until April 2028, Base earnings growth would give about 16% a year. Under my Base exit multiple of 14x, the return at today's price is 3.4% a year. The difference is the multiple, not the earnings: the business grows into its price but does not leave much over.

Growth-adjusted, the picture is the same. Non-GAAP earnings per share of about $3.18 this year growing to $5.70 in fiscal 2030 give a price-to-earnings ratio of 27 and a growth-adjusted ratio (PEG) of about 1.26. I use this only as a diagnostic, since non-GAAP earnings add back stock pay and acquisition amortization and the growth is acquisition-affected. Free cash flow to the firm is 1.9% of enterprise value in fiscal 2028 and 5.0% in fiscal 2031, so the cash yield arrives late.

How the buy price is set

Confidence C starts at a 42.5% margin of safety. I added nothing for acquisition risk, weak cash conversion or concentration, because they are already charged elsewhere: the acquisition and cash risks in the Bear case, the multiple and the Confidence score, and the exit-value dependence in the Confidence score. A 42.5% discount to the $76.56 Base gives a maximum buy price of $44.02. A single input moving by 15% changes the Base by less than 15%, so the buy price is not hostage to one assumption.

What the options market implies

Options prices are a free second opinion on risk. Free data I could reach shows an at-the-money implied volatility of about 101% a year, near the top of its 52-week range of roughly 50% to 112%. In plain terms the market prices a typical one-month move of about 29%, or roughly $61 to $111 from $85.80, a window that holds the December earnings report and the December 9 court hearing. I could not source put-to-call ratios or the skew, so I draw no conclusion about tail risk from the options market, and this check is only partly supported.

Reasons to own AVAV

  • A strategic portfolio. Switchblade, small drones, counter-drone, directed energy, space and cyber sit where defense budgets are growing.
  • Record demand. Funded backlog of $1.5 billion, a $940 million contract ceiling with $315 million funded, and large counter-drone and laser awards this year.
  • Operating leverage. If the margin reaches the 17.5% in my Base, free cash flow rises from -$30 million to $225 million in four years.
  • Low net debt. The 0% convertible notes are almost matched by cash and investments.
  • The price is not extreme. At 12% above Base, the market is paying for the plan and not for the Bull case.

What could go wrong?

  • Cash conversion. Free cash flow was about -$165 million in fiscal 2026 and negative again in Q1. Failing to turn backlog into cash is the main thesis breaker.
  • Program concentration. SCAR was cancelled in March 2026 after being described as a $1 billion franchise, and the $940 million contract has no named customer.
  • Integration. BlueHalo has already produced a $240.7 million impairment.
  • Dilution. Equity-funded M&A and convertible notes reduce per-share value if returns disappoint.
  • Controls and litigation. Material weaknesses in internal control were reported as of August 1, 2026, and the class action is pending.
  • Volatility. The options market implies a typical monthly move near 29%.

Management execution

Q1 revenue beat expectations, funded backlog reached a record and guidance was maintained. Against that stand the Space goodwill impairment, weaker Space, Cyber and Directed Energy revenue, material weaknesses in internal control and a lawsuit over statements about SCAR. Management credibility is unchanged at 13/20: good execution in the core business, with an acquisition caveat. Nothing new since September has changed that score.

What I would watch from here

Green

  • Fiscal 2027 revenue of $2.175 billion or more and adjusted EBITDA of $325 million or more
  • Adjusted EBITDA margin of 15% or more in the second half
  • Funded backlog of $1.5 billion or more, with orders flowing under the $940 million contract
  • Operating cash flow turning positive, capex intensity falling with scale
  • No new impairment, share growth below 3% a year
  • Internal control weaknesses remediated

  • Revenue of $2.05 to $2.175 billion, EBITDA margin of 13% to 15%
  • Funded backlog of $1.2 to $1.5 billion
  • Volatile cash conversion, capex staying near 13% of revenue
  • An isolated small impairment, share growth of 3% to 7%

Red

  • Revenue below $2.05 billion or EBITDA margin below 13%
  • Funded backlog below $1.2 billion, or another major program cancellation
  • Persistently negative free cash flow with capex rising without returns
  • A repeat impairment, or share growth above 7%
  • Control weaknesses persisting or worsening

The next review points are the Q2 fiscal 2027 report in December and the December 9 court hearing. Better results would raise the Base only if they lift the margin path, since the multiple stays at 14x.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. A major backlog loss or another program cancellation.
  2. Margins stuck near 14% and free cash flow still negative after fiscal 2028.
  3. A material goodwill impairment or a dilutive, equity-funded acquisition.

Stacking audit. Combining every generous choice, a 3-point higher margin, 2 points more growth, a 9% discount rate and a 16x exit, gives $124.9. Combining every conservative one gives $52.0. The shares at $85.80 fall inside that range, and the Base of $76.56 is not tuned toward either end. The strongest counter-argument is that AV is now a multi-domain defense platform with record backlog and contract awards, and that investors already pay 16.5x for earnings that could double by fiscal 2030. The Bull case carries that view. My Base declines to pay for margins and cash flow that have not yet shown up in a report.

Final verdict: hold at $85.80, watchlist for new money

AeroVironment is a good defense technology business with a record backlog, and at $85.80 it is priced about where my corrected model says it is worth. That is a reason to hold what you own, and it is not a reason to add. With Confidence C, wide scenarios and a primary value that depends on an exit multiple, the framework asks for a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives a buy price of $44.02.

VERDICT: HOLD (WATCHLIST) at $85.80; Triangulated Base $76.56; downside 10.8%; required MOS 42.5%; maximum buy price $44.02. The main thesis breaker is a failure to turn backlog into durable free cash flow and margins near 17%.

For an existing holder, the position is fairly priced and worth holding while the Q2 report tests cash conversion. New money waits for $44.02 or below with the thesis intact, or for Base to rise on proven margins. In September the buy price was $86.10 and the stock was $158.55; now the stock is $85.80 and the buy price is $44.02, so the shares are further above it in percentage terms than they were then.
