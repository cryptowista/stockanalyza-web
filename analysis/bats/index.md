## What British American Tobacco is

BAT is the third-largest tobacco company by volume, according to Morningstar, with brands including Dunhill, Kent, Pall Mall, Lucky Strike and Rothmans, and Camel and Newport in the United States. Smokeless products were 19.8% of group revenue in the first half, up 1.6 percentage points from the full year 2025. New Categories revenue was £1.93 billion in the half, up 16.8% (18.0% at constant rates), and the category contribution, a profit measure, was £257 million, up 54.7%, a margin of 13.3%.

| Americas and Europe (AME) | 4,402 | 1,443 | -2.1% |

The United States is 46% of revenue and 61% of adjusted profit, and it is the part that is growing. Asia-Pacific, the Middle East and Africa lost 16.5% of profit at constant rates, where BAT has pointed to fiscal and regulatory headwinds in Bangladesh and Australia. Group guidance puts the global cigarette industry volume decline at about 3% for 2026.

## Balance sheet, debt and share count

Adjusted net debt was £31.97 billion at June 30, and unadjusted net debt £32.56 billion. At the end of 2025 the ratio of adjusted net debt to adjusted EBITDA was 2.48 times, and the target is 2.0 to 2.5 times by the end of 2026. Borrowings including leases were £35.1 billion.

The company repurchased 14.6 million shares in the first half for £649 million, on the way to a £1.3 billion programme for 2026. Weighted diluted shares were 2,176 million, down 1.3%. I use 2,155.6 million shares in issue, the latest count I found, which is a third-party figure that may be out of date. That gives a market value of £94.9 billion at £44.01.

BAT also owns about 22.9% of ITC, the largest Indian cigarette company, according to AJ Bell, and sold a 2.5% holding in 2025 for £1,052 million of net proceeds. ITC's income is inside BAT's earnings and cash flow, and I do not value the stake separately.

## The Canadian settlement

In March 2025 a Canadian court approved a C$32.5 billion settlement of tobacco claims shared by BAT's subsidiary Imperial Tobacco Canada, a Philip Morris affiliate and a Japan Tobacco affiliate. BAT recorded a provision of £6.2 billion. The company's own "adjusted for Canada" figures exclude 85% of Canadian net income after tax from New Categories, down from 100% in 2025, which is paid to claimants. In the first half, £109 million of Canadian operating profit went to claimants, against £154 million a year earlier.

The settlement is funded from the Canadian business's cash and future sales, and ITCAN has said that illicit sales above 30% of the Canadian market put pressure on those payments. A payment of about $2.6 billion in 2025 cut free cash flow that year by 48.8% to £4.05 billion, according to Hargreaves Lansdown. I use the adjusted-for-Canada earnings throughout, because that is what is left for shareholders.

## Primary valuation: discounted free cash flow

I start from normalized free cash flow of £6.3 billion a year, my estimate. The anchor points are the 2025 free cash flow of £4.05 billion plus roughly £2 billion for the Canadian payment, the £2.29 billion of the first half with a second-half weighting, and a cash conversion above 95%. I deduct about £300 million a year for Fit2Win restructuring, my estimate based on the company's total cash cost of £620 million.

The cash flow grows 2.5% a year for the first five years, 1.5% for the next five, and 0% after that, discounted at 9.0%, my estimate of the cost of equity. Growth is below the 4% to 6% profit guidance because volumes fall and the Canadian payments continue. The terminal value is 45% of the total, below the 75% level that needs close checking. Divided by 2,155.6 million shares, the value is **£36.96**. The growth rates and the discount rate are my estimates.

## Secondary valuation: multiple of earnings

The second method applies 12 times a normalized adjusted-for-Canada earnings per share of 354p. I build that from the 2025 figure of 340.5p, add the middle of the 5% to 8% growth guidance, and deduct about 2.5% for the translation headwind the company guides. The 12 times multiple implies an earnings yield of 8.3% and a dividend yield of 5.8%. With growth of 4% to 5% a year, that is a total return of about 10%, a little above my cost of equity of 9%, which I accept because cigarette volumes decline every year and the Canadian settlement runs for years.

The model gives **£42.48**.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give £36.96 and £42.48, a divergence of 13.9%, below the 20% warning level. They share the same view of volumes and margins, so agreement is not independent evidence, but the cash flow method tests the dividend and the earnings method the market's own multiples. I weight discounted cash flow 60% and the earnings multiple 40%, because cash is what pays the dividend. The weighted value is £39.17. The table puts both methods on one basis.

## What is the market already pricing in?

## Reasons to own

- The United States is growing, with revenue up 8.5% and adjusted profit up 10.1% at constant rates in the half.
- New Categories revenue grew 18.0% at constant rates and their contribution margin is 13.3%, up 3.3 points.
- Fit2Win savings target was raised to about £700 million a year by 2028, with £105 million already realised.
- The dividend yields 5.6%, has grown for 27 years in sterling, and the buyback cut weighted diluted shares by 1.3% over the past year.

## What could go wrong?

- Volume decline. Global cigarette volume is expected down about 3% in 2026 and pricing has to offset it every year.
- Payout. Dividend and buyback together are about 104% of my normalized free cash flow, so a weaker second half would slow debt reduction.
- Asia-Pacific. Profit there fell 16.5% at constant rates, and the Bangladesh and Australia pressures could persist.
- Canada and regulation. The settlement is paid over years from Canadian sales, which illicit trade reduces, and Vuse depends on enforcement against illicit vapes in the United States.
- Discount rate. At 10% the primary value is £33.12.

## Management execution

The first half was in line, but the company still needs a stronger second half to reach guidance, which analysts at Hargreaves Lansdown noted. I read the guidance at the lower end of the medium-term ranges as cautious rather than weak.
