# TD Bank: C$13 billion of planned buybacks, and a price above my Bull value

TD, Toronto and New York. Published November 21, 2026. Price is the November 20, 2026 Toronto close of C$162.05, equal to about US$112.94 on the NYSE. Values in Canadian dollars. The fiscal year ends October 31, so "FY27" is the year to October 2027. ROE is return on common equity. AML means anti-money laundering.

## Verdict: REDUCE at C$162.05

The price is 25.5% above my Base value of C$129.17 and above my Bull value of C$152.16. Earnings are at a record, but the price needs a return on equity of about 19.6% for ten years. My maximum buy price is C$87.19.

TD Bank says it could return more than C$13 billion of capital in fiscal 2027, which is about 5% of its C$265 billion market value. The reason is not that growth is plentiful. US regulators capped the size of its U.S. banks at US$434 billion after its money laundering failures, and the bank has more capital than it can use: its common equity tier 1 ratio, the main measure of capital strength, is 14.3% against a 13.0% target. Adjusted return on equity is 16.0%, up 2.8 points in a year. At C$162.05 the shares trade at 2.33 times book value, and a year earlier they traded at 1.5 times.

The business is doing well. In the third quarter adjusted earnings per share rose 26% to C$2.77, Canadian banking set a record, and Wholesale Banking earned C$743 million, up 76% on an adjusted basis. The shares fell 1.95% on November 20 as the Toronto index reached its lowest level since May, and are still 40% above where they were a year ago.

My Base value is **C$129.17**, which is 25.5% below the price. The Bear value of C$97.26 is 40.0% below the price and the Bull value of C$152.16 is still 6.1% below it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is C$126.94. At Confidence B my rules require a 32.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of **C$87.19**, or C$84.84 to C$90.72 depending on the method. The verdict is Reduce for existing holders, who can sell part at this price, and no new money.

Valuation route: a large Canadian bank with a U.S. retail franchise under a regulatory asset cap, so I value it on residual income (earnings above the cost of equity, built on book value) as the primary method and on forward earnings as the second. Economic classification: Quality Growth (6/12). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

## What TD is

TD has C$2,111.9 billion of assets, C$991.0 billion of loans and C$1,260.7 billion of deposits. In the third quarter Canadian Personal and Commercial Banking earned C$2,095 million, U.S. Banking C$1,074 million (US$771 million), Wealth Management and Insurance C$841 million and Wholesale Banking C$743 million. U.S. Banking earned a return on equity of 10.2%, well below the group, which is the segment that carries the asset cap.

The strategic review that followed the AML settlement has done a lot already. TD sold its entire 10.1% stake in Charles Schwab at US$79.25 a share, for net proceeds of US$13.9 billion that added about 2.5 points to its capital ratio. It also sold a US$9 billion mortgage portfolio and cut jobs. Fiscal 2026 U.S. AML remediation spending is now about US$550 million, up from US$500 million, because of higher lookback costs.

## Capital and share count

Book value is C$69.69 per share on 1,638.4 million shares at July 31. Risk-weighted assets are C$653.4 billion. The common equity tier 1 ratio is 14.3%, the tier 1 ratio 16.1%, the total capital ratio 17.9% and the leverage ratio 4.5%. The bank repurchased 14.5 million shares in the quarter and reduced the share count by 4% in a year. The dividend is C$1.12 a quarter, a payout of 40.2% of adjusted earnings.

Management says it expects to reach 13% common equity tier 1 in the second half of fiscal 2027, and that doing so implies buybacks above C$13 billion. That is a plan conditional on conditions staying as they are, and not a commitment. I assume a total payout of 70% of earnings in the Base case, which lets book value grow about 4.5% a year at a 15% return, and I use 1,645 million diluted shares for per-share earnings.

## Primary valuation: residual income

Residual income values a bank as book value plus the present value of earnings above the cost of equity. I use a 9.0% cost of equity, which is my estimate: a Canadian long bond yield near 3.5% plus a 5.5% premium for equity risk at a beta close to 1. Base return on equity starts at 15.0%, slightly below the 16.0% of the last quarter and the 14.8% average of the nine months, fades to 14.0% by year five and stays there for ten years. Terminal return is 12.5% and terminal growth 3.0%. I fade the return because Canadian banking is mature, the U.S. business cannot grow its balance sheet past the cap, and some of today's return comes from provisions at the lower end of the range and from an unusually good quarter in trading.

## Secondary valuation: forward earnings

Adjusted earnings per share over the last four quarters were C$9.77. For FY27 my Base estimate is C$11.20, which is about 9% above the FY26 run rate of roughly C$10.30, helped by buybacks that shrink the share count by 4% and by cost savings, and held back by lower trading revenue and a higher provision. I apply 12.0 times, in the range where large Canadian banks have usually traded on forward earnings, at the low end of that range because the asset cap and monitor remain. The result is **C$134.40**.

The EPS estimates and multiples are mine and are not company guidance.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give C$125.69 and C$134.40, a divergence of 7%, which is below the 20% warning level. Both rest on the same earnings path, so agreement is not independent evidence. I weight residual income 60% and forward earnings 40%, because the first charges for the cost of equity explicitly and the second reflects what the market has paid for Canadian banks. The weighted value is C$129.17, a point inside the range. The two methods give a price that is 28.9% and 20.6% above their values, so the choice of method does not change the decision.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Holding the cost of equity at 9.0%, the price of C$162.05 requires a return on equity of about 19.6%, held flat for ten years, or a cost of equity near 8% with a return of 16.5%. The bank earned 16.0% in the best quarter of the cycle, 14.8% on average over nine months and 13.2% a year earlier. At the price, the stock trades at 2.33 times book value and 16.6 times trailing adjusted earnings. A year ago it traded at about 1.5 times book, so the rerating is already in the price, and the buybacks bought at this price return less capital per share than they did a year ago.

## How the buy price is set

The base margin of safety is 30% at Confidence B. I added 2.5 points for the regulatory risk: the monitor and the certification required before U.S. subsidiaries can pay dividends are events that can move the result in one step. The credit cycle is carried in the Bear scenario and not charged again. The maximum buy price is C$129.17 times (1 - 32.5%), which is C$87.19, or C$84.84 to C$90.72 by method. The price is 86% above it, and the stock would need to fall 46% to reach it.

## Dividend

The dividend is C$4.48 a year, a 2.8% yield, with a payout of 40.2% of adjusted earnings. In my Bear case EPS of C$8.80 covers it nearly twice. Buybacks are the larger return, and they are worth the most when the stock is cheap, which it is not.

## Reasons to own

- Adjusted return on equity of 16.0%, up 2.8 points in a year, with the fifth straight quarter of positive operating leverage.
- Capital of 14.3% common equity tier 1, with a plan to return more than C$13 billion in fiscal 2027.
- Structural cost savings of C$900 million realized in fiscal 2026, toward C$2.0 billion to C$2.5 billion.
- Record Canadian earnings and a Wholesale Banking business that earned a 16.7% return on equity in the quarter.

## What could go wrong?

- Regulation: a US$434 billion asset cap, a monitor appointed by the Justice Department and FinCEN, and board certification before dividends can leave the U.S. subsidiaries.
- Returns: U.S. Banking earns a 10.2% return on equity and the cap limits growth there.
- Valuation: the stock trades at 2.33 times book against about 1.5 times a year ago.
- Quality of the quarter: provisions at 0.37% and a record trading quarter are both reversible.

## Management execution

Execution since the settlement has been good. TD sold Schwab, ran down non-core U.S. portfolios, cut costs by C$900 million in a year and has bought back shares every quarter. The weaker point is the expense line for compliance: AML remediation spending rose from a US$500 million estimate to US$550 million in one quarter. Management has also said that "significant work" remains and milestones fall in 2026 and 2027, and that is the range of dates that matters.

## What I would watch from here

**Green:** fourth-quarter results in early December and the fiscal 2027 outlook confirm adjusted ROE near 16% with a provision ratio near 0.40%, buybacks continue, and regulators show a path to lifting the cap.

**Yellow:** remediation costs rise again, buyback pace slows, or Wholesale Banking earnings fall back sharply from this quarter.

**Red:** a new penalty or extension of the cap, adjusted ROE below 13%, or provisions above 0.50% of loans.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. The U.S. asset cap is extended or a new penalty follows from the monitor's findings.
2. Provisions rise above 0.50% of loans in a weaker Canadian or U.S. economy.
3. The cost of equity falls toward 8%, which on my Base path raises the value to C$151.19.

## Final verdict: reduce at C$162.05

TD is a stronger bank than a year ago, with higher returns, lower costs and a capital surplus it is returning to shareholders. At C$162.05 the market has paid for all of it and for more, and my Bull value of C$152.16 is below the price. My maximum buy price is C$87.19.

**Verdict: REDUCE at C$162.05. Triangulated Base value C$129.17, downside -20.3%, Confidence B (18.0/25). Required margin of safety 32.5%, entry range by method C$84.84 to C$90.72, maximum buy price C$87.19.**

The date I would mark is the fourth-quarter report in early December, together with the fiscal 2027 outlook. It should show how large the buyback is planned to be, and whether the U.S. remediation milestones still fall in the time that management gave.
