Cognex makes the cameras, sensors and AI software that let factories and warehouses see: reading barcodes, inspecting parts and guiding machines. After a two-year industrial slump, it just posted record revenue and a 32% adjusted EBITDA margin, and it is buying Intel's former RealSense depth camera business to move into robotic perception. The business is excellent. The price already pays for the optimistic version of the story.

At $57.70 on September 24, 2026, the shares trade 25.7% above my triangulated Base value of $45.90, a downside of 20.4% to Base. The Bear value of $35.03 is 39.3% below the price. The required margin of safety of 35% puts the maximum buy price at $29.84. For existing holders this is a hold. For new money it is a watchlist name.

What Cognex is

Cognex sells machine vision systems, sensors, barcode readers and software used in factory automation and logistics. Gross margins around 70% look software-like, but demand follows industrial capital spending, so industrial multiples apply, not software multiples. Revenue fell from about $1.04 billion in 2021 to about $838 million in 2023 before recovering to about $994 million in 2025, which proves the business is cyclical.

On September 22, 2026, Cognex agreed to buy RealSense for about $500 million in cash, plus a $56.5 million three-year retention program and about $50 million of restricted stock. RealSense makes depth cameras for robots and expects $80 to $90 million of 2026 revenue, growing more than 50%. Management sizes its market at about $600 million today and $1.6 billion by 2030. It is a sensible strategic move toward physical AI, but its returns are not yet proven.

Cash quality and stock compensation

Q2 free cash flow was 93% of GAAP net income and 89% of adjusted net income. Cash quality is strong. Cognex's adjusted EBITDA does not add back stock compensation, so it needs no adjustment. Free cash flow, however, is before stock compensation, which ran at about $46 million in research and selling expenses in 2025. With the new RealSense stock grants, about $60 million a year is deducted from free cash flow. The hard metric is normalized through-cycle cash flow, not a peak quarter.

Cash, shares and the acquisition

Cash and investments were about $755 million before the deal, with no debt. Paying about $500 million for RealSense leaves about $255 million. Shares outstanding are 168.2 million; the valuation uses about 170 million to include the new stock grants. Cognex authorized an additional $500 million of buybacks in February. At $57.70, market value is about $9.7 billion and enterprise value, after the deal, about $9.55 billion.

Secondary valuation: normalized free cash flow

Normalized post-deal free cash flow of $300 to $340 million, after integration and retention costs and an eventual RealSense contribution, less about $60 million of stock compensation, leaves about $260 million of hard free cash flow at the midpoint. At 26.5x, the midpoint of a 25x to 28x range, plus remaining cash, that gives $42.03 per share. Without the stock compensation deduction, the same midpoints give $51.38.

Historical cross-check

Cognex trades at about 34.5x trailing EV to EBITDA against a five-year average near 35.2x, about 9.6x sales against 8.3x, and about 33x cash flow, near its average. Paying the full historical premium at a cyclical recovery point, with an unintegrated acquisition, is aggressive. A normalized reference gives about $50.

What is the market already pricing in?

At the Base multiple of 20x, today's enterprise value implies normalized EBITDA of about $478 million, almost exactly the Bull case. The price requires the industrial recovery to continue, gross margins around 70%, EBITDA margins of 30% or more through the cycle, RealSense to reach Cognex-like margins, and meaningful value creation from physical AI. The market is pricing more than the legacy franchise.

How the buy price is set

Confidence B starts at a 30% margin of safety. Five points are added for cyclicality and five for integration risk, and five are subtracted for the strong balance sheet and cash conversion, for 35%. A 35% discount to the $45.90 Base gives a maximum buy price of $29.84.

Dividend

The quarterly dividend is $0.085, about $0.34 a year, a 0.6% yield. Coverage is excellent, but the dividend does not drive the thesis.

Reasons to own CGNX

  • Leadership. A global leader in machine vision with a deep installed base.
  • Economics. About 70% gross margins and excellent free cash flow conversion.
  • AI products. AI-enabled vision supports mix and pricing.
  • Operating leverage. Eight straight quarters of margin expansion.
  • Physical AI option. RealSense adds robotic perception in a fast-growing market.
  • Capital returns. Buybacks, a dividend and a debt-free balance sheet.

What could go wrong?

  • Industrial cyclicality. 2022 to 2023 showed how quickly revenue can fall.
  • RealSense. Integration and returns on a $500 million purchase are unproven. Failure of the recovery plus RealSense to produce sustained per-share cash flow growth is the main thesis breaker.
  • Valuation. The price already implies Bull-case earnings.
  • Competition. Machine vision technology keeps evolving.
  • A smaller cushion. Net cash falls from about $755 million to about $255 million.

Management execution

Record revenue, eight consecutive quarters of margin expansion, strong cash conversion and a debt-free balance sheet, with the 2026 margin target already exceeded. RealSense returns remain unproven. Management credibility scores 16/20, good.

The three most important thesis breakers

  2. RealSense failing to grow or reach Cognex-like margins.
  3. Per-share free cash flow failing to grow after the acquisition.

Adversarial review

The first draft's primary valuation and sensitivity table reconciled, implying about $255 million of post-deal net cash and 170 million shares. Three things were corrected. Its Bear value was rounded down from the model's $35.03 to $34. Its secondary free cash flow value of $46 was below its own ranges, which give $45.62 to $57.50 and $51.38 at the midpoints, and it did not deduct stock compensation, which free cash flow adds back; after that deduction it is $42.03. Its weighted Base was $47.10, stated as $47. The Base moved from $47 to $45.90 and the maximum buy price from $30.55 to $29.84. The draft's twelve-month price-touch probabilities were dropped as not part of the framework. The verdict is unchanged.

The strongest counter-argument is that AI machine vision plus RealSense can structurally accelerate growth enough to justify historical premium multiples. The Bull case awards faster scaling and a 23x multiple. The Base withholds full credit until RealSense proves Cognex-like margins and cash conversion.
