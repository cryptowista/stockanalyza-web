T. Rowe Price lost $28.3 billion of client money in the third quarter, more than in the first half, and still raised adjusted EPS

TROW, Nasdaq. Published November 22, 2026. Price as of the Friday, November 20, 2026 regular session close ($100.99). T. Rowe Price is a US asset manager with $1.86 trillion of client assets, about two-thirds of them retirement-related. AUM is assets under management. Net flows are client deposits less withdrawals. The effective fee rate is advisory fees as a share of average assets, in basis points (one basis point is 0.01%). Adjusted figures are the company's own measure that excludes items such as restructuring and the gains and losses of consolidated funds. Carried interest is a performance share the firm books on private investment funds.

T. Rowe Price saw $28.3 billion of net client outflows in the third quarter, more than in the first two quarters of the year combined, and adjusted EPS still rose 1.8% to $2.86. Higher average assets and carried interest helped. At $100.99 the shares trade at 10.1 times my FY27 earnings estimate, 9.9% below my Base value of $112.12.

My Base value is $112.12. The Bear value of $74.22 is 27% below the price and the Bull value of $146.67 is 45% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is $111.28. At Confidence C my rules require a 42.5% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of $64.47, or $63.55 to $65.86 depending on the method. The upside to Base is 11.0%. The price is below Base but far above the maximum buy price, so this is a Hold with the stock on a watchlist for new money.

What T. Rowe Price is

T. Rowe Price managed $1,862.7 billion at September 30, 2026, which is 5.4% more than a year earlier and 1.6% less than at the end of June. Equity strategies are $896.3 billion, or 48% of the total, multi-asset $680.6 billion, fixed income and money market $221.8 billion and alternatives $64.0 billion. Investors outside the United States hold 9.3%. The firm also administers $346 billion for others, of which $186 billion it manages itself.

Revenue grew 5.9% because average assets grew 9.5%, and the effective fee rate fell 1.3 basis points as clients moved to lower-fee products. Each basis point is about $189 million of annual revenue at Q3 average assets (my arithmetic). Capital allocation-based income, mostly accrued carried interest, rose to $59.9 million from $42.0 million, and accrued carried interest rose $63.1 million. After the related compensation that is about $0.14 a share (my arithmetic), which I exclude from my normalized earnings. GAAP EPS fell because non-operating income dropped to $17.6 million from $238.4 million.

Adjusted operating expenses rose 5.8%, in line with revenue, and the adjusted operating margin was 40.4% against 40.9%. Headcount was 7,640 against 7,830 a year earlier. Management said it expects adjusted operating expense growth near the middle of its 4% to 7% range for 2026 and that October flows were weak, with a pipeline suggesting some improvement in November and December.

Balance sheet, dividend and share count

Cash and equivalents were $3,774.3 million, or $17.69 a share, and investments were $4,185.2 million, which include seed capital and accrued carried interest of $440.9 million. Management put cash and discretionary investments at $4.9 billion. Stockholders' equity attributable to the firm was $11,370.5 million and there were 213.3 million shares. The release gave no debt figure, so I could not confirm that the firm has none. That is an open data gap, and I do not subtract any debt.

The firm paid $285 million of dividends in the quarter and did not repurchase shares, according to a summary of the filing. A summary of the first half reports $497 million of buybacks. Diluted shares fell to 213.6 million from 219.7 million a year earlier, so the earlier buybacks lifted EPS. On August 20 the firm agreed to buy F/m Investments, a fixed income and ETF manager with about $19 billion of assets, for an undisclosed price, expected to close in early 2027.

The model holds earnings flat for five years, because fee cuts and outflows offset market growth, then grows them 1.5% a year, with a terminal growth of 1.5% and a cost of equity of 10.0%, my estimate for a market-sensitive business. The terminal value is 44% of the total over 10 years, below the 75% level that needs close checking. The model gives $110.51. Cash is not added on top, because the earnings already include the return on it.

Secondary valuation: multiple of normalized earnings

The second method applies 11.5 times to the same $9.96. A business that pays out its earnings and grows about 1% is worth about 1 divided by the cost of equity less growth, which is roughly 11 times at 10%. I add half a turn for the net cash, the 40 consecutive annual dividend increases and the low capital needs. The multiple is my judgment and not company guidance. The result is $114.54.

Valuation triangulation

The two methods give $110.51 and $114.54, a divergence of 3.6%, well below the 20% warning level. They are not independent: both rest on the same FY27 earnings, so agreement says little about whether that estimate is right. I weight the discounted earnings 60% and the multiple 40%, because the first states the growth and the cost of equity I assume. The weighted value is $112.12. What is the market already pricing in?

At $100.99 the equity is worth about $21.5 billion, or 1.16% of assets under management. That is 10.1 times my FY27 earnings, an earnings yield of 9.9%, with a dividend yield of 5.1% and about 52% of FY27 earnings paid out. Holding everything else at Base, the price requires a cost of equity of 10.8% instead of 10.0%, or earnings that fall 2.3% a year for five years instead of staying flat. So the market already assumes that outflows and fee cuts shrink earnings slowly.

Dividend and payout

The quarterly dividend is $1.30 a share, or $5.20 a year, a yield of 5.1%. It was raised 2.36% from $1.27 in February, which the company called its 40th consecutive year of increases. The most recent dividend was paid on September 29. I did not find the next declaration. The February increase pattern suggests the next raise would come then, and I assume no cut in any case.

Dividends are about 52% of my FY27 earnings. The firm also holds $4.9 billion of cash and discretionary investments, which is about 4.4 years of dividends (my arithmetic). Fee pressure would have to be severe to threaten the payment.

Reasons to own

  • The adjusted operating margin is above 40% and adjusted EPS rose 1.8% in a quarter with $28.3 billion of outflows.
  • Cash of $3,774 million is $17.69 a share, and the firm has raised its dividend for 40 consecutive years.
  • Fixed income took in $3.4 billion in the quarter, and the F/m deal adds ETF and separate account capability.
  • Management expects Q4 outflows to moderate from Q3, and more than half of fund assets beat peers over one, three and ten years, according to summaries of the call.

What could go wrong?

  • Outflows. The $28.3 billion of Q3 outflows were mostly equity ($25.6 billion), and management said October stayed weak. FY27 earnings of $8.80, which is assets 5% lower, give a Base value of $99.07.
  • Fee rate. It fell from 39.1 to 37.8 basis points in a year, and one more basis point costs about $0.66 a share a year.
  • Markets. Markets added $135.6 billion to assets in nine months, and a fall of the same size would remove more than the outflows did.
  • Costs. Adjusted operating expenses grew 5.8% and the guidance is 4% to 7%, which leaves little room if revenue stalls.
  • Acquisition. The F/m price is not disclosed and the deal is expected to close in early 2027.

Management execution

Management cut headcount by about 2.4% over the year, held the adjusted operating margin near 40%, bought back shares until the third quarter and announced an acquisition in fixed income. It did not stop the outflows: net flows were -$7.9 billion a year ago and -$28.3 billion now. The next quarter will show whether the improving fund performance that management points to turns into flows.

Final verdict: hold at $100.99, watchlist for new money

T. Rowe Price earns well, pays a 5% dividend and is cheaper than my Base value by 9.9%, but the lead measure of the business, client flows, is getting worse. New money would need a price near $63.55 to $65.86, which would require a very different outlook for flows.

Verdict: HOLD at $100.99, watchlist for new money. Triangulated Base value $112.12, upside 11.0%, Confidence C (16.2/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by method $63.55 to $65.86, maximum buy price $64.47.
