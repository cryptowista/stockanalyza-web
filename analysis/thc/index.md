## What Tenet Healthcare is

Tenet runs two businesses. The hospital segment, which also contains Conifer, had $12.5 billion of revenue and $2.26 billion of adjusted EBITDA over the first nine months of 2026, an 18.0% margin. The ambulatory segment, built around United Surgical Partners International (USPI), had $4.1 billion of revenue and $1.56 billion of EBITDA, a 38.3% margin. USPI is a quarter of revenue and about 41% of EBITDA, and it runs 535 surgery centers and 25 surgical hospitals in 37 states.

The catch is who owns USPI's profit. Most surgery centers are partnerships with local physicians, so the consolidated numbers include earnings that belong to the partners. For 2026 Tenet guides to $900 million to $970 million of cash distributions to those partners, and net income to partners of $910 million to $960 million. Of the ambulatory segment's guided EBITDA of $2.16 billion to $2.22 billion, about $865 million to $895 million goes to partners. Any valuation that skips this line overstates what a shareholder owns.

## Secondary valuation: multiple of EBITDA after partners

The second method applies a multiple to my 2027E EBITDA of $4,605 million less $988 million to partners, which is $3,617 million. At 8.0 times, enterprise value is $28.9 billion. I subtract net debt of $10.67 billion, add the CommonSpirit note after tax, and divide by 80.5 million shares. The result is **$238.83**.

The 8.0 times is my judgment. Unlevered owner cash flow in 2027 is about $1.75 billion, so 8.0 times implies a perpetual growth rate of about 2.4% at a weighted cost of capital near 8.6%. A reader who prefers 7.5 times gets $216.36, and 8.5 times gives $261.29. I did not verify a current peer or historical multiple table, so I derive the multiple from growth and cost of capital and do not borrow one.

## What is the market already pricing in?

At $275.44 the equity is worth $22.2 billion. Holding everything else fixed, the price needs a cost of equity of about 9.5% instead of 10.5%, or terminal growth of 3.8% instead of 2.5%, or about $267 million a year more EBITDA than my Base in every year. That last gap is about 5.8% of my 2027E EBITDA, and my Bull case has $245 million more in 2027 and $660 million more by 2031. So the price needs something between my Base and my Bull.

On returns, my Base cash flows earn about 7.0% a year at $275.44, against my 10.5% cost of equity. The Bear gives -12% a year and the Bull 16.2%. If the price to 2027E earnings stayed at 15.4 times and every dollar of owner cash went into buybacks (my arithmetic), the shares would compound at about 10% a year to the end of 2030. The gap between 10% and 7% is the multiple: my model ends with Tenet worth about 11 times earnings, not 15.4. A Tenet that keeps a 15 times multiple is a Tenet that has proved the 2028 Medicaid and exchange worries wrong.

## Dividend and capital returns

Tenet pays no dividend, so there is no income to test. Capital goes to the surgery center acquisitions, the hospitals and buybacks, and management lists debt management as a fourth use. Buybacks of $1.615 billion over nine months were bigger than the owner free cash flow I estimate for the year, which means the company is also using excess cash and the CommonSpirit settlement to retire shares.

## Reasons to own

- Adjusted EBITDA without the Conifer timing grew 9.9% in the quarter, and the company raised its 2026 EBITDA outlook by $195 million at the midpoint to $5.05 billion to $5.20 billion.
- USPI earns a 38.3% EBITDA margin, and the CMS phase-out of the Medicare inpatient-only list over the next three years should push more complex procedures into outpatient settings.
- Net debt to EBITDA is 2.11 times, there are no note maturities in 2027, and the $1.9 billion revolver is undrawn.
- More than 90% of 2027 commercial contracts are signed, with renewals generally in the range of 3% to 5%.
- Diluted shares fell 9.0% in a year, and $1.875 billion of buyback authorization remains.

## What could go wrong?

- Exchange coverage and uninsured patients. Exchange admissions fell 18%, self-pay costs rose 45% in the quarter, and exchange patients are converting to uninsured one for one. HCA expects fewer losses in 2027 but still a meaningful payer mix effect.
- Medicaid supplemental payments. HCA expects $550 million to $650 million less in 2027, and the 2025 federal budget law starts to cut state directed payments in 2028. Tenet has said it is not modeling 2027 or 2028 in detail, because elections could change the rules.
- The Conifer cliff. $450 million of 2026 benefit is gone in 2027, and the CommonSpirit contract profit ends on December 31. Each extra $150 million of loss costs about $18 a share.
- Weak surgical volume. Hospital surgeries fell 1.1% and surgery center cases 1.5%, and Tenet's CEO described the surgical market as not strong right now.
- Refinancing cost. New debt priced at 6.25% adds interest each year, and each extra $100 million of interest costs about $11 a share.

## Management execution

Management raised its 2026 EBITDA outlook in the second and third quarters, from an original range of $4.485 billion to $4.785 billion to $5.05 billion to $5.20 billion now, and it cut net debt to EBITDA from 2.25 times at December 31 to 2.11 times. The weak points are definitional. Adjusted free cash flow is before $0.9 billion a year of partner distributions, the Conifer benefit is split between reported revenue and the contract termination line, and nine-month adjusted free cash flow of $2.52 billion is $0.44 billion below unadjusted free cash flow of $2.96 billion, a gap the release does not itemize. The buyback record is mixed: about $184 a share in the second quarter, about $263 in the third. I score management evidence 12 to 14 out of 20, a range because no dividend makes one dimension not applicable.

## Final verdict: hold at $275.44, do not add

Tenet is executing well, but at $275.44 the market is paying for the 2026 numbers with the Conifer money still in them. I value the shares at $241.53, 14.0% below the price. New money would need a price near $129.16 to $149.56, which is about half of today's price, so I would watch it and not add.

**Verdict: HOLD at $275.44, do not add. Triangulated Base value $241.53, upside -12.3%, Confidence C (15.0/25). Required margin of safety 42.5%, entry range by robustness check $129.16 to $149.56, maximum buy price $138.88.**
