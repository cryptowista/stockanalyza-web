Groupon told investors on November 9 that it will earn $60 million to $65 million of adjusted EBITDA this year. The same company spent $45.8 million of cash in the third quarter alone, and over the last four quarters its free cash flow was $8.8 million. At $12.40 the market is paying for the first number.

My Base value is $5.96 a share, the Bear value $0.96 and the Bull value $12.17. The Base blends a multiple of EBITDA after stock pay ($6.87) with a discounted owner free cash flow ($4.60). I require a 50% margin of safety, which puts the maximum buy price at $2.98. At $12.40 the call is Sell, because the price sits at my Bull case and 2.1 times my Base.

Valuation route: a flat-sales online marketplace with thin free cash flow and no dividend. I value it mainly on a multiple of EBITDA after stock-based compensation (SBC, shares paid to staff, which I call stock pay), cross-checked with a ten year discounted owner free cash flow before interest. Convertible notes less cash are deducted as net debt. The price was set from XTB and could not be matched to a dated close.

## What Groupon is

Groupon runs an online marketplace where local merchants sell discounted vouchers for experiences, services and goods. It reports gross billings, the total value of what customers buy, and keeps a share as revenue. Revenue was $498.4 million in 2025 and the company lost $83.5 million, according to filing data from a data service. A different service shows annual revenue above $3 billion at its peak in the mid 2010s, so this is a business that has already shrunk by about six times.

The third quarter shows a flat customer base and a bigger basket. Active customers were 16.2 million, roughly unchanged, with International up 4% and North America down 1%. Units fell 2% to 9.0 million while average order value rose 8%. North America local revenue was flat and local billings grew 4%. International local revenue grew 12% and billings 11%.

Management, led by chief executive Dusan Senkypl, describes the plan as turning Groupon into an AI-native company under a program it calls Project Foundry. It says AI resolved 84% of support contacts in the third quarter, up from 42% in January, and that LivingSocial relaunched on October 15 as an agent-first marketplace with more than 50,000 local experiences in public preview. These are company statements, and none of them has shown up in revenue yet.

Adjusted EBITDA over the last four quarters is $63.3 million. Subtract $34.2 million of stock pay and $29.0 million is left, and free cash flow for the period was $8.8 million. The company pays staff in shares, and a fall in stock pay from $11.1 million to $3.8 million a quarter coincides with the layoffs. I did not check the cause. If that level is not repeatable, EBITDA after stock pay is worth much less than it looks.

Cash flow is seasonal, with the fourth quarter strong and the other three weak. A free cash flow of at least $40 million for 2026, with minus $44.3 million in the first nine months, means roughly $84 million in the fourth quarter, by my arithmetic. Last year's fourth quarter was $53.0 million and most of it was given back in the next three quarters. Cash of $177.4 million at September 30 follows a weak quarter, and I do not add the fourth quarter inflow to it.

Taxes are a drag that adjusted EBITDA hides. The third quarter tax provision was $3.5 million on a near zero pretax result, about $14 million a year. I model about $7 million and show the higher figure in the stress test.

## Primary valuation: EV/EBITDA after stock pay

I start from EBITDA of $65 million for fiscal 2027 in the Base: the guided $62.5 million midpoint plus part of the cost savings, with revenue roughly flat. The Bear is $45 million, where the savings do not stick and sales fall 3% a year. The Bull is $85 million, where most of the $20 million to $25 million of savings reach profit. Stock pay is deducted at 3% of sales, $15 million, and I show the effect of a higher figure below.

I apply 8.0 times in the Base, 5.5 times in the Bear and 9.0 times in the Bull. The Base multiple needs a reason. Sales are flat, customers are flat, the savings are not yet proven in profit and net debt is 1.8 times EBITDA, so I do not pay for growth. But capital spending is only about 2% of sales, which makes the business cash light. At a 12% cost of capital, 8.0 times implies owner free cash flow growing about 4.0% a year forever, more than the 1% I assume for sales, which is why this method gives the higher of my two values. The Bear sits well below the middle of any normal range, because a shrinking marketplace with notes to refinance does not deserve a normal multiple.

## Secondary valuation: discounted owner free cash flow

This method takes EBITDA, deducts stock pay, cash taxes and capital spending, and discounts the result for ten years plus a terminal value. The Base has sales up 1% a year and the EBITDA margin rising from 12.5% to 13% over three years. Stock pay is 3% of sales, capital spending 2%, depreciation $17 million, tax 22% of operating profit, cost of capital 12% and terminal growth 1%. I use 12% because this is a small, indebted company with a shrinking history and a volatile share price.

I handle the 40% gap between methods through the confidence grade and add nothing separate for it, and I add nothing for leverage because the Bear case already carries the debt. $5.96 times 0.50 is **$2.98**, 76% below today's price.

## Dividend and buybacks

Groupon pays no dividend, and none of the release summaries I read mention a buyback. Cash fell by $118.6 million this year and $46.2 million of notes mature in March, so any return of cash would compete with debt. There is no yield to set against the valuation.

## Reasons to own Groupon

- Gross billings grew 6% in the third quarter, with International up 11% to 12%, which suggests demand is not collapsing.
- The restructuring should save $20 million to $25 million a year, a third of guided EBITDA if it all reaches profit, and I include part of it in the Base.
- Capital spending is about 2% of sales, so profit turns into cash more easily than in most marketplaces.
- The fourth quarter is guided to a large cash inflow, and cash of $177.4 million covers the $46.2 million of notes due in March.
- The shares have already fallen 20% since the results and the 52 week range is $9.17 to $29.90, so a good fourth quarter could move the price quickly.

## What could go wrong?

- **Profit quality:** EBITDA after stock pay was $29.0 million over the last four quarters and free cash flow $8.8 million.
- **Missed targets:** adjusted EBITDA came in below the company's own third quarter target and the revenue outlook was cut.
- **Leverage:** notes of $290.3 million against cash of $177.4 million, with $46.2 million due in March 2027 and $244.1 million in 2030.
- **Flat customers:** 16.2 million active customers and falling units, with growth coming from higher order values.
- **Cash seasonality:** the fourth quarter inflow has been given back later in the year before.
- **Unchecked items:** leases, the conversion terms of the 2030 notes, cash taxes and the reason stock pay fell.
- **My own numbers:** margin, multiple, cost of capital and tax are my estimates.

## Management execution

Management has moved fast on cost: 336 positions gone by the end of September and a program to run support on AI, with a new chief business officer and a new board member in the quarter. It has not delivered on its own numbers. The third quarter missed the company's EBITDA target and the revenue outlook for 2026 is now below the March range. I score management 5 out of 10, with credit for cost control and a deduction for the misses.

## Final verdict: sell at $12.40

Groupon has cut its costs, kept its customers and guided to a strong fourth quarter. The price has taken all of that on board: at $12.40 the shares trade at my Bull value and 2.1 times my Base, and the maximum buy price of $2.98 is 76% below the market.

For a holder, selling is the better use of the capital, because the Base return of 6.2% a year is below a 12% cost of capital. For new money the buy zone is near $2.98. The Bull case needs the cost cuts to land almost in full, and the price already assumes they do.

**Verdict: SELL at $12.40. Base $5.96, -51.9% against the price, Bear $0.96, Bull $12.17, Confidence D (12/25). Required margin of safety 50%, maximum buy price $2.98.**
