# Scotiabank: it hit its 14% return target in one quarter, and the price assumes it holds that for ten years

BNS, Toronto and New York. Published November 21, 2026. Price is the November 20, 2026 Toronto close of C$119.90, equal to about US$83.64 on the NYSE. Values in Canadian dollars. The fiscal year ends October 31, so "FY27" is the year to October 2027. ROE is return on common equity. PCL is the provision for credit losses.

Scotiabank earned an adjusted return on equity of 14.2% in the quarter to July 31, 2026, and its chief executive said the bank had exceeded its 14% target. It is one quarter. At C$119.90 the shares trade at 1.84 times book value, and my model says that price needs a return on equity of about 14.2% held for ten years, which turns a good quarter into a permanent condition.

The quarter itself was strong. Adjusted net income was C$2,973 million, up 18%, and adjusted EPS was C$2.28 against C$1.88 a year earlier. Canadian Banking earned C$1,071 million, up 12%. Global Wealth Management earned a record C$518 million, up 23%, and Global Banking and Markets earned C$647 million with record underwriting and advisory fees. Credit did not help: the provision for credit losses was C$1,079 million against C$1,041 million a year earlier. The shares closed at C$119.90 on November 20 after falling 2.9% that day and about 9% over the week, and I could not find a single cause in the material I read. They are still about 33% above where they stood a year ago.

My Base value is **C$116.62**, which is 2.8% below the price. The Bear value of C$80.02 is 33.3% below the price and the Bull value of C$132.65 is 10.6% above it. The probability-weighted value, with 25% on Bear, 50% on Base and 25% on Bull, is C$111.48. At Confidence B my rules require a 30% margin of safety, which gives a maximum buy price of **C$81.63**, or C$78.12 to C$83.98 depending on the method. The verdict is Hold for existing holders. New money should wait.

Valuation route: a large Canadian bank with a Latin American and Caribbean network, so I value it on residual income (earnings above the cost of equity, built on book value) as the primary method and on forward earnings as the second. Economic classification: Quality Growth (5/12). Values are present fair value estimates, not 12 month price targets.

## What Scotiabank is

The bank has C$1,548 billion of assets, C$770.6 billion of loans and C$1,006.0 billion of deposits. Of the C$2,953 million reported in the third quarter, Canadian Banking earned 36%, International Banking 26% (C$766 million), Global Banking and Markets 22% and Global Wealth Management 18%, with the Other segment losing C$49 million. International is the part that sets it apart from the other big Canadian banks, and it is the part that moves with Latin American credit and currencies.

The bank has been trimming that network. It sold its banking operations in Colombia, Costa Rica and Panama in the first quarter of FY26 in exchange for a 20.3% stake in Davivienda Group, booking a pre-tax loss of C$434 million. In June it announced a roughly C$500 million cash offer for the minority shares of Scotia Group Jamaica, which still needs approvals.

## Capital and share count

Common equity is C$79,212 million, or C$64.99 per share, on 1,219 million shares. Risk-weighted assets are C$492.9 billion. The common equity tier 1 ratio is 13.1%, down 20 basis points from the second quarter, the leverage ratio is 4.3% and the liquidity coverage ratio 126%. The dividend is C$1.14 a quarter, C$4.56 a year, about 55% of trailing adjusted earnings. I assume a total payout of 62% of earnings in the Base case, dividends plus buybacks, and 1,226 million diluted shares for per-share earnings.

## Primary valuation: residual income

Residual income values a bank as book value plus the present value of earnings above the cost of equity. I use a 9.0% cost of equity, which is my estimate: a Canadian long bond yield near 3.5% plus a 5.5% premium for equity risk at a beta close to 1. Base return on equity starts at 13.8%, below the 14.2% of the last quarter because the first two quarters were 13.0% and 13.2%, rises to 14.2% by year five and stays there for ten years. Terminal return is 12.5% and terminal growth 3.0%.

The cost of equity decides the verdict and I cannot verify it from a filing.

## Secondary valuation: forward earnings

Adjusted earnings per share over the last four quarters were C$8.28: C$6.35 for nine months of FY26 plus C$1.93 in the fourth quarter of FY25. For FY27 my Base estimate is C$9.30, about 7.5% above my FY26 estimate of roughly C$8.65, which is near the 7% a year growth objective the bank set for itself. I apply 12.0 times, in the range where large Canadian banks have usually traded on forward earnings. The result is **C$111.60**. The EPS estimates and multiples are mine and are not company guidance.

## Valuation triangulation

The two methods give C$119.97 and C$111.60, a divergence of 7%, which is below the 20% warning level. Both rest on the same earnings path, so agreement is not independent evidence. I weight residual income 60% and forward earnings 40%, because the first charges for the cost of equity explicitly and the second reflects what the market has paid for Canadian banks. The weighted value is C$116.62. The price is -0.1% above the first method and 7.4% above the second, so the choice of method does not change the decision.

## What is the market already pricing in?

Holding the cost of equity at 9.0%, the price of C$119.90 requires a return on equity of about 14.2%, held flat for ten years. That is the quarter just reported, kept. The bank earned 13.2% in the second quarter, 13.0% in the first and 12.4% a year earlier, all on an adjusted basis. At the price the stock trades at 1.84 times book value and 14.5 times trailing adjusted earnings. My Base case gives the bank credit for the target but not for beating it every quarter.

## How the buy price is set

The base margin of safety is 30% at Confidence B. I did not add or subtract points: Latin American credit and currency risk and the markets cycle are carried in the Bear scenario, and the 13.1% capital ratio is already reflected in the 62% payout assumption. The maximum buy price is C$116.62 times (1 - 30%), which is C$81.63, or C$78.12 to C$83.98 by method. The price is 47% above it, and the stock would need to fall 32% to reach it.

## Dividend

The dividend is C$4.56 a year, a 3.8% yield, about 55% of trailing adjusted earnings. In my Bear case EPS of C$7.40 covers it 1.6 times. The bank raised the quarterly dividend by 4% in May, to C$1.14.

## Reasons to own

- Adjusted ROE rose from 12.4% to 14.2% in a year, and every business line reported strong results in the quarter.
- Fifth straight quarter of margin expansion in Canadian Banking, with record wealth earnings and record underwriting and advisory fees.
- Capital is returned steadily: 8.6 million shares bought back in the quarter and C$6.3 billion returned this year, with CET1 at 13.1%.
- A 20.3% stake in Davivienda and a simpler Latin American footprint after the Colombia, Costa Rica and Panama sale.

## What could go wrong?

- Credit: provisions were C$1,079 million, up from C$1,041 million, and gross impaired loans are 1.00% of loans.
- International: Latin American credit, rates and currencies, which are less predictable than Canadian ones.
- Earnings quality: record fee income in wealth and capital markets depends on markets staying strong.
- Valuation: the price needs the quarter's 14.2% return to last for a decade, and the stock has already fallen about 9% in a week for reasons I could not pin down.

## Management execution

Management reached its 14% return target, set for fiscal 2027, a year early: adjusted return on equity was 13.0% in the first quarter, 13.2% in the second and 14.2% in the third. The bank also simplified its footprint and kept buying back shares. The weak point is the habit of adjustments: a C$373 million restructuring charge in the fourth quarter of FY25 and a C$434 million pre-tax loss on the Colombia, Costa Rica and Panama sale in FY26 mean reported EPS for nine months was C$6.00 against C$6.35 adjusted. The gap is small this quarter, but a bank that has "exceeded" a target in a single quarter has not yet shown it can hold it.

## What I would watch from here

**Green:** fourth-quarter results on December 2 show adjusted ROE at or above 14%, impaired provisions at or below 52 basis points of loans, and CET1 at or above 13.0% with buybacks continuing.

**Yellow:** adjusted ROE between 13% and 14%, or impaired provisions above 52 basis points, or a further fall in the capital ratio.

**Red:** adjusted ROE below 12.5%, impaired provisions above 65 basis points, or CET1 below 12.5%.

## The three most important thesis breakers

1. A rise in credit losses in International or Canadian consumer lending that lifts impaired provisions above 65 basis points.
2. A fall in wealth and capital markets fees that takes the return on equity back to 12% or 13%.
3. A cost of equity of 10%, which on my Base path lowers the value to C$99.65 at a 13.5% starting return.
