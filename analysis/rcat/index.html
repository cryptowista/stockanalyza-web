Red Cat: most of the cash goes to an Austrian engine maker before the drone ramp shows up, and the verdict is hold at $4.00

RCAT, Nasdaq. Published November 5, 2026. Price as of the November 4, 2026 regular session close. Latest reported quarter: April to June 2026. Third quarter results are due on November 16, 2026, after the close.

Hold at $4.00

At $4.00 Red Cat trades 13.6% above my Base value of $3.52 and about two and a half times my maximum buy price of $1.58. The company is about to spend roughly $230 million in cash, most of what it has left, on Steyr Motors, while it still has to show the second-half revenue ramp its 2026 guidance depends on and will likely need new shares in 2027. Six weeks ago this site rated Red Cat a Hold at $6.80 and set the buy price at $3.30. On November 5 the shares traded at $3.43. If the analysis had stood still, it would now be flashing buy. It doesn't, because the company that buy price was set for has changed: since then Red Cat has offered about €205 million in cash for Steyr Motors, an Austrian engine maker, which will use most of the $326 million it held in June before the drone business has shown it can turn revenue into cash.

At $4.00 on the November 4, 2026 close, Red Cat trades 13.6% above my Base value of $3.52. The Bear value is $1.01, 74.7% below the price, and the Bull value is $8.46, 111.6% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. The verdict is Hold, with new money waiting for $1.58 or less.

Valuation route: a fast-growing defense hardware company without profits or free cash flow, valued from a scenario path to 2029 operating profit, including Steyr Motors, with the cash burn until then, the new shares needed to fund it and ongoing dilution from share-based pay. A revenue multiple is used only as a diagnostic. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Red Cat is

Red Cat makes small military drones, mainly the Black Widow reconnaissance drone it delivers under the U.S. Army's Short Range Reconnaissance program, plus FlightWave and other aircraft, and since 2025 uncrewed surface vessels under the Blue Ops name. The U.S. Army accounted for about half of first-half revenue. The company has expanded its manufacturing space to about 270,000 square feet and says it now sells nine products at average prices in the hundreds of thousands of dollars.

On October 19, 2026 it offered €39 a share in cash for Steyr Motors, about €205 million, roughly $230 million, paid from its own funds. Steyr makes compact high-power engines for military vehicles, boats and as auxiliary power units for main battle tanks. Its board recommends the offer, the largest shareholder has agreed to sell its 15%, and the offer runs to December 18 with a 50% minimum acceptance and foreign investment approvals in Austria, Australia, Italy and the UK. Red Cat says Steyr's engines fit its uncrewed boats.

Management said 2026 is a second-half story, as 2025 was, and reaffirmed $150 to $180 million of revenue. With $35.7 million in the first half, the second half has to bring $114 to $144 million, more than three times the first. The chief executive said the company had $50 to $80 million of drones that could ship immediately if orders came. The third quarter, reported on November 16, is the first test.

In the first half the company used $78.7 million in operations and spent $12.6 million on capital projects, while raising about $264 million from new shares. It ended June with $325.6 million of cash.

Which numbers to trust

  • Revenue guidance is not a backlog. The $150 to $180 million depends on orders that were not yet signed in August. I assume $135 million, about 10% below the low end, because when the chief executive described the drones ready to ship, he tied them to contracts the company was still hoping to sign.
  • Gross margin is the real test. At 16% the company loses money on every dollar of overhead. Management targets about 30% late in 2026, helped by volume and by higher-margin boats.
  • Cash use includes inventory. Much of the first-half outflow went into $50 million more inventory. If it sells, cash comes back; if not, it is a write-down waiting to happen.
  • Share-based pay is real cost. It was $4.8 million in the first quarter alone. I add it back to cash and charge it as 2% dilution a year instead.
  • The fiscal year changed. Red Cat now reports on the calendar year, so older reports use a year ending in April and are not directly comparable.

After Steyr, Red Cat would have little cash left and a business that, on my Base, still uses about $52 million in 2027. That is why my Base assumes $150 million of new shares, priced at $3.75, before the business funds itself. At a lower price the dilution is larger: each $0.75 lower issue price costs about $0.15 a share in the Base.

Steyr itself is a sound but small business. It guides 2026 revenue of €56 to €61 million at an 8% to 12% operating margin, but after timing shifts in international defense projects it made €35.3 million of revenue and €1.5 million of adjusted operating profit in the first nine months, so the year depends on a strong fourth quarter. It reports an order backlog of more than €300 million to 2030, about €200 million of it legally binding. The price is roughly 3.5 times this year's guided revenue.

Primary valuation: the path to 2029

In the Base, Red Cat's own revenue grows from $135 million in 2026 and Steyr adds its engines from 2027, for $293 million in 2027, $390 million in 2028 and $470 million in 2029. The operating margin moves from -12% to 3% to 10% as gross margin reaches about 30% and costs grow more slowly than sales. I value 2029 operating profit at 18 times, a rate for a growing defense supplier, and discount everything at 13%. The Base value is $3.52.

Cross-check: what the assets are worth

A pre-profit company has no earnings to cross-check, so the second view looks at what stands behind each share after the Steyr purchase: about $26 million of net cash at the end of 2026, Steyr at its $230 million cost, and half the book value of inventory and equipment. That is about $2.06 a share. It is not a fair value, because it ignores the cash still to be burned, but it shows how much of today's price rests on the future rather than on what the company owns.

Valuation triangulation

  • Primary (path to 2029, Base): $3.52. This is the central value.
  • Asset check after Steyr: about $2.06 a share.
  • Nearby assumptions (2029 margin 8% to 12%, multiple 16 to 20 times): $2.67 to $4.50.
  • Market value less June net cash: about $300 million, or about 1.8 times the midpoint of 2026 revenue guidance, before Steyr.

How the buy price is set

Confidence is D, which carries a default margin of safety of 55%. I add nothing to it, because the funding risk sits in the new shares and the scenarios. That gives a maximum buy price of $1.58.

With the 2029 margin between 8% and 12% and a multiple between 16 and 20 times, the same margin gives buy prices from $1.20 to $2.02. The price of $4.00, and $3.43 on November 5, are above all of them, so the decision does not depend on which is right. The 52-week range is $3.26 to $18.78.

Red Cat pays no dividend, and with new shares likely, holders should expect their share of the company to keep shrinking until it earns money.

Reasons to own Red Cat

  • Revenue grew about six times in a year, driven by Black Widow deliveries under the U.S. Army's Short Range Reconnaissance program.
  • Manufacturing capacity has grown about fivefold, and $85 million of inventory is ready to ship against new orders.
  • Blue Ops boats and Steyr engines give it a second business at sea, with Steyr's backlog of more than €300 million.
  • Gross margin improved for two quarters and management targets about 30% by the end of 2026.
  • The share price has fallen about 80% from its 52-week high, so a successful second half would be a large surprise to the market.

What could go wrong?

  • The second half. Guidance needs more than three times the first half's revenue, and orders, not capacity, decide that.
  • Funding. After Steyr, cash is thin while the business still burns money. New shares at today's price would dilute holders heavily.
  • Customer concentration. The U.S. Army bought about half of first-half output; a program delay hits revenue directly.
  • The Steyr price. About 3.5 times guided revenue for a business that earned an adjusted operating margin of about 4% in the first nine months and needs a strong fourth quarter to meet its own guidance.
  • Inventory. $85 million of drones built ahead of orders could need write-downs if the designs or orders change.

Management execution

  • Production scaled fast: new facilities, nine products and quarterly revenue of $20 million from almost nothing a year earlier.
  • Guidance relies heavily on the back half of the year again, as in 2025, which makes each year a single bet on timing.
  • The share count rose 53% in a year. The chief executive adopted a plan in the first quarter to sell up to 1.8 million shares and has a forward sale contract on up to 1.5 million more.
  • The Steyr talks were broken off in July and revived in October as an all-cash offer, while the drone business is still loss-making.
  • Disclosure is optimistic in tone and light on backlog and program-level detail.

On my ten-point management check (two points each), Red Cat scores 7 of 20: credit for building production, little for capital allocation, dilution and forecast reliability.

What I would watch from here

The third quarter report on November 16 should show four things for the Base to hold: revenue of at least $40 million, gross margin above 20%, guidance for 2026 of at least $135 million, and a clear plan for funding after Steyr. The Steyr acceptance period ends on December 18.

Green

  • Fourth-quarter revenue above $80 million with gross margin near 30%.
  • New Army or international orders that reduce inventory.
  • Funding for 2027 secured without selling shares below $3.

Yellow

  • 2026 revenue guidance cut below $150 million.
  • Inventory above $100 million at year end.
  • Steyr acceptance below 75%, leaving minority shareholders.

Red

  • Third-quarter revenue below $25 million.
  • A large share sale below $2.50.
  • A delay or cut in the Army's Short Range Reconnaissance program.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. The second half delivers guidance and gross margin reaches 30%, which would move the value toward the Bull and make today's price cheap.
  2. Revenue falls well short and the company has to sell shares at low prices to fund 2027, which leads toward the Bear.
  3. Steyr does not deliver its backlog, so Red Cat has spent most of its cash on a business that adds little profit.

The weakest point is the 2029 margin, because two points move the value by about $0.60 and the company has never been profitable. The strongest bull argument is that demand for small military drones is real and Red Cat already holds the Army program. The strongest bear argument is that the company is spending its cash on an acquisition before proving its core business can fund itself.

Final verdict: hold at $4.00

Red Cat has the product, the factory and the customer. What it does not yet have is a quarter that proves the factory can sell at a profit, and after Steyr it will not have much of a cash cushion while it waits. The old buy price came within reach just as the reasons for it changed.

Verdict: HOLD at $4.00, watchlist for new money. Base $3.52, 12.0% below the price, Bear $1.01, Bull $8.46, asset check about $2.06, Confidence D (11.25/25). Required margin of safety 55%, maximum buy price $1.58, range $1.20 to $2.02 under nearby assumptions.

New money buys only at or below $1.58. For an existing holder the original cost is irrelevant: with the price near the Base, the holding is a bet on the second half and on funding, and I would size it as one. The next tests are the third quarter report on November 16 and the Steyr acceptance period ending December 18.
