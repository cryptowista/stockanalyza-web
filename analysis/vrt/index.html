Vertiv reported a quarter on October 28 that beat its own sales guide by $563 million: sales up 61% to $4.31 billion, orders more than doubled, guidance raised on every key measure. The shares are still 48.3% below their May 14 close of $376.23. A fall like that after a quarter like that looks like a gift. My model says it is a fair price, and the first reason is cash: Vertiv guides $2.7 billion of free cash flow for 2026, while the cash I count is about $1.9 billion.

At $194.54 the shares trade 11.2% above my weighted Base value of $175.00. The Bear value of $75.21 is 61.3% below the price and the Bull value of $291.68 is 49.9% above it. I assign no probabilities to the cases. Confidence is grade C, which gives a required margin of safety of 42.5% and a maximum buy price of $100.62. My rules say hold between that price and about $227, and trim above it. The price is inside the hold zone, closer to its upper end than to its lower one.

Valuation route: an industrial equipment maker that sells to a cyclical group of customers (data center builders), valued on owner free cash flow: adjusted operating profit after stock pay, taxed at 23%, less capital spending above depreciation, working capital and acquisitions. Primary method: a ten-year path with a terminal value, discounted at 10%. Cross-check: a multiple of owner free cash flow at the end of 2031, discounted back. Both are tested against a reverse valuation. Values are present fair-value estimates, not 12-month price targets.

What Vertiv is

Vertiv makes the equipment that keeps data centers powered and cool: uninterruptible power supplies, power distribution, thermal management including liquid cooling, prefabricated modules, and the services that maintain them. In the third quarter the Americas were $2,988.8 million of the $4,312.8 million of sales, with organic growth of 66.2% and an adjusted operating margin of 29.4%. Asia Pacific was $779.3 million (organic growth 48.3%, margin 17.0%) and EMEA $544.7 million (19.3%, 28.0%). Services were $675.7 million of the quarter.

Growth has two sources. About 55 points of the quarter's 61% came from organic growth and about 6 from acquisitions. Management is also building capacity: capital spending is guided at about 4.0% of revenue for 2026, and it described initial revenue opportunities for its 800 volt direct current power products in 2027 and broader deployments in 2028. On the Q3 call it also said 2027 should bring above-market sales growth and wider margins.

Vertiv stopped reporting orders and backlog each quarter in February 2026. The last backlog figure is $15.0 billion at December 31, 2025, which was about 1.07 times the $14.0 billion 2026 sales guide in place before the third-quarter raise. The Q3 release gives a growth rate for organic orders (more than 100%) but no amount, so the visibility that backlog gave in 2025 now has to be taken on trust.

Historical cross-check

The stock has moved violently. Its highest close of the past year is $376.23 (May 14, 2026) and the lowest is $149.83 (December 17, 2025). On November 5 it fell 14.0% to $176.73 in a session when the S&P 500 lost 4.4%, and Trefis found no company event behind it. On November 16 it fell 6.6% with other electrical equipment makers (Eaton, Emerson and nVent fell 3.4% to 4.6%) as NVIDIA turmoil weighed on AI stocks. Defense World shows a beta of 2.11.

Before the Q3 report TIKR put the forward price-to-earnings ratio at 26 times, against a five-year average of 27. At 27 times my 2027 adjusted earnings estimate of $9.88 a share, the shares would be $267. I do not use that figure. The average was set while margins were rising and the business was much smaller, and applying a growth-era multiple to margins that are now near their peak counts the same cyclical strength twice. At $194.54 the shares are on 27.2 times the 2026 guide of $7.15 and 19.7 times my 2027 estimate. A peer table on a matched basis is not available to me from free sources, so I do not use one.

A growth-adjusted ratio looks cheap (a PEG of about 0.7, from 27.2 times earnings and 38% growth from $7.15 to my $9.88), but I give it no weight. It rests on a one-year step in a build-out cycle, and a PEG on a cyclical rebound overstates how long growth lasts.

Volatility and options

I did not have current options data for Vertiv, so I cannot show what the options market expects for the next year. The history says enough: a fall of 48.3% from the May 14 close, a 14.0% fall on November 5, a 9.6% gain on November 9 and a 6.6% fall on November 16. Reasons to own Vertiv

  • Demand is accelerating: organic sales growth went from 18% in the second quarter to 55% in the third, and organic orders more than doubled.
  • Margins are widening: the adjusted operating margin was 25.2% against 22.3% a year earlier, with 29.4% in the Americas.
  • The balance sheet funds the expansion: $783 million of net cash after $425 million of capital spending in nine months.
  • Management has beaten its own guide: the third-quarter sales were $563 million above it, and the year's sales guide has been raised by $650 million.
  • The price is 48.3% below its May close and my Bull value is 50% above the price.

What could go wrong?

  • The AI build-out. The November sell-offs came with no company news; they followed worries about AI spending and political risk, and a pause by a few large customers would hit orders first and sales later.
  • Less disclosure. Vertiv stopped reporting backlog and orders amounts in February, so the best evidence of future sales is gone.
  • Cash flow built on advances. $2.3 billion of the rise in deferred revenue in nine months funds inventory that is up $1.8 billion. If advances slow, free cash flow falls faster than profit.
  • Execution. After the second quarter the stock fell more than 16% in a day on a revenue miss and concerns about project execution, and the third quarter then beat the guide by 15%.
  • Supply. Trefis lists reliance on single-source suppliers as a key risk; Vertiv signed a three-year agreement with Infineon on November 4 to secure power semiconductors.
  • Acquisitions. About 6 points of third-quarter growth came from acquired businesses, and contingent consideration cost $96.1 million in nine months.
  • Margin. The adjusted margin of 25.2% compares with 22.3% a year earlier and 19.3% over the first nine months of 2025. Each point of margin in every year is worth about $8 a share.

Management execution

The chief executive is Giordano Albertazzi and the executive chairman is Dave Cote. Management raised the 2026 sales guide by $650 million in one quarter and delivered the third quarter $563 million above the middle of its outlook. The offsets are one weak quarter (the second, with a revenue miss), the end of backlog disclosure and steady director selling. Assessment: 15/20, good. The next test is whether free cash flow holds up once customers' advances stop growing.

What I would watch from here

The next results are for the fourth quarter and the full year, expected in February. They need to show sales inside or above $4,313 million to $4,513 million, an adjusted operating margin of 25.3% to 26.3%, and a 2027 sales guide near $19 billion with a margin above 25%.

Green: moves me toward the buy zone

  • Fourth-quarter sales at or above $4.41 billion and a 2027 guide of $19 billion or more with a margin of 25.5% or more.
  • Deferred revenue steady or rising while inventory grows no faster than sales.
  • Adjusted free cash flow for 2026 at or above $2.7 billion, with a clear statement of how much is working capital.
  • A price at or below $100.62 with the business executing.

Yellow

  • Sales in the lower half of the guide ($4.31 billion to $4.41 billion) or a 2027 sales guide of $16.6 billion to $19 billion.
  • An adjusted operating margin of 23% to 25.5%.
  • Inventory growing faster than sales, or net cash below $300 million.

Red

  • Sales below $4.31 billion, a cut to the 2026 guide or a 2027 guide below $16.6 billion.
  • An adjusted operating margin below 23% in a quarter.
  • Deferred revenue falling by more than $500 million in a quarter without matching sales, or customers cancelling or rescheduling large orders.

Today none of my red triggers is hit. Two readings are yellow: inventory has grown 126% since December against nine-month sales growth of 39%, and about $0.8 billion of the guided free cash flow comes from advances, a lower tax rate and acquisitions left out. Organic growth, margin and net cash are green.

The three most important thesis breakers

  1. A pause in large customers' AI spending. 2027 sales of $16.6 billion with margins back to 2025 levels give a Bear of $75.21.
  2. A fall in margin. Two points lower in every year take the Base to $158.41, and four points to $141.82.
  3. A reversal of customers' advances together with an inventory write-down. The advances alone cost about $5.0 a share, but the signal would be a sharp drop in orders.
